The fail-short condition causes high current, resulting in an overload condition that turns off the SMPS – but, at the same time, damage continues and the capacitor burns open. Subsequently, maintenance personnel declare a no fault found (NFF)/cannot duplicate (CND) repair condition. The SMPS tests okay – the DC output voltage, ripple voltage, noise levels, and regulation of the supply under loading conditions are all within specifications. Upon further inspection after disassembling the circuit card assembly (CCA) – an enclosed metal frame – physical evidence in the form of color and smell reveals 1 of 22 parallel-connected capacitors has failed. Removal (desoldering) and testing of the capacitor confirms the capacitor has burned open, which reduces the effective total capacitance in the output filter.

Also suppose the customer specifications for prognostic enabling of the SMPS includes the following requirements: (i) the prognostic system shall detect degradation at least 200 operational days prior to functional failure; and (ii) the prognostic system shall provide estimates of RUL that shall converge to within 5% accuracy at least 72 operational days, ±2 days, before functional failure occurs. Functional failure is defined as a level of damage at which an object, such as a power supply, no longer operates within specifications.
