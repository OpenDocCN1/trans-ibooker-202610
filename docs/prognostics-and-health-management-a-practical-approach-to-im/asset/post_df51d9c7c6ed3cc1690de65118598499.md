
$$
\frac{\partial^2 l}{\partial V^2}=\frac{N}{2V^2}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{V^3}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(t_k-\mu)^2
$$

At the point $(\mu^*,V^*)$,

$$
\frac{\partial^2 l}{\partial\mu^2}=-\frac{N}{V},\quad \frac{\partial^2 l}{\partial\mu\partial V}=0,\quad \frac{\partial^2 l}{\partial V^2}=\frac{N}{2V^2}-\frac{1}{V^3}NV=-\frac{N}{2V^2}
$$

So

$$
I(\mu,V)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{N}{V}&0\\0&\frac{N}{2V^2}\end{pmatrix}
$$

with inverse

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(\mu,V)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{V}{N}&0\\0&\frac{2V^2}{N}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\sigma^2}{N}&0\\0&\frac{2\sigma^4}{N}\end{pmatrix}
$$

Therefore

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\mu)=\frac{\sigma^2}{N},\quad \operatorname{Var}(\sigma^2)=\frac{2\sigma^4}{N},\quad \operatorname{Cov}(\mu,\sigma^2)=0
$$

## 1.5 Expected Number of Failures

The expected number of failures in a given time period $[0,t]$ is an important characteristic of the reliable operation of an object, which may be an element, a unit, a block, or even an entire system. Clearly, this expectation depends on the way we react to failures. Three cases will be discussed in this section: minimal repair, partial repair, and failure replacement. We will also include lengths of time needed to perform repairs or replacements.

The material in this section is based on renewal theory, the fundamentals of which can be found, for example, in Cox (1970) and Ross (1996).

### 1.5.1 Minimal Repair

Consider first the case of minimal repairs, where each repair requires $T$ time when the object is not operational. Let $S_1$ denote the time of the first failure, $M(t)$ the expected number of failures in the interval $[0,t]$, and $X_t$ the actual number of failures in this interval. Then

$$
M(t)=E(X_t)=E_{S_1}\bigl(E(X_t\mid S_1)\bigr) \tag{1.78}
$$

where

$$
E(X_t\mid S_1)=\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } t<S_1\\
1 & \text{if } S_1<t<S_1+T\\
1+M(t-T)-M(S_1) & \text{if } t>S_1+T
\end{cases} \tag{1.79}
$$

Therefore

$$
M(t)=\int_0^{t-T}\bigl(1+M(t-T)-M(s)\bigr)f(s)\,ds+\int_{t-T}^{t}f(s)\,ds
$$

where $f(t)$ is the PDF of the TTF so that

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t)&=F(t-T)\bigl(1+M(t-T)\bigr)-\int_{0}^{t-T}M(s)f(s)\,ds+F(t)-F(t-T)\\
&=M(t-T)F(t-T)+F(t)-\int_{0}^{t-T}M(s)f(s)\,ds
\end{aligned} \tag{1.80}
$$

In the special case when $T=0$, this integral equation is modified as

$$
M(t)=M(t)F(t)+F(t)-\int_{0}^{t}M(s)f(s)\,ds \tag{1.81}
$$

where $M(0)=0$. We will show that with the failure rate $\rho(\tau)$,

$$
M(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\rho(\tau)\,d\tau \tag{1.82}
$$

solves this equation by substitution. Notice first that based on the integration domain shown in Figure 1.15,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{0}^{t}M(s)f(s)\,ds
&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\rho(\tau)\,d\tau\,f(s)\,ds
=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\tau}^{t}\rho(\tau)f(s)\,ds\,d\tau\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}\rho(\tau)\bigl[F(t)-F(\tau)\bigr]\,d\tau
=\int_{0}^{t}\rho(\tau)\,d\tau\,F(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\rho(\tau)\bigl(1-R(\tau)\bigr)\,d\tau\\
&=M(t)F(t)-M(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f(\tau)}{R(\tau)}R(\tau)\,d\tau
=M(t)F(t)-M(t)+F(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

so the right side of Eq. (1.81) becomes

$$
M(t)F(t)+F(t)-\bigl[M(t)F(t)-M(t)+F(t)\bigr]=M(t)
$$

The simple formula in Eq. (1.82) shows that $\{X_t\}$ is a non-homogeneous Poisson process with arrival rate $\rho(t)$, which has several important consequences.

If the failures are independent, then $X_t$ is a Poisson variable with expectation $\lambda=M(t)$, so

$$
P(X_t=n)=\frac{M(t)^n}{n!}e^{-M(t)} \tag{1.83}
$$

Assume that the repairable failures occur at times $0<t_1<t_2<\cdots$ and repairs are done instantly. Let $F_k(t)$ denote the CDF of $t_k$. Then

$$
F_k(t)=P(t_k\le t)=P(X_t\ge k)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\frac{M(t)^n}{n!}e^{-M(t)} \tag{1.84}
$$

and the corresponding PDF is its derivative,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_k(t)=F_k'(t)
&=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\left(\frac{nM(t)^{n-1}}{n!}\rho(t)e^{-M(t)}+\frac{M(t)^n}{n!}e^{-M(t)}(-\rho(t))\right)\\
&=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\left(\frac{M(t)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}-\frac{M(t)^n}{n!}\right)e^{-M(t)}\rho(t)\\
&=\frac{M(t)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}e^{-M(t)}\rho(t)
\end{aligned} \tag{1.85}
$$

### Example 1.12

In the special case when $X$ is exponential, $\rho(t)=\lambda$ and $M(t)=\lambda t$, so

$$
f_k(t)=\frac{(\lambda t)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\lambda e^{-\lambda t}=\frac{\lambda^k t^{k-1}e^{-\lambda t}}{(k-1)!} \tag{1.86}
$$

which is a gamma PDF with parameters $k$ and $\theta=1/\lambda$.

### Example 1.13

If $X$ is Weibull, then $\rho(t)=\frac{\beta}{\eta^\beta}t^{\beta-1}$ and $M(t)=\left(\frac{t}{\eta}\right)^\beta$, so

$$
f_k(t)=\frac{\left(\frac{t}{\eta}\right)^{\beta(k-1)}}{(k-1)!}\frac{\beta}{\eta^\beta}t^{\beta-1}e^{-\left(\frac{t}{\eta}\right)^\beta}
=\frac{\beta t^{\beta k-1}e^{-\left(\frac{t}{\eta}\right)^\beta}}{(k-1)!\,\eta^{\beta k}} \tag{1.87}
$$

### 1.5.2 Failure Replacement

Assume next that upon failures, failure replacement is performed, which requires $T$ time to complete. Then, similar to the previous case,

$$
E(X_t\mid S_1)=\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } t<S_1\\
1 & \text{if } S_1<t<S_1+T\\
1+E(X_{t-S_1-T}) & \text{if } t>S_1+T
\end{cases} \tag{1.88}
$$

Therefore, Eq. (1.78) implies that

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t)
&=\int_{t-T}^{t}f(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t-T}\bigl(1+M(t-s-T)\bigr)f(s)\,ds\\
&=F(t)-F(t-T)+F(t-T)+\int_{0}^{t-T}M(t-s-T)f(s)\,ds\\
&=F(t)+\int_{0}^{t-T}M(t-s-T)f(s)\,ds
\end{aligned}
$$

If $t\le T$, then the integral is zero, so

$$
M(t)=\begin{cases}
F(t) & \text{if } t\le T\\[2pt]
\displaystyle F(t)+\int_{0}^{t-T}M(t-T-s)f(s)\,ds & \text{if } t>T
\end{cases} \tag{1.89}
$$

If replacement is instantaneous, then $T=0$, so

$$
M(t)=F(t)+\int_{0}^{t}M(t-s)f(s)\,ds \tag{1.90}
$$

gives the equation for $M(t)$. There is no simple formula for the solution of this integral equation. However, the use of Laplace transforms provides an interesting insight. Let $M^*(s)$, $F^*(s)$, and $f^*(s)$ denote the Laplace transforms of $M(t)$, $F(t)$, and $f(t)$, respectively. Then from Eq. (1.90), we have

$$
M^*(s)=F^*(s)+M^*(s)f^*(s)
$$

implying that

$$
M^*(s)=\frac{F^*(s)}{1-f^*(s)}=\frac{\frac{1}{s}f^*(s)}{1-f^*(s)}
$$

If $g(t)$ denotes the inverse Laplace transform of $\frac{f^*(s)}{1-f^*(s)}$, then

$$
M(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau \tag{1.91}
$$

showing that $\{X_t\}$ is again a non-homogeneous Poisson process with arrival rate $g(t)$.

Let $0<t_1<t_2<\cdots$ denote times of failures. Then $z_k=t_{k+1}-t_k$ ($k=0,1,\ldots$ and $t_0=0$) are independent random variables with PDF $f(t)$. Then the density $f_k(t)$ of $t_k$ is the $k$-fold convolution of $f(t)$:

$$
f_k(t)=f(t)*f(t)*\cdots*f(t)
$$

Notice that

$$
E(t_k)=E(z_1)+E(z_2)+\cdots+E(z_k)=E(z_1)k=\mu k,
$$

where $\mu$ is the TTF mean value. Similarly

$$
\operatorname{Var}(t_k)=\operatorname{Var}(z_1)+\operatorname{Var}(z_2)+\cdots+\operatorname{Var}(z_k)=\operatorname{Var}(z_1)k=\sigma^2 k
$$

where $\sigma^2$ is the TTF variance value. If $z_k$ is exponential, then $t_k$ is gamma; if $z_k$ is gamma, then $t_k$ is also gamma; and if $z_k$ is normal, then $t_k$ is also a normal variable.

Based on these values, in other cases the PDF of $t_k$ can be estimated by a lognormal or gamma variable with a given expectation and variance; and confidence intervals can be constructed for any significance level selected by the user.

If repairs or replacements need $T$ time to perform, then the time of the $k$th failure is $\bar{t}_k=t_k+(k-1)T$, with the CDF

$$
\bar{F}_k(t)=P\bigl(t_k+(k-1)T\le t\bigr)=P\bigl(t_k\le t-(k-1)T\bigr)
=\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } t\le (k-1)T\\[2pt]
F_k\bigl(t-(k-1)T\bigr) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \tag{1.92}
$$

where $F_k(t)$ is the CDF of $f_k(t)$.

### 1.5.3 Decreased Number of Failures Due to Partial Repairs

Assume next that partial repairs are performed, resulting in the number of failures decreasing by a factor of $\alpha<1$. Then, similar to the previous cases,

$$
E(X_t\mid S_1)=\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } t<S_1\\
1 & \text{if } S_1<t<S_1+T\\
1+\alpha\bigl(M(t-T)-M(S_1)\bigr) & \text{if } t>S_1+T
\end{cases} \tag{1.93}
$$

So

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t)
&=\int_{t-T}^{t}f(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t-T}\Bigl[1+\alpha\bigl(M(t-T)-M(s)\bigr)\Bigr]f(s)\,ds\\
&=F(t)-F(t-T)+F(t-T)+\alpha M(t-T)F(t-T)-\alpha\int_{0}^{t-T}M(s)f(s)\,ds\\
&=\begin{cases}
F(t) & \text{if } t\le T\\[2pt]
F(t)+\alpha M(t-T)F(t-T)-\alpha\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-T}M(s)f(s)\,ds & \text{if } t>T
\end{cases}
\end{aligned} \tag{1.94}
$$

Assume again instantaneous repairs with $T=0$. Then (1.94) is modified as

$$
M(t)=F(t)+\alpha M(t)F(t)-\alpha\int_{0}^{t}M(s)f(s)\,ds \tag{1.95}
$$

### 1.5.4 Decreased Age Due to Partial Repairs

Consider finally the case where partial repairs decrease the effective age of the object by a factor $\alpha<1$. Then we have

$$
E(X_t\mid S_1)=\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } t<S_1\\
1 & \text{if } S_1<t<S_1+T\\
1+M\bigl(t-S_1-T+\alpha S_1\bigr)-M(\alpha S_1) & \text{if } t>S_1+T
\end{cases} \tag{1.96}
$$

since at time $t$ the effective age is $(t-S_1-T)+\alpha S_1$, where the first term is the length of interval $[S_1+T,t]$ and the second term is the effective age at time $S_1+T$ after repair. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t)
&=\int_{t-T}^{t}f(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t-T}\Bigl[1+M\bigl(t-s-T+\alpha s\bigr)-M(\alpha s)\Bigr]f(s)\,ds\\
&=\begin{cases}
F(t) & \text{if } t\le T\\[2pt]
F(t)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-T}\Bigl[M\bigl(t-s-T+\alpha s\bigr)-M(\alpha s)\Bigr]f(s)\,ds & \text{if } t>T
\end{cases}
\end{aligned} \tag{1.97}
$$

If $T=0$, then

$$
M(t)=F(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl[M\bigl(t-s+\alpha s\bigr)-M(\alpha s)\Bigr]f(s)\,ds \tag{1.98}
$$

Notice that the case $\alpha=1$ corresponds to minimal repair, and Eqs. (1.95) and (1.98) reduce to Eq. (1.81) as they should. Equations (1.81), (1.90), and (1.95) are usually called the renewal equations (Mitov and Omey 2014).
