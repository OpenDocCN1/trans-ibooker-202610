# KEY TAKEAWAY

Scaling is not about replication. It is about thoughtful, informed re-contextualisation that respects both the theory behind the intervention and the unique features of each new environment.

# Building a Coalition of Partners

# EXAMPLE IN CONTEXT

As The Literacy Links Project prepared to expand, the team recognised the need to build a coalition of partners who could share ownership of the work. An initial Scaling Forum was held at the local Education Centre, bringing together principals, literacy leads, and special education teachers from the five participating schools. St Brigid’s educators shared short classroom videos and honest reflections from the pilot, helping to build trust and spark discussion.

In small groups, participants identified both opportunities and challenges, such as limited release time and supporting children with diverse language needs. By the end of the session, the group had agreed on shared priorities such as maintaining the focus on explicit phonemic instruction, embedding professional learning, and keeping communication open between schools. A shared online space and monthly check-ins were established to sustain collaboration and ensure scaling remained a collective effort rather than an imposed initiative.

Scaling depends on buy-in from those involved, and without a coalition of committed partners, projects can struggle to take off. In DBR, partners are not simply passive recipients of an intervention; instead, they are co-designers, implementers, and long-term advocates of the work.
