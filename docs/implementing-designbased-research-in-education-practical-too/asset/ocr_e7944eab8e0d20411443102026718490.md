# Experiential Research

This approach draws on Design Thinking rather than educational research approaches. In Design Thinking, this is termed Immersive Empathy. The goal here is to immerse yourself in the research context and undertake the activities and processes in that context yourself in order to understand and empathise with your research partners more fully. Shadowing a student is a very good example of this. If your research context is a school, take a typical student timetable and follow it for a day. This can allow you to experience the mental, physical, sensory, and social demands of a school day: what it is like moving from class to class mentally and physically, how much time you have to interact with others during the day, where and when you can eat or rest, where or when you get any time to move or stand or walk. Without living that experience, it can be difficult to understand or empathise with the young learners in the setting. It is important to try to immerse yourself as much as possible in the environment: take your time, wear the appropriate clothes, bring the appropriate equipment, get involved, insofar as is possible, in the activities. It might be necessary to simulate some aspects of the experience to make it as complete as possible. For example, what is the school day experience like if you are visually impaired or hungry or have a mobility issue or don’t speak the language of instruction or have trouble taking things down from the board? You can take steps to simulate these experiences as best you can. Of course, there are limitations to how realistic it is to immerse yourself fully in the experience, but even taking that step to be fully out and embedded in the real-world research context is valuable and can generate insights and promote empathy.

# PRACTICAL TIPS: DOS AND DON'TS

Dos

+   1. Consider your personal biases and how to mitigate the effects of these.
+   2. Plan for the immersive activity carefully. You will probably need to observe the setting in order to determine where and when you should join and what you will need.
