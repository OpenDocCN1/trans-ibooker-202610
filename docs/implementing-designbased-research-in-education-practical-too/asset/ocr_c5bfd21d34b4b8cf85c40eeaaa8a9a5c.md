DBR encourages researchers to remain open to evolution while recognising that even successful interventions will likely require adjustment in response to different contexts. It also highlights the need for feedback loops at every stage of the scale-up, where insights from each implementation cycle contribute to the ongoing development of the design (Collins et al., 2004). Importantly, seeing scaling as a continuous aspect of the design process also empowers teachers. Rather than simply delivering a packaged programme or intervention, teachers are invited to engage in iterative, context-sensitive decision-making. This cultivates professional agency and promotes a deeper understanding of the intervention’s purpose and potential. Furthermore, by maintaining a design mindset during the scale-up, research teams are better positioned to capture the complexity and variability of educational environments, which leads to more nuanced findings and strengthens the external validity of the intervention.
