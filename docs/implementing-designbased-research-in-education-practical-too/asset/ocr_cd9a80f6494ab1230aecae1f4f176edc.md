# Structuring the Review

Creating a clear structure is an important first step. It can reduce the overwhelming feeling that often comes with searching for knowledge gaps across a large body of academic literature, while also helping you track your progress. Whether the review is written as a thesis chapter or an academic paper, a typical structure includes an abstract, introduction, methods, findings, discussion, and conclusion.

The methods section of the review is particularly significant. It should detail the type of review conducted, search strategies used, inclusion/exclusion criteria, approaches to analysis and any limitations. Always check with your institution and the target journal's guidelines for required formats of expectations.

# The Rapid Review Approach

Literature reviews help address urgent questions by drawing together current, evidence-based research. While systematic reviews often involve large teams and extended timelines, a rapid review can be conducted by a single researcher working within tighter constraints. Even so, careful planning is required to maintain quality and transparency.

Wollscheid and Tripney (2021) note that rapid reviews are more prone to bias than systematic reviews, largely because they cover a smaller portion of the literature. To reduce this risk and increase rigour, researchers can follow established reporting frameworks such as the PRISMA guidelines (Liberati et al., 2009). PRISMA provides a systematic way of documenting each stage of the review, from search strategy to inclusion criteria.

Using a structured approach like PRISMA not only strengthens transparency but also makes it possible for others to replicate the process. In theory, if the same criteria outlined in this review were applied again by another researcher, the results should be identical. Reporting in this manner enhances both the reliability and credibility of the review (Levac et al., 2010).

Rapid reviews can be efficient and practical, but following a clear reporting framework is essential to safeguard rigour and trustworthiness.
