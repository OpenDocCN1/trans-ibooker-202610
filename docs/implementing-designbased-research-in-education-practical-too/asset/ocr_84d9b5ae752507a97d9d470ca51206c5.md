# Design and Construction Phase

The design and construction phase is the second crucial step in the DBR process (McKenney & Reeves, 2012). It builds upon the insights gained during the analysis and exploration phase, moving from conceptual understanding to a tangible intervention. At this stage, researchers and partners collaboratively translate theory and data into actionable designs, producing prototypes that can be trialled, critiqued, and refined. Unlike traditional research approaches that often separate design from implementation, DBR treats design as an evolving, theory-informed process shaped by iterative testing and adaptation (Collins et al., 2004).

This section explores the core components of effective design:

+   - Foundations of the design phase: from theory to practice;
+   - Developing prototypes: iterative and adaptive designs;
+   - Collaborative engagement in the design process;
+   - Iterative design cycles.

# Foundations of the Design Phase: From Theory to Practice

The design process begins with translating theoretical frameworks into practical, classroom-ready solutions. Drawing from the earlier analysis, researchers establish clear objectives that address the identified educational challenge and map them to the relevant learning theories. Equally important is ensuring that designs are data-driven. Evidence gathered during the needs analysis, such as learner performance gaps, educator feedback, or contextual barriers, directly informs design decisions. For example, if the assessment data reveal that learners struggle with transferring abstract knowledge into practice, the intervention might incorporate simulations, guided enquiry, or modelling strategies. In this way, theory and data are interwoven to ensure the design is pedagogically robust and contextually relevant (Plomp, 2013; Reeves, 2006).

# Key Considerations for Design

+   - **Alignment with Theoretical Frameworks**: Ground the intervention in established theories to enhance pedagogical soundness.
