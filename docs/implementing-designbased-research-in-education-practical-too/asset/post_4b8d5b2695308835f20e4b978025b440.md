
- **Shared Learning**: Drawing on the strengths of researchers, educators, and learners to bridge theoretical knowledge with practical application.
- **Ongoing Reflection**: Creating space for both individual and collective reflection throughout the project.

Engaging collaboratively on topics that may not be familiar to all participants presents an opportunity to see problems from new perspectives and develop more inclusive solutions.

Collaboration becomes truly meaningful when it extends beyond dialogue and begins to shape real choices. This is where *shared decision-making* takes centre stage, transforming discussion into collective action that drives the DBR process forward.

## The Role of Research Meetings

Research meetings provide the main structure for shared decision-making. Held at different phases of the project, they allow participants to:

- Share emerging data;
- Test new ideas;
- Refine solutions collaboratively.

When these meetings are conducted with professionalism and reciprocity, they:

- Foster trust and mutual respect;
- Make space for diverse voices, including quieter or marginalised participants;
- Create mechanisms for addressing challenges such as data inconsistencies, methodological questions, or interpersonal tensions.

Further, true collaboration involves more than hearing all voices; it requires finding ways to balance them. This might mean:

- Developing strategies to mitigate power imbalances;
- Ensuring quieter members have opportunities to contribute; or
- Structuring agendas so that discussions stay focused without silencing creativity.

These practices transform shared decision-making from routine discussion into genuine collective ownership of the DBR process, thus moving beyond surface-level collaboration into action.

- What strategies might you use to address power imbalances or dominant voices during collaborative discussions?

## Collaboration and Feedback

Purposeful questioning is central to DBR. It drives collaborative feedback, stimulates critical thinking, and supports reflection, empowering teams to generate innovative solutions to educational challenges. Integrated into dialogue, questioning strategies not only sharpen ideas but also ensure that emerging solutions remain both practical and theoretically grounded. In this way, questioning forms the foundation of collaboration, creating spaces where diverse perspectives are valued and where collective knowledge leads to stronger design outcomes. ## Chapter Summary

In DBR, the human element is paramount. This chapter has highlighted how ethical awareness, effective communication, shared responsibilities, and reflective engagement with positionality form the foundation of successful research. By recognising the complexity of human interaction, researchers can build strong professional relationships, foster meaningful collaboration, and design interventions that are both context-sensitive and inclusive.

For early career researchers, this means embracing the emotional and intellectual labour that DBR entails. Rather than viewing these challenges as obstacles, they can be seen as opportunities for growth, moments where personal development and evolving worldviews enrich the knowledge co-created with others. Ultimately, DBR is not only about generating solutions for educational practice; it is also about transforming the researchers, educators, and learners who take part in the process.
