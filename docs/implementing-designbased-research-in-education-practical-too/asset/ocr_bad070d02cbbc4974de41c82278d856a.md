# Reflexivity

While positionality acknowledges where we stand as researchers, reflexivity describes how we critically engage with that stance throughout the research process. Reflexivity is the conscious awareness of how one's perspective, thoughts, and assumptions shape all stages of research, from the design to implementation, interpretation, and the representation of findings (Hibbert, 2013). It fosters accountability by recognising that researchers are part of a collective process, not isolated actors. At its core, reflexivity relies on self-reflection, bringing values and beliefs to the surface and ensuring research is conducted with professionalism and integrity.

Continuous reflexive practice is an essential skill that enables critical reflection and ongoing evaluation of one's work (Hibbert, 2013; Sullivan et al., 2016). It involves self-assessment and openness to feedback, even when perspectives differ (McDonagh et al., 2019). While it is nice to talk to like-minded people, engaging with alternative viewpoints broadens our understanding of why we think as we do and may also prompt us to reconsider aspects of our positionality. DBR supports this by centring collaboration, bringing members together around shared goals to improve teaching, learning, and the wider educational community (Bakker, 2018).

By integrating reflexivity and positionality throughout the research process, you can more effectively navigate power dynamics and clarify team roles and responsibilities. This approach fosters trust and transparency while equipping you to address challenges with confidence.

# PAUSE AND REFLECT

Some guiding questions for these reflexive moments might include:

+   - Whose perspectives are represented, and whose may be missing?
+   - How might my assumptions or values be shaping the research design or interpretation?
+   - In what ways could my role or background influence collaboration with others?
+   - Am I open to revising my views in response to evidence or feedback?
+   - How does this research align with the shared objectives of the DBR team and the wider educational community?
