## Reflexive Practice and Research Integrity

Up to this point, the review has been organised around four guiding RQs, presenting findings on purposes, frameworks, key outcomes, and implications. However, reviewing literature is not only about documenting what others have done; it is also about reflecting on how we, as researchers, approach the process. We now turn to this reflexive dimension, emphasising the importance of integrity, transparency, and critical self-awareness. By pausing to reflect on gaps, limitations, and evolving insights, we strengthen both the credibility of the review and our own development as researchers.

### PAUSE AND REFLECT

- Did you identify any aspects of the rapid review that I may have overlooked or important aspects of DBR that I failed to incorporate into my review questions?

Research integrity requires that researchers identify, acknowledge, and report any changes to the original scope of their study. Doing so not only promotes honesty and transparency but also helps others to avoid similar challenges.

For example, in this rapid review, an important gap was the absence of an RQ on design principles. This omission highlights the dynamic nature of research, where critical elements can sometimes be missed despite careful planning. Reflecting on such issues reminds us that research is an ongoing process of learning, whether in academic study, teaching practice, or policy collaboration.

In this case, I revisited the data to assess how design principles featured in the literature and incorporated this analysis into the review. By acknowledging and addressing this oversight, the review is both more transparent and more useful for others. Reflexive practice, therefore, not only enhances research integrity but also strengthens the relevance and impact of DBR studies.

## Addressing any Oversight

In this review, I initially overlooked the importance of including design principles in the guiding RQs. On reflection, I revisited the data and found that, while some articles referred to design principles explicitly, others only implied them or integrated them weakly. This underlines the importance of building attention to design principles into RQs from the outset.

## Design Principles

Of the 14 articles analysed, two studies specifically focused on design principles (Ndabezitha & Gravett, 2024; Poizat et al., 2024). Several others mentioned them in headings but gave little detail (Iskandar, 2024; Parth et al., 2024), while some did not engage with them at all (Agustiningrum, 2024; Kramer, 2024; Martel & Garcias, 2024). A further two studies (Donmez & Cagiltay, 2024; Peperkorn & Wegner, 2024) indicated that their theoretical frameworks were influenced by design principles, and four mentioned them as part of their key findings. One study (Poizat et al., 2024) went further, linking design principles directly to policy contributions.

**EXAMPLE IN CONTEXT**

Poizat et al. (2024) investigated methods to support practitioners in vocational and continuing education and training. The study focused on implementing and assessing enactivist-inspired design principles for training design. DBR was used as a scientific approach to explore how such principles could benefit practice. The findings suggested that enactivist design principles helped strengthen connections between workplace learning, training practices, and broader educational contexts. This study contributes to design knowledge by offering both comprehensive principles for vocational education and more specific enactivist-inspired principles for training design.

Oversights are a natural part of research. What matters is recognising them, documenting them, and, where possible, revisiting the data to strengthen your study.

## Limitations

There were two significant limitations to this rapid review:

- 1. The review was conducted within a six-week timeframe, limiting the comprehensiveness of the search and analysis.
- 2. The review was undertaken by a single researcher. A more rigorous process would include independent validation by a second reviewer (see Gough, 2007).

These limitations mean that the findings should be read as a snapshot of current DBR practices rather than an exhaustive account. Nonetheless, the review provides valuable insights that can guide both researchers and practitioners. The following discussion considers what these findings mean in practice.

## Discussion

This rapid review demonstrates how literature reviews not only consolidate knowledge but also reveal gaps in theory and practice. The diversity of theoretical frameworks across DBR studies illustrates both the richness of the field and the challenge of making sense of it. For example, encountering models such as the Four-Phase Model of Interest (Tillmann & Wegner, 2024) and the Enactive Perspective on Knowing, Learning, and Training (Poizat et al., 2024) highlighted areas that were initially unfamiliar to the researcher, underlining how literature reviews broaden professional knowledge and shape emerging study concepts.

The studies reviewed suggest that DBR can support both professional development and contextual innovation (Agustiningrum et al., 2024). At the same time, success is strongly influenced by teacher commitment, workload, and the degree to which theory and practice are meaningfully integrated. This finding highlights the need for long-term investment in professional development, particularly to prepare teachers for technology-rich and student-centred environments (Jita & Dhliwayo, 2024). For early career researchers, this demonstrates the importance of recognising not only opportunities but also the contextual barriers that may constrain innovation.

Another important reflection is the initial omission of design principles from the review questions. Addressing this gap strengthened the analysis and highlighted how design principles function as a bridge between theory and practice in DBR. They guide the development of interventions and ensure findings are transferable to real-world settings. Their initial absence underscores how easily critical elements can be overlooked and how essential reflexivity is to the research process.

In essence, a literature review is not just an academic exercise but a formative process that shapes the researcher’s knowledge, identity, and practice. By engaging critically and reflexively, researchers can create reviews that are both rigorous and personally transformative, laying a stronger foundation for DBR studies that follow.

The discussion is not only about what the literature says but also about how engaging with it transforms you as a researcher.

## Chapter Summary

This chapter has shown not only how to conduct a rapid review but also why it matters. A literature review provides more than a catalogue of existing studies; it is the space where researchers learn to think critically, confront limitations, and refine their focus. For students and early career researchers, the key lessons are threefold: be transparent in your process, remain attentive to design principles, and acknowledge the contextual constraints that shape your work. When these values are embedded from the outset, the literature review becomes both a foundation for DBR and a training ground for research integrity. By treating the review as a map of existing knowledge and a mirror for reflexive practice, we prepare ourselves to design studies that are rigorous, responsive, and meaningful.
