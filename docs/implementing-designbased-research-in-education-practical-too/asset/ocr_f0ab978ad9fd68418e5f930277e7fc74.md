# TIPS FOR OVERCOMING CHALLENGES

+   - Keep all partners informed of progress and decision-making to build trust and understanding.
+   - Be open to revisiting and adjusting the problem statement as new data are gathered and analysed.
+   - Use school assessment data or previous research findings to complement new data collection efforts.

The analysis and exploration phase is crucial for setting a strong foundation for DBR. Through comprehensive needs analysis and close collaboration with partners, researchers can ensure that their interventions are grounded in real-world educational challenges. This phase not only sharpens the focus of the research but also increases the likelihood that the intervention will be practical, effective, and embraced by those it aims to serve. The scenario below illustrates how this phase is applied in practice.

# EXAMPLE IN CONTEXT

Teachers and researchers begin by analysing classroom assessment data, observing lessons, and interviewing learners. They identify a significant problem: learners understand theoretical principles (e.g., Newton’s laws) but cannot transfer this knowledge into practical investigation. The team conducts a needs analysis, noting that lessons often lack structured scaffolds for linking theory to practice. In collaboration with educators, they define the research question: how can technology-supported simulations improve learners’ ability to apply physics concepts in experimental contexts?
