You should discuss these with your research partners to validate your assumptions and conclusions, as they are the experts in the research context. Your analysis will almost certainly generate many candidates for the best problem to solve. All of these possible problems or needs should be recorded for the final step in this phase: identifying one among the many that you have found to address in the next phase.

For this final step, the candidate problems should be categorised, clustered, consolidated, and prioritised, ideally with your research partners and your research team (even if that is only you and your supervisor). This can be done face-to-face or online in a workshop format with post-its or vignettes or photos or other representations of the identified issues that you can work through with your partners. The initial categorisation, clustering, and consolidation task will reduce the problem space. However, in order to prioritise and select, you will need to apply constraints and judgement. Some constraints will relate to feasibility, others to impact, and yet others to desirability. For this step, you and your research partners need to specify your constraints (budget, time, control, possible impact, etc.) and prioritise and select the most appropriate problem to solve based on that, as in the example below. It is very important at this stage to select something that you and your research partners can get behind and are motivated to solve. Without this motivation, your DBR project could lose momentum and fail to progress.
