# KEY TAKEAWAYS

**Ethical Integrity:** Acknowledging positionality is central to ethical practice. By recognising your perspective, you can better attend to power dynamics and uphold ethical standards in research relationships.

**Awareness of Bias:** Reflecting on how your background shapes your thinking helps identify and mitigate biases that may influence your work.

**Building Trust and Rapport:** Transparency about relevant values and experiences fosters trust with participants and supports open, respectful engagement.

**Data Interpretation:** Reflexivity encourages critical awareness of how personal perspectives shape analysis, leading to richer and more balanced interpretations.

**Professional Development and Growth:** Reflecting on positionality cultivates humility and adaptability, strengthening one’s professional disposition and reinforcing a commitment to ethical, rigorous practice.

Collectively, these insights underscore how positionality not only impacts the quality of research but also facilitates the ethical, relational, and professional growth of the researcher. For example, a researcher who believes strongly in children’s right to be heard might choose to prioritise children’s voices in interviews, while another might focus more on observations of behaviour.

This is where reflexivity comes in, actively pausing to notice how your own perspective shapes the choices you make and the meaning you draw from your research.
