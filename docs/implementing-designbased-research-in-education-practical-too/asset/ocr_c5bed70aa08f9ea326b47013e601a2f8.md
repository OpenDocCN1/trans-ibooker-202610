# Iterative Dialogue as Pedagogical Practice

Robin Alexander, a prominent figure in the field of dialogic teaching, underscores the significance of dialogue in stimulating and captivating learners’ attention during classroom discussions (Alexander, 2018). His principles of dialogue are derived from his research on dialogic, and elaboration, rather than being derived from a single definitive answer. EXAMPLE IN CONTEXT
When a preschool educator asks, “What do you think happens to a puddle after it rains?” children offer a range of possibilities, such as “Evaporation, soaking into the ground, the wind takes it away.”
Each response sparks another question, guiding learners to refine and expand their thinking together.

This glimpse into classroom practice shows how knowledge may be co-constructed by listening to others and sharing ideas, even with very young children. Instead of aiming for immediate closure, the educator encourages a process where children’s ideas accumulate and interconnect.
