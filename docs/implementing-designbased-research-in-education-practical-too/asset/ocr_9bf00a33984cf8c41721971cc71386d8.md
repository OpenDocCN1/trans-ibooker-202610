# Part 1: Communication and Collaboration in Design-Based Research

The spoken word is as old as our species; we observe this in children who naturally tend to speak from a very young age. Speech in itself is a tool that transcends borders and cultures, bringing us a mix of languages, dialects, and sounds that one person delivers to another. It is worth noting that speaking and communicating serve different purposes. Speaking involves the production of words spoken aloud, while communicating encompasses the exchange of meaning through various modalities, including speech, gestures, tone, and silence, ensuring mutual understanding.

As adults, the reality is that we often find it easy to speak, yet difficult to articulate our thoughts to communicate clearly and effectively. For example, have you ever had a conversation with someone and come away thinking, “What was that all about?” In a research context, we may struggle to communicate with clarity because we are not exactly sure what we want to say or even how to get our ideas into words. As professionals, we must overcome communication challenges to avoid confusion or misunderstandings. There is both a hard and an easier way to improve how we communicate. The hard way is to do nothing and hope we get better. The easier way is to develop communication skills and strategies that help to move your thinking from thoughts into clearly delivered pieces of information.

# The Role of Communication in Design-Based Research

In Design-Based Research (DBR), communication facilitates collaboration, knowledge generation, and the co-construction of innovative solutions throughout a research study’s timeline. In other words, from the beginning of an idea to the completion of a study, communication drives the DBR process.

From the initial conception of an idea to crafting a research proposal, the best way to communicate your ideas effectively to a diverse audience is to use clear and straightforward language. By articulating your ideas clearly, you ensure that your research objectives, purposes, and goals are effectively conveyed to all relevant partners, facilitating a seamless transition from a research proposal to the acceptance of a new research study.
