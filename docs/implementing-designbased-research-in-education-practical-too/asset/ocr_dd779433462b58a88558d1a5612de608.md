# *Why Pilot an Intervention?*

Piloting educational interventions has multiple advantages:

+   - **Early Identification of Issues**: Small-scale trials reveal technical, logistical, and instructional challenges that could disrupt larger-scale implementation (Damschroder et al., 2009).
+   - **Feasibility Assessment**: Pilot studies expose whether the intervention fits within existing educational structures and whether it can realistically achieve the intended outcomes.
+   - **Refinement Opportunity**: Data from pilot studies provide immediate insights for improvement, enhancing the overall quality and relevance (Sandoval, 2014).
+   - **Stakeholder Buy-In**: A successful pilot can build trust among partners by demonstrating an intervention’s potential effectiveness, thereby easing resistance during subsequent scaling (Coburn & Penuel, 2016).

# EXAMPLE IN CONTEXT

In the teacher education programme, the workshop series began as a pilot with a single cohort of 40 pre-service educators. This small-scale trial allowed the lecturers and school-based mentors to observe how the shift from lecture-based delivery to practice-based workshops would play out in a contained setting. The pilot revealed several issues: some students thrived in the collaborative planning tasks, while others struggled with the open-ended structure; lesson plans often showed creativity but lacked alignment with curricular objectives; and time management within sessions proved difficult.
