Coke example, a leap was made from falling sales to a problem with the flavour formulation, whereas a more open exploration at the start of the project might have yielded a different problem from taste to solve. This imperative involves staying human-centred but also keeping an open and curious mindset to make sure one goes wide and explores possibilities before converging on a solution. The mindset and methodologies of Design Thinking can provide a framework for such exploration.

Finally, design needs to make ideas tangible early so that they can be explored and tested and improved in an iterative fashion. A large-scale public failure such as New Coke could be avoided through an openness to iteration throughout the design process. The path to good design is not linear. It involves being open to small-scale successes and failures along the path, which brings us through iterative cycles of exploration, design, and testing. Iteration can involve change to all and any aspects of a project – not just changes to a designed solution but also changes to the base problem we are looking to solve. In this mindset, it is no longer a question of success and failure; instead, all outcomes generate learning and are equally valuable. DBR aims to design solutions to complex educational problems in the real world. Design Thinking offers mindsets and methodologies that DBR researchers can adopt in order to achieve these aims.
