# Beyond Speaking: The Art of Listening

Dialogue is an active process of communication that compels us to articulate our thoughts into words (Fisher, 2013). The art of listening transforms dialogue from a passive exchange into an active process of communication; however, effective dialogue requires expert listening skills, where individuals focus on the speaker’s message rather than rushing to express their own thoughts. In practice, this may prove more challenging than one might anticipate.

> PAUSE AND REFLECT
> 
> Can anyone communicate effectively without first understanding the fundamental need to listen?

The better we listen, the more information we absorb, which equips us to contribute meaningfully to the conversation. By attentively listening to what others think, feel, and experience, we can use their insights to inform and challenge our own assumptions. In his work with students, Robert Fisher (2013) cautions that, while we may hear, we do not always truly listen. This serves as a reminder to practice active listening in our interactions with both colleagues and learners.

An educational researcher plays a pivotal role in creating a supportive environment where all voices are heard and valued. One way to demonstrate and practice active listening is by summarising key points from what has been said, thereby clarifying whether the intended message was accurately conveyed (Elder and Paul, 2019). For instance, you may respond, “Am I correct in saying that you believe the education system can be improved?” You then pause to allow the speaker to clarify if you understood and interpreted their words as intended. This approach not only enhances communication but also conveys genuine interest in the contributions of others.
