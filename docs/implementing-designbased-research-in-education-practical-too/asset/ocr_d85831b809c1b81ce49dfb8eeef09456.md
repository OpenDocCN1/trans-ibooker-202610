Other research approaches might take place in the real world and be action-oriented and responsive but are not collaborative enterprises. Action research, for example, can be implemented within the researcher-practitioner’s immediate context of practice and not involve collaboration with others. Participatory action research, however, is inherently collaborative, as is DBR.

# Focus on Iteration and Non-Linearity

# Decision 5: Is the Approach Iterative and Non-Linear?

If you already know what you want to do, DBR is probably not the approach for you. This is because DBR is exploratory; it aims to examine a research context and theoretical field in order to identify a critical problem to understand and solve with the optimal solution through trial and further investigation. DBR progresses in iterative cycles of exploration, design, and evaluation whereby solutions are made tangible through prototyping and trialled in the research context. This is an inherently non-linear process where each iteration brings the researcher back to review their research questions and their designed solutions to refine and improve the intended outcome. The purpose of iteration is to learn and develop a greater understanding of the context and the underlying theory in order to come to a more appropriate solution. Other approaches, such as action research, progress in cycles as well. It should be noted here that DBR may move backwards and forwards even within one cycle of research, returning to refine the research question on the basis of the needs analysis, for example. In this way, DBR is inherently non-linear as it toggles between exploration and intervention and evaluation dynamically in order to address a complex problem in a theoretically grounded and contextually responsive way.
