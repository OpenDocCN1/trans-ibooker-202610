# DBRC Framework (2003)

The DBRC framework (2003) is a seminal framework in educational design research that emphasises iterative, collaborative approaches to solving complex educational problems. This framework consists of key phases, including identifying significant real-world educational challenges, designing innovative solutions grounded in theory, implementing and refining these interventions in authentic contexts, and generating insights that advance both practical and theoretical understanding. The DBRC framework highlights the importance of tightly linking the design and research processes to ensure interventions are effective and sustainable. A hallmark of the framework is its focus on iterative cycles of design, testing, and refinement, which allows researchers and practitioners to adapt interventions based on feedback and observed outcomes.

The DBRC framework has significantly contributed to the field of educational design research by bridging the gap between theory and practice. It has shifted the research paradigm from isolated, theoretical studies to context-sensitive, collaborative approaches that prioritise real-world impact. By emphasising the co-creation of solutions with educators and focusing on scalability and transferability, the DBRC model has enabled the development of interventions that are both practically relevant and theoretically robust. Its integration of design, implementation, and analysis has become a cornerstone of modern educational design research methodologies.
