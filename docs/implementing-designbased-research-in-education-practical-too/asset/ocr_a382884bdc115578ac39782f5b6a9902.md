The discussion is not only about what the literature says but also about how engaging with it transforms you as a researcher.

# Chapter Summary

This chapter has shown not only how to conduct a rapid review but also why it matters. A literature review provides more than a catalogue of existing studies; it is the space where researchers learn to think critically, confront limitations, and refine their focus. For students and early career researchers, the key lessons are threefold: be transparent in your process, remain attentive to design principles, and acknowledge the contextual constraints that shape your work. When these values are embedded from the outset, the literature review becomes both a foundation for DBR and a training ground for research integrity. By treating the review as a map of existing knowledge and a mirror for reflexive practice, we prepare ourselves to design studies that are rigorous, responsive, and meaningful.
