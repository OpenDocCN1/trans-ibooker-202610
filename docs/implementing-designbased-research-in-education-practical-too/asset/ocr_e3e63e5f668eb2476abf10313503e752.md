In essence, iterative cycles in DBR facilitate the continuous growth and development of an intervention, ensuring that it is thoroughly tested, deeply rooted in evidence, and responsive to the changing needs of the classroom or educational environment. Engaging partners throughout the process and refining the intervention with each cycle can further contribute to the long-term success of DBR interventions.

# EXAMPLE IN CONTEXT

By the fifth cycle of Reading Pathways, the app had evolved far beyond its original prototype. What began as a simple phonics app was gradually refined into a comprehensive platform that supported word recognition and fluency practice. Educators played a central role in shaping these developments: one requested a dashboard to track learner progress, while another suggested the need for offline access in classrooms with poor connectivity. Each suggestion was incorporated iteratively, and each cycle generated new theoretical insights into how digital scaffolds function within different classroom ecologies. The result was not only a robust, classroom-ready tool but also an evidence-based contribution to understanding the role of adaptive technology in literacy instruction.

# When to Conclude the Iterative Cycle

Knowing when to conclude the iterative cycle is a crucial aspect of DBR, as prolonged iterations without clear endpoints can lead to diminishing returns. The process of iteration is valuable in refining and improving interventions, but there are key indicators that guide researchers to decide when the cycle should come to an end.

One critical factor is data saturation. This occurs when further cycles no longer produce significant new insights or improvements in the intervention. Essentially, the data being collected becomes repetitive, offering little to no additional value to the refinement of the intervention or strategy being tested.
