# Maintaining Core Integrity

As interventions adapt to fit new settings, there is a risk of diluting the core principles that made them successful in the first place. Balancing fidelity with the foundational concepts with the flexibility which is necessary is a persistent tension in the scale-up process. Without a clear articulation of the “non-negotiables,” essential elements may be lost or misinterpreted.

# Partner Turnover

Changes in leadership, staffing, or policy direction can disrupt continuity. When key champions move on, interventions may lose momentum. Institutional knowledge can quickly dissipate unless robust structures are in place to support knowledge transfer and leadership succession.

# Evidence of Impact across Contexts

Gathering robust evidence of impact across diverse settings can be challenging, particularly when the measurement tools or evaluation criteria vary. Inconsistent data collection practices, or a lack of alignment between the evaluation goals and local priorities, may undermine confidence in the intervention’s effectiveness.

# Change Fatigue

Educators are frequently presented with a steady stream of new initiatives in schools, which can result in resistance or disengagement, especially when past efforts have failed to bring about lasting improvement. Establishing trust and demonstrating early signs of impact are important steps in addressing this scepticism.
