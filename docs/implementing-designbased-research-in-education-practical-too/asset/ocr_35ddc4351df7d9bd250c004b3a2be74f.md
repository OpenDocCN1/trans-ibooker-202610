| Phase 1 Problem Statement
Objective: Identify the problem, the study goals and objectives, and your plan for working with a team. |
| Key Questions to Consider | Supporting Actions |
| People: Who is DBR for? Who are the partners?
Domain: What are the key issues, the nature of learning, and the tasks each team member will explore?
Goals and outcomes: What is the primary purpose? What specific needs will be explored? Who will the study benefit? | Conduct a needs assessment through informal discussion. Talk to the people who use the service.
Clarify what individual team members will contribute to the project.
Identify the key educational outcomes to be explored.
Reach out to participants with a clear vision of your purpose, goals, and vision for the study. |

| Phase 2 Preparation and Initial Design
Objective: Define activities, communicative processes, and DBR members’ roles. |
| Key Questions to Consider | Supporting Actions |
| Activities: What kinds of activities are most likely to address the research problem effectively?
Communication: How will you communicate on an ongoing basis with other DBR partners?
Interaction: What kinds of interactions will drive the project forward?
Knowledge sharing: How will you share your progress with different partners? (participants and funders)
Collaboration: When do team members meet to collaborate?
Roles: How will DBR roles be defined? | Identify the activities that are likely to meet the aims of the project.
Discuss, with the team, a number of activities that may meet the demands of the study.
Discuss how often you will meet. Develop a tentative schedule.
Create a timeline where you will lead the team discussion concerning the DBR progression and encourage feedback.
Determine the team members’ roles and provide any required support, resources or training. |
