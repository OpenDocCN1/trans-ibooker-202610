In DBR, decision-making is an ongoing process that occurs at every stage of the study, from initial design through implementation and evaluation. As you progress, the focus shifts from the overarching DBR concept to the more detailed planning of evaluation within the fieldwork. Here, the emphasis is on how evaluation results can be utilised to inform real-time adaptations and refinements. These adjustments aim to bridge the gap between design and theory generation.

# Formative and Summative Evaluation

McKenney and Reeves (2019) emphasise that a hallmark of DBR is its cycle of design, implementation, evaluation, and refinement, which blends the creation of practical solutions with theory generation. Within these iterative cycles, evaluation plays a crucial role at multiple stages. Two complementary approaches are commonly used: formative evaluation and summative evaluation. Although they serve different purposes in DBR, together they ensure that an intervention is both well-designed and effective.

# Formative Evaluation in DBR

Formative evaluation takes place during the design and development of an intervention, with the purpose of improving it. In DBR, it is integrated into all phases and cycles of the research. Its primary aim is to provide feedback while the innovation (working design) is still in development. This enables researchers to identify weaknesses or areas for enhancement and to make iterative improvements before finalising the intervention.

# Summative Evaluation in DBR

Unlike formative assessment, summative evaluation is conducted once an intervention has been fully developed, often at the end of a DBR cycle or project. Its purpose is to assess the overall effectiveness and determine whether the design meets its intended outcomes under real-world conditions. In DBR, summative evaluation, therefore, provides a final appraisal of the intervention’s success.
