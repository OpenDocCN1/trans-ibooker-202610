(2004) introduced the term “design experiments,” reflecting a focus on the empirical study of learning in naturalistic settings. Their model views classrooms as laboratories for testing how learning theories operate in practice. It differs from other DBR models in its strong empirical orientation: the systematic collection and triangulation of quantitative, qualitative, and process data to understand the mechanisms of learning. While the DBRC and Barab and Squire stress partnership and theory-practice co-development, Collins and colleagues focus on methodological rigour, establishing DBR as a scientifically credible approach for analysing complex pedagogical phenomena.
