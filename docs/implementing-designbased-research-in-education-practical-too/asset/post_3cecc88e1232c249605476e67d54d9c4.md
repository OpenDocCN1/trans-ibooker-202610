
## Scaling Up a DBR Intervention: Checklist

Use this checklist as a planning and reflection tool as you prepare to scale your Design-based Research (DBR) project to new contexts. Tick off each item as you go, and make notes to support your thinking and documentation.

### Understanding the New Context

- Conducted a contextual analysis of the new setting
- Identified key differences in demographics, infrastructure, and teaching practices

### Building Stakeholder Support

- Engaged key stakeholders early (teachers, leaders, students, etc.)
- Clearly communicated goals and rationale
- Identified and supported local champions

### Designing for Flexibility

- Defined core vs. adaptable elements of the intervention
- Offered multiple implementation pathways (e.g., tech/no-tech)
- Encouraged practitioner-led innovation

### Collecting and Using Data

- Created a data collection plan (quantitative + qualitative)
- Collected data across different time points (pre/during/post)
- Used data to inform ongoing adaptations

### Anticipating Challenges

- Identified potential barriers and risks
- Planned for staff changes and training and mentoring
- Scheduled time for review and iteration

### Documenting Design Principles

- Tracked adaptations across settings
- Noted recurring patterns and successful practices
- Drafted design principles grounded in data and experience

Sustainability is also not something that happens by default; it must be purposefully built into the design from the very beginning. This means embedding practices within everyday routines, aligning the intervention with broader organisational priorities, and investing in those who will carry the work forward. When these elements are thoughtfully integrated, scaling becomes an opportunity to generate lasting, practice-informed knowledge that is both relevant across contexts and resilient over time.

## EPILOGUE

## DESIGNING RESEARCH, DESIGNING CHANGE

As we close this book, our aim has been to open a space for possibility. Design-Based Research (DBR) is not a fixed recipe but a way of working: it invites researchers to think with, rather than about, educators, learners, and communities. Throughout these chapters, we have explored how DBR bridges theory and practice, how it thrives on iteration and dialogue, and how it allows complex educational challenges to be addressed in context rather than in abstraction.

For early career researchers, DBR can feel both exciting and uncertain. It demands methodological rigour while embracing the unpredictability of real-world settings. It asks us to be designers as well as researchers; to listen deeply, to adapt when our plans meet reality, and to see “failure” as an opportunity for learning and redesign. These qualities can be challenging, but they are also what make DBR so powerful.

Looking ahead, DBR continues to evolve. New technologies, global educational shifts, and a growing emphasis on equity and inclusion invite fresh ways to co-design learning environments and study change. We encourage

you to use DBR not only to test ideas but also to imagine better futures for education – futures that honour learners’ voices, address systemic barriers, and create lasting impact.

Our hope is that this book has equipped you with practical tools and critical insight while reminding you that research is, at its heart, a creative and collaborative act. May your own design journeys be bold, reflective, and responsive to the complex, beautiful work of education.
