# Limitations

There were two significant limitations to this rapid review:

+   1. The review was conducted within a six-week timeframe, limiting the comprehensiveness of the search and analysis.
+   2. The review was undertaken by a single researcher. A more rigorous process would include independent validation by a second reviewer (see Gough, 2007).

These limitations mean that the findings should be read as a snapshot of current DBR practices rather than an exhaustive account. Nonetheless, the review provides valuable insights that can guide both researchers and practitioners. The following discussion considers what these findings mean in practice.

# Discussion

This rapid review demonstrates how literature reviews not only consolidate knowledge but also reveal gaps in theory and practice. The diversity of theoretical frameworks across DBR studies illustrates both the richness of the field and the challenge of making sense of it. For example, encountering models such as the Four-Phase Model of Interest (Tillmann & Wegner, 2024) and the Enactive Perspective on Knowing, Learning, and Training (Poizat et al., 2024) highlighted areas that were initially unfamiliar to the researcher, underlining how literature reviews broaden professional knowledge and shape emerging study concepts.

The studies reviewed suggest that DBR can support both professional development and contextual innovation (Agustiningrum et al., 2024). At the same time, success is strongly influenced by teacher commitment, workload, and the degree to which theory and practice are meaningfully integrated.
