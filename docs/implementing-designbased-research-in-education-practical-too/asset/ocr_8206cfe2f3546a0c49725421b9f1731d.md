+   - **Data-Driven Decisions:** Use empirical insights from the analysis phase to ensure designs meet the identified needs.
+   - **Balancing Feasibility and Innovation:** Designs should be innovative enough to extend practice but realistic enough to integrate smoothly into classrooms.

# *Developing Prototypes: Iterative and Adaptive Designs*

Prototypes are central to the design phase, serving as early versions of the intervention that are deliberately incomplete and open to refinement. They may take the form of lesson plans, teaching materials, digital tools, or assessment frameworks. A prototype is not a final product but a working model that can be tested, critiqued, and improved.

Designers are encouraged to start simple, perhaps with low-fidelity prototypes such as paper sketches, mock-ups, or pilot lesson scripts, before investing in more complex resources. This ensures that revisions can be made quickly and with minimal cost. Each prototype should be adaptive, capable of evolving in response to feedback from teachers, students, and researchers. As Barab and Squire (2004) note, the strength of DBR lies in its capacity to produce “flexible innovations” that emerge from iterative cycles of design and use.

# STRATEGIES FOR PROTOTYPE DEVELOPMENT
