
## Data Extraction

How can one effectively extract information from an article? Extracting useful data from articles depends on your RQs, familiarity with academic writing, and ability to interpret key findings. The task entails a substantial amount of information, therefore, to avoid becoming overwhelmed, break the task into smaller, manageable steps. Here are some strategies:

## PRACTICAL TIPS

- Read the abstract: Summarises the topic, method, and key findings.
- Scan the introduction: Reveals the study's purpose and background.
- Review the conclusion: Highlights main findings, gaps, and future directions.
- Read headings and subheadings: Guide you to relevant sections.
- Skim the methodology: Understand how the study was carried out.
- Read the discussion: Shows how findings relate to other work.
- Take notes: Capture insights, quotes, or key points you'll need later.
- Create a coding system: Helps organise your data and makes synthesis easier.

## Content Analysis

Content analysis is a widely used method in literature reviews. It helps synthesise findings by identifying themes, comparing theoretical frameworks, spotting knowledge gaps, and tracking areas of consensus or disagreement across studies (Creswell & Creswell, 2018).

Before committing to this method, consider whether it aligns with your research goals. In this review, content analysis was chosen because it provides a systematic way to organise and interpret findings across multiple studies. It can also be adapted to suit different scales of research, making it useful for both small and large reviews.

The main steps in content analysis are:

- Prepare the Data: Gather all relevant literature.
- Read and Take Notes: Record key ideas and findings.
- Code the Data: Assign labels to meaningful sections of text.
- Generate Themes: Group codes into broader categories.
- Interpret the Themes: Connect them to the RQs or theoretical frameworks.
- Represent the Results: Summarise findings using lists, tables, or narrative formats.

Content analysis is a flexible tool that helps turn large amounts of text into organised, meaningful insights that directly address your RQs.

## Results

In the context of this rapid review, the results illustrate how DBR is applied across varied educational settings, highlighting both innovations and challenges. All processes – screening, reading, coding, and data extraction – are complete, and the findings are presented in line with the four guiding RQs.

In total, 2,220 articles were retrieved from Academic Search Complete and ERIC, with six additional sources from Google Scholar. After removing duplicates and applying screening criteria, 46 articles remained. Following full-text screening, 14 articles were included in the final review. - Characteristics of the literature;
- Summary of findings;
- Significance of the findings.

This structure ensures the findings are reported systematically and can be easily compared across questions.

## RQ1: DBR Purpose and Processes

### Characteristics of the Literature

Recent DBR studies (2023–2025) addressed varied purposes, ranging from digital learning innovations to inclusive classroom tools. Examples include:

- Designing digital platforms for computational thinking and self-directed science learning (Agustiningrum et al., 2024).
- Supporting teachers to shift towards student-centred mathematics instruction (Soforon et al., 2024).
- Developing an eye-tracking tool to assist students with physical disabilities (Donmez & Cagiltay, 2024).
- Creating a diagnostic instrument for identifying scientific giftedness (Peperkorn & Wegner, 2024).
- Designing scaffolding tasks for English pronunciation and self-directed learning (Iskandar et al., 2024).
- Teaching robotics through a spiral curriculum to prepare teachers for digital education (Schröer et al., 2024).
- Implementing interdisciplinary instruction via prototype development (Kramer & Wegner, 2024).

### Summary of Findings

These studies illustrate the diversity of DBR applications. They tackle educational challenges by designing digital tools, developing interventions, and creating new learning experiences. Research methods included observations, interviews, and surveys, with participants spanning teachers, students, and researchers.

### Significance of Findings

The breadth of DBR highlights its adaptability and relevance to real-world educational issues. These studies show how DBR both generates practical solutions and deepens understanding of teaching and learning in authentic settings. By reviewing such examples, researchers can draw lessons from earlier work to guide their own designs.

## RQ2: Theoretical Frameworks

### Characteristics of the Literature

DBR studies employed a wide variety of theoretical frameworks, including:

- Student-centred and constructivist models (Dibaba et al., 2024; Soforon, 2024).
- Project-based and co-creation approaches (Iskandar, 2024; Martel & Garcias, 2024).
- Social constructivism and Communities of Practice (Dibaba, 2024; Jita & Dhliwayo, 2024).
- More specialised orientations such as enactivism (Poizat, 2024), situational interest (Tillmann & Wegner, 2024), and transdisciplinarity (Parth et al., 2024).
- Some studies relied solely on DBR principles without naming an explicit framework (Donmez & Cagiltay, 2024; Peperkorn & Wegner, 2024).

### Summary of Findings

The reviewed studies show that DBR is compatible with diverse theoretical orientations. Design principles often shaped how these theories were interpreted and applied in practice.

### Significance of Findings

This diversity demonstrates DBR's flexibility. Researchers can align their study with theories that best suit their context, rather than feeling constrained by a single framework. Such a wide choice enables a closer fit between research aims, values, and practice, producing more meaningful and relevant outcomes.

## RQ3: Key Findings

### Characteristics of the Literature

The studies produced a wide range of findings, such as:

- Professional development gains for teachers and context-specific innovations (Agustiningrum, 2024).
- Shifts towards student-centred tasks when supported by structured interventions (Soforon, 2024).
- Eye-tracking technology improving accessibility (Donmez & Cagiltay, 2024).
- Diagnostic tools for giftedness (Peperkorn & Wegner, 2024).
- Robotics and spiral curricula enhancing computational thinking (Schröer et al., 2024).
- Guided play and co-created curricula showing promise but facing challenges in classroom uptake (Martel & Garcias, 2024; Ndabezitha & Gravett, 2024).
- Barriers such as workload, time, and weak experimental designs limiting impact (Dibaba, 2024; Kramer, 2024).

### Summary of Findings

DBR shows strong potential to design innovations and support professional learning, but success depends on contextual factors such as teacher engagement, leadership support, and sufficient time for implementation.

### Significance of Findings

These findings reinforce that DBR is not only a research method but also a collaborative, adaptive process. By respecting both human and systemic factors, DBR can drive meaningful change in real classrooms. For early career researchers, this highlights the need to plan realistically, anticipating both opportunities and barriers.

## RQ4: Implications for Research or Practice

### Characteristics of the Literature

Across the reviewed studies, implications included:

- The need for sustained professional development, especially for technology-rich environments (Agustiningrum, 2024; Jita & Dhliwayo, 2024).
- Better understanding of barriers and enablers for student-centred teaching (Soforon, 2024).
- Inclusive solutions through technologies such as eye-tracking (Donmez & Cagiltay, 2024).
- Methodological advances such as improved diagnostics for giftedness (Peperkorn & Wegner, 2024).
- Cross-sector and interdisciplinary applications of DBR (Kramer, 2024; Martel & Garcias, 2024).

### Summary of Findings

A consistent theme is the value of long-term, context-specific professional development to support teachers. DBR functions not only as a research methodology but also as a vehicle for professional growth and collaborative learning.

### Significance of Findings

The implications highlight DBR’s dual role in advancing educational research and empowering practitioners. For DBR to be effective, interventions must be sensitive to context, responsive to challenges, and supportive of sustained professional learning. This ensures that innovations are not only tested but also embedded in educational communities, leading to lasting change.
