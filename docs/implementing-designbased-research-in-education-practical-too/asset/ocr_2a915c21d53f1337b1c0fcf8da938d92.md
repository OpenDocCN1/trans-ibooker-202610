# Types of Literature Reviews

Choosing the right type of literature review depends on your research goals, the time and resources available, and the depth of analysis you require. There are several types of reviews, each with its own strengths and limitations. Common approaches include systematic reviews, scoping reviews, rapid reviews, narrative reviews, and meta-analyses, among others. See Grant and Booth (2009) for a thorough overview of literature review types.

+   - **Systematic reviews** aim to synthesise knowledge by assessing the quality of existing research, identifying what is already known, and pinpointing gaps in the evidence (O’Reilly et al., 2022).
+   - **Scoping reviews**, by contrast, are used to map a broad area of research and highlight emerging trends, with less emphasis on evaluating quality (Arksey & O’Malley, 2005). While both approaches are valuable, they are also time-intensive and may not be feasible for all projects.
+   - **Rapid reviews** provide a more efficient alternative by summarising current evidence quickly, often to inform policy or practice (Wollscheid & Tripney, 2021). The trade-off is that rapid reviews may not capture every relevant study or explore all nuances in the literature.

In this chapter, a rapid review is used. This review type is particularly useful when reviewing recent publications, as it allows researchers to narrow database searches or exclude certain study types in order to manage time effectively (Smela et al., 2023). By sharing a rapid review in real-time, this chapter aims to give new researchers a practical sense of the process and highlight the key decisions that shape both the scope and the outcomes of a review.

Once you have chosen the most suitable type of review, the challenge becomes how to organise the process in a clear and manageable way.

> **KEY TAKEAWAY**
> 
> No single review type is “best”; the most appropriate choice depends on the purpose of your study, the questions you are asking, and the resources you have available.
