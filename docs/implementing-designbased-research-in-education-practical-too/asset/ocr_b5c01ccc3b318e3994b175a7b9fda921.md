# Be Human-Centred

Taking a human-centred approach is a baseline orientation for all DBR work. It is not just to be learner-centred or child-centred or teacher-centred; it is to be human-centred. This requires us, as researchers, to focus on the people in the research context in a holistic way, acknowledging and exploring the many roles they may inhabit in their own lives (parents, teachers, learners, friends, peers, mentors). As educational researchers, this mindset draws us away from a narrow focus on specific educational roles but situates each person at the centre of the multiple systems that interact in their lives. If we wish to approach this systematically and theorise the holistic perspective, the bioecological systems theory (Bronfenbrenner & Morris, 2006) can provide a useful theoretical framework. It can help us, as researchers, to maintain this focus on the holistic view of the research contexts and our research partners as humans who are dynamic agents within this context.
