table below. Formative evaluation is diagnostic and improvement-oriented; its goal is to inform design changes by identifying what does and does not work during development. In turn, summative evaluation is judgement- and outcome-oriented; its goal is to determine the overall success of the fully developed intervention and whether it has achieved its aims.

| Evaluation Type | Purpose | Timing | Objective | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Formative Evaluation | Improve the design and development of an intervention | During the design and development phases | Provide feedback to researchers for iterative improvements | Identifying weaknesses and areas for enhancement |
| Summative Evaluation | Assess the overall effectiveness or success of a fully developed intervention | After the intervention has been fully developed | Determine if the design meets its intended outcomes in real-world conditions | Evaluating the final effectiveness of the complete intervention |

In practice, formative and summative evaluations complement each other. Formative evaluation keeps the DBR process responsive and grounded in practice so that by the time an intervention is complete, it has already undergone cycles of feedback and refinement. Summative evaluation then verifies the effectiveness of the intervention in achieving its goals, validating both the design choices and the theoretical insights gained during the process.
