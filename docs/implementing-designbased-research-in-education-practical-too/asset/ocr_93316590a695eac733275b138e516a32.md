# Terminology

This phase of the process – identifying the problem to solve or research question to address – is fundamental to good design and, by extension, to good DBR. There are many names for this phase in Design Thinking and Human-Centred Design, and it can encompass one or more stages in the design process depending on the design model used:

+   - IDEO Design Framework: Inspiration;
+   - IDEO’s seven steps of Design Thinking Process: Frame a Question – Gather inspiration – Synthesise for Action;
+   - Stanford’s D-School Design Thinking Toolkit: Empathise and Define;
+   - Stanford’s D-School Process: Need finding;
+   - Enterprise Design Thinking Toolkit (IBM): Observe;
+   - Systems thinking: Gaining clarity, reframing.

In product design, it might be close to user research or market research or human factors research. However, the crucial difference between a Design Thinking approach and product design is that the former involves the people in the research context at the centre of the activity at all levels.

The terms might change, but the fundamental processes are the same across these different frameworks. This phase is about trying to understand deeply the needs and lived experiences of the people in your research context and distilling all of that into the best, workable question to answer or right problem to solve now.

This chapter sets out both the mindsets and the methodologies of the work, focusing on this particular phase of the DBR process.
