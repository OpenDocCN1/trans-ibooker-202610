# Introduction

A comprehensive literature review is a critical first step in any Design-Based Research (DBR) study. This chapter explains why the review matters, the key decisions to consider before starting, and the different types of reviews that may be employed. It includes a real-time demonstration of a rapid literature review, providing readers with a practical sense of the process while also addressing common challenges and limitations. With this foundation in place, we now turn to why the literature review is indispensable for DBR and how it establishes the basis for the study.

# The Rationale for the Literature Review

In educational research, DBR plays a vital role across contexts ranging from early childhood education to higher education and beyond. As a methodology, DBR seeks to build evidence-based teaching and learning practices by drawing on existing knowledge where others have designed and tested innovations in real-world settings.

If your aim is to generate new educational practices, understanding prior work can both inspire your design and strengthen your study. Reviewing the literature allows you to see how others have approached DBR, the methods they have used, the findings they have generated, and the implications for both research and practice. It is equally important to examine the theoretical frameworks that underpin DBR studies, since these shape both design choices and analytical approaches. A review also ensures that your work does not simply duplicate what has already been done.
