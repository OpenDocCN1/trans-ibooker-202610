# Generating Design Principles through Scale-Up

# EXAMPLE IN CONTEXT

After two years of expansion, the *Literacy Links Project* had grown into a vibrant professional learning network spanning six schools. What began as a small co-designed project in one classroom had evolved into a collaborative ecosystem where educators, literacy leads, and researchers continuously learned from one another. Through analysis of implementation notes, data dashboards, and educator reflections, the team began to identify recurring patterns that pointed to deeper, transferable insights. These findings were not simply about what worked in each school, but about *why* it worked and under what conditions it could thrive.

From this process, three design principles emerged that captured the essence of sustainable scaling. First, professional learning was most effective when led by *internal mentors* who modelled and coached within their own schools, rather than relying on external facilitators. Second, *data reflection* had the greatest impact when integrated into existing staff meeting structures, reinforcing shared responsibility for literacy outcomes. Third, *contextual flexibility* proved crucial: schools that adapted materials and schedules while holding firm to the instructional core achieved the strongest results. These principles became the project’s theoretical contribution, showing how scaling, when treated as an iterative design, deepens both understanding and practice across contexts.

Scaling provides a rich opportunity to extend not only the reach of a DBR intervention but also to refine and articulate the underlying design principles that support it. As interventions are adapted and tested across multiple settings, patterns emerge that help distinguish between core features and contextual adaptations. These insights can be translated into generalisable design principles, which guide future implementation and contribute to the broader field of educational innovation.

Design principles in DBR serve as theoretically grounded heuristics that inform how an intervention works, for whom, and under what conditions (Anderson & Shattuck, 2012). They are not rigid rules but flexible guides that help shape design decisions. As an intervention scale, real-world variability reveals which elements are essential for success and which can be modified.
