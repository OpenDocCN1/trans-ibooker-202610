This perspective is reflected in models such as McKenney and Reeves’ (2012), where implementation spans analysis and exploration, design and construction, and evaluation and reflection. Depicted as a continuous, sliding-scale process, it begins with small-scale piloting and expands in complexity and scope as the designs mature, reinforcing its integrative role across all phases of DBR.

Because implementation occurs in dynamic contexts, flexibility is essential. DBR anticipates and embraces unexpected events, such as technical glitches, fluctuating learner engagement, or unanticipated interpretations, as valuable sources of insight. These moments are not treated as disruptions but as data that drive refinement. Each round of implementation, therefore, becomes both a practical trial and a site of theoretical reflection, simultaneously strengthening the design and expanding our understanding of how learning unfolds in context.

This chapter examines the implementation phase in greater depth, focusing on key dimensions that shape its success. These include:

+   - The role of piloting as an entry point,
+   - The importance of understanding implementation as an iterative enquiry,
+   - The importance of balancing fidelity with flexibility,
+   - Strengthening researcher and educator relationships through collaboration,
+   - Identifying best practices for implementing educational interventions,
+   - Identifying and addressing challenges associated with the implementation phase.

A natural starting point for implementation is piloting, where small-scale trials provide a low-risk opportunity to test feasibility, identify practical obstacles, and generate early insights before moving toward larger-scale enactments.
