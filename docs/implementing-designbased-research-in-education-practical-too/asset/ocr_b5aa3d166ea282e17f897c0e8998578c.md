# Focus on Action

# Decision 1: Will This Study Involve an Intervention or Action of Some Kind?

The essential characteristic of DBR is that it aims to address a need in a real-world context. This entails action or intervention of some kind as part of the research process. Not all educational research includes this explicit focus on action. Educational research often aims to describe situations as they stand using quantitative, qualitative, or mixed methods. For example, ethnographic approaches and phenomenological approaches aim to provide a rich description of particular contexts of study, but they do not aim to change those contexts. Other approaches focus on correlational or causal relationships in relation to past events; for instance, a cohort study that looks at the relationship between parental involvement in education and educational outcomes. Again, these studies do not aim to change the prevailing situation but to explore and understand it better.

The focus on action is not itself a sufficient condition for a DBR study as research with an action orientation can derive from many different world views and different research traditions and take different approaches. For example, a randomised control trial within a strong positivist paradigm can examine the outcomes of an action in a very controlled context. In contrast, a participatory action research project within a transformative paradigm can explore the direct and indirect impacts of action on the participants and stakeholders within a wide context of study.
