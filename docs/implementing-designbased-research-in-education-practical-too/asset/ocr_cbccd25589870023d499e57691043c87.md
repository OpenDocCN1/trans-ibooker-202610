+   3. Immerse yourself in the moment. This is like taking on a participant-as-observer role except that the core focus here is participation in the moment to experience and feel first-hand what it is like to be in that environment.
+   4. Record your observations as soon as possible after the experience.

# Don'ts

+   1. Don’t analyse — try to remain immersed in the moment, analyse later.
+   2. Don’t assume your experience is the “typical” experience, as yours is only simulated and time limited.

# KEY TAKEAWAY

Remember that what people say is not always the same as what they do, think, or feel. Use a range of methods to try to explore the full empathy map of Say-Think-Feel-Do.

# Analysis and Synthesis

The goal of this phase in DBR is NOT about finding solutions; it is still all about finding the best questions to address, for now, in your research context. All of the methods we have discussed will generate lots of data about your research context from a range of perspectives. The purpose of this step of the need-finding phase is to distil all the insights down in order to identify a good problem to solve in the design phase. This stage is all about synthesising the learning from the empathy work you have completed. In educational research terms, this is an analysis phase drawing on the relevant analytic tools, as appropriate (thematic analysis, conversational analysis, content analysis, visual analysis, etc.). In Design Thinking terms, this is called synthesising insights for action.
