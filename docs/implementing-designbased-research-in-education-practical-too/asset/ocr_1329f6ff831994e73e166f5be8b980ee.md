# Professional Relationships and Power

Although collaborative, experiential, and problem-based learning are well established in education, educators do not always engage deeply with the theoretical foundations of interventions or understand how they translate into practice (Loughran, 2012). DBR addresses this gap by creating a space where theory and practice intersect, enabling educators to act as co-researchers and contribute their professional expertise to the research process.

Power dynamics inevitably shape these working relationships. As McDonagh et al. (2019) note, the way adults perceive their classroom role influences their sense of power and control. For DBR researchers, it is, therefore, essential to acknowledge and respect educators’ professional identities, treating them as co-researchers with unique insights (Bakker, 2018). Listening carefully to their experiences both validates their autonomy and generates knowledge that strengthens practice and research alike.

Challenges arise, however, when educators lack a strong sense of agency in their everyday practice. In such cases, questions about professional identity and achievement can surface, making it even more important for DBR to create inclusive and empowering spaces.
