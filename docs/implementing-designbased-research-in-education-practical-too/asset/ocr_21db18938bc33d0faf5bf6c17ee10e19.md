DBR aims to act in a culturally responsive way that acknowledges possible differentials of power but does not seek to dismantle or change those power structures. Another surface similarity, that of engaging deeply with research partners at all stages of the research process, is also implemented in DBR to different ends and somewhat by different means. In participatory approaches, such as participatory action research, the goal is to develop partners’ understanding of their own context and empower them to transform it. In DBR, there is a nuanced difference in that participants are design partners, experts within the context of study about the validity and usefulness of a chosen solution within the frame of their own experience. Social Design-Based Experiments (SDBEs) do take an explicitly transformative approach within the parameters of DBR (Gutiérrez et al., 2020), but standard DBR research does not necessarily incorporate this focus.

# KEY TAKEAWAY

The initial needs-finding phase of DBR would greatly benefit from the kind of embedded engagement of an ethnographic approach — to observe and interrogate the lived experiences within the context of study. Similarly, where iterative cycles of investigation have identified a possible effective formulation of an intervention, this might be interrogated in an experimental or quasi-experimental study of a scale appropriate to the phase of the research. Overall, DBR might best be presented as a structure within which to develop a long-term research agenda that is responsive to the context and findings and adopts appropriate methodological approaches for particular phases of the work.
