DBR researchers would argue strongly that DBR is the ideal approach to develop theory as it can be conceptualised, developed, and validated in real-world, iterative research cycles; thus, rendering the theoretical and ontological constructs explored more robust and more detailed.

Theory is and should be constantly evolving. A theory represents the current best approximation of knowledge in an area, and it is the goal of science to extend, supersede, integrate, and otherwise modify theory to develop further our knowledge to the next best approximation. The constructs of a theory can support us to observe, analyse, and develop our understanding in a given area.

Halliday and Martin (1993) discuss how this tension is represented in scientific writing – while scientists acknowledge that reality is dynamic, they construct knowledge and write about it in a way that is static. In DBR, researchers acknowledge and harness the dynamic nature of theory in order to design and evaluate interventions and deepen understanding.

The emergence of mixed methods research (MMR) as a mechanism to integrate both broad and deep perspectives on phenomena of interest in productive ways (Onwuegbuzie et al., 2023) was also a driver and enabler of the DBR approach in educational research. MMR has become standard in educational research since the paradigm wars of the 1970s and 1980s. Through earlier decades, two great traditions of social science research – positivist approaches using quantitative methods and interpretivist approaches adopting more qualitative methods – were pitted against each other as oppositional positions in examining and understanding the world. Pragmatism offered a philosophical underpinning that could bridge this paradigmatic divide. Ponce (2021) outlines how MMR developed during this time period as a research approach grounded in a pragmatist philosophy. In pragmatism in general and DBR specifically, it is not a question of “anything goes,” but rather the aim is to enlist and leverage available resources, using the breadth of available methods and approaches to generate useful understandings and solutions to real-world issues.
