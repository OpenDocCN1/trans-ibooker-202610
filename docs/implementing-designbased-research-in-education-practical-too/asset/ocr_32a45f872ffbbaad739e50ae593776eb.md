# EXAMPLE IN CONTEXT

Ciara was feeling a little nervous. She had been working with a small team of educators for two months, co-developing an intervention to help young children develop critical thinking skills. The activities were going well, and the children seemed highly engaged. But now it was time for the first full-cycle evaluation. Ciara knew this meant more than just collecting data; it required honest conversations about the design, its effects, and her own role in shaping it. She sat down with her laptop, reviewing observation notes and video clips from the sessions. In one clip, a confident child had taken over a storytelling exercise, dominating the quieter voices in the room. At the time, Ciara had been proud of the activity, but on closer review, she realised that not all the children had equal opportunities to speak.
