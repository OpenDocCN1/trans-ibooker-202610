+   - **Shared Learning**: Drawing on the strengths of researchers, educators, and learners to bridge theoretical knowledge with practical application.
+   - **Ongoing Reflection**: Creating space for both individual and collective reflection throughout the project.

Engaging collaboratively on topics that may not be familiar to all participants presents an opportunity to see problems from new perspectives and develop more inclusive solutions.

Collaboration becomes truly meaningful when it extends beyond dialogue and begins to shape real choices. This is where *shared decision-making* takes centre stage, transforming discussion into collective action that drives the DBR process forward.

# The Role of Research Meetings

Research meetings provide the main structure for shared decision-making. Held at different phases of the project, they allow participants to:

+   - Share emerging data;
+   - Test new ideas;
+   - Refine solutions collaboratively.

When these meetings are conducted with professionalism and reciprocity, they:

+   - Foster trust and mutual respect;
+   - Make space for diverse voices, including quieter or marginalised participants;
+   - Create mechanisms for addressing challenges such as data inconsistencies, methodological questions, or interpersonal tensions.

Further, true collaboration involves more than hearing all voices; it requires finding ways to balance them. This might mean:

+   - Developing strategies to mitigate power imbalances;
+   - Ensuring quieter members have opportunities to contribute; or
+   - Structuring agendas so that discussions stay focused without silencing creativity.
