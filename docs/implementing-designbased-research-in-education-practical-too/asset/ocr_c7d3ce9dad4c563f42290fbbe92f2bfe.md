# DESIGNING RESEARCH, DESIGNING CHANGE

As we close this book, our aim has been to open a space for possibility. Design-Based Research (DBR) is not a fixed recipe but a way of working: it invites researchers to think with, rather than about, educators, learners, and communities. Throughout these chapters, we have explored how DBR bridges theory and practice, how it thrives on iteration and dialogue, and how it allows complex educational challenges to be addressed in context rather than in abstraction.

For early career researchers, DBR can feel both exciting and uncertain. It demands methodological rigour while embracing the unpredictability of real-world settings. It asks us to be designers as well as researchers; to listen deeply, to adapt when our plans meet reality, and to see “failure” as an opportunity for learning and redesign. These qualities can be challenging, but they are also what make DBR so powerful.
