# Table 6.1 Summary Table of Rapid Review Findings

| Research Question (RQ) | Main Findings | Key Implications |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1: DBR Purpose and Processes | DBR studies addressed diverse purposes: digital platforms, inclusive technologies, teacher professional development, giftedness diagnostics, robotics, interdisciplinary learning. Methods included interviews, observations, and surveys. | DBR is highly adaptable, tackling varied educational challenges. Researchers can learn from past projects to design context-sensitive innovations. |
| RQ2: Theoretical Frameworks | Wide range of frameworks: constructivism, socio-cultural theory, project-based learning, enactivism, situational interest, co-creation, communities of practice. Some relied only on DBR principles without naming a framework. | DBR is flexible: researchers can align their study with theories that suit their context. Careful framework selection strengthens the relevance and meaning of findings. |
| RQ3: Key Findings | DBR supported teacher professional development, created new learning tools, improved accessibility, and engaged students. Success was shaped by teacher engagement, leadership support, time, and resources. Some studies reported minimal impact or design challenges. | DBR is collaborative and context-dependent. Researchers must anticipate barriers and design studies that respect both human and systemic factors for sustainable impact. |
| RQ4: Implications for Research and Practice | Sustained, context-specific professional development emerged as crucial. DBR informed innovations in technology, pedagogy, inclusion, and cross-sector collaboration. | DBR has a dual role: advancing research while empowering educators. To be effective, it must address local contexts, support long-term PD, and foster collaboration. |
