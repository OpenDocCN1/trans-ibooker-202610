# Summary of Findings

These studies illustrate the diversity of DBR applications. They tackle educational challenges by designing digital tools, developing interventions, and creating new learning experiences. Research methods included observations, interviews, and surveys, with participants spanning teachers, students, and researchers.

# Significance of Findings

The breadth of DBR highlights its adaptability and relevance to real-world educational issues. These studies show how DBR both generates practical solutions and deepens understanding of teaching and learning in authentic settings. By reviewing such examples, researchers can draw lessons from earlier work to guide their own designs.

# RQ2: Theoretical Frameworks

# Characteristics of the Literature

DBR studies employed a wide variety of theoretical frameworks, including:

+   - Student-centred and constructivist models (Dibaba et al., 2024; Soforon, 2024).
+   - Project-based and co-creation approaches (Iskandar, 2024; Martel & Garcias, 2024).
+   - Social constructivism and Communities of Practice (Dibaba, 2024; Jita & Dhliwayo, 2024).
+   - More specialised orientations such as enactivism (Poizat, 2024), situational interest (Tillmann & Wegner, 2024), and transdisciplinarity (Parth et al., 2024).
+   - Some studies relied solely on DBR principles without naming an explicit framework (Donmez & Cagiltay, 2024; Peperkorn & Wegner, 2024).

# Summary of Findings

The reviewed studies show that DBR is compatible with diverse theoretical orientations. Design principles often shaped how these theories were interpreted and applied in practice.
