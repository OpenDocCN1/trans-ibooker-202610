# EXAMPLE IN CONTEXT

At Greenwood Primary School, the first classroom trials of the *Reading Pathways* app were anything but smooth. In one class, the Wi-Fi dropped mid-lesson. Another educator discovered the tablets hadn’t been charged. A few learners tapped randomly through the phonics games, while others needed one-on-one support just to navigate the interface.

Far from derailing the project, these real-world challenges became data points. The team added an offline mode, simplified the login screen, and created an “educator assist” feature to let educators pause and annotate a session in real time. They also included a paper-based fallback option with QR codes linked to audio prompts.

These adaptations didn’t dilute the research but made it stronger. By designing *within* the realities of classroom life, the team created a more robust, usable tool. Furthermore, by observing how the intervention fared in unpredictable conditions, they gained richer insights into how digital supports function within the ebb and flow of everyday teaching.

# *Strategies for Effective Real-World DBR*

To conduct DBR successfully in authentic settings, researchers should consider the following strategies:

+   - **Collaborative Design**: Work with educators from the start to co-design interventions that are practical and feasible within the constraints of the classroom. Plan and communicate well in advance, ensuring that all parties understand the timelines and expectations of the research. Researchers should also remain flexible when adjustments need to be made and, if issues arise, try to work within the school’s existing timetable and constraints rather than creating additional pressure.

+   - **Contextual Adaptation**: Be prepared to adapt the intervention in response to unique contextual factors that may arise, ensuring that the research remains flexible.

+   - **Continuous Feedback Loops**: Maintain an open line of communication with educators and learners to gather ongoing feedback and make necessary adjustments during the implementation phase.
