DBR interventions may start out as small-scale pilots, even paper or wireframe prototypes for walking through with partners, but they develop through iterations of implementation, evaluation, and design into interventions of increasing scale in the real world. DBR researchers work through cycles to progressively identify the best problem to solve and refine a solution for the real-world context of the study. Effective solutions are those that address the correct problem and are usable in context. This is another example of why it is so important to focus on the research context in a holistic way that encompasses the individuals and the systems within which they interact. This allows the researcher to explore, with the research partners, what effectiveness and usability mean for these partners.

# Focus on Responsiveness

# Decision 3: Will the Research Be Responsive to Change during the Research Process?

DBR projects are highly dynamic as they are responsive to the context of study. The mindset of openness and curiosity entails that DBR researchers remain open to what may arise or be learned in the research context. This can affect the problem they intend to address, the solution they have designed, or adaptations that need to be made through an intervention based on what is happening in real-time within the study context. An experimental study cannot implement change in this responsive way.

The thinking behind this is that, because the real world is a changeable and messy place, in order to be effective, DBR projects need to be able to adapt. With respect to this notion that research must be adaptable and must maintain ethical accountability and rigour, all changes and their rationale need to be documented fully and consistently (Obczovsky et al., 2025).
