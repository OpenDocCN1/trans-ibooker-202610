# Understanding Evaluation and Reflection in Design-Based Research

Evaluation and reflection are fundamental to Design-Based Research (DBR), guiding one’s understanding, the design refinement, and theoretical development of interventions within authentic contexts. In DBR, both evaluation and reflection work in tandem to achieve a dual role: improving educational practice while also generating useful theory. This chapter explores the significance of evaluation and reflection in DBR, drawing on case studies to show why these processes are crucial for ensuring both practical effectiveness and theoretical insight.

# Evaluation as a Continuous Process

In traditional research, evaluation is often treated as a discrete phase at the end of a project. In contrast, DBR embeds evaluation throughout the iterative research process. It occurs not only after implementation but also during design iterations. Evaluation in DBR informs the design decisions, validates learning outcomes, and contributes to theory development.

Even during the initial planning and preparation of your DBR study, some decisions you make can form part of the evaluation process. The table below illustrates examples of early decision-making.
