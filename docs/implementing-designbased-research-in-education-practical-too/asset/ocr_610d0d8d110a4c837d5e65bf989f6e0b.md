# Rapid Review in Real-Time

The following section models how to structure and document a rapid review for inclusion in a thesis or journal article. It is important to note that this represents only one possible approach; there are many effective ways to report a rapid review depending on context, purpose, and disciplinary conventions.

A useful starting point is the introduction. Although some researchers write this section after completing the review, drafting it early can serve as a working guide that clarifies focus and intent. The introduction should explicitly outline the review’s purpose, scope, rationale, and relevance and conclude with a set of guiding questions or objectives that justify the use of a rapid review approach.

To support this process, consider the following reflective questions:

# PAUSE AND REFLECT

+   - What is the purpose of this rapid review?
+   - What is the scope of this rapid review?
+   - What is the rationale behind this rapid review?
+   - What is the methodology for DBR?
+   - What is the relevance of DBR?

What are the guiding questions or objectives for this rapid review?

As you work through these reflective questions, you may find that they require careful attention and reflection. However, much of this groundwork has likely already been established in your research proposal, where many of these questions may have been explored in greater detail. Building on your proposal, the current task is to establish that foundation and frame your review in a way that aligns with your study’s aims and objectives.
