# FORMATIVE EVALUATION

By the third week, researchers noticed that a few confident children tended to dominate the discussions while quieter ones stayed on the sidelines. This raised an important question: were all children getting a chance to share their ideas and practise critical thinking? To address this, the team added structured turn-taking and individual questioning strategies. Over time, more children began sharing their thoughts, and their ideas showed deeper reasoning and a wider range of perspectives.

# SUMMATIVE EVALUATION

At the end of the study, the team reviewed the overall impact of the storytelling approach and identified several design principles to guide future practice. One key principle was the importance of using individual questioning strategies when appropriate or necessary to ensure that every child’s thinking was recognised and extended.

# PAUSE AND REFLECT ...

The following prompts, adapted from O’Reilly (2024) in their DBR study on critical thinking in preschool settings, are designed to support your own planning and evaluation:

+   - What kinds of data would best help you understand how children are developing critical thinking in your classroom?
+   - How might you tell the difference between surface-level engagement and deeper, more thoughtful reasoning?
+   - What strategies could help make reflection an ongoing, shared part of your design process?

As you think about these questions, also take a moment to consider the focus and wording of your own evaluation tools:

+   - Are your questions designed to generate the kind of information you truly need?
+   - Could you refine or reframe them to better capture and support the development of the skills you want children to practise?
