# Co-Design and Shared Ownership

In the implementation phase, collaboration often takes the form of co-design meetings and continuous dialogue. During this phase, researchers bring ideas from theory or prior studies, while educators contribute practical wisdom and an evolving knowledge of real-world interactions around what engages students, what fits the school culture, and what constraints (time, curriculum, resources) must be considered. Ideally, the intervention is not something handed to educators; rather it is a journey of discovery designed jointly. When educators help shape the intervention, they are more invested in implementing it with fidelity and insight, which consequently fosters shared ownership of the research design and process.

# EXAMPLE IN CONTEXT

When the first cycle of mathematics workshops began in the teacher education programme, collaboration between university lecturers and school-based mentors quickly proved essential. The lecturers brought a strong theoretical framework for designing tasks that linked mathematical concepts to curriculum standards, while the mentors contributed practical insights about what pre-service educators typically struggled with during school placement.

As the cycle unfolded, unexpected issues emerged: students were enthusiastic about creative problem-solving activities but often overlooked opportunities to make explicit curricular connections. Instead of treating this as a failure, the research team and mentors met after each session to compare notes.
