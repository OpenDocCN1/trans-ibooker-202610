+   4. Make note of any critical incidents, surprising or unusual behaviour or any contradictions between what people say and what they do.
+   5. Make note of objects and artefacts that may act as clues to behaviour.
+   6. Use photo, video, screen capture, and other media to capture observational data for later analysis (if you have consent for these).
+   7. Be specific and descriptive.
+   8. Write up additional field notes as soon as you can after the observation.

Don'ts

+   1. Don't interfere in what is happening unless you are clearly a participant.
+   2. Don't make assumptions about what you cannot observe (e.g., the motivation behind a certain kind of behaviour). You could follow up and ask your research partners about these unobservable aspects in interviews.
+   3. Don't assume what you see is typical of the setting unless it is repeated on multiple observations.

# Self-Documentation

Another method to explore the research context is by asking your research partners to document their own experiences through a text, photo, video, or any other suitable artefact. This can be hugely valuable as it generates data on the research context, which is exclusively from the perspective of your research partners. No matter how carefully you observe a setting, you cannot see the world through someone else's eyes. With self-documentation, you are getting your research partners' view of the world directly. With observations, you have to work hard not to bring your biases to bear on what you see. With self-documentation, there is no issue where you exclude your research partners' unique lens and focus. Self-documentation allows your research partners to share what is meaningful for them in the research context. For example, the photos you take as an external observer in a school might be very different from those that teachers or students might take, as they will focus on what they feel is important or difficult or interesting in that school. This process does require discussion, and perhaps training, so that you and your partners share a clear understanding of what kinds of things are relevant and important to document.
