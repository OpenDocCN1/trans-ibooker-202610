# Recent Developments in DBR Thinking

In recent years, scholars have begun to extend traditional models of DBR by integrating new theoretical perspectives that emphasise motivation, embodiment, and lived experience. These contemporary approaches move beyond viewing DBR purely as a methodological cycle and instead position it as a flexible framework for exploring how learning unfolds in authentic contexts. Two notable examples include Tillmann and Wegner’s (2024) application of the four-phase model of interest development within a DBR framework and Poizat et al.’s (2024) enactive perspective on DBR, which reimagines design and learning as co-constructed through action and experience.
