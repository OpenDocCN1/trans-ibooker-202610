# IMPLEMENTING DESIGN-BASED RESEARCH IN EDUCATION

Implementing Design-Based Research in Education is a vital guide for researchers and educators seeking to transform educational theory into impactful practice. Grounded in the principles of creativity, empathy, and collaboration, this book examines how Design-Based Research (DBR) can tackle real-world educational challenges while generating rigorous and usable theory.

This comprehensive resource delves into the phases of DBR, offering step-by-step guidance catering to both novice and experienced researchers. It covers key topics such as building sustainable partnerships, understanding the lived experiences of learners and others in the research environment, facilitating creative design workshops, and grounding research in robust theoretical frameworks. Through case studies, practical examples, and detailed discussions, this book highlights the potential of DBR to inform policy and practice. Readers will also gain valuable insights into strategies for fostering empathy, designing interventions, evaluating outcomes, promoting innovation, and scaling projects in educational research.

Ideal for both novice and seasoned DBR practitioners, this book provides the tools and inspiration needed to navigate the complexities of educational research with confidence.

## ABOUT THE AUTHORS

Dr Ann Devitt is Associate Professor of Language Education at the School of Education and a Fellow of Trinity College Dublin, Ireland. She currently serves as the Head of School in the School of Education and the Academic Director of The Learnovate Centre, an Enterprise Ireland-funded centre of excellence for the future of work and learning. Her research interests lie at the intersection of technology and education, in particular language and literacy education.

Dr Jen O’Sullivan is a Lecturer in Literacy Education at Marino Institute of Education, Ireland, where she teaches across undergraduate and postgraduate teacher education programmes. Her PhD research, grounded in DBR, explored the role of phonemic awareness in supporting early literacy in a lower socioeconomic school context. She is a past president of the Literacy Association of Ireland and a Fulbright Scholar.

Dr Catherine O’Reilly is a Research Fellow in the School of Education at Trinity College Dublin, Ireland. She completed her PhD at Trinity College Dublin in 2024, where her research focused on early childhood education and the development of critical thinking skills in young children through storytelling. Her current research investigates the interplay and learning of young children with technology in the digital age, an area that harmonises her practical experience with her academic expertise.

## PREFACE

The idea for this book germinated with the authors as they worked together and separately through a number of DBR projects. We have engaged through this work with the tension between practical real-world application and the goal of generating theory. Our research approach has always sought to develop theory that can do work in the world, aligning ourselves with Lewin, who states that “there is nothing as practical as a good theory” (Lewin, 1943, p. 8).

With each new project came new realisations and new learning about the process: how to develop mutually beneficial and sustainable partnerships with communities of learners, educators, and other stakeholders; how to develop a deep understanding of the lived experiences and needs of these research partners; how to facilitate creative and productive design workshops; and how to ground all of this activity within a powerful theoretical framework.

This book is the book we wanted on our own shelves. Like DBR itself, it aims to tackle the tension between the practical and the theoretical. It deals in-depth with how to work through the phases of DBR in detail and also addresses the theoretical grounding for the approach and its components. Newcomers to the methodological approach could use the book as a step-by-step guide to the process. Experienced DBR researchers could use the book to explore practical and theoretical elements of the approach in greater detail – how to scale, how to develop empathy, and how to promote creativity.

In the messy real world of educational research, we hope that this book will offer you, our readers, support and encouragement to strive for deeper understandings that will be useful for policy and for practice and to do this with creativity, empathy, and joy.
