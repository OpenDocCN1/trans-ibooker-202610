# An Integrative Perspective

Together, these newer contributions illustrate how DBR continues to evolve. Earlier frameworks, such as those by the DBRC (2003) and McKenney and Reeves (2012), focused mainly on DBR as a structured process for designing, testing, and refining educational interventions through iterative cycles. The newer models build on this foundation but widen the lens. Tillmann and Wegner demonstrate how DBR can be used to study and support motivation, that is, how the learners’ interest develops and changes over time, while Poizat and colleagues show how DBR can attend to the lived experience of learners and teachers by viewing learning as something that is enacted through real activity, not just observed from the outside. Both perspectives remind researchers that DBR is not only about improving instructional designs but also about understanding the complex human processes that unfold within them.

# Chapter Summary

Each of the frameworks and models described in this chapter has contributed uniquely to the evolving landscape of educational enquiry, offering structured yet flexible approaches for bridging theory and practice. Collectively, they foreground key principles that underpin effective educational innovation: iterative refinement, collaborative design, real-world contextualisation, and the co-generation of practical and theoretical knowledge. While they can differ in emphasis with some prioritising scalability, and others ecological validity or systemic change, they share a commitment to addressing authentic educational problems through rigorous, responsive enquiry.

For researchers and practitioners, the diversity of these frameworks and models is a strength. Depending on the nature of the research question, the context, and the desired outcomes, one may be more suitable than another. For example, projects aiming to influence large-scale policy or systemic reform may benefit from the DBIR approach, while classroom-level interventions may be better served by models such as Reeves’ or Collins et al.’s design experiments. Understanding these nuances empowers researchers to adopt frameworks and models that best align with their goals, ensuring both methodological integrity and practical impact.
