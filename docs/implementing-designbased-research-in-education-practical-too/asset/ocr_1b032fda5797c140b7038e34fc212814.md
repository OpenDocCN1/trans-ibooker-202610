# Challenges in the Analysis and Exploration Phase

While the problem identification and analysis phase is essential for ensuring that a DBR intervention is both relevant and impactful, it is not without its challenges. Because DBR is conducted in authentic educational contexts, researchers must navigate the complexities and constraints of real-world practice.

+   - **Complexity of Educational Settings**: Schools and classrooms are dynamic systems, influenced by multiple factors such as curriculum demands, learner diversity, educator expertise, resource availability, and policy requirements. These factors interact in unpredictable ways, making it difficult to isolate a single, clearly defined problem. As Barab and Squire (2004) argue, the authenticity of DBR research brings richness but also increases the messiness of design, requiring flexibility and openness to an evolving understanding of the problem.
+   - **Diverse Partner Opinions**: All partners, such as educators, school leaders, parents, and learners, may hold differing priorities and expectations. For example, an educator may be most concerned with immediate classroom management issues, while school leaders may prioritise alignment with school improvement plans. Negotiating these perspectives requires careful communication, transparency, and compromise. Penuel and Gallagher (2017) highlight that engaging partners in co-design is essential for building shared goals, but it can also be time-consuming and politically sensitive.
+   - **Time Constraints**: Conducting a comprehensive needs analysis can be difficult within the limited timelines imposed by school calendars, funding cycles, or research deadlines. Researchers must strike a balance between gathering sufficient data to understand the problem deeply and moving forward with the design of interventions in a timely manner. Reeves (2006) notes that this tension between rigour and practicality is a defining feature of DBR and must be managed through iterative cycles that allow problems and solutions to be progressively refined.
