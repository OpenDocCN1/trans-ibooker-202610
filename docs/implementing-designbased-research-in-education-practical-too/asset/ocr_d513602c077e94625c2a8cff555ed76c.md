# What Can Help

The cornerstone of overcoming this challenge is relationship-building and trust. From the outset, make it explicit that educators are not merely the “implementers” of someone else’s design but valued collaborators whose professional expertise shapes the project. Clarify how their involvement strengthens both the intervention and their own professional growth, for example, by connecting the research to a problem the school community already prioritises (“This project builds on your school’s focus on increasing engagement in science”). Equally important is reframing the nature of research involvement. Assure educators that the project does not evaluate them personally but instead focuses on the design’s effectiveness in real-world conditions. Position DBR as a supportive, problem-solving process rather than a judgement of their competence.

To reduce role conflict, formalise moments when educators are explicitly acting as co-researchers. This might involve scheduled reflection meetings, guided by protocols or key questions, where educators can analyse data, discuss adaptations, and share insights. Structuring this reflective time signals that research activities are part of professional practice. Over time, buy-in often grows organically as educators witness tangible benefits in learning and/or engagement. Initial hesitation frequently turns into enthusiasm when educators see their contributions making a visible difference. By acknowledging concerns openly, providing recognition (e.g., professional development credit, acknowledgement in reports), and framing the work as a shared endeavour aimed at improving learning, researchers can usually transform tentative participants into strong allies.

+   **PRACTICAL TIPS**
+   
+   - Involve educators in co-design to build ownership and investment.
+   - Reframe the dual role as an asset, not a liability.
+   - Use structured reflection sessions to legitimise educators’ role as co-researchers.
+   - Emphasise shared goals such as better outcomes for students, rather than surveillance of teaching.
