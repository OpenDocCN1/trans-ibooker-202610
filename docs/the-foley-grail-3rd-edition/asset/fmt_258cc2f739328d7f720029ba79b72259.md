
Carl Stalling is considered the first cartoon composer to include music puns in his music for cartoons. He is one of the three composers, along with film composer Max Steiner and MGM cartoon composer Scott Bradley, credited with inventing the click track, which allowed musicians to play a musical score to a film not yet completed. Stalling utilized instrumentation to mimic sound effects for his cartoons and orchestrated lush arrangements for his musicians.

In 1993, composer Richard Stone carried the Warner Brothers animation baton with his inventive music in *Animaniacs*, *Taz-Mania*, *The Sylvester and Tweety Mysteries*, and *Tiny Toon Adventures*, as well as *Pinky and the Brain*. Stone’s introduction to film music was as a music editor, who edited film scores for such films as *Witness* (1985) and *Platoon* (1986), after completing his bachelor’s degree studying cello at Indiana University in Bloomington, Indiana. He continued the Warner Brothers house style of humor and complicated orchestrations for his cartoon compositions. Even with the addition of sound effects and Foley in animated films and cartoon shows, music still accompanies the action to highlight various story points.

## Who Walks Like That, Really?

The first issue that a Foley artist must engage with is that animation is a story world unto itself. Forget about anything about physics. Animators play with science and art like no one else would imagine. The characters in animation walk like no one walks. The humans in an animated feature will possess enough gravity to make them credible, but the purpose of animation is to take the audience members somewhere they have never been. The suspension of disbelief is taken as far as possible.

Animals in animation are anthropomorphic. This means they possess human qualities while remaining animal-like. Animators study animals carefully to watch how they move and how they respond. But they also work with the narrative to ensure that the animal creatures maintain relatability. They often talk and display empathy and comprehension. A Foley artist has to move like the animated animal animated animal responds in the story world.

The footsteps of animated characters may or may not follow normal patterns. The viewers may see left, right, left, right. But then again, they may not. The reason for this is that animated characters are moving to serve the story, humor, and relationships. Also, the timing of animation is not the timing of live action. Rabbits may hop slower, then faster, to accommodate dialogue. A donkey may walk with a humorous gait to add personality to the character. The Foley artist may be called upon to create a special kind of walk for an animated character. Take nothing for granted when performing Foley footsteps for animation. And remember that animated features and episodic cartoons are very different.

Additionally, the footwear that animated characters wear is likely to be from a different time period. Thus, shoe auditions are imperative. Listen to a variety of shoes carefully. There is no production track to work with. All of the sounds will be artificially constructed. The Foley artist is creating the ground surfaces, the walls, the furniture, the clothing, and the footwear; in short, everything the character wears and touches. It is an exciting adventure. Everything the Foley artist does actually matters.

## Proper Properties

The challenging aspect of creating props in an animated feature that is situated in a make-believe world is that one does not know what that world is supposed to sound like. This is fantastic and fraught with possible dangers. If the Foley artists have good communication with those who make the final decisions, then they will get the information they need to make excellent choices regarding floors, costumes, props, etc. However, they will also be prepared to make alternative tracks if they do not know what the sound editors are doing for the film. This is the precarious aspect. There is always the possibility that the editors are assuming that Foley is creating certain special sounds and the Foley artists are expecting the editors to be handling those duties.

Additionally, there are special synthesists who create special design effects that complement animation projects. The Foley artists need to know who is responsible for what sounds. This is always the main question in every film, television, or game project, but in animation, it is more critical than ever. More often than not, there will be specially designed sounds that will need to be imagined. Who gets the honor?

## Technological Transitions

It is interesting that, as film has gone to digital and animation uses more computer-generated imagery (CGI), the actual process of making an animated film has not changed much. The tradition of hand-drawn storyboarding has survived the technological convergence. Additionally, if sound supervisors are involved early enough in the process, some of the sound is recorded early. But, typically, it is done in postproduction. The voices, however, are done before any animation is rendered.

One change, however, is that there are more temporary sounds added in by picture editors. This can be useful if it helps the sound supervi-sor get a better idea of the trajectory the supervising animator wishes to take with the sound design.

One facet that complicates performing Foley for animation is that the picture is not totally finished when the Foley artists work on it. They will be working on a scene that is partially rendered and partially in pencil, perhaps with parts of it missing completely. There is a leap of faith that comes with performing Foley for animation. The flow the artist is trying to create with her or his imagination and creativity has to continue even when the picture abruptly disappears or changes drastically.

The rhythm may alter significantly for story purposes, and that can create challenges. When this happens, the principle to keep in mind is that the animated story world is created out of whole cloth. There is nothing real to begin with, so the artist is not locked into anything other than what the animators, the actors, and the sketchy sounds that are on the track from previous temp dubs offer. The Foley artist may be wacky or realistic, understated or overblown, whatever is appropriate to the narrative and the world created by the animators. Keep in mind that the story for animation is written particularly with the animators in mind. That is what is so unique about animation. The story allows the characters and the audience to go somewhere fresh and new. Sound effects and Foley can create new aural concepts that were not considered part of any world before.
