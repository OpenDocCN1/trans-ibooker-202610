
Cornstarch is for the squeak. If the cue is on concrete, use some corn meal for the body of the snow and a little bit of cornstarch for the squeak. Squeaks record differently on every stage and with every microphone, so this is trial and error. If the cue is in dirt, use a little bit of cornstarch in the dirt. If the snow is slushy, use a wet chamois on concrete and some wet mud for the snow. If the snow is frozen over, use some shaved ice if possible. Otherwise, use coffee grounds, wet concrete, and trial and error until it sounds right.

### 6. Ice skating: block of ice and either an ice skate or a heavy bladed knife
This can be tricky, but the motion of the skate has to match the cuts of the scene. Watch the impacts of the skater. Also, a pen sometimes works well on the ice. I have also used concrete with some water on it and a knife or a sharp pen.

### 7. Boat dock: wooden pallet
There is nothing like having a wooden pallet on a Foley stage. There are many uses for it, but this one is essential. The creaks are particular to every wooden pallet. They are used for moving furniture and large appliances. They are a necessity for every well-equipped Foley stage.

### 8. Bus steps: metal square with rubber mat under it
This works well, unless the bus is in busy traffic. Then forget the rubber mat. Decide what is aesthetically pleasing for the scene.

### 9. Escalator: heavy metal surface with rubber mat under it
Same as for bus steps with conditions: more expensive venues have nicer sounding escalators. Use discretion.

### 10. Bare feet footsteps
May not be necessary on carpet; on a hard surface overplay slightly, but use the mixer as a guide. Walk toe-heel instead of heel-toe if it does not record right. Experiment.

### 11. Children’s footsteps: lighter and smaller
The trick to children’s footsteps is that they are faster, smaller, and lighter.

### 12. Running footsteps: be impressionistic
It is not always possible to get every footstep. So, look for the basic feel of the rhythm. The edits will not match; get the basic feel of the run. Chances are, in a scene where someone is running, there will be music. But the biggest mistake is to get caught up in a rhythm and stick with it. Pay attention to changes as the edits change.

### 13. Leather car seat: chiropractor block or leather jacket
Be tasteful with this. Think about the quality of the car.

### 14. Horse footsteps: coconuts with meat baked inside and hot glued in
This takes practice. Figure out the best way to hold them. Do you want to hang on and keep your wrists lose? Do you want to have straps to keep them on? Follow the back feet. Horses lead with their back feet and fall onto their front feet when galloping or running. Watch the edits. Rather than be precise, be rhythmic. Watch the musicality of the horse. This is also true for other animals, by the way.
Microphones should be positioned as follows: one boom microphone over the top of the horses and one small body microphone buried in the dirt (if possible) to give the horses extra weight. Dig the dirt up underneath so the hooves do not get hollow. The dirt should continue to fill up in the coconuts.

### 15. Cats: either hands with fingernails or gloves with small tacks in the fingers
The trick to cats is that they do not walk with their claws out. They use the pads of their feet. So, they walk very lightly. They only use their claws when climbing on cloth or when kneading.

### 16. Dogs: bare hands with fingernails, gloves with small tacks in the fingers, or fake nails
The trick to dogs is that they use their claws more than cats, but not consistently. They slide around. So, use the claw sounds to help dogs get their balance and to establish that they are around. Too much claw sounds fake. Also, the dog collar and dog chain are very useful in “selling” the dog.

### 17. Water: plastic pool if no built-in tank
The trick here is to avoid hitting the plastic with the water. Fill the pool deep enough to get a variety of pitches, but not so deep that when water splashes out of the pool it will hit hard surfaces, splash onto concrete or wood, or hit a microphone stand. Deep glugs sound nice and have plenty of bass. Hit the surface of the water with flat hands to record light splashes. Play with the pitches. Be very careful not to splash the microphone. If there is a hydrophone, that is great. Most artists do not. Use a boom microphone and aim it down, but not directly at the water or it will get splashed. The mixer can try various positions. Also, try two microphones to see how they record. Be adventurous. Every room is different.

### 18. Goop: wet chamois, body lotion, mouth noises, hair gel, sour cream, hand soap
This is a trial-and-error thing. One really needs to see what works. Normally, it will take several tracks to get goop just right.
One can also get a chicken from the grocery store. It is expensive and full of bacteria, so be mindful. Wash hands and the area it is used on. The bacteria from a chicken is deadly when not cooked. Take this warning seriously and use Lysol.

### 19. Bones: celery
Need I say more?

### 20. Grit: coffee grounds right from the can
Get the cheapest can of coffee and keep it dry. Use it sparingly and grind it down with underfoot if the concrete is supposed to be slightly gritty. If the surface is supposed to be in a sketchy part of town, use more.
Be careful. Grit pops on a microphone. That is why Foley artists will use coffee instead of dirt. However, one can use dirt, just be mindful.

### 21. Kisses: yes, Foley artists kiss their hands
It can get sloppy.

### 22. Hugs: Foley artists typically hug themselves
Get the right clothing.

### 23. Claps: be mindful of the bounce in the room

### 24. Cloth passes: soft blue jeans
If doing one cloth pass for the entire scene to fill in for any ADR, use jeans or generic cloth. If special cloth is needed, then do only that cloth on its own track. Rule: less is more. Do not overdo cloth. I can promise that if the cloth movement is overdone, the rerecording mixer will not play it.

## SAFETY TIPS
1. Use eye goggles when working with wood splinters or glass. Splinters and chards are not fun.
2. Gloves are essential when working with any kind of debris.
3. Have hygienic masks on the stage when doing a lot of dirt cues. Lungs need to last a lifetime.
4. Have a broom and a mop on the stage. Keep in clean.
5. Keep the props organized.
6. Do not put yourself in danger. It is a movie.
7. Water can get rancid and attract scary critters. Clean out tanks, pools, and receptacles.
8. Do not drink out of a glass, cup, or anything without washing it.
