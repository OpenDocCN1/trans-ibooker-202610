
Foley began as an arcane addition to edited film sound and has developed into an essential part of film, television, web series, games, animation, trailers, radio plays, and commercials.

In 1989, when I appeared on *Late Night with David Letterman* demonstrating Foley, very little had been written about Foley, and few professionals were interviewed or even credited for their work.

While there is still a large percentage of film goers who have little knowledge of postproduction sound and the professionals who create the soundscapes Foley is an intriguing and mysterious art that attracts a great deal of interest.

I usually ask film and sound professionals if they have some parting words for readers interested in Foley. The following are some of the responses I have received.

#### [Mark Mangini](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_1)

(supervising sound editor, sound designer, rerecording mixer, musician)

Sound is a dramatic player. It’s not as interactive a process as I think it needs to be.

When asked what he thinks prepares someone to be an expert in sound:

> Some of the best training they can get is taking acting and screenwriting classes.

#### [Jody Thomas](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_2)

(Foley artist, story editor, creative executive)

It’s really empowering to get to work with people I think are great, and that we get along and have a good time.

#### [Karin Roulo](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_3)

(Foley and ADR mixer, engineer)

When commenting on what a good Foley artist looks for in a footstep cue:

> You look at how he or she is portraying the character.

#### [Robert Deschaine](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_4)

(Foley and ADR mixer, rerecording mixer, facility manager, actor)

There are different ways to approach a project. Some thought as an editor, and others did it as an artist or actor.

#### [Meltem Baytok](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_5)

(Foley artist)

When asked how she perceives her process when performing Foley:

> They are my toys, and I am playing to make the right emotions.

#### [David E. Stone](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_6)

(supervising sound editor, dialogue editor, effects editor, animator, educator)

Everything that moves and causes friction has a different context.

#### [Michael Semanick](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_7)

(rerecording mixer, Foley and ADR mixer, music engineer)

You’ve got to pay attention to the artist and the picture.

You can make it your own and sometimes you can make it better. A good \[Foley\] artist can say, “OK, that’s good … now I’ll just sweeten it.”

When discussing people’s obsession with new bells and whistles in technology:

> I think they forget to actually listen.

#### [Richard Portman](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_8)

(rerecording mixer, engineer, educator)

I teach the aesthetics and the theory of sound. My classes are lecture classes and then they \[the students\] have to come in and sit down and put it all together.

When they \[the students\] come in they know nothing. That’s the great strength. They don’t come in with something on their minds and then you have to destroy that before you can teach them what to do.

First you’re a writer, then a director. It’s a talking book. That’s the key. If you can’t hear the sound, then that’s bad printing. It’s smudged.

#### [Miguel Barbosa](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_9)

(Foley artist, editor)

Sometimes I have to rush a lot.

When asked how he responds to hearing lots of different opinions about how Foley should be done:

> Having different views and approaches are helpful.

#### [Mark Pappas](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_10)

(Foley editor)

We have the ability to make things better now, and I think sometimes we do.

I think the most important thing is to be on top of everything with the technology because of how quickly it changes.

Try to search out the people who are the best in line with what it is you want to do.

#### [Misa Jakovljevic](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_11)

(Foley artist, editor)

When asked what he thinks about when performing Foley:

> Where is the soul in that sound?

#### [Jerry Trent](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_12)

(Foley artist, dancer)

Most of the real things don’t sound like the real things.

You have to know what goes on in making a movie, what goes on off camera and on.

#### [Tom Fleischman](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_13)

(rerecording mixer)

It’s all about the story.

#### [Alyson Dee Moore](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_14)

(Foley artist)

It’s kind of like how people eat a corn cob: some people eat straight across, in very neat rows. Some eat around and some eat all over. I’m more of an eating in clean rows type.

When commenting on some good advice she got (from your embarrassed author):

> You are the prop.

#### [Aleyna Seymen](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_15)

(Foley artist)

Everything is about feeling.

#### [David Jobe](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_16)

(Foley and ADR mixer)

There is no such thing as a perfect mic in Foley.

When asked what he pays attention to in recording Foley:

> The aesthetic choices, the quality of how they \[the Foley artists\] paint the sound, the perspective and richness of the sound.

#### [Cornelia Baumgartner](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_17)

(sound designer, Foley artist, composer)

In every object lives a sound.

#### [Andy Malcolm](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_18)

(Foley artist)

The ultimate Foley for a drama is recorded on location.

#### [Kevin O’Connell](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_19)

(rerecording mixer)

Mixers are artists. If a light bulb goes out, we call an engineer!

#### [Julien Naudin](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_20)

(Foley artist)

When asked to describe American Foley:

> \[Playfully laughs\] Whoosh, whoosh!

#### [Scott Hecker](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_21)

(supervising sound editor)

Get it in the ballpark.

When commenting on too many cues or choices in Foley:

> The last thing I want is what I call “clogging the arteries.” It’s just too much.

His advice to others wanting a career in sound:

> Watch as many movies and TV shows as you possibly can and listen to what you are about to endeavor. Go to school on other people’s professionalism.

#### [Greg Barbanell](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_22)

(Foley artist, editor)

Feeling good about a movie you’re working on is a plus. Keep it simple.

#### [Ele Matelan](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_23)

(Foley artist)

We’re all magpies.

#### [F. Hudson Miller](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_24)

(supervising sound editor)

The spice that’s all around it.

#### [Heikki Kossi](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_25)

(Foley artist, editor)

There can be rules, but every project is different, and you have to rethink everything.

#### [Rick Owens](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_26)

(Foley artist)

I try to hear the Foley in my head, and sometimes I vocalize it.

#### [Don Sylvester](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_27)

(supervising sound editor)

Give as much as the actors give on the set.

#### [Joan Rowe](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_28)

(Foley artist, actor)

I am not someone who listens to the Foley. I want it to support the drama of the piece.

#### [Karan Singh](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_29)

(Foley artist)

Whenever you’re performing, you have to be the character, and the soul of the actor should come at you.

#### [Richard James Hinton](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_30)

(Foley artist, editor)

It was the most frustrating job for the first three years. One day you wake up and you are more focused on the quality.

#### [Hilda Hodges](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_31)

(Foley artist)

After all these years, I still love it.

#### [Marco Ciorba](#content.xhtml#bck_sec15_32)

(Foley artist)

The Foley can add some soul to the story.
