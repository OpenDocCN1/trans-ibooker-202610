
# Foley 术语表

## 术语

**Land the cue,** land a cue To get the Foley performance accurately and with the correct style or character. Usually used to describe exceptional performance on a first or second attempt or “take.”

**Laundry list** Term of art for a casual list of requested footstep and prop cues for the Foley artist that has no time code, details, or specifics.

**Leader** Film with no sound recorded used as a blank in picture or soundtrack, usually found at the beginning of a reel or as space holder for future sound effects.

**Looping** Term of art that refers to ADR in contemporary usage. Derives from the historical practice of recording an actor’s lines without synchronization, which could be played in a “loop” for others to listen and critique.

**Low end** Term of art that refers to sounds that are darker and deeper in timbre, i.e., “low-end rumble” used to effect mystery or fear.

**M&E** Music and effects. Refers to soundtrack that includes a complete mix of effects, music, and production tracks, with dialogue edited out to be used for foreign films where language must be replaced. Usually a more complete series of effects than a domestic release where the original language remains.

**Mag** Magnetic tape. Used for recording sound, music, dialogue, and Foley prior to digital recording.

**Manipulations** Another term for prop Foley.

**Marry, married effects and Foley** Term of art that refers to effects that rely on both edited effects and performed Foley effects for a complete sound event.

**Metal ring off** Term of art that refers to the resonance that hangs on in a recording after impact is recorded.

**Motion capture** Also known as performance capture, it is the practice of recording the movement of actors or objects for 3D animation for live-action and animated films, as well as games.

**Mouth sounds** Foley sounds performed using the mouth; usually supplements other Foley sounds. Often comical.

**Multiple tracks** Use of more than one recording track for various aspects of one sound event.

**Optical sound** Sound that was recorded on optical film, prior to magnetic tape. The sound track was on a stripe of film.

**Panning** The practice of mixing a Foley effect to have sound travel from one side to another, i.e., left to right as a person walks across the screen.

**Paper life** Term of art giving a sense of the presence of paper without specific movements delineated.

**Platform** Educational term that refers to a planned curriculum that builds skills and knowledge from the first course to final courses; each course relies on knowledge from the previous course.

**Postproduction** The process of filmmaking beginning with picture editing and ending with the final mix or dub.

**Predub** The process of mixing sound effects, Foley, ADR, and dialogue in their own groups before the final dub or mix where all sounds are blended together.

**Predubs** The collected sound materials separated by “food groups” into dialogue, principal ADR, group ADR, sound effects, background effects, Foley footsteps, and Foley props.

**Pro Tools** Digital recording, editing, and mixing system that can be used on a Macintosh or PC computer. Industry standard.

**Production track** The sound track recorded during production, usually focused on dialogue in the scene. Sounds recorded in production are included and utilized as a blueprint for sound effects and Foley.

**Pull, pulled** Term of art that refers to auditioning and compiling sound effects from a library for use in a soundtrack.

**Punch in** Technique used in Foley mixing where the mixer picks a precise spot to begin recording in the middle of a prerecorded cue and enters the cue at that point.

**Quarter-inch** tape Recording tape used in reel-to-reel recording.

**Read** Term of art that refers to how something is interpreted by the audience.

**Recorded wild** Custom Foley sound effects that are recorded on a Foley stage without sync for a specific purpose; usually recorded with variations.

**Recordist** Sound professional who assists the mixer. Historically, duties included preparing the magnetic tape and ensuring quality in the recording. Contemporary recordists most often assist with setting up recording sessions for mixers, troubleshooting Pro Tools, and keeping track of Foley cues.

**Ring off** Residual sound that is recorded after the impact of a prop is completed.

**Scuffs** Footstep cues that are not full footsteps, but have a characteristic quality of muted impact.

**Shoe element** Quality of shoe(s) used; character of shoe used.

**Single stripe** Magnetic film that has one track available to record sound.

**Snow design** Specific combination of substances used to create specific snow sounds, depending on the quality of weather depicted.

**Soundman** Historic term of art relating to the profession of making or recording sounds for film, radio, and television.

**Stems** The specific groups of dubbed sound material used in soundtracks, i.e., dialogue stems, sound effects stems, music stems.

**Streamers** Lines that move across the film screen or monitor that alert mixers and Foley artists to the beginning of a cue to be performed.

**Sweet** spot The prime part of a sound that defines the character of the sound; comes from sports terminology.

**Sweeten** To enhance a sound that already exists; to add a quality not already existing.

**Synthesist** A sound professional who creates special sounds and musical effects using synthesized keyboards and instruments. The synthesist is often mistakenly referred to as a sound designer.

**Temp** dubs A temporary mix of sounds to present in a film or film clip, not intended to be the complete soundtrack. Usually used for audience testing.

**Topping production** Adding Foley or sound effects with sound already in production; requires precise sync and is played in concert with the production track.

**Trim bin** Metal bin used in editing that allows editors to collect unnecessary film or hang bits of film to be replaced in the soundtrack.

**Turn over, turnover** The convention of giving the most recent version of film to the next step in process; usually used in reference to picture editing giving the final edited version of film to sound editorial for effects, Foley, ADR, and music.

**Walk the Foley** Slang term of art referring to performing the Foley effects for the film. Comes from the practice of footsteps being the critical part of Foley that needed to be replaced.

**Walk, walking** Perform Foley footsteps.

**Walker** Foley artist. Slang.

**Watching directors** Term coined by Robert L. Mott, referring to directors who watch what is used for a sound rather than listening to the sound itself.
