This may have a detrimental effect on the respiratory tract, particularly for aircrew with temporary or permanent respiratory ailments. Recent research taking samples of cabin residue found high levels of tricresyl phosphate (TCP) (Michaelis 2011). Exposure to this can lead to symptoms of drowsiness, respiratory problems, and neurological illness. There have also been incidents of smoke and fumes in the cabin air as a result of oil or hydraulic fluid entering the engine bleed air as a result of leakages in the lubrication system. This situation has been reported widely on the internet (aerotoxic.org) and there have been incidents of pilots reporting loss of concentration and neurological disorders, and even some aircrew deaths that have been attributed to this (Sunday Express 2013; Learmount 2014). The term aerotoxic syndrome has been used to describe this condition. This is an example of an emergent property or an unexpected consequence of providing a comfortable cabin environment. The example is a relatively simple technical integration, but the emergent property is a complex state of affairs, as can be seen by the lack of agreement as to whether or not it even exists.
