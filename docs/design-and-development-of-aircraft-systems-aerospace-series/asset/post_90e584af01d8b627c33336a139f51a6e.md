This section goes on to consider a simplified method for calculating the fuel weight penalty incurred by the direct effects of the addition of a system to an aircraft. ## 12.6.2 Basic Formulation of Fuel Weight Penalties of Systems

In this section a basic method is formulated to predict the fuel weight penalties of aircraft systems for a single flight phase, based on the three factors identified in Section 12.6.1. The first step in deriving fuel weight penalties equations is to make an assumption about aircraft drag. Thus, the following relationship is used to represent drag:

$$\mathrm{drag}=\frac{\mathrm{weight}}{\mathrm{lift/drag\ ratio}}$$  \hfill (12.1)

In fact, Eq. (12.1) is a simplification since it only holds true if the lift/drag ratio is constant.

Consider an aircraft of weight $W_A$ (excluding system weight) flying at Mach number $M$. Then, the range $dR$ covered by the aircraft over the period $dt$ is given by:

$$dR=aMdt$$  \hfill (12.2)

where $a$ is the speed of sound.

During the period $dt$, the aircraft mass of fuel may be expressed as:

$$(f+\Delta f_W+\Delta f_P+\Delta f_D)\,dt=-d(M_F=\Delta M_F)$$  \hfill (12.3)

where the negative sign is indicative of the fuel weight decreasing as flight time ($t$) increases, and:

- $f$ = rate of fuel used by aircraft without system
- $\Delta f_W$ = rate of fuel used due to system weight
- $\Delta f_P$ = rate of fuel used due to system power off-take
- $\Delta f_D$ = rate of fuel used due to system drag
- $M_F$ = mass of fuel used excluding system effect
- $\Delta M_F$ = extra mass of fuel used due to system effect.

Eq. (12.2) may be rearranged and substituted into Eq. (12.3) in order to eliminate the $dt$ term, and consequently rearranged to give Eq. (12.4):

$$dR=\frac{-aM\left[d\left(M_F+\Delta M_F\right)\right]}{f+\Delta f_W+\Delta f_P+\Delta f_D}$$  \hfill (12.4)

In this case, thrust specific fuel consumption (sfc) (c) is assumed constant and may be expressed as:

$$c = \frac{f + \Delta f_W + \Delta f_P + \Delta f_D}{\text{total drag}}$$  \hfill (12.5)

Thrust sfc is defined as fuel flow rate per unit thrust, and drag is equal to thrust in this case. Thus, by recalling the assumption that was made to write Eq. (12.1), aircraft drag (excluding the effect of the system) can be written as:

$$\text{drag} = \frac{\text{weight}}{\text{lift/drag ratio}} = \frac{W_A + W_F}{r}$$  \hfill (12.6)

where:

- $W_A$ = aircraft empty weight excluding system
- $W_F$ = weight of fuel used excluding system effect
- $r$ = lift/drag ratio.

Therefore, aircraft drag including the effect of the system weight may be expressed as:

$$\text{weight drag} = \frac{W_A + \Delta W_A + W_F + \Delta W_F}{r}$$  \hfill (12.7)

where:

- $\Delta W_A$ = system weight
- $\Delta W_F$ = extra weight of fuel used due to system effect.

The total aircraft drag, including the drag increase due to system direct drag increase ($\Delta D$), and the effective increase in drag due to the engine power off-take required by the system ($\Delta f_P / c$), may be expressed as:

$$\text{total drag} = \frac{W_A + \Delta W_A + W_F + \Delta W_F}{r} + \Delta D + \frac{\Delta f_P}{c}$$  \hfill (12.8)

Substituting Eq. (12.8) into Eq. (12.5) gives:

$$c = \frac{f + \Delta f_W + \Delta f_P + \Delta f_D}{\left[ (W_A + \Delta W_A + W_F + \Delta W_F)(1/r) + \Delta D + \Delta f_P / c \right]}$$  \hfill (12.9)

Rearranging Eq. (12.9) gives:

$$f + \Delta f_W + \Delta f_P + \Delta f_D = \frac{c}{r} \left( W_A + \Delta W_A + W_F + \Delta W_F + r \Delta D + \frac{r \Delta f_P}{c} \right)$$  \hfill (12.10)

Substituting Eq. (12.10) into Eq. (12.4) yields:

$$dR=\frac{r}{c}\cdot\frac{-aM\left[\mathrm{d}(W_F+\Delta W_F)\right]}{\left(W_A+\Delta W_A+W_F+\Delta W_F+r\Delta D+\frac{r\Delta f_p}{c}\right)}$$  \hfill (12.11)

Substituting $M_F=W_F/g$ and $\Delta M_F=\Delta W_F/g$ and integrating Eq. (12.11) gives the aircraft range, $R$:

$$R=aM\frac{r}{cg}\ln\left(\frac{W_A+\Delta W_A+W_{FO}+\Delta W_{FO}+r\Delta D+\frac{r\Delta f_p}{c}}{W_A+\Delta W_A+r\Delta D+\frac{r\Delta f_p}{C}}\right)$$

where:

- $W_{FO}$ = weight of fuel used to fly range, $R$, excluding system
- $\Delta W_{FO}$ = extra weight of fuel used to fly range, $R$, due to system
- $g$ = gravitational constant of acceleration.

It is convenient at this point to define $t$ as the time taken to fly range $R$ ($R=aMt$). Eq. (12.12) can then be simplified to:

$$t\frac{cg}{r}=\ln\left(\frac{W_{FO}+\Delta W_{FO}}{W_A+\Delta W_A+r\Delta D+\frac{r\Delta f_p}{C}}+1\right)$$

Finally, Eq. (12.13) can be rearranged to give the total weight of fuel used by the aircraft with the system fitted, $W_{FO}+\Delta W_{FO}$:

$$W_{FO}+\Delta W_{FO}=\left(W_A+\Delta W_A+r\Delta D+\frac{r\Delta f_p}{c}\right)\left(e^{\frac{ctg}{r}}-1\right)$$  \hfill (12.14)

From Eq. (12.14) it is simple to obtain the weight of fuel used by an aircraft with the system excluded ($W_{FO}$) by setting $\Delta W_{FO}=\Delta W_A=\Delta D=\Delta f_p=0$, giving:

$$W_{FO}=W_A\left(e^{\frac{ctg}{r}}-1\right)$$  \hfill (12.15)

Equally, from Eq. (12.14) it is simple to obtain the increased weight of fuel used due to the system ($\Delta W_{FO}$) by setting $W_{FO}=W_A=0$, yielding:

$$\Delta W_{FO}=\left(\Delta W_A+r\Delta D+\frac{r\Delta f_p}{c}\right)\left(e^{\frac{ctg}{r}}-1\right)$$  \hfill (12.16)

From Eq. (12.16) the fuel weight increase due to the three components identified in Section 12.6.2 can be written in three separate equations as follows:

**Fuel weight increase due to system weight:**

$$(\Delta W_{FO})_{\Delta W_A}=\Delta W_A\left(e^{\frac{ctg}{r}}-1\right)$$  \hfill (12.17)

**Fuel weight increase due to system power off-take:**

$$(\Delta W_{FO})_{\Delta f_p}=\frac{r}{c}\Delta f_p\left(e^{\frac{ctg}{r}}-1\right)$$  \hfill (12.18)

**Fuel weight increase due to system drag:**

$$(\Delta W_{FO})_{\Delta D}=r\Delta D\left(e^{\frac{ctg}{r}}-1\right)$$  \hfill (12.19)

Provided the required data is available Eqs (12.17)–(12.19) can be used to directly calculate the fuel weight penalties of a system.

## 12.6.3 Application of Fuel Weight Penalties Formulation for Multi-phase Flight

In Section 12.6.2 equations to calculate the fuel weight penalties incurred by a system for a single flight phase were derived. Of course, any real aircraft flight will involve multiple phases of flight at different operating conditions. Therefore, this section goes on to consider how these equations may be applied to a multi-phase flight. First, it is assumed that there is a step change in conditions between flight phases. It is then convenient to define a variable, $F$, which represents the fuel weight penalty incurred due to the system in all subsequent flight phases. Therefore, $F$ is the sum of the system fuel weight penalties in all flight phases that take place after the flight phase being considered. It is thus clear that $F=0$ in the final flight phase. Applying this weight penalty to Eq. (12.16) produces an equation for the fuel weight penalty due to the system for a single flight phase, $i$, of a multi-phase flight.

$$(\Delta W_{FO})_i=\left(\Delta W_A+F_i+r\Delta D+\frac{r\Delta f_p}{c}\right)\left(e^{\frac{ctg}{r}}-1\right)$$  \hfill (12.20)

If $n$ is defined as the total number of phases in the flight, then the fuel weight penalty due to the system throughout the entire flight is:

$$\Delta W_{FO}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\Delta W_{FO})_i$$  \hfill (12.21)

## 12.6.4 Analysis of Fuel Weight Penalties Formulation for Multi-phase Flight

Differentiation of Eq. (12.20) with respect to time results in an equation for the instantaneous increase in fuel flow rate due to the system, $\Delta f$, for a given flight phase, $i$:

$$\Delta f_i=\left(\Delta W_A+F_i+r\Delta D+\frac{r\Delta f_p}{c}\right)\frac{c}{r}e^{ctg/r}$$  \hfill (12.22)

Eq. (12.22) can be split into three equations describing the contributions of system weight, off-take power, and direct drag increases, as follows:

$$\left(\Delta f_{\Delta W_A}\right)_i=\left(\Delta W_A+\left(F_{\Delta W_A}\right)_i\right)\frac{c}{r}e^{ctg/r}$$  \hfill (12.23)

$$\left(\Delta f_{\Delta f_p}\right)_i=\left(\frac{r\Delta f_p}{c}+\left(F_{\Delta f_p}\right)_i\right)\frac{c}{r}e^{ctg/r}$$  \hfill (12.24)

$$\left(\Delta f_{\Delta D}\right)_i=\left(r\Delta D+\left(F_{\Delta D}\right)_i\right)\frac{c}{r}e^{ctg/r}$$  \hfill (12.25)

Considering Eq. (12.24) for instantaneous fuel flow rate increase due to system power off-take, it can be shown that at the end of the final flight phase, where $t = 0$, the instantaneous fuel flow rate increase due to the system is only equal to the direct increase due to the system power off-take. At all earlier stages of the flight it is higher than this due to the engine thrust needing to be higher to overcome the extra drag caused by the necessary fuel being carried.

## 12.6.5 Use of Fuel Weight Penalties to Compare Systems

When comparing systems, the overall system weight penalty should be used, $W_T$, where $W_T$ is the system weight plus the additional fuel carried due to system effects:

$$W_T=\Delta W_A+\Delta W_{FO}$$  \hfill (12.26)

Substituting Eq. (12.16) into Eq. (12.26) yields:

$$W_T=\Delta W_A+\left(\Delta W_A+r\Delta D+\frac{r\Delta f_p}{c}\right)\left(e^{ctg/r}-1\right)$$  \hfill (12.27)

It is often the case that a more massive system (greater $\Delta W_A$) will incur a smaller fuel weight penalty ($\Delta W_{FO}$) than a less massive system and thus potentially a lower overall system weight penalty ($W_T$), particularly for long-range missions. Therefore, the system choice will be dependent upon the range that the subject aircraft most commonly flies.
