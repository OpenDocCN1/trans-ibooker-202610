# Discussion

Composite colliders can be more efficient and also prevent problems caused by two colliders that are right next to each other. When two flat colliders are next to each other, an object sliding over them can “trip” over the point where they meet because of precision problems that affect most physics engines. This problem is solved by removing the corner completely.

Figure 5-7 shows an example of this. The top object does not use a composite collider, and contains three box colliders in a row, with the lines between them visible. The bottom object also has three box colliders in a row, but is configured to be part of a composite collider. The lines between them are gone, and the final collider is one large rectangular shape with no corners.
