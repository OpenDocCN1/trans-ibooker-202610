### An overview of Sustainable Development Goal 15

Melanie Sartore-Baldwin

While the majority of the United Nation’s 17 Sustainable Development Goals appear to focus on human life, SDG 15 is concerned with all life on land. Specifically, SDG 15 is to “protect, restore and promote sustainable use of terrestrial ecosystems, sustainably manage forests, combat desertification, and halt and reverse land degradation and halt biodiversity loss” (General Assembly, 2015, p. 14). Thus, the intent of SDG 15 and its associated targets (listed in Table 44.1) is to protect the relationships between all life forms and the habitats in which they live. While some may view this goal as separate from the goals focused on human life, a great deal of literature has identified the inextricable link between environmental health, nonhuman animal health, and human health (e.g., Wilcox et al., 2004). Further, some argue that the protection of biodiversity is inherent in all 17 goals, thus identifying the profound importance of SDG 15 (Opoku, 2019).

Earth exists as a harmonious biosystem in which organisms and the environment exist as a single, self-regulating system (Lovelock, 2003). As such, the biologically diverse organisms (i.e., biodiversity) within this system adapt for survival within changing environments. As Lovelock (2007) noted, humans’ actions have threatened the stability and inhabitability of the planet such that the livelihoods of humans, nonhuman animals, and the environment as a whole continue to be threatened. The manner in which all of these entities’ livelihoods are interrelated is referred to as a social-ecological system (SES). This complex and dynamic system comprises the interdependent relationships among humans, nonhuman animals, and the environment in which human behavior is integral in shaping ecosystems, and humans are reciprocally dependent upon ecosystems for their well-being and societal development (Fischer et al., 2015). While traditional approaches to studying social-ecological systems have focused on aiding humans in things like combatting diseases (e.g., Roger et al., 2016), there has been little-to-no work that has employed the same approaches from the vantage point of nonhuman animals and their habitats, particularly in the sport literature (cf. Sartore-Baldwin & McCullough, 2018). Thus, the predominant view is that human well-being is more important than the well-being of other living entities. Simply put, these approaches have been characterized as anthropocentric (Mi et al., 2016).

The anthropocentric view of ecosystems revolves around the use of ecosystem goods, services, and processes for human needs and for human gain (Binder et al., 2013). Ecosystem goods refer to the tangible items and products produced through and from ecosystem processes

### 44.1 理论基础和构建

#### 44.1.1 社会-生态系统

社会-生态系统视角以这样一个观念为指导：自然系统和社会系统并不作为独立实体存在（Berkes & Folke, 1992）。生物圈（即自然和社会系统的总和）存在为一个动态的全球生态系统，容纳所有生命的存在及其之间的关系。生物圈的社会维度指的是人类和人类在众多组成部分（即经济、文化和生态；Folke 等，2016）中的行动。生物圈的自然或生态组成部分由所有其他生命形式组成。因此，社会-生态方法来审视和研究生物圈强调了人类、社区、文化、经济等在地方和全球层面上相互依存的关系（Folke 等，2016）。

大量的社会-生态研究发现，过去两个世纪发生的深刻的全球变化可归因于人类和人类行为。事实上，虽然研究人员曾经关注随时间发生的自然环境和地质变化（即全新世），研究人员开始将这些变化与现在人类在过去两个世纪的深刻环境、地质和整体行星变化中所扮演的角色联系起来进行审视（即人类世；Crutzen & Stoermer, 2000；Steffen 等，2007）。虽然无法识别特定的个人、群体和社区对这些变化负有全部责任，但社会-生态研究表明，社会和生态领域之间的联系产生了对几乎所有相关者而言不可持续且不公平的未来（Leach 等，2018）。因此，所有各方的健康都岌岌可危。

#### 44.1.2 健康、生态与公平

健康定义因学科而异。一般而言，健康是指人类和非人类动物的个人、群体和人口的福祉和活力，强调前者而非后者（Rapport 等，1998）。尽管存在这种人类中心主义的观点，但没有健康的星球，任何生命体都无法繁荣和健康，从而证明了跨学科采用社会-生态方法审视健康的需求。在这些方法中，公平、正义、错误和正义的问题是固有的，表明需要批判性探究来审视、识别和挑战不公正的社会结构和制度以及其中不平等的权力关系（Buse 等，2018）。也许更重要的是，需要探究动物、非人类动物及其生活栖息地健康之间存在的相互依存关系（Buse 等，2018）。

已经采用了多种方法来研究人类、非人类动物和环境之间的相互联系及其与健康和疾病的关系，两者主要关注人类过程和结果（Buse 等，2018；Roger 等，2016）。一健康主要处理生物医学问题和与人畜共患病相关的问题。生态健康侧重于与生物多样性保护和人类福祉相关的社会经济和环境问题。虽然两种方法都主要关注人类福祉，其次是动物及其栖息地，但生态健康方法承认了生命体与其环境之间的重要联系。生态健康还基于这样一个前提：人类无法在不健康的环境中健康。

生态健康被定义为“理解和促进健康和福祉的系统性、参与性方法，其背景是社会和生态互动”（Waltner-Toews, 2009, 第 520 页）。这种生态公共卫生方法提出“一切皆重要”对于健康和福祉而言（Morris, 2010）。Kickbusch（1989）确定了该方法的四个原则：共生（即关爱生命共同体）、公平（即正义）、可持续性（即生态完整性）和全球/共同责任。这些原则提供了理论解释的开端，说明为何以及如何实现更生态的公共卫生方法，并强调作为集体运作以获得统一的健康观的重要性，该观包括文化、社会、物理和生物（Buse 等，2018）。也许最重要的是，它们明确解决了与非人类动物和自然栖息地相关的公平问题。在采用这种方法时特别有用的理论是生态社会理论（Krieger, 2001），一种整体方法，考虑“健康产生的每一个生态尺度和社会背景”（Buse 等，2018，第 422 页）

迄今为止，很少有作品从整体视角审视体育、体育组织和自然环境。虽然一些作品声称已做到这一点，但未能将非人类动物的健康和福祉及其栖息地纳入生态系统理论和实践讨论中仍然存在。因此，借鉴公共卫生文献并采取每个生命体都很重要的立场，以下各节提供了体育与自然环境中所有生命体之间关系的更深入背景理解。

#### 44.1.3 体育、社会公平与人类中心等级制

在上述所有公共卫生模型中，都隐含地关注公平。事实上，在人类中心文化中审视社会-生态系统时，可持续性和公平性问题不能单独解决（Petrosillo 等，2015）。相反，为了确定确保所有生命体安全、公平和可持续未来的最佳路径，必须探索两者之间的相互联系。认识到这一点，需要确定体育背景下与 SDG 15 相关的当前公平问题并提供背景。例如，人类中心等级制已被确定为体育和体育组织结构中固有的（Sartore-Baldwin & McCullough, 2018, 2021）。Cudworth（2005）将这些关系存在的制度、过程和实践称为人类中心等级制，并认为物种支配与性别、种族、能力、社会阶层和其他社会建构的支配体系相交。Cudworth（2005）对人类中心等级制的概念化与物种主义不同，因为它描述了建立人类对自然世界支配的复杂关系体系，而不仅仅是非人类动物压迫的意识形态体系（即物种主义）。

支配是“系统性权力关系的一般描述，这些关系阻碍了个体生物、群体、微观或宏观景观‘繁荣’的潜力”（Cudworth, 2014，第 28 页）。采用这一定义可以广泛审视人类对环境及其所容纳的非人类生命的支配在世界各地如何以不同形式和实践变化。因此，在主导体系中，可以识别出塑造人类中心等级制的特定类型和权力实践，也可以识别出社会

domination present. Calvo (2008) focuses on three primary types and practices of power that represent the different degrees of human domination: oppression, exploitation, and marginalization. Oppression refers to the extreme degrees to which humans apply dominatory power over other species. Exploitation refers to the use of animal materials and behaviors as a resource from which humans benefit. Marginalization is akin to anthropocentrism, as it refers to the rendering of species to the extreme periphery, thus making them nearly insignificant.

There are five primary areas of anthroparchic cultures: production, domestication, polity, violence, and anthropocentrism (Cudworth, 2011, 2014). Production refers to the relationships formed with nature as humans produce needed items such as food and fuel. Within the sport context, humans use natural resources, such as wood and animal hide, for the production of sport equipment, facilities, and venues. Likewise, nonhuman animals are used to create animal sport as a whole. Domestication and reproduction of plants and animals involves the breeding of plants and animals for specific purposes and can refer to the actual and symbolic beings that are safely domesticated or dangerously not. Plants and animals are domesticated and reproduced by humans for the specific purposes of sport participation and consumption. Racehorses are often selectively bred from championship bloodlines, for example, and specific types of grass are bred for the racetracks on which these horses will compete, as well as for golf courses and other sporting venues.

The third area, the political, involves the institutional entities that can create, perpetuate, and change systemic domination either directly or indirectly (Cudworth, 2014). While the safety and well-being of human athletes are of primary concern and regulated by governmental laws and organizational policies, the same is not ensured for animal athletes. Animal athletes have no voice and cannot report abuses and mistreatment. As a result, there is no assurance that the rules and regulations established to protect animal athletes, the likes of which are quite lacking, are being followed. In addition, the rules and regulations that do exist reinforce the use of animals as goods in the sport context. For example, the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA) has a list of 60 rules that are presumed to ensure the proper treatment of livestock in their competitions. While it is important that these rules focus on the welfare of the livestock, they fail to recognize animal athletes as possessing rights, cognitions, emotions, and so on. Thus, humans are dominant, as they determine what they believe to be what is best for these animal athletes and use animals to satisfy the interests of humans. The fourth area is systemic violence. Various forms and degrees of violence are used by humans to “control” animal athletes, as things like electric cattle prods, spurs, whips, and crops are all used to elicit desired behaviors. Animals are also killed and dismembered to make the aforementioned sport equipment (e.g., baseball gloves and saddles).

Within the sport context, the type of domination varies, as does the outcome (Cudworth, 2014). These types of dominance represent not only anthroparchy but also the importance of justice and equity within cultures that are human-centric, systematic, and emphasize the social dominance humans possess over all other living entities (Calvo, 2008). Namely, that justice and equity are rarely recognized for nonhuman living entities despite their required existence for sustaining human life (Leach et al., 2018). For example, the thoroughbred racehorse who has won the Triple Crown will experience domination differently than the Sumatran tiger, whose likeness is used as a school mascot. Similarly, the cow who was slaughtered to make burgers for sport spectators at the stadium will experience human domination differently than the Malamutes and Siberian huskies used for sled-dog racing. These distinctions are important to consider and can perhaps be better explained by further differentiating between animals used as sport participants and animals used as sport-related materials.

### 44.1.4 Nonhuman animal as participants

Morgan and Meier (1995) distinguish between three types of animal sport. The first emphasizes human athletic excellence and includes horse racing, polo, dressage, jumping, eventing, and certain rodeo events. The second type involves human athletic skill by pitting human against animal in events such as hunting, fishing, and bullfighting. The third type does not involve human athleticism but rather animal athletic prowess, and involves pitting animals against each other in deadly combat. Events within this third category include cockfighting, dog fighting, dog racing, and horse fighting. While the events and participants distinguish each type of animal sport, one commonality exists across all three—the animal athlete is not a voluntary participant. Indeed, animal sport is human-centric and produced through the use of coercion and violence between human and animal. The animal athletes have been domesticated and are often bred for the sole purpose of killing, fighting, racing, and so on. These activities are well established and often sanctioned through laws and other regulatory bodies.

While a complete historical review of the uses and abuses of animals within sport is beyond the scope of this chapter, the manner in which animals have come to serve humans within this context can be attributed to anthroparchal norms. The anthroparchal relations that constitute and reinforce these norms include marginalization, oppression, and exploitation. They coalesce with the intersection of capitalism, patriarchy, and social class to create and reinforce human dominance (Cudworth, 2011, 2014). The impact of these other structural inequalities is evident within the different types of sport that involve the use of animals. For example, while dogfighting was common and accepted among royals and aristocrats during the 19th century, its modern existence is predominantly affiliated with the lower and middle classes (Evans et al., 1998). Thoroughbred horseracing, on the other hand, is predominately associated with the upper class and elite, as some racehorses are purchased for millions of dollars and, likewise, millions of dollars are placed on the outcome of the races in which horses compete. Conversely, dogs used for dogfighting may be acquired from local shelters or obtained online for little or no cost. While horseracing is legal, and to a large extent celebrated, dogfights are illegal and linked to nefarious activities like drug trafficking, homicide, rape, and illegal gambling (Forsyth & Evans, 1998).

Events like dog fighting and cockfighting also serve as an extension of masculinity, as men are the primary partakers of these events. The behaviors within the ring are traditionally masculine (Kalof & Taylor, 2007; Martin, 1984). The performance of these animals, good and bad, is viewed as a reflection of the owner, thus suggesting that, for example, the toughest, most aggressive dog will win and therefore be owned by the toughest, most aggressive man with high social standing (Evans et al., 1998; Kalof & Taylor, 2007). Hunting and hunting weaponry also possess gendered and sexualized associations, as they glorify masculine dominance through killing, violence, and the use of weapons (Kalof et al., 2004). Bowhunting in particular has been described as “manly, exciting, intimate, and—above all—sexual” (Kalof et al., 2004, p. 240). Further, the arrow itself has been described as a phallic symbol (Langness, 1974).

The aforementioned examples support the contention that sport is anthroparchal. Indeed, within sport, humans have formed relations with animals such that humans possess power over all other species and exert it through oppression, exploitation, and marginalization (Cudworth, 2011). While the effects of these relations vary as a result of other structural inequalities (e.g., patriarchy), the fundamental point is that sport exists as a system of human domination over animals and nature. This domination is not only evident within animal sport where animals are forced to be participants, but also through the use of animal-based products, materials, and items within the sport context.




### 44.1.5 非人类动物作为材料和物品

动物身体部位被用作运动器材，它们的身体被用来在体育赛事中喂养观众，它们的形象和卡通形象被用来代表球队（即吉祥物）。因此，即使动物没有被强迫参与，动物在体育体验中也扮演着重要角色——即消费和利润产生。例如，据估计，3,000头牛的牛皮被用来为美国国家橄榄球联盟提供一个赛季所需的足够橄榄球（Yurcaba, 2015）。根据美国国家热狗与香肠委员会的估计，2014年期间在所有美国职业棒球大联盟场馆共消费了约2,140万根热狗。2015年，据估计在MLB场馆消费了约1,850万根热狗。根据美国国家鸡肉委员会的数据，在2016年卡罗莱纳黑豹队与丹佛野马队之间的超级碗比赛中，共消费了130万只鸡翅，重达1.625亿磅。虽然这些以及其他消费性统计数据中丧生的动物数量并不为人所知，但所消费的动物产品 sheer 数量表明，每年有数十亿动物死于为体育迷提供食物和物资。

迄今为止，有一些不涉及残忍的运动器材选择，但没有任何体育特许经营采用这些商品。然而，一些体育特许经营和场馆已采取措施应对美国目前的肉类消费水平，因为肉类和其他动物产品的生产是气候变化的主要贡献者（Hedenus et al., 2014）。一些例子包括马里兰大学特拉华大学水獭队的主场——马里兰体育场Capital One Field（前称Byrd Stadium）。它现在提供素食和纯素选择，如鹰嘴豆泥、素食热狗、新鲜水果杯和“byrd”沙拉。几座美国国家橄榄球联盟体育场也提供各种素食和纯素选择。费城老鹰队的主场林肯金融球场为不吃肉的顾客提供黑豆汉堡、素食塔可和面包蘑菇茄子三明治等选择。此外，一些知名运动员（例如乔·纳马斯、维纳斯·威廉姆斯和超马跑者Vlad Ix el）也推广了他们自己的素食和纯素饮食，意在倡导动物和地球。

肉类的生产和消费不仅对地球有害，而且与男子气概、力量、阳刚之气相关联，因此是父权制的象征（Adams, 1990; Hedenus et al., 2014）。在体育中，一个以男性为主导的领域（见Messner, 1992），父权制以三种主要方式影响人类对其他物种的支配。第一，猎杀动物取肉是人类，主要是男性，彰显其对自然和其他物种的权力的方式（Kalof et al., 2004）。第二，正如前文所述，体育观众的肉类消费不仅过量，而且在历史上与男性体育体验同义（Brady & Ventresca, 2014）。最后，认为从肉类中获取蛋白质是增肌增力的必要条件，使得肉类消费被正常化为运动员，特别是男性运动员的必需品（McGann, 2004）。体育中男子气概与肉类之间的这些联系难以挑战，因为它们深深植根于体育的男性文化中。然而，也有一些例子，传统上具有男子气概的运动员，如NFL线卫Arian Foster，采用了纯素饮食并保持了其运动能力。有趣的是，Foster“决定”成为纯素者的决定在流行媒体中受到了性别化和种族化的评论（Brady & Ventresca, 2014），这表明媒体可能是许多人类中心主义规范的主要 perpetuator。

动物在体育语境中服务人类的另一种方式是通过作为吉祥物代表体育队伍。选择动物作为吉祥物的依据是动物的特征以及动物代表会带来好运的信念（Slowikowski, 1993）。被选来代表体育队伍的动物是基于其被认为具有的攻击性和凶猛倾向而被选中，或因为它们在更大层面上代表荣誉（Sloveno, 1994）。虽然有一些例外，但温顺的动物通常不会被选为吉祥物。球迷与其吉祥物之间建立的联系可以非常强大，因为它可以影响情绪、态度和行为（Callais, 2010）。吉祥物被用来产生收入，但很少，如果有的话，被使用的动物形象会得到任何金钱援助。事实上，在大多数情况下，很少或根本没有努力去保护和改善吉祥物所代表的动物的生计、条件和体验，也没有球迷意识到濒危动物吉祥物所遭受的困境（cf. Baltz & Ratnaswamy, 2000）。

### 44.1.6 三个D：降级、荒漠化和森林砍伐

人类中心主义一词指的是人类对所有非人类自然（即环境；Cudworth, 2005）的支配。因此，非人类动物受到支配，植物物种也是如此。这种支配的影响通常通过生态足迹或碳足迹等简化隐喻来反映，传达个人行为对自然环境的影响程度。然而，这些指标所缺乏的是承认自然也具有内在价值。自然可以被定义为“非人类制造的物理和生物世界”（Sandifer et al., 2015, p. 2），主要以两种常常冲突的方式被看重——内在地和工具地。自然的工具价值与人类赋予生态系统或社区的价值以及从中获得的利益相关，生态系统或社区是指所有生物和非生物实体相互联系的整体（Pelenic et al., 2013）。内在价值是指为其固有价值而珍视某物（Pelenic et al., 2013）。人类是生态系统不可或缺的组成部分，几乎完全依赖自然生存，却常常未能认识到自然世界的内在价值。因此，我们继续将足迹指标作为环境可持续性使用。

体育的生态足迹代表其运营、功能和结果对自然环境的影响（Casper & Pfahl, 2015）。例如，举办一场数百万球迷参加的体育盛会。虽然一些观众可能依赖公共交通，这取决于地点和可用性，但许多观众会长途飞行或驾车往返于场馆。由此产生的交通碳排放可以与一个小国家的排放相媲美（Goldblatt, 2020）。赛事本身也需要大量能源的使用并产生大量废物。食物和非食物物品以大量生产并运输，其中许多最终成为垃圾。在某些地区，这种废物的 mass 生产增加了本已难以管理的垃圾和污染数量。例如，在2016年里约奥运会和残奥会期间，本已污染的瓜纳巴拉湾水域成为多个体育项目的举办地。据估计，1万升受污染的废水和90吨漂浮垃圾污染了该湾，导致有毒的水可能使参赛者生病（Mavropoulos, 2016）。这些令人震惊的污染水平因奥运会的举办而增加。由于巴西基础设施的破碎，这一长期存在的问题对该湾周围的自然栖息地以及栖息地内的生物造成的冲击是灾难性的（Fischer et al., 2015）。

应该指出的是，在里约奥运会期间，组委会确实实施了许多建议的做法以减少废物和碳排放，但恢复该湾完整性的努力仍在进行中（Alves Martins et al., 2020; Fischer et al., 2015）。可以认为，如果2016年奥运会没有在里约举行，恢复工作会比目前更进一步。因为在组委会和国际奥委会的决策中考虑的是环境的工具价值而非内在价值，



the ecosystem continues to struggle. While not typical to consider intrinsic value when making global decisions, organizations can opt to do so and have profound impacts as a result.

Taken together, the examples used in explaining animal sport, the use of animals and animal products within sport, and the degradation of natural habitats exemplify anthroparchy. Further, they suggest a profound need to better understand sport’s anthroparchal culture. This is particularly true with regard to the issues of sport-related sustainability and sustainable development, which, to date, have lacked any focus on interspecies equity. The next chapter offers suggestions for researchers and practitioners to integrate the interests of all species and natural habitats into discussions of sustainability by merging the models that focus on the health, well-being, rights, wants, and needs of the natural environment within the sport context.

### 44.2 结论

The purpose of this chapter was to introduce and define SDG 15 within the sport context. More specifically, the purpose was to highlight the importance of sustaining all lifeforms on Earth to sport researchers, practitioners, and even spectators so that sport and sport organizations can help "protect, restore and promote sustainable use of terrestrial ecosystems, sustainably manage forests, combat desertification, and halt and reverse land degradation and halt biodiversity loss" (General Assembly, 2015, p. 14). Borrowing models from the public health, justice, and sport management disciplines, sport and sport organizations were identified as part of an anthroparchic social-ecological system that has failed to address the needs of nonhuman animals and the habitats in which they live when implementing sustainability efforts, interventions, and practices. By employing the systems thinking paradigm to this issue, the root of this failure was identified and suggestions for changed offered.

### 衡量可持续发展目标15

Melanie Sartore-Baldwin

联合国可持续发展目标15的目的是“保护、恢复和促进陆地生态系统的可持续利用，可持续管理森林，抗击荒漠化，制止和扭转土地退化，制止生物多样性丧失”（General Assembly, 2015, p. 14）。因此，该目标本身就是为了改善自然环境及其所有居民——人类和非人类的健康。事实上，环境健康与其居民健康之间的相互关系正变得日益受到认可（Coutts et al., 2014）。因此，个人、社区、组织和机构的做法已受到环境需求的指导（例如，Hoffman & Jennings, 2015）。可以说，自然环境是体育组织的首要利益相关者，因为作为居民，人类有切身利益去维持一个稳定的生活环境，以便享受体育运动（Mallen & Chard, 2011）。体育组织已采取了一些行动来应对地球日益加剧的压力，但这些行动本质上主要是被动的，旨在建立合法性和竞争优势（Babiak & Trendafilova, 2011; Trendafilova et al., 2014）。因此，它们并未解决促进过度消费和掠夺地球自然资源的深层次系统结构和心智模式。Sartore-Baldwin和McCullough（2018）通过其生态中心管理实践的概念模型解决了这一不足，其组成部分与EcoHealth和生态公共卫生研究方法一致。因此，下面呈现了这些组成部分的整合模型。

#### 45.1 公共卫生、体育与SDG 15

如前一章所述，EcoHealth被定义为“在社会和生态互动的背景下理解和促进健康与福祉的系统性、参与式方法”（Waltner-Toews, 2009, p. 520）。在此定义中可以识别出五个重要要素（Dakubo, 2010）。首先，强调人类健康和生态系统健康以及两者之间的联系。重要的是，尽管存在平等重要性的概念，这种方法以及其他整合方法仍然本质上是人类中心主义的（Assmuth et al., 2020）。第二个重要要素是强调各层面健康之间的联系，尤其是在宏观和微观层面之间。这一要素固有的认识是，每个组成部分或生态系统健康层面的总和构成了一个更大且层级复杂的系统。第三个要素强调社会权力差异可能对某些社会群体产生更不利的影响。以文化意义为例，种族和性别相关的文化意义可以塑造个人与环境的关系，进而影响其健康和福祉（Dakubo, 2010）。第四个要素确定了所有层面的利益相关者参与的重要性。同样，第五个要素强调了通过跨学科审视和理解生态系统健康而引发的共同干预的迫切需求。

Dakubo（2010）提出的关键要素确定了生态系统存在所固有的复杂性水平。为了更好地理解这些要素，Charron（2012）确定了EcoHealth研究的六项原则：系统思维、跨学科研究、多利益相关者参与、可持续性、性别与社会公平，以及知识到行动。正如Lisitza和Wolbring（2018）指出的那样，这些原则中的每一项都与社会正义的更大讨论相关联。虽然在此背景下社会正义通常与人类的权利相关联，但非人类动物及其栖息地的权利也应被考虑。

#### 45.1.1 系统思维

系统思维是生态系统公共研究方法和Sartore-Baldwin和McCullough（2018）提出的生态中心管理实践模型两者不可或缺的组成部分。系统思维（ST）是一种抽象但结构化的认知努力，其重点在于整体、其部分以及两者之间的空间之间的关系（Cabrera et al., 2008）。系统思维中采用的最常见工具之一是Maani和Cavana（2007）的四层思维模型。视觉化为冰山，该模型确定了人类与周围世界互动的不同方式。第一层被称为事件层，由冰山可见的尖端表示。在这一层，人们注意到并意识到在本地、国家或全球范围内发生了一些变化。在组织中，管理者倾向于以被动的方式应对这一层级，并未能认识到事件几乎肯定是更深层次问题的症状。因此，采取了快速修复措施，但由于未识别出根本原因，事件会再次发生并重新出现。

Maani和Cavana的比喻冰山的水下部分代表了模型的其余层级。模式是思维的下一个层级，位于水面之下，表明它们与事件相关联。模式是反复发生的事件集合（即数据点）的结果，可以相互关联，模式展示了随时间的变化。下一个层级，系统结构，表示一种深入的思维方式，其中模式和趋势之间的相互作用被检验以寻找因果关系。也是在这一层级，系统组成部分之间的关系被揭示为抑制性、限制性或支持性。最深层的思维层级是解释事物为何以其方式运作的原因的层级。具体而言，心智模式层级代表了我们做事的基础所形成的价值观、信念和假设。这一层级的思维影响系统结构的构建、行为规则以及个人日常生活的进行方式。

将系统思维和Maani与Cavana的四层思维模型应用于体育背景的最佳解释是一个例子。2014年3月，国家野生动物联合会（NWF）发布了一份题为《Mascot Madness: How Climate Change is Hurting School Spirit》的报告。该报告详细说明了气候变化的影响如何对代表学校吉祥物的命运产生不利影响，如佛罗里达大学（Gators）、堪萨斯州立大学（Wildcats）、孟菲斯大学（Tigers），甚至俄亥俄州立大学


University (Buckeyes)。诸如极端天气、气温升高、海平面上升和极端干旱等现象，使俄亥俄州立大学的公羊、贝勒大学的熊、密歇根大学的狼獾以及雪城大学的橙色等吉祥物的长期生存受到质疑。因此，表明地球气候变化的*事件*暗示了某些体育队伍吉祥物生存之间存在关联；然而，这些事件表明的是一个更大的问题。

吉祥物因行星变化而受到负面影响的长期趋势和*模式*，与地球自然资源消耗增加以及自然无法跟上人类需求有关。同样，过度消费也可以被确定为至少部分塑造了组织总体上，尤其是体育组织的、可持续性和环境意识实践的原因。具体而言，为应对自然资源日益减少，世界各地的体育组织都推出了环境可持续性举措，旨在减少其对地球的影响（Trendafilova et al., 2014）。保护地球环境的政府政策和社会压力指导了这些努力。这些政策和压力代表了促进环境正义原则的*系统性结构*。这些系统性结构、趋势和事件所源自的信念体系将自然资源的使用与人类需求、欲望和动机联系起来。因此，可持续性举措背后的指导信念和*心智模型*是人类中心主义的（即以人为中心），很少甚至从不涉及生态正义问题。

使用系统以认识和接受个人共同责任。跨学科研究，在本研究中，指研究人员、从业者和利益相关者参与知识生产过程的过程（Polk, 2015）。跨学科研究本质上是参与性的，并且当聚焦于现实生活问题时，可以产生旨在从多个角度解决这些问题的共同知识。因此，与其将任何一方确定为顾问或顾问，研究活动是在协作环境中进行的，该环境“既嵌入在研究和实践中，又与之隔绝”（Polk, 2015, p. 111），这是因为所有各方都有集体责任来解决感兴趣的问题。结果是知识被协作生产。

Polk（2015）确定了采用这种跨学科知识共同生产方法时的五个重点领域、三个研究阶段和三种研究策略。重点领域包括让多个利益相关者参与整个知识生产过程（即包容性）、在研究和实践中从利益相关者那里获得深入贡献（即协作）、通过各种利益相关者的见解充分准确地定义所检查的问题（即整合）、开发评估和反思过程以检查结果（可用性），以及允许在整个过程中对所做选择进行持续探究（即反思性）。研究过程包括制定、生成和评估问题、项目设计、数据、研究结果和影响。所有这些重点和研究过程共同作用，以为所有参与者生产最开放、最包容、最具协作性和跨学科性的有用知识（Polk, 2015）。理想情况下，这些信息将被转化为旨在从多个角度解决全球问题的政策、实践和法律。此外，这些信息可用于挑战存在于基础系统结构和心智模型中的结构性不公正（Marshall et al., 2018）。

### 45.1.3 基于公平的可持续性和地球法理学

虽然实施受地球需求强烈影响的国际法律在产生地方生态中心法律和组织实践方面将是理想的，但目前缺乏任何可识别的举措来实现这一目标。然而，这种缺乏举措为体育组织提供了引领全球变革前沿的机会。因此，与其采用专门关注人类需求的法律和原则，治理将专注于整个地球及其所有居民的需求，而不仅仅是人类（Koons, 2009）。在全球层面，这可以通过采用地球法理学的原则来实现。地球法理学是一种基于系统、基于正义的法律理论，将行星需求置于其他需求之上。因此，由于其内在价值以及在生态系统和行星功能中的必要性，自然及其所包含的一切都应获得法律考虑。简单地说，法律和政策应基于人类与地球的关系，而非人类能从中获取什么。

在组织内部，可持续发展通常被解释为解决当前和未来人类需求的一种方式。然而，生物多样性的重要性已成为一些人的关注焦点。Haughton（1999）将可持续发展作为社会和环境正义问题来处理，确定了指导可持续发展过程的五个相互关联的基于公平的原则。前两个原则，代际公平或未来性和代内公平是指在世代之间和世代内部建立更好的公平，



respectively. Specifically, intergenerational equity is concerned with the distribution of resources from one generation to another, and intragenerational equity is concerned with the processes by which resources are distributed within the current generation. The third principle, geographical equity, involves considering the impact of local decisions and actions at the global level. Haughton also refers to this as transfrontier responsibility. The fourth principle, procedural equity, also discussed as the participation principle, focuses on the right of access to information for all parties impacted by negative environmental impacts. The fifth and final principle, inter-species equity, “places the survival of other species on an equal basis to the survival of humans” (p. 236) and emphasizes the importance of preserving ecosystems and retaining biodiversity.

While Haughton’s (1999) equity principles are not mutually exclusive from one another, sustainability’s human-centered history has led some principles to receive a great deal more of attention and action than others. Adopting an equity-based perspective, however, does require that the interests of all are incorporated, not just those of humans. As Earnshaw (1999) notes, the anthropocentric approach to the traditional concepts of sustainability and sustainable development promotes the exploitation of resources for the sole purpose of maintaining human quality of life. Earnshaw refers to conventional sustainability as exploitation-based sustainability because it “regards all living and non-living systems other than the human species as means by which to maximize wealth” (p. 116). Exploitation-based sustainability thus views the Earth, its ecosystems, and the nonhuman animals within them as natural, renewable resources for humans to use as they see fit while seeking a way to ensure that future generations can use them to suit their own needs.

In contrast to anthropocentric sustainability and sustainable development, an ecocentric approach recognizes the importance of protecting the health of Earth’s ecosystems and all living entities housed within them (Imran et al., 2014). From this perspective, nonhuman beings have no less right to life than humans, and as such, sustainability involves the equitable protection of all life. Thus, rather than viewing ecosystems, nonhuman animals, and the environment as resources and objects needed to fulfill the needs of the current and future generations, these entities are considered subjects whose needs must also be met to ensure survival (Imran et al., 2014). Adopting an ecocentric perspective, Earnshaw (1999) identifies the necessity to transition from the traditional exploitation-based sustainability approach to an equity-based sustainability approach. Focusing specifically on interspecies equity, Earnshaw points out that nonhuman animals value many of the same fundamental rights that humans possess. For example, just as humans value life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness, so too do animals value their lives, their freedom, and their friendships and families. Further, animals also wish to live free from harm, fear, and exploitation (Broom, 2016; Veenhoven, 2000). Thus, while not identical to humans, nonhuman animals experience and enjoy their own quality of life and wish not to suffer physically, emotionally, and psychologically (Earnshaw, 1999).

While a great deal of research affirms that nonhuman animals are sentient creatures (see Duncan, 2006), there exists reluctance by some to believe or accept that animals possess awareness, emotions, cognition, and senses. This latter perspective is the result of ideological belief systems that have hierarchically placed humans above all other living beings. For some, this belief system is rooted in cultural and religious beliefs, while for others, the use and abuse of nonhuman animals is financially driven and legally maintained (Francione, 1996). Regardless of its manifestation, however, this ideology or “set of socially shared beliefs that legitimates an existing or desired social order” (Nibert, 2003, p. 8) represents speciesism, a system akin to sexism, racism, ethnocentrism, and so on (Gruen, 2009).

### 45.1.4 Knowledge to action and leverage points

A shift in perspective and to ever-changing conditions and not rely on maintaining the status quo (Holling, 2001). Implicit in this response is the necessity to consider social well-being (Armitage et al., 2012).

Whereas a great deal of research has adopted the systems and resilience thinking approach to examining the issue of global sustainability, little achievement has been made. Casarejos (2020) suggests that perhaps this is the result of failing to challenge the “ingrained social values, perspectives, and habits that deter regenerative changes at both the individual and collective levels” (p. 3). Indeed, for long-term change to occur, events must be understood as aspects of larger trends, and trends must be understood as indicative of underlying systemic structures and mental models (Maani & Cavana, 2007). Specifically, Casarejos asserts the necessity of acknowledging and celebrating nature and all of its complexity and all levels. One way of doing this is by seeking out leverage points. Through extensive scrutinization, leverage points can be identified and used as places to create small changes that may subsequently result in larger, systemic changes (Casarejos, 2020; Meadows, 2008).

Abson et al. (2017) suggest three realms of leverage points that can be used to create interventions that will transform sustainability such that system change has been made. The first realm, reconnecting people with nature, involves informing people of the value of the natural world through their perceptions and interactions—specifically, identifying human dependency on the natural environment to live a good life. The second realm, restructuring institutions, involves challenging institutions as well as institutionalization. Institutionalization refers to the ways in which organizations unquestionably accept “the way things are done” (Scott, 1987 p. 505). The third realm, rethinking knowledge production and use, involves examining and rerouting knowledge content and flow within systems in relation to sustainable transformation goals. These three realms are not mutually exclusive from one another, despite being presented individually. The interactions among them are also important for creating transformational change.

The realms identified by Abson et al. (2017) are perhaps best understood in the sport context through the use of an example. In 2019, Marco Lambertini, the Director General of the World Wildlife Foundation (WWF) and International Union for Conservation of Nature member (IUCN), wrote an open letter to members of the IUCN detailing the interdependent relationship between sport and the natural environment. In this letter, Lambertini (2019) points out that nature is the foundation for sport, and in many instances, it even represents the stadiums in which sport takes place. While fairly intuitive, this connection with nature is not often articulated among sport fans and athletes or within sport organizations. As Abson et al. (2017)

suggest, however, making this relationship more explicit (i.e., [re]connecting patrons with nature) has the potential to create change. For example, sport teams with team mascots that are endangered species animals could help these species by connecting sport fans with information about both their plight and ways to subsequently help. Research has demonstrated that once sport fans are aware of these issues, they are motivated to both learn more and take action to help (Baltz & Ratnaswamy, 2000; Sartore-Baldwin & McCullough, 2019).

The second realm, challenging institutions, and institutionalization, requires structural change within sport organizations (Abson et al., 2017). One way that this can be accomplished is by incorporating the mission of other organizations into the mission of sport organizations. For example, the International Olympic Committee recently released guidelines called “Sustainability Management in Sports” as part of their Sustainability Essentials series. The intent of this document was to provide guidance to sport organizations such that a focus on environmental sustainability is incorporated into the purpose, mission, and actions of these organizations and become established as “business as usual” practice (International Olympic Committee, 2020). Inherent in this guide is also the intent of challenging how things have traditionally been done (i.e., institutional norms). The third realm, questioning existing knowledge within sport organizations, can be accomplished by acknowledging the importance of varied sources of information and subsequently seeking out these sources for the purposes of collaboration. For example, Lambertini (2019) credits the two-decade-long relationship between the WWF and the IOC for enhanced sustainability at the London 2012 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Collaborations occur at the community and local levels as well, as evidenced by NASCAR’s partnering efforts with venue-specific organizations to include renewable energies and efficient energy use into their organizational practices (NASCAR, n.d.).

The leverage points identified by Abson and colleagues (2017) represent identifiable and reasonable starting points for creating systemic change. As components of resilience thinking, leverage points are crucial for transformation and sustainability. However, just as social-ecological systems continuously adapt, so too do leverage points; thus, scrutiny is important (Casarejos, 2020). Leverage points themselves possess different characteristics, the likes of which characterize them as shallow and deep (Meadows, 2008). The realms identified by Abson et al. (2017) are particularly important because of their deep nature and the subsequent impact on organizations. Indeed, the integration of creating institutional change, (re)establishing connections to nature, and producing and using new knowledge forms presents exciting potential for change. Further, and most importantly for SDG 15, they have a profound potential to deconstruct the anthropocentric mental models that continue to halt change within sport organizations.

### 45.2 Measuring SDG 15 in sport

Indeed, the aforementioned suggested leverage points provide organizations with a blueprint that can be used to meet many of the SDGs, including SDG 15 and its targets. Across all of the 17 SDGs, there are 167 targets and over 300 indicators identified. For SDG 15 alone, there are 12 targets and 14 indicators for SDG 15, but this does not mean that outcomes related to SDG 15 are mutually exclusive from outcomes related to other SDGs. Further, and as noted by Costanza et al. (2016), measuring sustainability outcomes is done after the fact and is thus “a prediction of which characteristics of the system might ultimately be sustainable” (p. 350). Simply put, measurement mechanisms and indexes are quite complex and complicated and thus are typically examined aggregately. Historically, the most common aggregate indicator used is a country’s gross domestic product (GDP) because of the assumption that it is a representative of



社会福祉（Costanza et al., 2014）。随着时间的推移，人们开发出了替代性衡量指标，以更好地反映地球及其居民的需求和福祉。

Costanza 等人（Costanza et al., 2016, 2018）已确定了当代综合衡量指标的三大类。第一类是将社会和环境因素纳入经济衡量指标。第二类在性质上是主观的，依赖于通过调查收集的福祉数据。第三类使用加权福祉指标，如预期寿命和住房。在每一类以及跨类别中都开发了众多模型。这些后者模型是混合型的，因为它们从三个角度评估社会福祉——经济、社会和环境（Costanza et al., 2016）。此外，它们不是线性模型，而是系统性模型，优先考虑“在健康的星球上实现共享福祉”（Costanza et al., 2018, para. 8）。为了实现这一目标，主张这些模型的国家、机构和组织坚持尊重行星边界、满足人类和非人类的需求、强调资源公平分配，并在更大、公正且有回应的治理体系内运作。此外，它们还认识到体育和体育组织在保持地球宜居性以及为所有生命形式找到免受伤害而生活的方式方面所承担的共同责任（Sartore-Baldwin & McCullough, 2018）。

像 Adidas 这样的体育组织认识到自身的责任，因此塑造了其可持续发展工作，以反映经济利益、地球利益以及地球上所有生命利益的可持续未来。这种整体方法包括创新、可衡量的目标以及后续里程碑，用以记录其努力的有效性。在单一体育组织可以作为典范的程度上，其他体育组织可能会效仿（Orr et al., 2020; Sartore-Baldwin & McCullough, 2018）。此外，体育组织效仿得越多，最终可能导致形成一种更具社会和环境意识的消费者文化，从而形成一种循环关系，即消费者期望组织以生态公正的方式运作。

### 45.3 结论

本章的目的是提供一个将体育和体育组织整合到行星健康和福祉更大讨论中的模型。借鉴公共卫生、正义和体育管理学科的模型，体育和体育组织被确定为人类中心社会生态系统的一部分，该系统在实施可持续发展努力、干预措施和实践时未能解决非人类动物及其栖息地需求。通过将系统思维范式应用于这一问题，确定了失败的根源，并提出了改变建议。此外，还提供了实施和衡量可持续发展目标 15 的蓝图。

在最后的“帮助动物”阶段，CC 参与者参与的活动说明了他们最近建立了情感联系的同一动物教练所面临的一些保护挑战。例如，与犀牛相关的游戏突出了偷猎问题，工作人员利用这段时间指导参与者如何采取具体行动来帮助缓解他们动物导师所面临的保护挑战。

知识传递与期望的行为改变之间有时会出现有据可查的差距，言语上的行动承诺与实际的亲环境行为之间存在不一致。CC 通过引导将新获得的环境知识应用于具体、可实现的行为来弥合这一差距。这反映了 CC 的经验，即最有效的环境知识传递既能激发情感参与，也能指导受众在自己的


lives. 相反，关于将知识转化为有意义行动的不确定性可能导致参与者兴趣下降，动力会迅速消散。

CC 的“从野生动物学习”模式旨在强调我们与其他动物之间的共同点以及相互依存关系。CC 对共享环境的概念给予了特别关注，其活动旨在通过探索人类和野生动物共同拥有的生存需求（例如食物、水、住所和空间）来促进共存文化，使用现实世界的例子揭示我们如何相互联系。

完成 CC 项目的参与者中，有 85% 的人同意或强烈同意以下陈述：“我经常感到与植物和动物相连。”此外，77% 的受访参与者随后同意或强烈同意“如果没有动物，他们现在不会像现在这样幸福。”

参加过 CC 项目的儿童还被进一步鼓励将他们新获得的知识与父母、亲戚和朋友分享，成为变革的推动者和同龄教育者，成为他们自身的一部分，并为社区提供进入“基层”的途径，从而提高人们对共享环境遗产价值的更广泛认识，并促进对老一代的态度转变。

因此，CC 的方法试图克服一些有利于环境行为的障碍，并培养对野生动物和自然环境的更大热情、欣赏和同情，激励年轻人并帮助他们成为社区内的变革大使。

CC 解决了儿童需要与自然世界建立更直观的关系，以便他们成为负责任的国家和全球公民的需求，巧妙地将知识获取融入游戏、技能、练习、歌曲和活动中，以一种赋予儿童共同工作并采用健康核心价值观的方式，同时激励他们寻求实施有利于环境的行为。该项目的核心尊重信息也很容易在孩子们喜欢一起喊的团体口号中记住：“尊重自己（拍手，拍手，拍手）；尊重彼此（拍手，拍手，拍手）；尊重你的环境，耶！”

此外，CC 的项目并非完全基于足球。响应需求，CC 已成功将其活动修改为基于橄榄球的项目。相比之下，我们的“探险”项目（面向探险度假村游客的儿童）利用飞盘和哈基萨克等活动进行。CC 方法中的不变之处在于其基于体验式学习理论的基础。体育活动（足球或其他替代项目）构成了每个 CC 项目的大部分内容，提供了一条通往学习的体验式途径，随后由我们的合格教练带领反思和分析每个活动，将体育活动与每个课程的关键保护信息联系起来。

现有关于体育社会发展的文献中，CC 等项目在多大程度上能够实现可持续发展目标成果仍未得到充分探索。尽管如此，CC 项目参与者的反馈表明，即使是短暂的干预也可能产生持久而深远的影响。因此，我们相信 CC 在鼓励环境意识的发展和对自我、他人和环境的尊重文化方面取得了成功。
