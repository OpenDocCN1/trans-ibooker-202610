# ON THE THEORY OF CATALOGUING / CLASSIFICATION

A catalogue is a systematic arrangement of materials in a library or information centre, designed to enable users to identify, locate and access resources efficiently. The modern catalogue has evolved from simple manuscript lists to complex digital databases supporting remote access and interoperability.

# 1. PRINCIPLES OF CATALOGUING

+   - The function of the catalogue is to state which works a library possesses and where they may be found.
+   - Cataloguing rules must be applied consistently to ensure uniformity and predictability.
+   - Bibliographic records should describe both the intellectual content and the physical carrier of the item.
+   - Authority control ensures that names, titles and subjects are standardised.

# 2. CLASSIFICATION SCHEMES

| Scheme | Base | Focus | Use |
|--------|------|-------|-----|
| Dewey Decimal | Numeric | Knowledge by discipline | Public libraries |
| Universal Decimal | Numeric + symbols | Faceted knowledge | Specialised libraries |
| Library of Congress | Alpha-numeric | Broad knowledge classes | Academic libraries |

# 3. STRUCTURAL FRAMEWORK OF CATALOGUE ENTRIES

![](bbox=[0.15, 0.51, 0.85, 0.62])

Each catalogue entry typically comprises a heading (main entry), a description of the item, and tracings. The heading may be a personal author, a corporate body, or a title. The description follows a standard order of areas: title and statement of responsibility, edition, publication, physical description, series and notes.

# 4. MAINTENANCE AND PRESERVATION

Catalogues require continuous maintenance to reflect acquisitions, withdrawals and corrections. Preservation of the catalogue itself is fundamental to sustained access. In digital environments this includes backups, data migration and format migration to guard against technological obsolescence.

# 5. CONCLUSION

The theoretical foundations of cataloguing and classification remain central to the organisation of knowledge. A robust catalogue not only provides physical access but also intellectual access to the world's recorded knowledge.
