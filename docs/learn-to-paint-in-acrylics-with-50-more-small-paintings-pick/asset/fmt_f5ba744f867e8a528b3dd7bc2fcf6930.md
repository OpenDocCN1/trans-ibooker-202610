
**2** Mix a pale blue from Titanium White, Unbleached Titanium, Ultramarine Blue and a tiny hint of Cadmium Yellow Medium, and paint the ice cream, using a #6 flat brush. Then paint the cone in Yellow Ochre, using flat, slab-like strokes to create the texture of the wafer.

![Images](images/f0097-01.jpg)

**3** For the chocolate topping, mix a rich brown from Burnt Sienna and Ultramarine Blue and apply with a #4 filbert brush. Paint the cherry in a warm red mixed from Napthol Red and a little Cadmium Yellow Medium, using the #1 round brush.

![Images](images/f0097-02.jpg)

**4** Now develop the form using tints and shades, keeping the transitions in value gradual on the spherical shapes; the shadow on the cone runs parallel to the edge. For the shades, add a little Mars Black to the blue mix for the ice cream; mix Napthol Red and Mars Black for the cherry; mix Burnt Sienna and Yellow Ochre for the cone. Add a little Unbleached Titanium to the brown mix to lighten the right side of the chocolate. Highlight the cherry with Titanium White.

![Images](images/f0097-03.jpg)

**5** For the background, mix Unbleached Titanium, a little Ultramarine Blue and a little Napthol Red. Using the #6 flat brush, paint the background, cutting out the shape of the cone as you do so. Add a highlight to the cone, using a mix of Unbleached Titanium, Cadmium Yellow Medium and Yellow Ochre, again making it run parallel to the cone’s edge. Paint the sprinkles any colour you like, cleaning the brush between colours.

![Images](images/f0097-04.jpg)

Although this is a very simple subject, the lighter and darker values make it look convincingly three-dimensional. If you find it difficult to assess the values, try half closing your eyes as you look at it: this subdues the colours and makes it easier to judge how light or dark things need to be.

### 31 Complex ellipses: Button flower

We’ve already looked at painting a single ellipse (see page 74). Your challenge here, with multiple ellipses in the same painting, is to make each one as symmetrical as possible. A really useful tip is to try to draw ellipses in one fluid movement, rather than in two halves – otherwise there’s a risk that you’ll make the ends of the ellipse pointed or blunt. This really is a case of practice makes perfect. The exact shape of an ellipse will depend on your viewpoint, so just use your eyes and draw what you see. You will notice that as your viewpoint gets lower, the ellipse will appear narrower until it disappears altogether and all you can see is a curve. Conversely, as your viewpoint gets higher, the ellipse will appear broader until, when you view your subject from directly overhead, it looks like a complete circle.

#### Materials

![Images](images/f0098-02.jpg)

**1** Using a #1 round brush and Yellow Ochre thinned with glazing medium, draw two ellipses, one inside the other, to represent the raised area of the centre button.

![Images](images/f0098-03.jpg)

**2** Now draw the ellipses of the button petals, making sure they’re evenly spaced and consistent in size. Although you won’t see the full ellipses in the finished painting, it really helps to put them in here so you get a feel for the overall shape. Allow to dry.

![Images](images/f0099-01.jpg)

**3** Using a #4 filbert, paint the flower centre in Cadmium Yellow Medium. While that’s drying, mix a pale grey from Unbleached Titanium and a small amount of Mars Black. Paint the petals, leaving a little of the white of the canvas showing through to represent highlights. Apply a second layer of Cadmium Yellow Medium to the centre to get a good, opaque coverage.

![Images](images/f0099-02.jpg)

**4** Add a little more Mars Black to the grey from step 3 and paint the shadows on the petals, using the #4 filbert brush turned on its side. (Note that the interiors of those buttons are also a series of ellipses.) For the stem, mix a mid-tone green from Cadmium Yellow Medium and a tiny bit of Mars Black. The shadows on the centre petal are Yellow Ochre, while the highlight is Titanium White plus a very tiny amount of Cadmium Yellow Medium.

![Images](images/f0099-03.jpg)

**5** Mix a bright blue from Ultramarine Blue, Titanium White and a tiny amount of Unbleached Titanium. Still using the #4 filbert, paint the background, cutting out the shape of the flower as you do so. Switch to the #1 round brush and mix some Ultramarine Blue, Unbleached Titanium and a little Mars Black for the cast shadows under the flower and stem. Also use this colour for the buttonholes on the centre flower. Paint the buttonholes on the petals in Mars Black.

![Images](images/f0099-04.jpg)

Getting the shape of the ellipses right is crucial to make this image look convincingly three-dimensional. Look around your home for another circular subject, such as a shallow round bowl, and try drawing it from different viewpoints to see how the shape changes.

### 32 Multiple geometric shapes: Toy rocket

This project combines most of the geometric shapes we looked at earlier in this chapter into a single image. They’re still instantly recognizable, but the important thing to remember is that light and shade affect each element slightly differently. Look at the highlights, for example: on the cylinder (the main body of the rocket), the highlight is a straight vertical line, while on the nose of the rocket it’s angled and follows the shape of the cone. Remember, too, to keep the ellipses consistent: the bottom of the cone, the two stripes and the bottom of the rocket itself all need to be parallel and to follow the same arc.

#### Materials

![Images](images/f0100-02.jpg)

**1** Using Yellow Ochre thinned with glazing medium and a #1 round brush, draw the rocket as a series of geometric shapes on top of one another – a sphere, cone and cylinder. The two ellipses of the cylinder should be symmetrical and correspond in size and pitch. Add little crescent shapes at the base for the landing gear.

![Images](images/f0100-03.jpg)

**2** Block in the colours with the #4 bright brush. The red sphere and cylinder are Napthol Red mixed with a little Cadmium Yellow Medium. The cone is Cadmium Yellow Medium; you may need two layers. For the landing gear, use a mix of Ultramarine Blue and Burnt Sienna.

![Images](images/f0101-01.jpg)

**3** Add Titanium White to the yellow and Unbleached Titanium to the red mix to create the highlights. Apply them with the #1 round brush; the highlight runs straight down on the cylinder but is angled on the cone. Paint the porthole on the side of the rocket in Mars Black, and the light planes of the landing gears in a mix of Ultramarine Blue and Unbleached Titanium.

![Images](images/f0101-02.jpg)

**4** With a #4 filbert brush, apply Yellow Ochre to the shaded left-hand side of the cone. Add a little Mars Black to Napthol Red and paint the shaded sides of the sphere and cylinder. Brighten the highlights with Titanium White. Mix Napthol Red, Yellow Ochre and Cadmium Yellow Medium. Using a #6 flat brush, paint the background, cutting out the shape of the rocket as you do so.

![Images](images/f0101-03.jpg)

**5** Paint the cast shadows in a mix of Napthol Red and Mars Black, using the #4 bright brush. The finishing touch is to paint the stripes in a mix of Ultramarine Blue, Mars Black and Unbleached Titanium with the #1 round brush, making sure they follow the same arc as the top and bottom of the cylinder. Circle the porthole with the same colour mix. When dry, add highlights to the stripes in Titanium White.

![Images](images/f0101-04.jpg)

Try painting a similar scene with the rocket in a different position – perhaps lying on its side, so that the ellipse of the base is more rounded – and see how that affects the shape and proportions of the various elements.
