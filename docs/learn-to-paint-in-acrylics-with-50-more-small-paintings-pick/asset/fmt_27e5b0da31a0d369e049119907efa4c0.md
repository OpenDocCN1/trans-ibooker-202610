
#### 5
Using a #6 flat brush and an orange-red (Napthol Red + Cadmium Yellow Medium), paint the background, cutting out the shape of the pancake stack as you do so. Using a #1 round brush, refine the butter and pancakes: shadow side of butter (Yellow Ochre + Cadmium Yellow Medium); left side of butter (Titanium White); light colour on pancakes (butter colour from step 4); cast shadows on butter and right-hand side of stack (Burnt Sienna). Using a #4 bright brush, paint the shadow to the right of the pancakes with a mix of Napthol Red, Mars Black and lots of glazing medium.

![Images](images/f0085-04.jpg)

Repeat step 1, varying your viewpoint. Viewed from directly overhead, the ellipse will appear more like a circle; the more you lower your viewpoint, the narrower the ellipse will seem.

### 25 Creating a unified colour scheme: Cactus
This project uses a very limited palette to create an almost monochromatic image made up of green and various tints and shades of grey – an analogous colour scheme (see page 26). Using analogous colours is a classic way to create a calm, peaceful mood. But there’s a twist here: sometimes in painting, the things you can’t see are just as important as those that you can. Even though it’s not immediately obvious, the same colour – Cadmium Yellow Medium – is used in every single mix, which helps to unify the painting. This colour is also applied as a thin, transparent glaze over the whole image, subtly modifying both the colours beneath and those laid on top to add a soft, warm glow.

#### Materials
![Images](images/f0086-02.jpg)

#### 1
Using Yellow Ochre and a #1 round brush, draw the cylinder of the pot and put in a horizontal line for the tabletop. Mix a dark green colour from Mars Black, a little Cadmium Yellow Medium and glazing medium. Using a #4 filbert brush, block in the oval leaf shapes and add a stroke to the base of the pot. Let dry.

![Images](images/f0086-03.jpg)

#### 2
Mix a very thin, transparent yellow-green glaze from Cadmium Yellow Medium, Yellow Ochre, a little Mars Black and plenty of glazing medium. Using a #6 flat brush, apply this mix over the entire canvas. Note how the glaze affects the green of the cactus – it started out as quite an acidic green, but the glaze softens it. Let dry.

![Images](images/f0087-01.jpg)

#### 3
Mix a mid-toned grey from Unbleached Titanium, Mars Black and a tiny bit of Cadmium Yellow Medium and paint the pot with the #4 filbert brush. Add more Cadmium Yellow Medium to push the grey colour towards green. Using a #6 flat brush, apply this over the background, cutting out the shape of the pot and cactus as you do so. The warmth of this background colour is partly due to the yellow glaze underneath.

![Images](images/f0087-02.jpg)

#### 4
Paint the tabletop in a light grey mix of Unbleached Titanium, Cadmium Yellow Medium and a little bit of Mars Black. Note how well all the colours harmonize with each other: this is because the Cadmium Yellow Medium and Mars Black are common to all the mixes.

![Images](images/f0087-03.jpg)

#### 5
Darken the tabletop mix slightly and paint the light side of the pot. The shadow colours on the wall and pot are all mixed from the same colours in varying proportions – Mars Black, Unbleached Titanium and Cadmium Yellow Medium. Paint them using the #4 filbert brush. Using the #1 round brush and Titanium White, paint the spines on the cactus and the highlight on the pot.

![Images](images/f0087-04.jpg)

Greens and greys predominate in this simple still life – but note how the initial thin yellow glaze imparts a warm glow to the whole painting.

### 26 Multiple spheres: Toy panda
In this project, we’re building up a complex form from a series of interlocking spheres. Treat them in exactly the same way as a simple sphere (see page 76), but take the time to assess each one individually to see where the shifts from light to dark occur. For example, the panda’s protruding snout shields one side of its face from the light, so that side of the face is a little darker than the shaded side of the body. These are small, subtle differences, but they help to convey the variations in the round forms. Experiment with different brushstrokes, too, to see what textures you can create. Dabbing the paint on will create a slightly mottled effect, which is perfect for the fake fur this cuddly panda is made from, whereas a dry-brush treatment might be more appropriate for a toy made from a woven fabric such as linen.

#### Materials
![Images](images/f0088-02.jpg)

#### 1
Using a #4 filbert brush and Unbleached Titanium thinned with glazing medium, draw a series of circles and ellipses for the various parts of the panda. The proportions don’t have to be perfect; these are just initial guide lines.

![Images](images/f0088-03.jpg)

#### 2
Fill in the black parts of the panda, using the #4 filbert brush and Mars Black thinned with a little glazing medium.

![Images](images/f0089-01.jpg)

#### 3
Mix a pale grey from Unbleached Titanium and a little Mars Black. Using a #1 round brush, fill in the shaded white areas on the left-hand side of the spheres. Add more Unbleached Titanium as you work across to the right-hand side, moving out of the shade and towards the light source.

![Images](images/f0089-02.jpg)

#### 4
Apply pure Titanium White for the lightest parts of the head and belly. Mix a dark grey from Mars Black and a little Unbleached Titanium. Indicate the eyes, draw the mouth and paint the lighter sections on the tops of the ears, nose, arms and legs (see final painting). Using a #6 flat brush and a mix of Ultramarine Blue and Unbleached Titanium, paint the background, cutting out the shape of the panda as you do so.

![Images](images/f0089-03.jpg)

#### 5
Mix the cast shadow colour from Ultramarine Blue, a little Mars Black and Unbleached Titanium and apply with a #4 bright brush. With the #1 round brush and Titanium White, paint the whites of the panda’s eyes. When dry, paint the pupils in Mars Black.

![Images](images/f0089-04.jpg)

At first glance, this might look like quite a complicated object to paint – but, like most things, it can easily be broken down into a series of simple geometric shapes.

### 27 Hatching brushstrokes: Bee
This project gives you the chance to practise using very short, controlled brushstrokes to create the soft, furry texture of this (literally) larger-than-life bee. These hatching strokes are built up on top of a mid-toned ground colour, allowing some of the underlying colour to remain visible so that you can gradually build light and dark tones on top and create a real sense of depth and form. The shapes are very simple – just circles and egg shapes for the different parts of the bee’s body. This, combined with the bold patterning of black stripes on a yellow-orange ground, allows you to focus on making textural brushstrokes without having to worry too much about your drawing skills.

#### Materials
![Images](images/f0090-02.jpg)

#### 1
Using a #1 round brush and Yellow Ochre, draw the outline of the insect as a series of very simple shapes – circles for the head and thorax, an elongated egg shape for the abdomen and wing, and lines for the legs and antennae.

![Images](images/f0090-03.jpg)

#### 2
Using a #4 filbert brush and leaving a space for the eye, block in the head and body with Yellow Ochre mixed with a tiny bit of Cadmium Yellow Medium. Let dry.

![Images](images/f0091-01.jpg)

#### 3
Now paint the legs, stripes and head details, using the #1 round brush and Mars Black. The round brush has a fine tip, so it’s perfect for painting small details.

![Images](images/f0091-02.jpg)
