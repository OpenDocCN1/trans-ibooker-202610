## Chapter 2. Use Cases and Scenarios

This chapter explores various use cases of confidential computing and how it protects data privacy in different scenarios. These scenarios include the lift and shift of sensitive workloads in regulated industries, sovereign government workloads, data clean rooms, confidential AI, and other use cases.

### Lift and Shift

Today’s confidential computing technologies are an enabler for organizations, particularly in highly regulated industries, that want to migrate existing sensitive workloads to the public cloud with minimal effort or add further protection to workloads already running in the public cloud. With VM-level and container-level isolation technologies, organizations can now migrate their most sensitive VM- and container-based workloads to confidential computing-enabled infrastructure with no changes to their code, accelerating the adoption of these technologies.

### Sovereign Government Workloads

Governments and public institutions require a high degree of data sovereignty due to the sensitive nature of the data they manage. Moreover, the presence of threats such as insider risk and espionage present substantial hurdles for governments when considering the use of a public cloud, since doing so requires trust in the cloud operator. Confidential computing reduces the need for inherent trust in cloud operators by implementing methods that ensure the protection of both data and code from unauthorized access, even by the cloud operator. Confidential computing enables highly confidential workloads to run in the cloud and enables governments to adopt a never-trust, always-verify security mindset when migrating and running their workloads in the cloud. Microsoft Azure addresses these requirements with Microsoft Cloud for Sovereignty.

### Data Clean Rooms

The goal of a data clean room is to allow multiple data providers to share data with model and algorithm providers to gain insights from a broader, previously unavailable set of data, all while maintaining governance and privacy rules. Confidential computing enables data clean rooms by providing cryptographic verification that data providers do not have access to data or associated models even when computation is taking place on them. This reinforces the zero-trust principle by guaranteeing that no party is allowed to access data or models.

### Confidential AI

Confidential AI is a collection of hardware-based technologies such as general-purpose CPUs and GPUs that enable the establishment of trusted execution environments (TEEs), designed to safeguard data and models throughout the AI lifecycle, even while data and models are in use. The objective is to secure data throughout all phases of AI data processing, including data collection, preprocessing, training, and deployment of AI models. Confidential computing plays a crucial role in facilitating confidential AI solutions, as it enables access to sensitive datasets while addressing security and compliance concerns with minimal performance overhead when compared to technologies like homomorphic encryption. Confidential computing enables data providers to authorize the use of their datasets for very specific purposes, such as training or adjusting an agreed-upon model while maintaining the confidentiality of the data throughout the computation. Data providers can also request proof via attestation that their datasets are only being processed in a TEE.

In recent years, generative AI has emerged as a significant area of innovation and disruption. The popularity of large language models (LLMs) such as generative pre-trained transformer (GPT), Large Language Model Meta AI (LLaMA), and many others has grown exponentially. Because these models and related workloads rely heavily on GPUs, Microsoft and NVIDIA are collaborating closely, as discussed in the blog post “Azure Confidential Computing with NVIDIA GPUs for Trustworthy AI”, to bring GPU-accelerated computing to confidential computing on Azure.

This is important because when data is transmitted to or from the CPU to a GPU, it is always encrypted and can only be decrypted in a hardware-protected, isolated enclave within the GPU. There, it can then be processed to build AI models or produce AI inference outputs. For a more in-depth technical examination of NVIDIA’s GPU security model, please read the white paper “Confidential Compute on NVIDIA Hopper H100”.
