# 3.2. Loss Functions of the IF-GAN

The loss function of the IF-GAN is given below:

ℒ = λ₁ · ℒ_cGAN(G, D) + λ₂ · ℒ_L1(G) + λ₃ · ℒ_softmax(E) + λ₄ · ℒ_IL(E)

where the hyperparameters λ₁, λ₂, λ₃, and λ₄ are used to balance the four terms. ℒ_L1 is the dominant loss for expressive face generation and ℒ_softmax is the dominant supervision signal to classify the expressive face. ℒ_cGAN must be jointly optimized with ℒ_L1 to better produce outputs with the distribution of real data. Larger ℒ_IL results in inseparable features and slow convergence according to [5]. Therefore, the weights of ℒ_L1 and ℒ_softmax are higher than other losses.

The cGAN loss function ℒ_cGAN(G, D) is defined as:

ℒ_cGAN(G, D) = 𝔼[log D({I_SE, I_AE})] + 𝔼[log(1 − D({I_SE, G(I_SE)}))]

where G(I_SE) is the generated image Î_AE, {I_SE, G(I_SE)} is a fake tuple, and {I_SE, I_AE} is a real tuple.

Competing against the discriminator D, G(·) learns the true data distribution using L1 distance:

ℒ_L1(G) = 𝔼[‖I_AE − G(I_SE)‖₁]

E is jointly trained with D and G, using softmax loss and expression label l_e to define expression loss:

ℒ_softmax(E) = 𝔼[log p(l_e | {I_SE, I_AE})] + 𝔼[log p(l_e | {I_SE, G(I_SE)})]

Island loss ℒ_IL(E) [5], which simultaneously reduces the intra-class variations and increases the inter-class differences, is employed jointly with the softmax loss to train expression classifier E:

ℒ_IL(E) = Σ_{i=1}^M ‖x_i − c_{y_i}‖₂² + α Σ_{j=1}^N Σ_{k≠j}^N ( (c_k · c_j)/(‖c_k‖₂‖c_j‖₂) + 1 )

where M is the number of images; N is the number of expression classes; c_k and c_j denote the k^th and j^th expression centers; (·) denotes dot product; α = 0.03 balances the two terms. Minimizing the island loss pushes same-class samples closer while separating them from other samples.

# 4. EXPERIMENTS

# 4.1. Experimental Datasets

BU-3DFE [26]: Following [1, 12], we used 1,200 images of six basic expressions (anger, disgust, fear, happiness, sadness, and surprise) with high intensity, i.e., the last two levels.

CK+ [27, 28], with 118 subjects, has 327 videos labeled with one of seven expressions, i.e., contempt and six basic expressions. As in [5], the last three frames from 309 videos labeled as one of six basic expressions formed a 927-image experimental dataset.

MMI [29] has 208 sequences of 31 subjects displaying six basic expressions in frontal-view. Like [4], we collected three peak frames in the middle, for a total of 624 images.

RAF-DB [7] is a large spontaneous FER dataset of thousands of subjects with various head poses, lighting conditions, and occlusions. We used the RAF-DB single-label subset (12,271 training images and 3,068 testing images) labeled with one of neutral or six basic expressions.

For the posed facial expression datasets, we used a 10-fold cross-validation strategy, i.e., each dataset was split into 10 subsets, mutually exclusive by subjects. For each run, there were 8 training, one validation, and one testing subsets. Reported results are the average of 10 runs. RAF-DB experiments used the standard training and testing sets.

# 4.2. Preprocessing

We employed the given cropped RAF-DB face images. BU-3DFE, CK+, and MMI face regions were aligned based on the eye centers and nose tip, detected by MTCNN [30] and then scaled to 256 × 256 with random horizontal flipping and random -5° and 5° rotations before randomly cropping to 224 × 224 patches during training. The center-cropped patches are used in testing.
