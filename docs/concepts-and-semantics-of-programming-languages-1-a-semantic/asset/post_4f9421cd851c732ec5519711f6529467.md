We recall that, given a partial order (D, \(\sqsubseteq\)), the upper bounds and lower bounds of a subset \(Y \subseteq D\) are defined by:

\(Maj(Y) = \{d \in D \mid \forall y \in Y \, y \sqsubseteq d\} \qquad Min(Y) = \{d \in D \mid \forall y \in Y \, d \sqsubseteq y\}\)

The supremum \(\sqcup Y\) of \(Y \subseteq D\) is the least upper bound of Y, if there exists:

\(\forall d \in D \, (\forall y \in Y \, y \sqsubseteq d) \Rightarrow \sqcup Y \sqsubseteq d\)

The least element (minimum) of \(Y \subseteq D\), if it exists, is the only element in \(Min(Y) \cap Y\). The proposition below can be proved easily.

PROPOSITION 4.3.— \(\bot : \text{Mem} \mapsto \text{undef}\) is the least element of [\(\text{M} \to \text{M}\)].

Given a partial order (D, \(\sqsubseteq\)) and a function \(f : D \to D\), an element \(d \in D\) is a fixpoint of \(f\) if and only if \(f(d) = d\). If it exists, the least fixpoint of \(f\), denoted as lfp(f), is a fixpoint of \(f\) such that:

\(\forall d \in D \, (f(d) = d \Rightarrow \text{lfp}(f) \sqsubseteq d)\)

## 4.7.1.2.2. Complete partial orders

The least fixpoint of a functional is obtained in an incremental way by constructing a sequence of increasingly precise approximations. The least fixpoint is the least upper bound of this sequence of approximations. Complete partial orders provide a framework for establishing the existence of a least upper bound in these situations. Given a partial order (D, \(\sqsubseteq\)), a subset \(Y \subseteq D\) is a *chain* if its elements are totally ordered:

\(\forall y_1, y_2 \in Y \, y_1 \sqsubseteq y_2 \text{ or } y_2 \sqsubseteq y_1\)

A ccpo — *chain-complete partial order* — is a partial order (D, \(\sqsubseteq\)) such that, for any chain \(Y \subseteq D\), \(\sqcup Y\) exists. If \(\sqcup Y\) exists for every subset \(Y \subseteq D\), the partial order (D, \(\sqsubseteq\)) is a complete lattice (and consequently a ccpo).

LEMMA 4.4.— Every ccpo (D, \(\sqsubseteq\)) has a least element \(\bot = \sqcup \emptyset\).

PROOF.— \(\emptyset\) is clearly a chain, and since (D, \(\sqsubseteq\)) is a ccpo, \(\sqcup \emptyset\) exists. Since \(\sqcup \emptyset \in D\), to show that \(\sqcup \emptyset\) is the least element in D, it suffices to prove that it is a lower bound of D. Using the definition of \(\sqcup \emptyset\), we have:

\(\forall d \in D \, (\forall x \in \emptyset \, x \sqsubseteq d) \Rightarrow \sqcup \emptyset \sqsubseteq d\)

and since \(\forall x \in \emptyset \ x \sqsubseteq d\) is always true, we have \(\forall d \in D \ \sqcup \emptyset \sqsubseteq d\) and thus \(\sqcup \emptyset\) is a lower bound of \(D\).

The following lemma, which is an immediate consequence of definitions, will be used as follows.

LEMMA 4.3.— Given a ccpo \((D, \sqsubseteq)\) and a chain \(Y \subseteq D\), we have:

\(\sqcup(Y \cup \{\bot\}) = \sqcup Y\)

The following proposition characterizes the least upper bound of a chain of partial functions from \(\mathbf{M}\) to \(\mathbf{M}\).

PROPOSITION 4.4.— \(([\mathbf{M} \rightarrow \mathbf{M}], \sqsubseteq)\) is a ccpo such that for any chain \(Y\):

\(\sqcup Y : \text{Mem} \mapsto \begin{cases} f(\text{Mem}) & \text{if there exists } f \in Y \text{ such that } f(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef} \\ \text{undef} & \text{else} \end{cases}\)

PROOF.— Let \(Y\) be a chain in \(([\mathbf{M} \rightarrow \mathbf{M}], \sqsubseteq)\).

First, let us prove that \(\sqcup Y\) defines a function. If a function \(f \in Y\) exists such that \(f(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\), then for any function \(f' \in Y\) such that \(f'(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\), we have \(f'(\text{Mem}) = f(\text{Mem})\). Since \(Y\) is a chain, by definition, either \(f \sqsubseteq f'\) and thus \(f'(\text{Mem}) = f(\text{Mem})\) since \(f(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\), or \(f' \sqsubseteq f\) and since \(f'(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\) we have \(f(\text{Mem}) = f'(\text{Mem})\).

Now, let us show that \(\sqcup Y\) defines the least upper bound of \(Y\). Given \(f \in Y\) and \(\text{Mem} \in \mathbf{M}\), if \(f(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\), then by construction we have \((\sqcup Y)(\text{Mem}) = f(\text{Mem})\) and \(f \sqsubseteq \sqcup Y\). So \(\sqcup Y\) defines an upper bound of \(Y\). Now we show that this is the least upper bound of \(Y\): let \(g\) be an upper bound of \(Y\), we have to prove that \(\sqcup Y \sqsubseteq g\). Take \(\text{Mem} \in \mathbf{M}\) such that \((\sqcup Y)(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\); by definition, there exists \(f \in Y\) such that \(f(\text{Mem}) = (\sqcup Y)(\text{Mem})\) and since \(f \sqsubseteq g\) (since \(g\) is an upper bound of \(Y\)), we obtain \((\sqcup Y)(\text{Mem}) = f(\text{Mem}) = g(\text{Mem})\) and thus the conclusion.

## 4.7.1.2.3. Continuous functions

The computation of the least fixpoint of a functional \(F\) is based on the continuity of this function. Given two partial orders \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)\) and \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\), a function \(f : D_1 \rightarrow D_2\) is monotonic when it preserves the order, i.e. if and only if:

\(\forall x, y \in D_1 \ x \sqsubseteq_1 y \Rightarrow f(x) \sqsubseteq_2 f(y)\)

The lemma below is easy to prove since \(\sqsubseteq\) is a transitive relation.

LEMMA 4.4.— Let \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)\), \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\) and \((D_3, \sqsubseteq_3)\) be three partial orders. If the functions \(f : D_1 \rightarrow D_2\) and \(f' : D_2 \rightarrow D_3\) are monotonic, then \(f' \circ f : D_1 \rightarrow D_3\) is monotonic.

The following lemma establishes a property over the least upper bounds of a chain and of its image by a monotonic function.

LEMMA 4.5.— Let \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1), (D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\) be two ccpo and \(f : D_1 \to D_2\) a monotonic function. If \(Y\) is a chain of \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)\), then \(f(Y)\) is a chain of \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\) such that \(\sqcup_2 f(Y) \sqsubseteq_2 f(\sqcup_1 Y)\).

PROOF.— If \(Y = \emptyset\), then \(f(Y) = \emptyset\) is a chain of \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\) and since \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\) is a ccpo, following lemma 4.2, \(\sqcup_2 \emptyset\) is the least element in \(D_2\) and thus \(\sqcup_2 \emptyset \sqsubseteq_2 f(\sqcup_1 \emptyset)\). If \(Y \neq \emptyset\), then if \(x_2, y_2 \in f(Y)\), by definition, there exists \(x_1, y_1 \in Y\) such that \(x_2 = f(x_1)\) and \(y_2 = f(y_1)\). Since \(Y\) is a chain of \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)\), either \(x_1 \sqsubseteq_1 y_1\) and so \(x_2 = f(x_1) \sqsubseteq_2 f(y_1) = y_2\), or \(y_1 \sqsubseteq_1 x_1\) and so \(y_2 = f(y_1) \sqsubseteq_2 f(x_1) = x_2\) (as \(f\) is monotonic). \(f(Y)\) is therefore a chain of \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\).

Let us prove that \(\sqcup_2 f(Y) \sqsubseteq_2 f(\sqcup_1 Y)\). By definition, \(\forall y \in Y y \sqsubseteq_1 \sqcup_1 Y\), and since \(f\) is monotonic, \(\forall y \in Y f(y) \sqsubseteq_2 f(\sqcup_1 Y)\), hence \(f(\sqcup_1 Y)\) is an upper bound of \(f(Y)\). We know that \(\sqcup_2 f(Y)\) is the least upper bound of \(Y\) and so \(\sqcup_2 f(Y) \sqsubseteq_2 f(\sqcup_1 Y)\).

Given two partial orders \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)\) and \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\), a function \(f : D_1 \to D_2\) is said to be _continuous_ if and only if it is monotonic and keeps the least upper bounds of the chains, i.e. if and only if \(f\) is monotonic and \(\sqcup_2 f(Y) = f(\sqcup_1 Y)\) for any non-empty chain \(Y\) in \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)\). The function \(f\) is said to be _strict_ if the property is also verified for an empty chain:

\(\sqcup_2 f(\emptyset) = \sqcup_2 \emptyset = \bot_2 = f(\bot_1) = f(\sqcup_1 \emptyset)\)

Once again, the composition of two continuous functions is itself a continuous function.

LEMMA 4.6.— Take three ccpo \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)\), \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\) and \((D_3, \sqsubseteq_3)\). If \(f : D_1 \to D_2\) and \(f' : D_2 \to D_3\) are continuous, then \(f' \circ f : D_1 \to D_3\) is continuous.

PROOF.— Functions \(f\) and \(f'\) are continuous, so we can show that \(f' \circ f\) is monotonic, as stated in lemma 4.4. Let \(Y\) be a non-empty chain in \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)\). Since \(f\) is continuous, by definition, \(\sqcup_2 f(Y) = f(\sqcup_1 Y)\) and \(f\) is monotonic. According to lemma 4.5, \(f(Y)\) is a non-empty chain in \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\) and since \(f'\) is continuous, we obtain \(\sqcup_3 f'(f(Y)) = f'(\sqcup_2 f(Y))\); since \(\sqcup_2 f(Y) = f(\sqcup_1 Y)\), we obtain \(\sqcup_3 (f' \circ f)(Y) = f'(f(\sqcup_1 Y))\) and thus \(\sqcup_3 (f' \circ f)(Y) = (f' \circ f)(\sqcup_1 Y)\), which proves that \(f' \circ f\) is continuous.
