The following lemma establishes a property over the least upper bounds of a chain and of its image by a monotonic function.

LEMMA 4.5.- Let $(D_1, \sqsubseteq_1), (D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)$ be two ccpo and $f : D_1 \to D_2$ a monotonic function. If $Y$ is a chain of $(D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)$, then $f(Y)$ is a chain of $(D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)$ such that $\sqcup_2 f(Y) \sqsubseteq_2 f(\sqcup_1 Y)$. 

PROOF.- If $Y = \emptyset$, then $f(Y) = \emptyset$ is a chain of $(D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)$ and since $(D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)$ is a ccpo, following lemma 4.2, $\sqcup_2 \emptyset$ is the least element in $D_2$ and thus $\sqcup_2 \emptyset \sqsubseteq_2 f(\sqcup_1 \emptyset)$. If $Y \neq \emptyset$, then if $x_2, y_2 \in f(Y)$, by definition, there exists $x_1, y_1 \in Y$ such that $x_2 = f(x_1)$ and $y_2 = f(y_1)$. Since $Y$ is a chain of $(D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)$, either $x_1 \sqsubseteq_1 y_1$ and so $x_2 = f(x_1) \sqsubseteq_2 f(y_1) = y_2$, or $y_1 \sqsubseteq_1 x_1$ and so $y_2 = f(y_1) \sqsubseteq_2 f(x_1) = x_2$ (as $f$ is monotonic). $f(Y)$ is therefore a chain of $(D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)$. 

Let us prove that $\sqcup_2 f(Y) \sqsubseteq_2 f(\sqcup_1 Y)$. By definition, $\forall y \in Y y \sqsubseteq_1 \sqcup_1 Y$, and since $f$ is monotonic, $\forall y \in Y f(y) \sqsubseteq_2 f(\sqcup_1 Y)$, hence $f(\sqcup_1 Y)$ is an upper bound of $f(Y)$. We know that $\sqcup_2 f(Y)$ is the least upper bound of $Y$ and so $\sqcup_2 f(Y) \sqsubseteq_2 f(\sqcup_1 Y)$. 

Given two partial orders $(D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)$ and $(D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)$, a function $f : D_1 \to D_2$ is said to be _continuous_ if and only if it is monotonic and keeps the least upper bounds of the chains, i.e. if and only if $f$ is monotonic and $\sqcup_2 f(Y) = f(\sqcup_1 Y)$ for any non-empty chain $Y$ in $(D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)$. The function $f$ is said to be _strict_ if the property is also verified for an empty chain:

$\sqcup_2 f(\emptyset) = \sqcup_2 \emptyset = \bot_2 = f(\bot_1) = f(\sqcup_1 \emptyset)$

Once again, the composition of two continuous functions is itself a continuous function.

LEMMA 4.6.- Take three ccpo $(D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)$, $(D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)$ and $(D_3, \sqsubseteq_3)$. If $f : D_1 \to D_2$ and $f' : D_2 \to D_3$ are continuous, then $f' \circ f : D_1 \to D_3$ is continuous.

PROOF.- Functions $f$ and $f'$ are continuous, so we can show that $f' \circ f$ is monotonic, as stated in lemma 4.4. Let $Y$ be a non-empty chain in $(D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)$. Since $f$ is continuous, by definition, $\sqcup_2 f(Y) = f(\sqcup_1 Y)$ and $f$ is monotonic. According to lemma 4.5, $f(Y)$ is a non-empty chain in $(D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)$ and since $f'$ is continuous, we obtain $\sqcup_3 f'(f(Y)) = f'(\sqcup_2 f(Y))$; since $\sqcup_2 f(Y) = f(\sqcup_1 Y)$, we obtain $\sqcup_3 (f' \circ f)(Y) = f'(f(\sqcup_1 Y))$ and thus $\sqcup_3 (f' \circ f)(Y) = (f' \circ f)(\sqcup_1 Y)$, which proves that $f' \circ f$ is continuous.
