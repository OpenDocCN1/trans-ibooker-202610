and since \(\forall x \in \emptyset \ x \sqsubseteq d\) is always true, we have \(\forall d \in D \ \sqcup \emptyset \sqsubseteq d\) and thus \(\sqcup \emptyset\) is a lower bound of \(D\).

The following lemma, which is an immediate consequence of definitions, will be used as follows.

LEMMA 4.3.— Given a ccpo \((D, \sqsubseteq)\) and a chain \(Y \subseteq D\), we have:

\(\sqcup(Y \cup \{\bot\}) = \sqcup Y\)

The following proposition characterizes the least upper bound of a chain of partial functions from \(\mathbf{M}\) to \(\mathbf{M}\).

PROPOSITION 4.4.— \(([\mathbf{M} \rightarrow \mathbf{M}], \sqsubseteq)\) is a ccpo such that for any chain \(Y\):

\(\sqcup Y : \text{Mem} \mapsto \begin{cases} f(\text{Mem}) & \text{if there exists } f \in Y \text{ such that } f(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef} \\ \text{undef} & \text{else} \end{cases}\)

PROOF.— Let \(Y\) be a chain in \(([\mathbf{M} \rightarrow \mathbf{M}], \sqsubseteq)\).

First, let us prove that \(\sqcup Y\) defines a function. If a function \(f \in Y\) exists such that \(f(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\), then for any function \(f' \in Y\) such that \(f'(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\), we have \(f'(\text{Mem}) = f(\text{Mem})\). Since \(Y\) is a chain, by definition, either \(f \sqsubseteq f'\) and thus \(f'(\text{Mem}) = f(\text{Mem})\) since \(f(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\), or \(f' \sqsubseteq f\) and since \(f'(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\) we have \(f(\text{Mem}) = f'(\text{Mem})\).

Now, let us show that \(\sqcup Y\) defines the least upper bound of \(Y\). Given \(f \in Y\) and \(\text{Mem} \in \mathbf{M}\), if \(f(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\), then by construction we have \((\sqcup Y)(\text{Mem}) = f(\text{Mem})\) and \(f \sqsubseteq \sqcup Y\). So \(\sqcup Y\) defines an upper bound of \(Y\). Now we show that this is the least upper bound of \(Y\): let \(g\) be an upper bound of \(Y\), we have to prove that \(\sqcup Y \sqsubseteq g\). Take \(\text{Mem} \in \mathbf{M}\) such that \((\sqcup Y)(\text{Mem}) \neq \text{undef}\); by definition, there exists \(f \in Y\) such that \(f(\text{Mem}) = (\sqcup Y)(\text{Mem})\) and since \(f \sqsubseteq g\) (since \(g\) is an upper bound of \(Y\)), we obtain \((\sqcup Y)(\text{Mem}) = f(\text{Mem}) = g(\text{Mem})\) and thus the conclusion.

# 4.7.1.2.3. Continuous functions

The computation of the least fixpoint of a functional \(F\) is based on the continuity of this function. Given two partial orders \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)\) and \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\), a function \(f : D_1 \rightarrow D_2\) is monotonic when it preserves the order, i.e. if and only if:

\(\forall x, y \in D_1 \ x \sqsubseteq_1 y \Rightarrow f(x) \sqsubseteq_2 f(y)\)

The lemma below is easy to prove since \(\sqsubseteq\) is a transitive relation.

LEMMA 4.4.— Let \((D_1, \sqsubseteq_1)\), \((D_2, \sqsubseteq_2)\) and \((D_3, \sqsubseteq_3)\) be three partial orders. If the functions \(f : D_1 \rightarrow D_2\) and \(f' : D_2 \rightarrow D_3\) are monotonic, then \(f' \circ f : D_1 \rightarrow D_3\) is monotonic.
