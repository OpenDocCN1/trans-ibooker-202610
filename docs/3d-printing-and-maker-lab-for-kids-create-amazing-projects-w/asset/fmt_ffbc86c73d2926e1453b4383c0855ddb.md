Most 3D printers will accept files in a specific .STL format. In SketchUp, you will need to download an extension software from the Extension Warehouse to convert your designs into .STL format. Fig. 8 shows how to get to the Extension Warehouse.

![Image](images/f0123-04.jpg)

# LAB 23: Basic 3D Shapes

In this Lab we learn how to use SketchUp to create basic 3D shapes. Unlike Tinkercad, which offers a toolbox of precreated 3D shapes, SketchUp offers a toolbox of 2D shapes. We can use these 2D shapes to create 3D shapes by extruding them—stretching them out in a specific direction.

![Image](images/f0124-01.jpg)

## THE DESIGN PROCESS

### Creating a basic box/rectangular prism and cylinder:

1. Select the `Rectangle` tool from the Ribbon at the top of the screen. Then click and drag your cursor to create a rectangle of dimensions small enough to view on the screen (fig. 1). You can draw the rectangle anywhere on the work area.

![Image](images/f0125-01.jpg)

**FIG. 1:** *Draw a rectangle by selecting the* `Rectangle` *tool from the menu.*

2. Now select the `Push/Pull` tool from the Ribbon (fig. 2). Point it at the top face of the 2D rectangle (fig. 2) and drag to the desired height (about an inch, or 25 mm). Feel free to readjust the height using the `Push/Pull` tool to give you a feel for the tool.

![Image](images/f0125-02.jpg)

**FIG. 2:** *Stretch the rectangle using the* `Push/Pull` *tool.*

3. You can use the `Tape Measure` tool to measure the dimensions of the 3D shape (fig. 3). The `Text` tool to the right of the Tape Measure tool will let you label your measurements by simply selecting it and then clicking on the desired edge to be measured.

![Image](images/f0126-01.jpg)

**FIG. 3:** *Use the* `Tape Measure` *tool to find the dimensions of the shape. Add a label with the* `Text` *tool.*

4. Now, to create a cylinder. Similar to step 1 above, select the `Circle` tool (fig. 4) and draw a circle so it lies flat on the workspace. Note that the outline of the circle will change color to match the axis it corresponds to.

![Image](images/f0126-02.jpg)

**FIG. 4:** *Add a circle to the workspace using the* `Circle` *tool.*

5. Follow step 2 to extrude the circle to convert it to a cylinder (fig. 5).

![Image](images/f0126-03.jpg)

**FIG. 5:** *Stretch the circle into a cylinder.*

# LAB 24: Removing Material from a 3D Object

In this Lab we learn how to use SketchUp to create basic holes in 3D shapes. Unlike in Tinkercad, which offers a hole tool, in SketchUp we have to use the `Push/Pull` tool to literally push a hole into a 3D shape.

![Image](images/f0128-01.jpg)

## THE DESIGN PROCESS

1. Draw a cylinder in SketchUp with a diameter of about 2 inches, similar to Lab 23 (shown [here](#ch02.xhtml#page_124)). Arrow 1 in fig. 1 shows the initial circle drawn using the `Circle` tool, and arrow 2 shows the circle extruded into a cylinder using the `Push/Pull` tool.

![Image](images/f0129-01.jpg)

**FIG. 1:** *Create a cylinder.*

2. We now draw another circle on the front face of the cylinder. The size of the second circle is slightly smaller than that of the cylinder 1; this will determine the size of the hole in the napkin ring. Arrow 1 in fig. 2 shows the circle for the hole. Arrow 2 shows it being pushed back into the cylinder to the bottom edge of the cylinder. Note that if you do not push it to the edge, the material will get pushed in the opposite direction to form another cylinder.

![Image](images/f0130-01.jpg)

**FIG. 2:** *Using the* `Push/Pull` *tool to draw a circular hole in SketchUp.*

# LAB 25: Removing Material and Combining Basic Shapes

In this Lab we extend our basic shape-making knowledge of SketchUp to making composite shapes to design menu holders. Restaurants often have specials that are listed on a single sheet of paper. A menu holder needs to be able to grip the paper as well as to keep the menu from flopping over. One way to keep a single sheet of paper from flopping over is to bend it or give it some curvature, which increases its rigidity.

![Image](images/f0132-01.jpg)

## THE DESIGN PROCESS

1. The base of the menu holder is simply a narrow rectangular strip that is no more than 2 mm thick. The length should be more than 50 mm (fig. 1).

![Image](images/f0133-01.jpg)

**FIG. 1:** *Create the base of the menu holder from a rectangular strip.*

2. Designing the curved walls by creating a cylinder using the process in Lab 23 (shown [here](#ch02.xhtml#page_124)). Draw a circle with center on the circumference that almost covers the cylinder completely (fig. 2).

![Image](images/f0133-02.jpg)

**FIG. 2:** *Make a cylinder and add a cutting circle.*

3. Use the `Push/Pull` tool to extrude the overlapping circle to make another cylinder of the same thickness as the first cylinder (fig. 3).

![Image](images/f0133-03.jpg)

**FIG. 3:** *Make the cutting circle into a cylinder.*

4. Use the `Erase` tool to erase all the faces and lines except the small portion that will be the wall of the menu holder (fig. 4).

![Image](images/f0134-01.jpg)

**FIG. 4:** *Erase everything except the crescent shape that will be one of the walls for the menu holder.*
