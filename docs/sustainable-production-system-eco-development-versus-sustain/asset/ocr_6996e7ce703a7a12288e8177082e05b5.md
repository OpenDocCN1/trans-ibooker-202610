# 6.2.2.2.2. Step 2: structuring the problem by describing three components of the associated system (categories of actors, issues and situations to be assessed)

Refining the identification of the problem in this way leads us to consider that its structuring implies addressing “the question of the validity and relevance of scientific knowledge to address complex problems” (van der Sluijs et al. 2008a, 2008b). The objective that will govern this structuring will then be to support the actors through a set of reflections and formalizations, which can be qualified as “quality control of knowledge” (ibid.).

The problem we have described involves practitioners with diverse rationalities who each express multiple and specific issues. However, it is fairly easy to identify three categories – practitioners of political economy, market economy and financial economy – that we will distinguish, by forcing the caricature a little, by the evaluation practices that characterize them.

| Characteristics of evaluation practices | Political economy | Market economy | Financial economy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posture | actor | Agent | Entity |
| Purpose | sustainability | solvability | Profitability |
| Purpose of the evaluation | Territorial functions | useful functionality | Recomposed utilities |
| Valued links | Systemic | trades | Risks |
| Methods and tools | Multiple and adaptable | Few in number and fairly standardized | Standardized because they are globalized |
| Temporality | Plural | Particular | Singular |
| Territorial anchorage (proximities) | Spatial, organizational and institutional | Organizational and institutional | Institutional |
