Stiegler (2006) describes as *individuation* the phenomenon corresponding to the “psychological and social biological formation of the individual who is still incomplete”²³. Stiegler calls for a policy based on a re-articulation between psychic and collective individuations and a technique – *technique* referring here to a concrete and effective environment linking the “I” and the “we”. In Stiegler’s study, the *transindividual* is what produces the social and through which the diachronizing co-individual of the “I” generates the synchronizing transindividual of a “we”³.

Interactions between individuals generate singularity, rhythms specific to each one, but also a group in which they constitute and nourish their individualities together through a process that can be approached intrinsically and according to a particular temporality. As a social institution, as well as a technique producing its environment within this institution, the economy – political, industrial and market – is thus the combination of a synchronizing transindividuation and a diachronizing co-individuation.

The inclusion of a social group in a synchronism of the relationship to wealth at the universal level of this group is inseparable from the diachronic negotiations around value, conducted by different subgroups considering various productive phenomena at different time and territory scales.

The synchronism of the relationship to wealth, as it is addressed in the democratic process for example, is directly affected by the diachronic influences of negotiations around the value of productive phenomena. During the democratic process, people collectively identify themselves and co-evolve the plural social value systems that will be mobilized during negotiations around the value of productive phenomena, negotiations that, in turn, influence the political process.

Political economy is an economy of the re-embedding of the economy in the social and democratic relationship – that is, in a relationship with the constraints of reality. It must take into account multiple levels (singular, particular and universal) and different temporal representations of wealth and value. This is all the more necessary and delicate when the reality in question is recognized as the result of systemic phenomena, functions and emergences that must be addressed both independently and in their interactions.
