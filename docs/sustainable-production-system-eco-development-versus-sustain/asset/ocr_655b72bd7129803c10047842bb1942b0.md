> *Problematic:* The risk of tension on aggregate resources is proven (DRIEE IDF 2012). The exploitation of the sands and gravels of the alluvial valleys of the Seine and Marne will be insufficient to meet demand. While interregional solidarity is part of the response to this risk³, better management at the level of the regional sector should also be considered. The transport of aggregates over long distances is limited, particularly in terms of profitability⁴ and environmental acceptability. In addition, exporting regions will show decreasing capacities in the medium term.

# Box 4.1. Context of the AGREGA project (Douguet et al. 2018)

Compared to other sectors, aggregate producers currently have no demand levers, they can only respond to them, without influencing market developments. This situation makes it difficult to anticipate future needs – which are highly dependent on the speed of development in the Ile-de-France region (Chamaret 2015).

Two scenarios for responding to increasing demand have been identified (DRIEE 2012). The first involves a total consumption of 37.9 Mt/year with a regional dependency ratio maintained at 45%. The second considers the same consumption, but considers additional contributions exclusively from external regions, which leads to an increase in the dependency ratio⁵.
