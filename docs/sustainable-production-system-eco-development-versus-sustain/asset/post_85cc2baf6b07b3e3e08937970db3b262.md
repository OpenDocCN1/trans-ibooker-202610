However, this quality of information is not expressed in precision or quantity, but in adequacy, i.e. in a report on the way in which it must be composed and presented, in order to be mobilized by other actors, in other spaces and times of representation.

Returning to the *theory of social systems*, we have associated the contract that seals a territorial project with one of the three positions of *communication*: that of communication, that is, of the container – or support – that allows the diffusion of information, which will be interpreted in the expectation of success.

This position – communication – when held by the mediating artifact that is the contract, has two particularities. It anchors Communication in the duration of the contractual commitment and stabilizes during this period an island of rationality within the space of representation of the territorial project. It mobilizes indicators to characterize the performance clauses that reflect levels of ambition and adequacy to the requirements of this project.

The contract therefore has a strong structural dimension. It contextualizes both the information mobilized during political deliberations around the ecological, economic and social dimensions of a territory’s performance, and the information mobilized during accounting valuations. Thus, the *discounting* logic that underlies neoclassical valuation practices (see section 5.1.2.1) by reducing to the present time the financial value of economic flows expected from an “all other things being equal” future, shatters when confronted with the dynamics of the changing contractual landscape.

In other words, the condition for the validity of the neoclassical hypothesis would lie in the theoretical acceptance and empirical observation of the non-existence of the influence of political deliberations on social representations and contractual practices. Of course, we reject this hypothesis and will on the contrary seek to qualify the ways and means of expression of this influence.

Chapter 6 will propose to analyze the system of representations associated with the contract through the three degrees of heritage identities of the territorial informative and cognitive assessment (connectivity, resilience and materiality). It will then propose to integrate this analysis into another, which will be carried out according to three directions of co-evolutionary differentiation of the representations associated with the territorial IS (micro/meso, intra-/interentity and monetary/non-monetary).

This approach will make it possible to qualify and measure an *acceleration value* conferred by the institutionalization of the territorial IS: the value of the social deliberative work that transforms the relationships between niches, regimes and landscapes of the sociotechnical system, and in this respect the potential for decoupling.

This will include determining how to compare not only social, ecological and economic performance, but also performance in terms of systemic interactions between these dimensions and therefore performance in terms of the *propagation* of representational capacities and the sociotechnical governance *process* proposed by Bosch et al. (2013) (see section 3.1.1.2).

## Development, Changing the Compass and the Map

### 6.1. Dynamic Modeling of Cost Systems

An economic evaluation compatible with an ambition for sustainable development must not stop at the work of the figure in an institutionalized framework at a given time and place. It must account for the evolution of institutional possibilities and how they intertwine with changes in social representations, which form the basis for micro-, meso- and macroeconomic evaluations and their relationships. Considering that the aggregate model of a single representation of the quality of an economy by GDP is too archaic, the aim is not only to produce a methodology for making “round trips” between these micro-, meso- and macro-levels of evaluation, but also to put them into a system.

The limit that we identify to a one-way processing of information goes beyond the problem of destroying information by the aggregative approaches mobilized by national accounts. This type of processing does not relate to the influence that activities considered alone have on the activity of an organized territorial unit that they are likely to constitute (a functional ecosystem at a mesoeconomic level for example). Indeed, even if this constraint was removed – which could be promoted by a better connectivity of the micro non-financial assessment and the ESA 2010 – being satisfied with a simple data feedback would be a fundamental error.

This approach would mean, on the one hand, that the methods used by information systems (ISs) practitioners to describe problems specific to one level of analysis have no influence on those used by other practitioners to describe problems specific to another level – in other words, that the territorial IS is totally *resilient.*

We would also have to assume that the data transmitted from one level of analysis to another necessarily always correspond to the essence of the information relevant to the analyses carried out at these two levels, i.e. they allow the actors at these different levels to code and interpret information to which they each attribute a high level of *materiality.*

We proposed to mobilize the logic of ecological economy systematized by *CARE®* accounting. It should be recalled that this invites us to consider that, while nature is *priceless*, collective territorial actions to preserve, maintain and renew natural capital have a *cost* whose recognition must lead to the depreciation of economic assets (Richard 2012). *CARE®* also applies this logic to human capital, while specifying that it can be applied to any other type of capital. Seizing this opportunity, we will choose to refer more broadly to social capital and also to consider sociotechnical governance capital, as well as a capital for the propagation of the representation capacities that we have just mentioned. The *dynamic modeling of cost systems* (DMCS) approach will thus involve the co-development of ecosystem, social, economic, governance and propagation assets.

We will set out two complementary modeling principles.

The first is that this logic of joint amortization of different types of capital should not only be considered at the microeconomic level. It can be systematically included in the revaluations carried out within the *ESA 2010* balance sheets. Such a correlation would make it possible to account – particularly through governance and propagation capital – for the indivisibility of the different working scales that drive the collective maintenance of the sustainability of territorial development. The design and evaluation of this maintenance must therefore be approached in a logic of distinction and conjunction between these two scales.

The second is that this joint logic of depreciation must take into account the state of the functional heritage described by the territorial informative and cognitive balance sheet.

### 6.1.1. Linking shared representations and activity costs

### 6.1.1.1. Transition function

The quality of communication between different levels of representation is a major determinant of the meeting of individual and collective interests when designing and implementing integrated product-service systems (PSS) in a whole that promotes the sustainability of a functional territorial ecosystem. At least that is what we suggested. The ambition of the DMCS approach is to propose principles for validating this hypothesis and measuring these qualities.

The aim is to describe a co-evolution between territorial sustainability considered as a collective performance, and the activity costs of the entities, and then to consider the driving role of the shared informative and cognitive heritage, through an analysis of the territorial IS – the state and institutionalization dynamics of this system can, in our opinion, be considered as indicators of the state and dynamics of this heritage.
