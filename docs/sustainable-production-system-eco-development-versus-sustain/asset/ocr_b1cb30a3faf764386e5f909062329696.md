In short, ecological economists refuse to subject the environment to neoclassical economic standards and reasoning. Instead, they propose to integrate the economy into the biosphere by respecting the cycles, and therefore the temporalities of the latter (Georgescu-Roegen 1971, 1979, 1995; Passet 1979, 1985). In this respect, the ecological economy converges with eco-development. However, it differs from it in its purpose – the economic sphere in its relations with the ecological sphere, not “development” – and in the fact that it does not always mention, expressly, a social purpose21.

Practicing the ecological economy does not mean excluding the social sphere: the notions of ecosystem service and natural capital are social representations, and the institutionalization of ex-ante ecosystem management systems is a social action. On the contrary, the ecological economy implies making the social reality the place for the recognition of emergence phenomena whose qualification forms the field of economics in the living world.
