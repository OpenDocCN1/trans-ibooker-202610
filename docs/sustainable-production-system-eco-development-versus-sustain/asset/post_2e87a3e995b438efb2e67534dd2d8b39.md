Pilot application region: Ile-de-France, due to the particular complexity of local issues related to an increasing trend in aggregate consumption², more strongly than elsewhere, due in particular to the implementation of Greater Paris.

Problematic: The risk of tension on aggregate resources is proven (DRIEE IDF 2012). The exploitation of the sands and gravels of the alluvial valleys of the Seine and Marne will be insufficient to meet demand. While interregional solidarity is part of the response to this risk³, better management at the level of the regional sector should also be considered. The transport of aggregates over long distances is limited, particularly in terms of profitability⁴ and environmental acceptability. In addition, exporting regions will show decreasing capacities in the medium term.

## Box 4.1. Context of the AGREGA project (Douguet et al. 2018)

Compared to other sectors, aggregate producers currently have no demand levers, they can only respond to them, without influencing market developments. This situation makes it difficult to anticipate future needs – which are highly dependent on the speed of development in the Ile-de-France region (Chamaret 2015).

Two scenarios for responding to increasing demand have been identified (DRIEE 2012). The first involves a total consumption of 37.9 Mt/year with a regional dependency ratio maintained at 45%. The second considers the same consumption, but considers additional contributions exclusively from external regions, which leads to an increase in the dependency ratio⁵.

The response to Ile-de-France’s demand for aggregates involves both an increase in production effort in terms of volume, an increase in the quality of aggregates (ready-mixed concrete for buildings) and an effort to maintain the current level of dependence on external regions. This contradictory injunction is a source of great technical and organizational complexity for end users (development managers in the Ile-de-France region) and for stakeholders in the sector.

In a circular economy approach, the process of improving the construction aggregates sector in Ile-de-France involves broadening the scope of its analysis in order to qualify the performance of this sector in terms of reducing material flow. However, broadening the scope of the performance area considered may also lead to the study of how the aggregates economy fits into the major cycles of the biosphere (carbon, water, etc.).

The reflection then focuses on the first of the three levels of decoupling, described at the end of Chapter 2.

It is possible to further expand the analysis of performance; this time focusing on the social and economic effects generated by this sector at different scales of territory. This involves collectively building the *meaning* of this industrial sector in terms of social and economic sustainability, beyond a factual description of its effects in terms of material flows and ecological sustainability.

The reflection then focuses on the second level of decoupling: the one that reflects the relationship between multifunctionalities produced within the sociotechnical system and impacts on the energy-material flow, and therefore on the ecosystem.

The contributing web portal *ePLANETe.Blue* (see section 3.2) was mobilized during the AGREGA project to represent the industrial sector and then to collectively construct the meaning that can be given to the effects of its circularization. The issues considered to describe the system and produce a deliberation matrix were identified by a socioeconomic study (Chamaret et al. 2015) conducted to initiate the AGREGA project. This study identified 11 sustainability issues for the Ile-de-France aggregates supply chain. Their understanding was then further developed during the project (Douguet et al. 2018).

### Limiting the contribution to climate change
- Energy consumption and GHG emissions for the extraction, transformation and especially the transport of aggregate resources (impact varies according to materials and modes of transport).
- Difficulties in accessing the resource leading to a progressive distance from production sites and therefore an increase in the distances to be covered.

### Protecting water resources
- The use of aggregates (especially alluvial deposits, the most popular) can have impacts: water consumption (washing and processing), risk of proximity to water catchment areas, etc.

### Meeting the demand for aggregates
- Ile-de-France consumes around 30 Mt of aggregates per year, mainly for concrete for housing and future transport infrastructure.
- Geographically heterogeneous demand, lack of visibility on future needs (some point to risks of tension around aggregate resources): lack of information that penalizes collective planning.

### Ensuring long-term economic profitability
- Need for visibility regarding the level of local recycling of deconstruction materials from the construction industry: to consider how to meet regional demand, the heavy land investments to be made by quarrying companies,⁶ etc.

### Preserving resources
- Aggregate reserves still high overall in France, but exhaustible resource.
- Observation of a scarcity of alluvial deposits, a high-quality material now reserved for noble uses such as construction (previously used for roads).
- Need for alternatives to the so-called “natural” or “virgin” resource, i.e. directly resulting from extraction (alternatives to which recycling could contribute).

### Maintaining jobs
- Careers provide jobs, most of which are low skilled and non-relocatable⁷.
- The increase in needs, the exhaustion of stocks and the high use of imports are weakening employment in the Paris region quarries.
- Recycling would slow this increase and also contribute to the creation of non-delocalizable employment.

### Ensuring the safety and quality of the structures
- Competition between quality/safety of construction and preservation of the aggregate resource.
- Strong anchoring of a preference for virgin (“natural”) aggregates even when it does not correspond to a technical, normative or regulatory requirement.
- R&D in the various trades (builders, concrete and cement manufacturers, quarries): ensure the same quality of work with fewer virgin materials and support the development of new techniques related to recycling.

### Delivering the resource where it is needed
- Aggregate is a heavy but inexpensive resource so the share of transport in its final price is significant: this price doubles every 50 km. Massive transport would improve profitability.
- Need to take into account the configuration of the Ile-de-France region and the transport problems (road traffic, difficulties in crossing the capital via the Seine) – the supply must be made from all sides⁸.
