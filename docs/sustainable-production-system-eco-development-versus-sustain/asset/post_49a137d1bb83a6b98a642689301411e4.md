
INTEGRAAL™ is then qualified not only as an approach, but as a meta-method – the meta prefix refers to a level of abstraction higher than that of direct apprehension of field study objects.

INTEGRAAL™ is divided into six steps:

- identify the problem (describe the problem, the territory, the project, the scales, the beneficiaries, the partners, etc.);
- structure the problem (define the categories of actors, performance issues and alternatives through an iterative process of documentation and appropriation of the problem studied, based in particular on consultation of the actors concerned);
- represent the system (identify and mobilize tools to represent the system within which the problem arises);
- evaluate and deliberate (evaluate alternatives from a multi-criteria, multi-stakeholder perspective);
- analyze and communicate (communication of study results and recommendations);
- question the results and start again (identify the remaining problem, and start the cycle again).

## 6.2.2.2. Counting in a moving frame

To conclude this last chapter, we propose to apply INTEGRAAL™ to the situation of interest to us: territorial economic Communication with a view to sustainable development.

### 6.2.2.2.1. Step 1: identifying the problem to be described helps to avoid misunderstandings about the objective, scope and expectations of each person

Our problem is that no economic model, neither capitalism nor any other, is or can be intrinsically compatible with ecological limits, nor with those of social groups. For Box (1979), “all models are false, but some are useful”.

In a perspective of sustainable development, as in others, economic reality exists for the modeler only through the action by which he grasps information from the territorial processes of social objectification of values. The degree of utility of the false model produced by the economist will depend on how the transcription of the system-object he/she understands makes it possible to facilitate a dialogue with stakeholders. Questions of precision and exhaustiveness are secondary, and the performance of a model depends on the feedback it provides on the way its user represents reality.

Laville (1998), for example, considers that the market economy “is attractive because of the simplicity of the regulatory methods that characterize it, which has enabled it to gain an increasingly prominent place”. Let us broaden this discussion a little. If the French sustainable development strategy was based on the financing of “responsible enterprise” (see introduction), instead of also targeting the strengthening of relations between the public economy and the popular economy; and before that, if the capture of eco-development was carried out in favor of the “container” concept of sustainable development (see section 1.1.1), is it because the “successful” models match the sustainability issues?

Let us then specify our problem: a model does not have a fixed utility and value, but rather one related to the social system likely to mobilize it, thus depending on a given, detailed and evolving territorial informative and cognitive heritage.

### 6.2.2.2.2. Step 2: structuring the problem by describing three components of the associated system (categories of actors, issues and situations to be assessed)

Refining the identification of the problem in this way leads us to consider that its structuring implies addressing “the question of the validity and relevance of scientific knowledge to address complex problems” (van der Sluijs et al. 2008a, 2008b). The objective that will govern this structuring will then be to support the actors through a set of reflections and formalizations, which can be qualified as “quality control of knowledge” (ibid.).

The problem we have described involves practitioners with diverse rationalities who each express multiple and specific issues. However, it is fairly easy to identify three categories – practitioners of political economy, market economy and financial economy – that we will distinguish, by forcing the caricature a little, by the evaluation practices that characterize them.

Putting these three categories of practitioners in the spotlight when assessing the quality of the knowledge mobilized in a territorial Communication for sustainable development refers the notion of socioeconomic negentropy.

This negentropy is the only issue we consider here: the strength of cohesion between the three levels of DMCS decoupling, i.e. the ability to anchor financial work in a social expression of the conditions of territorial sustainability, without deteriorating the actors’ capacity for expression, and the readability of this expression.

In other words, the issue of socioeconomic negentropy to which we will refer concerns the ability to carry out jointly but separately the valuation of common assets and the valuation of assets derived from private property products. The term valuation is derived here from a pragmatic logic, with particular reference to valuation theory, which marks “a clear distinction between prizing and appraisal, between ends and means” (Dewey 1939).

By mobilizing it, we illustrate how to respond to the issue of socioeconomic negentropy implies precautions when mobilizing couples [indicator € – meta-information] to describe prices, costs and values. And we thus come to describe the third axis of structuring our problem, after the actors and the issues, that of the situations to be analyzed.

To try to represent things in a simple way, we will consider the same situation but subject to two analytical constraints.

The situation in question is that of a cooperative transaction giving rise to a market economy contract (e.g. a multifunctional PSS co-production). And we will consider its embedding in a set of contradictory injunctions: on the one hand, the multi-criteria analysis based on the expression of value judgments necessary for the choices that determine costs. On the other hand, the actuarial perspectives that combine with market price dynamics to form the basis for financial projections.

To summarize the structuring of the problem at hand, three categories of actors (political, market and financial) are led together to articulate – and even combine – two evaluation scenarios (one rather sociofunctional, and the
other technicofinancial) in order to sanction a territorial contract of negentropic economic Communication.
