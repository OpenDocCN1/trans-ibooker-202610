As well as redefining it, it is the recharacterization of the place of the notion of wealth that should be taken as a risk. Producing the analytical framework that will make it possible to approach the political economy by comparing ethical and market practices involves clarifying the relationships between “wealth”, economic “value” and social “values”.

*Wealth* can be considered as a whole corresponding to what the good is at the *universal level* of a given social group. In a sustainable political economy of development, we believe that the notion of wealth must correspond to a situation of sustainable co-evolution between ecological and social systems – the latter being considered in the broad sense, i.e. by including economic communities. And the collective formulation of what this wealth is induces an intersubjective framework for representing what is important and what is crucial, i.e. a capacity whose improvement can be associated with *progress*. The latter term here is understood in the sense of a progression toward sustainable freedom from constraints linked to a set of critical thresholds associated with ecological and socioeconomic systems.

*Value* is a social representation, a composite artifact decision support for action that is structured at the *level of the particular*, i.e. a subset of the social group that has represented wealth at the level of the universal system. Value is an intersubjective consensus resulting from (value) judgments about how an activity or phenomenon promotes access to wealth, i.e. contributes to the joint removal of critical thresholds associated with ecological and socioeconomic systems.

Value is thus the result of a non-aggregate and negotiated integration of different components (of use, non-use and exchange), which takes place when a phenomenon collectively perceived as productive is qualified as it allows access paths to wealth. A productive phenomenon therefore does not produce value, and it has value in that it is considered productive within a group, which leads to assuming and claiming the character of social, spatial and temporal relativity of economic value.

*Plural social value* systems, rooted in people’s cognition, i.e. at the *level of the individual* of the social group that represented wealth, are mobilized when making the judgments that come into play during negotiations on the value of productive phenomena. These systems can be reorganized by these negotiations, whose processes and terms influence – and are influenced by – collective representations of wealth at the universal level.
