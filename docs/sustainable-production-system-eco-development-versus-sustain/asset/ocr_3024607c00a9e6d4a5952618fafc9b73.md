Based on an approach proposed by Parker and Benson (1988), Bounfour and Epinette (2006, p. 27) suggest assessing the (financial) *value* of a new IS.

*Value of a new information system =*

*Traditional advantages – traditional costs + link value + acceleration value + restructuring value + innovation value.*

Their method consists of extending a *discounted cost–benefit* analysis to “*value linking* or acceleration values, resulting for example from benefits arising from the combination of resources between activities or functions; restructuring values, associated for example with changes in organizational modes in a department, department or function; innovation values, resulting from changes in procedures and methods of conducting activities, following the introduction of the new system” (Bounfour and Epinette 2006, p. 27).

What is this *value linking*? And to what would we associate these values of connection, restructuring, innovation and acceleration, if we were to base the expression on the DMCS approach? It is certainly in the *territorial proximities* of Colletis *et al.* (2005).

In our opinion, the *link value* expresses the measurement of a state of *institutional proximity* conferred by a new IS – whether it is an intra- or interorganizational territory. It is considered in the *connectivity* dimension – one of the dimensions of the analytical space within which the *adaptive cycle* that we have mobilized to describe a *cooperative transaction* evolves.

The *restructuring value* would express the measure of the renewal of the relationship to *organizational proximity* that results, within this new IS, from the adaptation of indicator and meta-information repositories following a cooperative transaction. It is to be expressed in the *resilience* dimension of the adaptive cycle space.

The *value of innovation* is considered in the third of the dimensions of the evolutionary space that we have associated with the adaptive cycle – the analytical dimension of the relationship between the *materiality* of information. This value is related to an IS performance in terms of transformation of the capacity to interpret the territorial anchorage of *homogeneous production units* with regard to contributions, non-market uses and contractualized services in which the entities they represent are involved.
