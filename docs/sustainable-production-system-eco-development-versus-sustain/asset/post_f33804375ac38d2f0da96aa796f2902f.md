There is room for maneuver for socioeconomic appropriation of national accounting practices. It is all about representations, the propagation of concepts, the capture of these concepts, power games and therefore politics.

The map to which we refer has the particularity of being dynamic, made up of both social expression and digital networks, that of an information system in perpetual evolution. For Maurice and Reineix (2011), “the dynamic aspect of complexity imposes the idea of a topology that is not rigid, but can evolve, transform itself in time and space. In this drawing, networks appear as simple elementary physical supports whose arrangement can lead to a more complex and evolving hierarchical system. The concepts of emergence, of information transmission with an abstraction dimension not present in networks, have led to the idea of encapsulating these networks in a space that better represents this layer of information as a junction”.

This layer of information is not only shared between the actors, it does not only ensure the junction between them for the dissemination of *circulating information*, nor the only support for the emergence of *organizing events* in the sense of Morin. It is *Communication* in the sense of Luhmann (1978), for whom “it is not humanity, but a system of communication that selectively integrates the physical, chemical, organic and psychological potential of humanity and which, in the perspective of this selectivity, has its own reality and autonomy as a system”.

How to navigate then? The ultimate characteristic of a system being linked to the fact that it is constantly structured and reconstructed, following a differentiation from an environment that is specific to it (Bertalanffy 1968; Luhmann 1984; Le Moigne 1977), we could approach the institutionalization of the information system as an autopoiesis, which would lead us to consider a network of processes for producing *mediating artifacts* that is carried out by adaptive cycles in the topological space of territorial controversy maps. And we would choose as the fundamental invariant of this network the *pattern of organization* of territorial representations, whose simplified understanding leads us to distinguish three axes of “evolutionary differentiation” that would be the micro/meso representations, intra/inter entity and monetary/non-monetary representations that the actors make of the system.

The territorial information system could then be considered as the vector of interpenetration by which:
- a local functional ecosystem would perceive the autopoiesis of the territorial functional ecosystem as part of its own system and thus consider territorial dynamics as determinants of its own performance;
- a territorial functional ecosystem would perceive the co-evolution of local functional ecosystems – each with autopoietic behavior – and then adapt the territorial valuation process accordingly;
- a monetary representation system of the valuation process would perceive a non-monetary representation system as part of its own system (microaccounting / multi-dimension analytical balance sheet; core meso accounts / satellite accounts );
- a non-monetary representation system would perceive a monetary representation system as part of its own system (need to finance the maintenance/renewal of support functions).

The institutionalization of the territorial information system would thus refer to one of the characteristics of autopoiesis: self-reference, which from a sociological point of view is for Luhmann (1981) “a result of the evolutionary differentiation of systems”.

However, we will refuse to retain from the concept of autopoiesis the first cognitivist formulations that refer to the vision of machinery that would ensure the identical reproduction of its own components and their only in what they are at a given moment. It would be the same mistake to consider a functional economy in which the service would exist without the functional heritage, i.e. forgetting the history of the relationships between components of the system.

This approach would indeed make it difficult to approach the territorial heritage, which we have certainly qualified as informative and cognitive, but also functional and to which we have often associated the proposal of Colletis and Pecqueur (2004) inviting us to integrate, beyond the links and with them, the time dimension, i.e. the relationship and its historicity.

There is something more in the word *relationship* than in the word *link*, something more than temporary and compartmentalized utilitarian commitments. The relationship invites us to think of both cohesion and constraint; it invites us to approach the notion of limit no longer as a border but as a field of attractions and fragilities, no longer as a limit *state* but as a *regime* of functional dependence.

If *socioeconomic negentropy* then describes the *state* of cohesion of social, ecological and economic subsystems, even measuring the state of an “alter GDP” system anchoring growth in a pool of complementary indicators would only provide a picture, a snapshot, and therefore would not be sufficient to assess sustainability.

Montévil and Matteo (2015) reinterpret autopoiesis by emphasizing the interdependence between the parts of an organism and the historical nature of this interdependence. Historicity is then a causal regime just as structuring as the processes of renewal of the components of these parts. These ideas refer to the concept of *anti-entropy* structured by Longo and Montévil (2012, 2014).

Considering the closure of a system by the historicity of a functional dependency allows a step aside. It should be recalled that the monetary indicator is part of the *DMCS* approach in a very specific function: that of a tool for monitoring the quality of economic information. It is used to judge the coherence of the meta-information systems associated with it at different *monetarization localities*; to identify whether one of them is captured by the attraction of one of the *financialization singularities* that distort territorial space and time by dispelling both meta-information and the ability to represent it; and to produce a mapping of socioeconomic negentropy – a new layer to be built on the mapping of controversies when it comes to investing in cooperation mechanisms for sustainability.

The monetary indicator is not the “output product” of the *DMCS* assessment. It is one of the inputs. This upheaval is not “Copernican”. The *DMCS*’s output product is information on individual and collective representations of the sustainability of the territory. It tools the re-embedding of the economic into the social and democratic relationship, i.e. the political economy.

The product of the *DMCS* evaluation should rather be compared to the expectations of approaches such as the Contributory Economy – which gives rise to the *Contributory Learning Territory* experiment conducted in Seine Saint-Denis by the *EPA Plaine-Commune* and the *Research and Innovation Institute* (*Institut de recherche et d’innovation – IRI*) of the Centre Pompidou.

This Institute is chaired by Bernard Stiegler, for whom “wealth is knowledge, such as it makes it possible to generate bifurcations that enrich reality in a way that is irreducible to simple deterministic or probabilistic calculations – and this is what gives value, based on the consideration and concretization of what Aristotle called τιμιώτατα (‘honesty’) which designate what, in noetic life, has no price: which is the most precious, and which has no price because it is what constitutes the standards from which prices can be evaluated” (Stiegler 2018).
