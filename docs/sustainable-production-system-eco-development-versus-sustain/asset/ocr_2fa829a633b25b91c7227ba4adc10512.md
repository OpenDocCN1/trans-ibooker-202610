This study identified 11 sustainability issues for the Ile-de-France aggregates supply chain. Their understanding was then further developed during the project (Douguet et al. 2018).

# Limiting the contribution to climate change

+   - Energy consumption and GHG emissions for the extraction, transformation and especially the transport of aggregate resources (impact varies according to materials and modes of transport).
+   - Difficulties in accessing the resource leading to a progressive distance from production sites and therefore an increase in the distances to be covered.

# Protecting water resources

+   - The use of aggregates (especially alluvial deposits, the most popular) can have impacts: water consumption (washing and processing), risk of proximity to water catchment areas, etc.

# Meeting the demand for aggregates

+   - Ile-de-France consumes around 30 Mt of aggregates per year, mainly for concrete for housing and future transport infrastructure.
+   - Geographically heterogeneous demand, lack of visibility on future needs (some point to risks of tension around aggregate resources): lack of information that penalizes collective planning.

# Ensuring long-term economic profitability

+   - Need for visibility regarding the level of local recycling of deconstruction materials from the construction industry: to consider how to meet regional demand, the heavy land investments to be made by quarrying companies,⁶ etc.

# Preserving resources

+   - Aggregate reserves still high overall in France, but exhaustible resource.
+   - Observation of a scarcity of alluvial deposits, a high-quality material now reserved for noble uses such as construction (previously used for roads).
