### 6.1.2.2.1. Incorporation of implementation work

By describing a new institutionalization of the territorial IS, the DMCS proposes a system for incorporating the implementation work.

The combination of the adaptation of the Comptabilité Universelle® (Universal Accounting)-type state balance sheet and a reform of the general accounts that would follow the CARE® logic would indeed make it possible to modulate the financial valuation of entities for the valuation of ecosystem, social, economic, governance and propagation assets and liabilities.

Replicating this approach at the level of the ESA 2010 accounts, based on a modeling of the joint productions within the functional ecosystem account, would lead this same integration process at the mesoeconomic level while promoting a dialogue with the macroeconomic level.

This incorporation of the individual and collective components of work makes it possible to ensure the joint and differentiated reading of the specific and shared facets of use value, which can and must, in our opinion, be correlated by an integrated micro–meso co-amortization.

### 6.1.2.2.2. Incorporation of representation work

This proposal for a territorial IS also organizes – and this is the core of our research – a system for incorporating representation work. The informative and cognitive balance, applied to communications between entities, PSS and functional ecosystem governance, makes it possible to determine the level of informative and cognitive heritage incorporated into the territorial IS (see section 5.2.2).

We will thus consider that this incorporation of the work of representation:
- is carried out according to the level of informative and cognitive heritage, which determines the modulation of the depreciation of shared use values recorded as assets in the territorial functional ecosystem account;
- is calibrated jointly for each entity involved in a PSS, by depreciating each asset line of the CARE® model associated with it (depreciation always based on the territorial informative and cognitive balance sheet, and with the same level of modulation for each asset line).

This approach can be interpreted as an application of the precautionary principle described in Chapter 1 (see section 1.1.1.2). Indeed, if the ISs shared by the actors of a territory do not allow them collective descriptions of sufficient quality to reflect both sustainability issues and microeconomic strategies likely to meet them, then individual activities and maintenance processes will be more costly.

However, a technical difficulty, inherent in the modeling of joint productions, appears when it comes to distributing the quantities of work among the outputs of the same process. For Dumesnil and Levy (1982), the characteristic of this type of model is that work “is inseparably incorporated into these various products in the same productive act”.

Are we going to depreciate the assets of all the entities in the territory uniformly according to the collective capacities of representation? Or is it possible to determine depreciation levels specific to one or the other of the PSSs?

Let us return to the case study of the aggregates sector. The KerBabel™ for You (K4U) evaluation algorithm presented in Figure 6.8 reflects a situation in which one “PSS aggregate production” has lower propagation performance of the representation capacities than the other PSS. It can then be assumed that the CARE® depreciation applicable to entities of this PSS is greater than that applicable to PSSs descriptive of other phases of the aggregate lifecycle.

## 6.2. Social value of acceleration

According to the proposals introduced in the previous section, the *incorporation of implementation work* at the meso–macro territorial level, which gives rise to a unit of shared use value having meaning at this territorial level, can only be compared with a monetary representation constructed relative to this level. The unique depreciation rate resulting from the *incorporation of representation work* gives rise to the construction of a monetary representation specific to each of the different micro- and mesoeconomic evaluation localities, which can be adjusted according to governance and propagation performance.

The DMCS is therefore an approach of micro–meso integration of the construction of the meaning of monetary representation. Its purpose is to reduce the gap between the composition and interpretation of information, thus reducing the dissipation of this information – a dissipation inherent in territorial economic communication.

As paradoxical as it may seem, a production cost and a market price are not commensurable, or at least they are only commensurable in Luhmann’s *communication* position (the Mitteilung). As soon as one is in the position of *information*, or in the position of *waiting for success*, a couple [*territorialized utility – indicator €*] is no longer fungible with a couple [*financialized utility – indicator €*]. On the other hand, it may remain fungible with another couple [*territorialized utility – indicator €*] in the context of a deliberation, a cooperative transaction, a contract, or any other social activity likely to maintain the link between the three positions of territorial *Communication*. A new couple [*territorialized utility – indicator €*] is then born, and its expression does not imply erasing the couples that gave birth to it.

One of the objectives of our approach is to ensure that the constructions of these two types of monetary representations – costs and prices – cannot only coexist in parallel realities – *monetarization localities* and *financialization singularities* – but meet without destroying each other.

Joël de Rosnay (1975) defined the concept of a *system* as “a set of elements in dynamic interaction, organized according to a goal”. The process of assessing the sustainability of development has the characteristics of a system, organized for the purpose of scientific investigation. It mobilizes “elements” – subsystems – of individual and collective representations organized in dynamic interactions around and by methods and tools for analyzing ecological, social and economic *realities*. These three realities are subsystems of the purpose of the overall sustainability analysis system. They are in co-evolution within the same level of realities: that of more or less consensual representations and interpretations of these phenomena, whose trans-subjective realities will always remain beyond the analyst’s reach.

The economy of sustainable development is therefore an economy of the practice of living knowledge. This pertains not only to the knowledge of living things, which as a tool, particularly the IS, allows us to come as close as possible to trans-subjective realities. We distinguish between tools and practice because the intrinsic relevance of an evaluation system is to be distinguished from the quality of its mobilization, and therefore its appropriation by the actors. For this reason, we strongly support our research with socioeconomic analyses.

We cannot jointly address different intersubjective realities. But among the shared knowledge heritage that would make it possible to renew the practice of economic evaluation is a concept that is both nomadic and unifying: *entropy*.

The concept of entropy was introduced in the field of thermodynamic physics (Carnot 1824; Clausius 1850) and adopted by biology, information sciences, cybernetics and others.

In its applications to systems science, this concept provides a better understanding of the dissipation of economic information. It will make it possible to argue in favor of using the DMCS approach to establish interfaces between *monetarization localities and financialization singularities*.

If we are particularly interested in the economics of sustainable development, entropy is a bridging concept between ecological, social and economic realities. This idea of a bridge means that entropy must not be applied identically in either of these realities, but rather serve as a guiding principle for a coherent and integrated path of recognition of what is not sustainable.
