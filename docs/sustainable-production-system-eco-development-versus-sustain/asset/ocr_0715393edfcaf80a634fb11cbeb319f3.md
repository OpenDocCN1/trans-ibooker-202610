This is another way of saying what Marie-Andrée Caron (2012) evokes. “A new logic is emerging: beyond the rigor of the figure, it is the obtaining of a figure to be discussed that is important in a dialogical space made up of the parties concerned by the social costs generated by the company’s activities. Accounting thus acts more as a mechanism to integrate social debates into the company’s current practices, and gradually leaves the role of a social order mechanism to which it has hitherto been largely confined.”

While methods for improving accounting communication are emerging at the micro and macro levels, the way in which they are deployed does not make it possible to overcome the dualism between the niche and the sociotechnical landscape. Indeed, CARE® and Comptabilité Universelle® are opening up to the territory, but in an approach to the valuation of immaterial capital that is admittedly renewed and more sustainable but, at best, “inter-micro”. The ESA 2010 satellite accounts on the other hand, as currently envisaged, feed a strictly macro, even regional, intersectoral and functional approach.

The question we are now asking is quite simple: how do we organize and structure an information system that can feed a real Communication between the micro and the macro?

The aggregation of added value, if not accompanied by a system allowing the repositioning of information through cognition, constitutes the paroxysm of the destruction of economic information. And public statistics, if they are based on an aggregated information processing model, cannot structurally compensate for this gap, linked to the transition between micro- and macroeconomies.

Giraud (2015) describes this problem in these terms: “The second aspect³⁵ with which I will end this catalogue of the mistakes of contemporary ‘economic science’ is perhaps the least easy to understand at first sight. This is the possibility of emergence phenomena, as understood, for example, by statistical physics. If you aggregate the behavior of several million individuals, do you have any reason to believe that what will come out of it will look like the behavior of a meta-individual? Of course not. This is, moreover, what we observe empirically.”
