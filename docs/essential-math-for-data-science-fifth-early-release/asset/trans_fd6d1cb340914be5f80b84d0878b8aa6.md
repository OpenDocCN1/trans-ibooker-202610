

## 什么是影子 IT？

Shadow IT (or Shadow “Information Technology”) is a term describing office workers who create systems outside their IT department. These systems can include databases, scripts, and processes, as well as vendor and employee-made software without the IT department’s involvement.

影子 IT（或影子“信息技术”）是一个术语，用来描述在 IT 部门之外创建系统的办公室工作人员。这些系统可以包括数据库、脚本和流程，以及供应商和员工制作的未经 IT 部门参与的软件。

Shadow IT used to be frowned on in organizations, as it is unregulated and operating outside the IT department’s scope of control. When finance, marketing, and other non-IT departments decide to hand-spin their own rogue IT operations, it certainly can create hidden costs to the organization in inefficiency and security concerns. Nasty politics can ensue where IT departments and non-IT departments clash, accusing each other of not staying in their lane or simply co-opting roles for job security.

在组织中，影子 IT 过去常常不被看好，因为它缺乏监管，且在 IT 部门的控制范围之外运行。当金融、营销及其他非 IT 部门决定自行搞起“流氓 IT”操作时，这肯定会给组织带来低效率和安全隐患等隐形成本。当 IT 部门和非 IT 部门发生冲突时，会引发肮脏的政治斗争，双方互相指责对方不守本分，或者单纯为了保住工作而揽职。

However, one benefit of the data science movement is it has made shadow IT more accepted as a necessity for innovation. Non-IT folks can prototype and experiment with creating datasets, Python scripts, and regression tools. The IT department can then pick up these innovations and support them formally as they mature. This also allows the business to become more nimble. A business rule change can be a quick edit to a Python script or in-house database, rather than filing a ticket with the IT help desk. Whether that change should be subject to rigorous testing and red tape is certainly a cost trade-off for responsiveness.

不过，数据科学运动的一项好处是，它使得影子 IT 作为创新的必需品被更广泛接受。非 IT 人员可以在创建数据集、Python 脚本和回归工具方面进行原型设计和实验。随后，IT 部门可以将这些创新拾起来，在其成熟后提供正式支持。这也让企业变得更加敏捷。业务规则变更可以是对 Python 脚本或内部数据库的快速编辑，而不是向 IT 帮助台提交工单。该变更是否应经过严格测试和繁文缛节，这肯定是响应速度与成本之间的权衡。

Overall, if you find yourself in a Shadow IT role (which is likely) make sure you understand the risks and play nice with the IT department. If you are successful, it can be empowering and rewarding to support your business this way. If you sense possible conflict with your IT department, be upfront with your leadership and let them know. If you can truthfully say your work is “prototyping” and “exploration” then your leadership can argue it is outside the IT purview. Never go rogue without your leadership’s support though, and let them deal with the interdepartmental politics.

总的来说，如果你发现自己身处影子 IT 角色（很可能如此），请务必了解风险，并与 IT 部门友好相处。如果你成功了，以这种方式支持业务会令人感到自信且有成就感。如果你感觉到与 IT 部门可能存在冲突，要向领导坦诚并告知他们。如果你能诚实地说自己的工作是“原型设计”和“探索”，那么你的领导可以主张这不在 IT 部门的职权范围内。不过，在没有领导支持的情况下绝不能独断专行，让他们来处理跨部门政治。

## Does Your Dream Job Not Exist?

## 你的梦想工作不存在吗？

While you can always walk away when a role, always be sure to assess how realistic your expectations are too. Are the cutting-edge technologies you are pursuing perhaps too cutting-edge?

虽然你随时可以离开某个职位，但也务必评估一下你的期望有多现实。你追求的前沿技术，也许太过“前沿”了吗？

Take natural language processing for example. You want to build chatbots using deep learning. However, the number of actual jobs that do this are scarce, as most companies do not have practical need for chatbots. Why? Chatbots are simply not there yet. While companies like OpenAI have interesting research like GPT-3, that is mostly what it amounts to: interesting research. GPT-3 in the end is a probability-based pattern recognizer chaining words together, and therefore it has no common sense. There is research demonstrating this, including some by Gary Marcus at New York University.

以自然语言处理为例。你想用深度学习构建聊天机器人。然而，实际从事这项工作的职位寥寥无几，因为大多数公司并没有聊天机器人的实际需求。为什么？聊天机器人尚未成熟。虽然 OpenAI 等公司有 GPT-3 等有趣的研究，但这大多不过是有趣的研究罢了。归根结底，GPT-3 只是一个基于概率的模式识别器，将单词串联在一起，因此它没有常识。有研究证明了这一点，包括纽约大学 Gary Marcus 的一些研究。

https://cs.nyu.edu/~davise/papers/GPT3CompleteTests.html

This means creating chatbots with broader applications is an open problem, and has yet to be a value proposition for a great majority of businesses. If natural language processing is something you really want to pursue, and you are finding a disconnect with career opportunities, your best bet might be to go into the academic world and do research. While there are companies like Alphabet that do academic-like research, many of those employees came from academia.

这意味着创建具有更广泛应用的聊天机器人是一个开放问题，对于绝大多数企业来说，尚未成为价值主张。如果你真正想追求自然语言处理，却发现与职业机会存在脱节，那么你的最佳选择可能是进入学术界进行研究。虽然 Alphabet 等公司也进行类似学术的研究，但这些员工中的许多人来自学术界。

So again, keep your expectations realistic as you navigate the job market. If your expectations exceed what the job market can provide, strongly consider the academic route. You also should consider this route when the type of jobs you want frequently requires a PhD or specific academic credentials, and this becomes a barrier to doing your dream job.

因此，在求职过程中，请保持期望现实。如果你的期望超出了就业市场所能提供的，请强烈考虑学术路线。当你想要的工作类型经常要求博士学位或特定的学术资格，而这成为你从事梦想工作的障碍时，也应考虑这条路线。

## Where Do I Go Now?

## 我该何去何从？

Now that we covered the data science landscape, how to gain an edge with further skill development, and career issues to watch out for, where do we go from here? And what is the future of data science?

既然我们已经涵盖了数据科学版图、如何通过进一步的技能发展获得优势，以及需要留意的职业问题，那么接下来我们该去哪里？数据科学的未来又是什么？

First consider the burdens of having a data scientist job title. It carries an implicit requirement to have knowledge without boundaries, mostly due to the lack of standardized definition with a restricted scope. If we learned anything from the last 10 years watching the data science movement, definition matters. A data scientist is evolving to be a software engineer with proficiency in statistics, optimization, and machine learning. A data scientist may not even have that “data scientist” title anymore. While this is a much broader umbrella of requirements than when the data scientist was declared the “sexiest job of the 21st century” it is becoming necessary to have these skills.

首先，考虑拥有数据科学家职位名称所带来的负担。它隐含地要求具备无边界的知识，这主要是因为缺乏范围受限的标准化定义。如果我们在过去10年中从观察数据科学运动学到了什么，那就是定义很重要。数据科学家正在演变为精通统计学、优化和机器学习的软件工程师。数据科学家甚至可能不再拥有“数据科学家”这个头衔了。虽然与数据科学家被命名为“21世纪最性感的工作”时相比，这是一个范围更广泛的要求伞状结构，但掌握这些技能正在变得必要。

Another option is to specialize in favor of more focused titles, and this has been happening increasingly for the past few years. Roles like computer vision engineer, data engineer, data analyst, researcher, operations research analyst, and advisor/consultant are making a comeback. We are seeing less of the “data scientist” role and this trend is likely to continue in the next 10 years, primarily due to role specialization. It is certainly an option to follow this trend.

另一种选择是朝着更专注的职称进行专业化，过去几年这种趋势越来越明显。计算机视觉工程师、数据工程师、数据分析师、研究员、运筹分析师以及顾问/咨询师等角色正在卷土重来。我们看到的“数据科学家”角色越来越少，而且这一趋势在未来10年很可能会继续，主要是因为角色专业化。遵循这一趋势当然是一个选择。

It is critical to note that the labor market has changed drastically, and this is why you need the competitive edges listed in this chapter. While data scientists were seen as unicorns in 2014 commanding 6-figure salaries, nowadays a given “data scientist” job at any company can easily receive hundreds or thousands of applications while only offering a 5-figure salary. Data science degrees and boot camps have created an enormous boom of supply in data science professionals. Therefore it is competitive landing jobs marketed as “data scientist” or “data science” in general. This is why going after roles like “analyst”, “operations research”, and “software developer” is not necessarily a bad idea! Vicki Boykis, a machine learning engineer at Tumblr, probably says it best on her blog article Data Science is Different Now:

必须指出，劳动力市场已经发生了巨大变化，这就是为什么你需要本章列出的竞争优势。2014年，数据科学家被视为独角兽，拿着六位数的薪水；如今，任何公司的一个“数据科学家”职位都可能收到数百甚至数千份申请，却只提供五位数的薪水。数据科学学位和训练营在数据科学专业人才供应方面制造了巨大的繁荣。因此，找到标榜为“数据科学家”或一般“数据科学”的工作很有竞争力。这就是为什么去追求“分析师”、“运筹”和“软件开发人员”等职位并不一定是个坏主意！Tumblr 的机器学习工程师 Vicki Boykis 在她的博客文章《Data Science is Different Now》中或许说得最好：

> Remember that the ultimate goal... is to beat the hordes doing data science degrees, bootcamps, and working through tutorials.
>
> You want to get your foot in the door, **get a data-adjacent position**, and move towards whatever your dream job is, while finding out as much as you can about the tech industry in general.
>
> Don't get paralysis by analysis. Pick a small piece of something and start there. Do something small. Learn something small, build something small. Tell other people. Remember that your first job in data science will probably not be as a data scientist.
>
> —Vicki Boykis

## Conclusion

## 结论

This was a different chapter from the rest in this book, but it is important if you want to navigate the data science job landscape and effectively apply the knowledge in this book. It might be disconcerting to learn statistical tools and machine learning, only to find much of the job landscape will pivot you into other work. When this happens, take the opportunity to continue learning and gaining skills. When you integrate your essential math knowledge with a programming and software engineering proficiency, your value will increase tenfold just from having an understanding of the IT and data science gap.

本章与本书其他章节不同，但如果你想在数据科学就业版图中导航，并有效应用本书中的知识，它就很重要。学习统计工具和机器学习，却发现大部分就业版图会将你引向其他工作，这可能会让人不安。当这种情况发生时，抓住机会继续学习和获得技能。当你将基本的数学知识与编程和软件工程熟练度相结合时，仅仅了解 IT 和数据科学之间的差距，你的价值就会增加十倍。

Remember to eschew the hype in favor of practical solutions, and do not get so caught up in the technical purview that you become blindsided by market forces. Understand management and leadership motivations, as well as the motivation of people in general. Understand *why* things work, not just *how* they work. Be curious *why* a technique or tool solves a problem, not just *how* the technical aspects of it operate.

请记住，要摒弃炒作，转而采用实际解决方案，不要过于沉迷于技术范畴以至于被市场力量蒙蔽。要了解管理层和领导的动机，以及人们普遍的动机。要理解事情*为什么*有效，而不仅仅是*如何*有效。要好奇一种技术或工具*为什么*能解决问题，而不仅仅是*它的技术方面如何运作*。

Learn not for the sake of it, but to develop capabilities and pair the right tool to the right problem. One of the most effective ways to learn is to pick a problem (not a tool!) you find interesting. Pulling that thread leads to another thing to be curious about, and then another, and then another. You have a goal in mind, so continue going down the right rabbit holes and know when to pull yourself out of others. It is absolutely rewarding to take this approach, and it is amazing how much expertise you can gain in a short amount of time.

学习不是为了学习而学习，而是为了发展能力，并将合适的工具与合适的问题配对。最有效的学习方法之一是选择一个你感兴趣的问题（不是工具！）。沿着这个线索追寻下去，会引出另一个值得好奇的事物，然后是另一个，再另一个。你心中有一个目标，所以继续钻研正确的“兔子洞”，并知道何时从其他“兔子洞”中抽身出来。采取这种方法绝对很有收获，你会惊讶于在短时间内能获得多少专业知识。
