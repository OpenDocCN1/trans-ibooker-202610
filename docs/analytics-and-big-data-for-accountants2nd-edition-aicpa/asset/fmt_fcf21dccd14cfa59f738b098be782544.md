
The purpose is not to overwhelm but to review a myriad of examples to heighten the reader's awareness of the many different applications of big data and to trigger thoughts about how big data may be applied in your company.

## Examples of big data

### Dominos Pizza^([4](#c07.xhtml#c07-fn-04))

Data are captured from text messages, Twitter, Pebble, Android, Amazon Echo, and others and populated into the Dominos Information Management Framework. In addition, it's combined with data from third-party sources (USPS, geocode information, demographic data, and competitor data) to create in-depth customer segmentation (85,000 structured and unstructured data sources are entered daily).

Dominos processes between 55% and 58% of orders online. Telephone or in-person orders make up the remainder.

Dominos can assess individual buying patterns, multiple consumers in the same household, the dominant buyer, reliance on coupons, and the channel that is used to access Dominos. This allows Dominos to customize its coupons and product offers based on the customers' profiles.

### Criminals and genealogy

Law enforcement agencies have been confronted with many unsolved cases involving heinous crimes. Unfortunately, some of these cases have gone unsolved for decades. Within recent years, however, the genealogy a new crime fighting tool has emerged — the genealogy database. Never designed to be a resource to find criminals, the databases have become just that. The databases themselves first did not want to be known for this purpose as privacy and ethical uses were very important to them. In some cases, the police would load a false profile with DNA from a crime and find a connection. Not to the criminal but to a relative of the criminal. The enforcement agency would then trace information on the family tree and see if any individuals may have been in the area when the crime was committed. This is the current case for "The Golden State Killer" whose trial was scheduled to take place in early 2020.

### Hockey and big data

Automated data tracking technology is part of the 2019-20 season. Pucks and players' shoulder pads will contain tracking chips. Sensors will be placed around the rink. Every moment big data will be collected for position, speed, direction, distance, and other key metrics.

The sensor chips will deliver data points 200 times per second for five skaters and a goalie per team. For a 60-minute game this will create roughly 9,360,000 logged events per category in a game^([5](#c07.xhtml#c07-fn-05)).

### John Deere

John Deere is one of the pioneers in the big data industry. It has developed a system within the internet of things framework where data from farmer's machines can interact with specialists in their facilities to predict when machines will fail. Deere then sends out the necessary repair parts to users so that the machine's downtime is minimized because there is no delay in getting the right parts. In terms of meeting the world's growing food predictions, John Deere is also working with connecting field readings of individual farmers with weather and other overarching information to maximize crop output and resource allocation.^([6](#c07.xhtml#c07-fn-06)) With respect to farmers' privacy, John Deere allows farmers to opt out of this information retention if farmers prefer not to have their data accessible to other parties.

### Royston Diesel Power

Royston Diesel Power, developer of engine fuel management systems primarily used in shipping, uses big data to drive efficiency in the shipping industry. Real-time sensor data allows continuous monitoring of the ships systems in an offsite location. Additional sensors make it easy to plan navigational routes that take vessel performance, meteorological data, and other factors into account. This holistic approach to ship guidance increases arrival time accuracy and efficiency in resource usage. Port authorities also are able to check in on the ships' data to monitor potential congestion in the ports and improve cargo transfers. Operational management and energy management are increasingly commonplace with regards to big data, and these will continue to grow, as well as predictive maintenance and performance optimization.^([7](#c07.xhtml#c07-fn-07))

### 911 call centers

U.S. emergency response call centers have been transitioning to store information on the cloud and have focused their effort on how to record multimedia communications. The amount of big data that one single 911 interaction creates is massive because there is the initial phone call or text to 911, then there are records that the call center creates, and then the dispatch information to the police or emergency response team, and then the data that follows is also recorded. One company, NICE, can take text to 911 recordings, as well as metadata from the text stream (who called, location, date and times, and so on) and allow agencies to record these with an additional platform, AGENT511. Having all this information in one accessible place allows quality assurance evaluations to take half the time and improve emergency response times.^([8](#c07.xhtml#c07-fn-08))

### U.S. Department of Defense

The Department of Defense spent a record $7.4 billion on modern technology to update systems and increase capabilities so that the current technology does not become obsolete. According to former Deputy Defense Secretary Robert Work, "The U.S. military can either lead the coming revolution, or fall victim to it."^([9](#c07.xhtml#c07-fn-09)) He also said, "Rapid advances in artificial intelligence — and the vastly improved autonomous systems and operations they will enable — are pointing towards new and more novel warfighting applications involving human-machine collaboration and combat teaming." $7.4 billion is a 32% increase over spending levels in 2012, with AI specifically receiving the highest increases in spending.

### Royal Bank of Scotland

The Royal Bank of Scotland uses natural language processing by text analytics. Natural language processing is the activity of taking spoken word and translating into actionable items for a computer. The organization takes all of the data created by customer complaints and then analyzes said emails, survey responses, and recorded customer calls to understand why a customer is complaining. They then use this knowledge to implement adjustments and change the customer experience going forward.^([10](#c07.xhtml#c07-fn-10))

### Dignity Health

Dignity Health, the largest hospital provider in California, uses big data to scan patients for life-threatening conditions that are tricky to spot. For example, sepsis is one of the leading causes of death as well as a leading cause for re-admission to the hospital. The signs and symptoms of sepsis are often indistinguishable from other health issues, such as a fever that could easily be attributed to an initial cause. To decrease error and minimize the time from a patient's onset of sepsis to getting them treated, Dignity Health uses a Sepsis Bio-Surveillance Program, which monitors vital signs of all patients and key markers that may point to the start of sepsis. If the program notices that enough warning signs are present, the system alerts nurses via the patient's medical record. Then the nurses transfer the patient to the ICU, where full measures are taken to de-escalate the situation. The system has been able to lower sepsis mortality rates across Dignity Health's hospitals by 5% and delivered significant cost savings because patients spent less time in intensive care due to the early action.^([11](#c07.xhtml#c07-fn-11))
