
# What are the trends in big data?

How are corporations reacting and planning to use big data in the coming years? An organization could gain a significant competitive advantage if it knows how to access and use big data. The trends in this chapter should be contrasted with your organization’s big data approach and used as benchmarks in the company’s strategic planning sessions.

## Top big data and analytics trends for 2020

Let’s discuss some of the trends impacting 2020 and the future. We will then consider trends that were highlighted in the previous edition of the course.

The activity surrounding big data continues to increase and is becoming commonplace in many organizations. In the following sections, we will attempt to identify some of the current trends for 2020, as well as recognize trends that were prevalent over the last couple of years. Professionals will identify the following as possibilities to understand and exploit. Businesses that choose to ignore the potential of big data risk falling behind their competitors and possibly not having the ability to catch up.

### The following business intelligence (BI) trends are new for 2020^([1](#c03.xhtml#c03-fn-01))

1.  Becoming a real-time enterprise is no longer optional.
2.  Big data is just data. Next up? Wide data.
3.  Graph analytics and associative technology surpass SQL.
4.  DataOps plus self-service is the new agile.
5.  Active metadata catalogs are the connective tissue for data and analytics.
6.  The emergence of Data Literacy as a service.
7.  Multifaceted interactions will move us beyond search.
8.  Ethics and responsible computing are now critical.
9.  “Shazam” for data: What’s possible.
10. Independence versus stack: The sequel.

### The following BI trends were new for 2019^([2](#c03.xhtml#c03-fn-02))

*   **The rise of explainable artificial intelligence (AI)** — In addition to conclusions and recommendations, AI will also provide explanations to help users more clearly understand the basis for the recommendation.
*   **Natural language humanizes your data** — Systems will be able to understand a user’s intent behind a query. This will make for easier access to and manipulation of data.
*   **Actionable analytics put data in context** — BI platforms will allow users to analyze and take action as opposed to waiting on other people to review and recommend action.
*   **Data collaboratives amplify social good impact** — Public- and private-sector organizations strive to use technology in a “data for good” movement.
*   **Codes of ethics catch up to data** — Ethics policies must be upgraded (for regulations such as GDPR), to incorporate rapidly changing technology and data practices.
*   **Data management converges with modern BI platforms** — Organizations must determine how to capture, clean, define, and align disparate data.
*   **Data storytelling is the new language of corporations** — Leaders must create a culture where teams are responsible for identifying and communicating data insights.
*   **Enterprises get smarter about analytics adoption** — Analytics projects are not successful unless employees adopt the tools/systems into their daily business operations.
*   **Data democracy elevates the data scientist** — Data scientists develop soft skills to communicate findings in a simple, actionable way.
*   **Accelerated cloud data migration fuels modern BI adoption** — Organizations must rethink their data strategy from on-premise storage to cloud storage.

## Big Data survey

Gartner & Co. is a leading consulting company specializing in information technology.

Gartner, Inc. highlighted the top strategic technology trends that organizations need to explore in 2020.^([3](#c03.xhtml#c03-fn-03))

Gartner defines a strategic technology trend as one with substantial disruptive potential that is beginning to break out of an emerging state into broader impact and use or that is rapidly growing with a high degree of volatility reaching tipping points over the next five years.

The top 10 strategic technology trends for 2020 are:

1.  **Hyperautomation** — Includes machine learning (ML) and robotic process automation (RPA)
2.  **Multiexperience** — Conversational platforms, VR, augmented reality (AR), and mixed reality (MR)
3.  **Democratization of expertise** — Access to technical expertise for all
4.  **Human augmentation** — Cognitive and physical improvements to humans
5.  **Transparency and traceability** — Personal data, ethics, ownership
6.  **The empowered edge** — Data closer to user
7.  **Distributed cloud** — Decentralization of cloud
8.  **Autonomous things** — Robots, drones, ships, appliances
9.  **Practical blockchain** — Remains immature but has potential
10. **AI security**

### Increased use of drones

Gartner forecasts that the drone market will grow at a compound annual growth rate of 40% between 2018 and 2028 as they provide significant productivity improvements. The installed base will grow from 324,000 to more than 9 million worldwide.^([4](#c03.xhtml#c03-fn-04))

### Internet of things — Connectivity^([5](#c03.xhtml#c03-fn-05))

Gartner, Inc. forecasts that the enterprise and automotive internet of things (IoT) market will grow to 5.8 billion endpoints in 2020, a 21% increase from 2019. By the end of 2019, 4.8 billion endpoints are expected to be in use, up 21.5% from 2018.

Utilities will be the highest user of IoT endpoints, totaling 1.17 billion endpoints in 2019, and increasing 17% in 2020 to reach 1.37 billion endpoints. “Electricity smart metering, both residential and commercial will boost the adoption of IoT among utilities,” said Peter Middleton, senior research director at Gartner. “Physical security, where building intruder detection and indoor surveillance use cases will drive volume, will be the second largest user of IoT endpoints in 2020.”

Building automation, driven by connected lighting devices, will be the segment with the largest growth rate in 2020 (42%), followed by automotive and healthcare, which are forecast to grow 31% and 29% in 2020, respectively (see the following table). In healthcare, chronic condition monitoring will drive the most IoT endpoints, while in automotive, cars with embedded IoT connectivity will be supplemented by a range of add-on devices to accomplish specific tasks, such as fleet management.

## Accenture trends and surveys

In February 2020, Accenture published its Accenture Technology Vision 2020^([7](#c03.xhtml#c03-fn-07)), an annual forecast of the next three years of technology trends. The five key themes are:

### The I in Experience

Leading businesses are working with customers to create digital experiences. People still want the many benefits of customization, but they are skeptical of the non-transparent methods that companies use to deliver it. The rise of 5G and AR put more pressure on leaders to make this a reality.

### AI and me

The full potential of AI has moved beyond being a mere automation of simple tasks to being a powerful collaboration tool between human employees and machines. Successful businesses will understand the importance of context in human-machine interaction and take advantage of advances that help them collaborate on a larger scale.

### The dilemma of smart things

The very notion of product ownership is radically changing. When people buy products, they are in many cases no longer purchasing physical, finished items but conduits for evolving experiences. Companies must recognize this and work to design their products and ecosystems to accommodate ongoing change.
