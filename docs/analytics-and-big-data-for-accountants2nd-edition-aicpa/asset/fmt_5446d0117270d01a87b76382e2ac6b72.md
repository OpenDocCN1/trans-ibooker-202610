Leaders are weaving these technological building blocks together to set a course for the futures of their companies.

## NewVantage Partners 2020 annual big data Executive Survey^([9](#c03.xhtml#c03-fn-09))

According to NewVantage Partners’ latest annual big data Executive Survey, mainstream firms are pushing hard to realize business outcomes from big data and AI.

*   Only 14.6% of firms have deployed AI capabilities.
*   AI is the most disruptive technology dropped from 80.0% in 2019 to 69.5% in 2020.
*   73.4% state that business adoption of big data is still a struggle.
*   37.8% have created a data-driven organization.
*   26.8% have developed a data-driven culture.
*   People and process challenges are the biggest barriers to becoming data-driven (90.9%).
*   Most struggle to identify a single point of accountability for data in the organization.
*   The reason to pursue big data and AI investment are for primarily offensive reasons (89%).
*   Investment in blockchain appears to be fading.
*   More than 70% say that the organization is receiving measurable results.

The most impactful disruptive technology is AI and machine learning.

## Introduction

![](`images/fig4-1.png`)

[Figure 4-1:](#c04.xhtml#ifig4-1) Big Data Goals

All business endeavors must increase value for shareholders or stakeholders. In this chapter, we’re going to consider the strategic implications of big data to an organization. What are the goals an organization should have in regard to big data? What type of strategies should be created to achieve the goals? How does an organization begin the journey of implementing big data? The first step for all these questions is to determine the overall objective of the organization and how big data will be used to help achieve that objective.

For-profit organizations focus on increasing enterprise value as their main objective or purpose. The main objective or purpose for not-for-profits must be to enhance the lives, services, and opportunities for the customers or stakeholders. In [figure 4-1,](#c04.xhtml#fig4-1) notice that the center purpose is money, time, or value.

The first circle outside money, time, or value represents business insights. For the organization to enhance the enterprise or stakeholder’s value, it must consider business insights that affect the customer, operations, or predictions about future results. When acted upon, the insights should yield an increase in the organization’s value.
