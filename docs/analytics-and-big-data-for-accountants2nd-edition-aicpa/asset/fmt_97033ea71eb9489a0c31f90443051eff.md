
# Big Data Architecture, Sources, and Business Insights

## The Second Circle: Big Data Architecture

The second circle represents big data architecture. The architecture indicates that for the organization to achieve business insights, it will most likely be required to make an investment in big data architecture. Big Data architecture has a myriad of hardware, software, and consultant options. It also involves software programs such as `Hadoop`, `R`, and `MapReduce`. It is safe to assume that without making an investment in the big data architecture, it will be difficult to generate business insights relating to Big Data that will yield increased value.

## The Final Circle: Big Data Sources

The final circle represents big data sources. This level encompasses all of the structured and unstructured sources of big data that have been covered previously. If an organization is unwilling or incapable of accessing and analyzing big data sources, the organization will not be able to generate insights that will lead to increased enterprise or stakeholder value.

## Goals of Big Data

The overarching goal of big data analytics is to inform businesses. By analyzing data and trends, businesses can gain valuable insights that can be applied in nearly every area of operations. Some specific ways that big data can contribute to achieving the goal of informing businesses include the following:

*   Monetize data or interpret data to realize competitive advantage which can be monetized.
*   Analyze operational effectiveness — machine sensors, product failures, and traffic patterns.
*   Create a reliable, scalable, and capable infrastructure that aids the data gathering, analysis, and inferences.
*   Access and use internal and external data that are structured, unstructured, and streaming.
*   Predict business, social, political, economic, technological, and environmental trends.
*   Take action based on prescribed scenarios.

## Business Insights Associated with Big Data

The following list attempts to convey the wide variety of applications of big data. Many of the data components relate to multiple industries.

### 1. Customer Analytics

As noted in a previous chapter, one of the main sources of big data comes from transactions. This kind of information from customers can be used to gain insights into many aspects of customer behavior, including the following:

1.  Dropping product or service
2.  Analyzing customer behavior while on company website
3.  Monitoring customer usage of products to detect manufacturing or design problems
4.  Identifying high-value customers
5.  Identifying cross-selling opportunities as well as up-selling opportunities
6.  Determining which customers not to engage
7.  Identifying, targeting, and retaining customers
8.  Combining clickstream data with transactional data to improve customer profile
9.  Limiting product offerings to those that interest the customer
10. Determining any aspect of customer behavior or product preference
11. Identifying customer segmentation
12. Recording and analyzing customer service and support issues
13. Engaging brand advocates and changing the perception of brand antagonists
14. Empowering customers to sell your products
15. Enabling customers to locate items more quickly
16. Improving loyalty or net to promote are scores
17. Analyzing smartphone or mobile data — called detail record processing, social analysis, churn prediction, GEO mapping
18. Analyzing point of sale data
19. Creating forums — crowd creativity, crowd solutions

### 2. Manufacturing

Manufacturers now have access to real-time data from a variety of process activities that allow them to gain insight into many factors, including the following:

1.  Tracking of product quality or defects
2.  Supply chain management and planning
3.  Optimizing machines
4.  Engineering Analytics
5.  Predictive maintenance
6.  Process and quality analysis
7.  Warranty claim potential (based on social media comments or complaints)
8.  Enterprise resource planning — operations, service delivery, supply chain management, and automation of routine decisions
9.  Continuous improvement to processes and procedures

### 3. Research and Development

Recently, the federal government recognized the potential value that lies in big data for research and development. A new initiative was launched that intends to extract knowledge from collections of digital data to help solve challenges on a national level. On a business level, big data can help with the following:

1.  Monitoring product quality
2.  Identifying customer needs for potential new products
3.  Soliciting input from customers regarding products
4.  Improving products based on call center data

### 4. Distribution

As warehouses and distribution centers become increasingly high-tech, they now generate information that can be used to monitor and track labor, inventory, and equipment, including the following:

1.  Monitoring product shipments
2.  Identifying variances in logistic costs
3.  Determining inventories levels
4.  Using location data like `GPS`
5.  Using Radio-frequency identification
6.  Using distribution optimization

### 5. Logistics

"Information of Things" is a huge new source of data in logistics. It can be used to track goods and provide insight into the following:

1.  Demand forecasting
2.  Supply chain analytics
3.  Tracking
4.  Delivery forecasting
5.  Travel industry — Searchers, pricing, bundling (air, hotel, car, ship, entertainment)

### 6. Marketing

Marketing departments are no stranger to using data to determine customers' habits. With great access to data, they can apply their insights to the following:

1.  Determining marketing campaign effectiveness
2.  Determining channel effectiveness
3.  Monitoring and improving customer experience
4.  Tailoring marketing campaigns based on location and demographic data
5.  Providing advertising and public relations campaigns — Demand signaling, targeted advertising, sentiment analysis, customer acquisition, promotions, and other advertising mediums
6.  Offering brand sentiment analysis
7.  Providing product placement optimization
8.  Providing response modeling
9.  Providing retention modeling
10. Providing market-based analysis
11. Providing net promoter scores
12. Providing customer segmentation

### 7. Predictions

Big Data may enable predictions to be made in areas other than business. Some of these include the following:

1.  Crimes, threat analysis
2.  Weather
3.  Investments
4.  Mineral location
5.  Astrophysics
6.  Health
7.  Relationships

### 8. Operations Analysis

Operations analysis leverages information from machine sensors to improve operations in many ways, including the following:

1.  More accurate and timely decision-making
2.  Deviation analysis of logs and operational data
3.  Facility layout — Either in manufacturing or retail
4.  Supply chain optimization
5.  Dynamic pricing
