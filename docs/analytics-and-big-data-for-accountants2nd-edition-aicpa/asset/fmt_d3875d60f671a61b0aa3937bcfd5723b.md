### Power BI — Desktop

This next section demonstrates a tool that builds on the accountant’s existing knowledge of Excel and PowerPivot. Accountants must be able to analyze data and understand the meaning of the data from a historical perspective, find data and new meaning from a discovery perspective, predict what will happen beyond our traditional “what-if” analysis, and finally prescribe the actions that should be taken. The tools available today move us far beyond traditional spreadsheets. Accountants can do their career and current employer a great disservice by not learning and mastering the tools.

First off, Power BI is available for free. Download the software and begin experimenting with it. Next, find some datasets that you might be interested in. It is helpful if you already have a sense of the data because it will allow you to connect expected results with your trials on the new software. There are many free datasets that you can experiment with. Google large, free datasets and begin the process. First look for existing `xlsx` files, `csv` files, or `txt` files. They are not the complicated datasets, but we are trying to build familiarity prior to difficulty.

Download the dataset. In this example, data from Walmart stores covering a several year period was chosen. The information had stores, departments, weekly sales, dates, markdowns, holidays, some economic data, and the like. The information was downloaded in `zip` files that contained `csv` files. `CSV` files can easily be brought into Excel and Power BI.

When Power BI loads, the following screen appears:

Building on your existing Excel and PowerPivot knowledge may shorten your learning curve.
