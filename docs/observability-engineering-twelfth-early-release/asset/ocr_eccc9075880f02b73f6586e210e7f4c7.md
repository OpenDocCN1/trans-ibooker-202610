# Colophon

The animal on the cover of *Observability Engineering* is a maned wolf (*Chrysocyon brachyurus*). Maned wolves are the largest canids in South America, and can be found in Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Bolivia, and parts of Peru. Their habitat includes the Cerrado biome, which is made up of wet and dry forests, grasslands, savannas, marshes, and wetlands. Despite the name, maned wolves are not actually wolves but a separate species entirely.

Maned wolves have narrow bodies, large ears, and long black legs that allow them to see above tall grasses as they run. They stand nearly three feet tall at the shoulder and weigh around 50 pounds. Much of their bodies are black besides their characteristic mane of long, golden red hairs that stand straight up when danger is near. Unlike gray wolves and most other canid species that form packs, the maned wolf is solitary and often hunts alone. They are omnivorous and crepuscular, venturing out during dusk and dawn to prey on small mammals, rabbits, birds, and insects. They also scavenge for fruit and vegetables such as lobeira, a small tomato-like berry whose name means “fruit of the wolf.”

Maned wolves live in monogamous pairs sharing a territory of 10 miles during the breeding season, which lasts from April to June in the wild. In captivity, maned wolves give birth to litters of one to five pups. Both parents have been known to groom, feed, and defend their young in captivity, but are rarely seen with their pups in the wild. Pups develop quickly: they can see and hear by day 9, they start eating regurgitated food after a month, and are weaned by 15 weeks. Young maned wolves are usually considered fully grown and ready to leave their parents’ territory after one year. The maned wolf is classified as “near threatened” by the IUCN, mostly due to loss of the habitat. Many of the animals on O’Reilly covers are endangered; all of them are important to the world.
