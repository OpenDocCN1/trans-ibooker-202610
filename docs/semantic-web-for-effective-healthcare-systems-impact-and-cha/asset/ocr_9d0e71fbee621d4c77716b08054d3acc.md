Standardization could be accomplished via public involvement and robust investment in translational research [21].

# 9.12 Future Perspectives

The dream on the horizon is optimizing huge quantities of data to find safe and effective therapies. The challenge lies in the multi-axes of patients’ features to be inspected. For example, demographics, anthropometrics, family history, signs and symptoms, laboratory findings, radiographic images, diagnoses, or omics measures. The need for readily available data in the individual patient digital record system, the resemblance of the patient’s record to the standard patient, and the capacity to analyze such big data in practice either on healthcare facility or population level are challenging. Hence, the rationale for upgrading the learning health system to almost uniform patients’ subgroups stage [40].

A precision medicine assembly, chaired by the National Research Council, has submitted a novel biological and medical taxonomy, which would be designed to identify and serve the multi-axes of fundamental science and medical features as a matrix describing disorders endo-types [41]. Ontologies can embed findings from research data and the well-structured medical data, leading to computerize-able fundamental science and medical findings to show current knowledge and enable novel conclusions. Embedding such data allows categorizing disorders over many sorts of data structurally for further potential, powerful, and sophisticated patients’ classification.

# 9.13 Conclusion

Ontologies are expected to create more robust and interoperable healthcare information systems. They also could transfer, reuse, and share patient data. Their most vital advantage might be supporting the assimilation of data and knowledge. Precision medicine can be described as wide-ranging information based along with ontological systems, which executes pattern recognition of individual patient via identifying unique profiles to reduce much of the complexity as a result of presenting a distinct, precise definition of this domain. However, introducing a modern discipline, the case for personalized and precision medicine, generates enthusiasm about its implied applications yet uncertainty regarding its practical achievement for the abundance of the accompanying data which pretends significant hurdles in their analysis.
