### Abstract

Ontologies and AI have developed into two approaches for representing and extracting information effectively used in knowledge-based frameworks. Ontologies are a formal outcome of the knowledge elicitation process from an expert by knowledge engineers, and knowledge is not commonly included during this process. The motivation behind the two approaches is the extraction of structured knowledge. Clinical ontologies provide support to computer-aided clinical decision support. Computer-aided clinical decision support systems can likewise be deterministic. For example, a rule-based system makes clinicians aware of drug–drug interactions. The rules are derived directly from the clinical ontology. Artificial intelligence techniques are applied to extract meaningful information in several problem domains by discovering interesting patterns (relations) in large volumes of information. The fundamental objective of the inferred information and similarity measures is to make a relevant segment for each specific part, and not, generally speaking, full-scale division. Ontologies are the standard systems for expressing real-world entities in clinical and healthcare fields. However, many AI approaches do not allow the use of domain ontologies to guide the learning process. This chapter provides an overview of the use of knowledge semantics and ontologies in healthcare and clinical applications of machine learning, and also describes how the Unified Medical Language System (UMLS) is used in the AQ21 rule-learning program.

### 11.1 Introduction

Recent advances in Machine Learning (ML) have made it applicable to a wide range of problems, including those in clinical, healthcare, and wellbeing areas. These techniques can make accurate predictions in uncertain conditions by discovering patterns distributed over large amounts of information.

A quality of many of the most up-to-date techniques is also the capacity to combine Natural Language Processing (NLP) tools with learning from structured information [1]. Most of the novel techniques are statistical and focus on the analysis of numeric data. Domain knowledge for such methods is limited to the ad hoc encoding of attributes in the data or prior parameters of the model being learned, as in the case of deep learning of neural networks.

Surprisingly, not many AI techniques take into consideration modeling domain knowledge so as to guide the learning process.

The introduced research investigates the use of ontologies and knowledge semantics to guide the learning process by providing information additional to that already present in the data. The research is motivated by the presence of rich biomedical ontologies designed to be used by information integration strategies, NLP algorithms, and the Semantic Web. This presence of domain knowledge provides an ideal opportunity to supplement pure data for AI with connections that span over the data.

This section introduces biomedical ontologies and AI-guided learning, presents a way to tackle semantic data management, and discusses ontology in supervised learning.
