# 13.4  Semantics-Powered Healthcare SOS Engineering

Systems-of-Systems (SOS) Engineering is one of the sub-fields of Systems Engineering. SOS is a composition of systems consisting of independent, distributed and evolving constituent systems acting jointly toward a common goal by exploiting the synergies between them. Power grid technology, transportation, production and military enterprises are some areas where examples of working SOS systems are available. Nielsen et al. [46] review the current state in model-based techniques in SOS engineering.

A breakout session [47] held at the National Center for Research Resources Conference on the Future of Telehealth recognized telehealth as a SOS based on scale and complexity. The session participants identified key topics on telehealth and discussed the known barriers from the points of view of (1) the patient, (2) the system, and (3) the data. From the perspective of data, barriers identified hindering data collection, compilation, and transmission between healthcare providers, and accessibility of data for research purposes were (a) lack of data standards, and (b) issues around data security, privacy, and trust.

Application of semantic technologies to resource-constrained IoT-based systems is difficult because of the distributed and heterogeneous nature of resources. Ryu et al. [48] present an integrated semantic service platform (ISSP) to create ontological models in various IoT-based service domains of a smart city. This ISSP method attempts to address problem areas in providing integrated semantic services in IoT systems such as semantic discovery, dynamic semantic representation, and semantic data repository.
