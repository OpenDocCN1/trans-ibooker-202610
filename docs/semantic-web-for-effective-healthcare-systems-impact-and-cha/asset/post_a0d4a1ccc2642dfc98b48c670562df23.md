## 9.10 Personalizing Care for Chronic Illness

Chronic patients are complicated medical cases that need harmonious interplay of various professionals. An accurate intervention for these

patients necessitates a critical investigation of every patient’s conditions individually and compliance with authentication based typical interference schemes to these types of conditions. Various additional experimental incidents in the field of medical science like incorrect diagnosis, non-observed comorbidities, absent information, non-observed relevant complaints, or prevention in which discovery relies on inference capabilities of the concerned professionals. For instance, one personalization method adjusts the ontology content to the observed features in the medical record of a particular patient, rendering a personalized ontology encompassing only the relevant clinical information to handle this patient by the healthcare professionals. Another personalization method employs the modified ontology of an enduring individual patients to change generic medical intervention plans into individual ones. Eventually, for comorbid patients, this method infers with incorporating various personal plans into a unified individualized one. Subsequently, the ontology can also be utilized as the information based, decision supporting computer added tools to assist healthcare providers to identify exceptional incidents like mistaken diagnosis, non-observed comorbidities, absent information, non-observed relevant complaints, or preventive procedures [39].

## 9.11 Roadblocks Toward Precision Medicine

The greatest dilemma is instituting confidentiality legislation without accounting for the universal big data all around. The confidentiality and privacy of the patient are required to preserve patients’ confidence and collaboration. However, a novel ethics model might be needed to match confidentiality legal aspirations’ with the community and future patient’s demands for disseminating clinical data toward the community wellness.

Another dilemma is the endeavor and price of introducing into and extracting data out of computerized medical records. Submitting hand-operated structured data by healthcare givers is time-consuming and non-adaptable. Growing attempts on standardized application portals with computerized medical records from repositories of data and devices might serve (for example, data gathered and submitted by patients). Consistently extracting medical content from dictated remarks or voice recordings of the patient visits’ is growing applicable [39]. ## 9.12 Future Perspectives

The dream on the horizon is optimizing huge quantities of data to find safe and effective therapies. The challenge lies in the multi-axes of patients’ features to be inspected. For example, demographics, anthropometrics, family history, signs and symptoms, laboratory findings, radiographic images, diagnoses, or omics measures. The need for readily available data in the individual patient digital record system, the resemblance of the patient’s record to the standard patient, and the capacity to analyze such big data in practice either on healthcare facility or population level are challenging. Hence, the rationale for upgrading the learning health system to almost uniform patients’ subgroups stage [40].

A precision medicine assembly, chaired by the National Research Council, has submitted a novel biological and medical taxonomy, which would be designed to identify and serve the multi-axes of fundamental science and medical features as a matrix describing disorders endo-types [41]. Ontologies can embed findings from research data and the well-structured medical data, leading to computerize-able fundamental science and medical findings to show current knowledge and enable novel conclusions. Embedding such data allows categorizing disorders over many sorts of data structurally for further potential, powerful, and sophisticated patients’ classification.

## 9.13 Conclusion

Ontologies are expected to create more robust and interoperable healthcare information systems. They also could transfer, reuse, and share patient data. Their most vital advantage might be supporting the assimilation of data and knowledge. Precision medicine can be described as wide-ranging information based along with ontological systems, which executes pattern recognition of individual patient via identifying unique profiles to reduce much of the complexity as a result of presenting a distinct, precise definition of this domain. However, introducing a modern discipline, the case for personalized and precision medicine, generates enthusiasm about its implied applications yet uncertainty regarding its practical achievement for the abundance of the accompanying data which pretends significant hurdles in their analysis.
