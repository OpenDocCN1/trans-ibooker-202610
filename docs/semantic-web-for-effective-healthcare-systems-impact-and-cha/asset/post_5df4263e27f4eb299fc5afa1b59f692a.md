## 12.6.4 Orphanet-INSERM (Institut National de la Santé et de la Recherche Médicale)

Orphanet is a very distinctive information portal for collecting the resources and improving the knowledge about rare diseases to develop its diagnosis and treatment of the affected patients. It also maintains its nomenclature called ORPHAcode, which helps in improving the perceptibility of rare diseases in the healthcare and research information retrieval systems [19]. Orphanet has been established in 1997 by the INSERM i.e. French National Institute for Health and Medical Research in France. But since 2000, it gained much importance by the European Union, and the initiative was supported by a grant from the European Commission and was eventually grown to a Consortium of 40 countries all over the world [19].

It has a platform called “Orphadata” that provides scientific professionals with high-quality data sets regarding rare diseases, also the drugs that are in a reusable form [19].

The webpage of orphaned INSERM consists of the following features:

- I. It gives permission to access the classification, inventory, and encyclopedia of rare diseases along with its genes involved.
- II. It has an inventory of all the orphan drugs.
- III. It consists of a directory of patient organizations.
- IV. It has a directory of professionals and institutions.
- V. It also consists of a directory of expert cultures and medical laboratories providing diagnostic tests.
- VI. It gives access to the collection of thematic reports and the directory of currently ongoing research projects, registries, clinical trials, and biobanks.

## 12.6.5 Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities: MedDRA

MedDRA is a multilingual platform that allows users to access and retrieve information in their native language. This terminology is extensively used for use of data entry, analysis, retrieval, and display regarding stages of drug development and devices [20]. It has a very straightforward and sophisticated analysis used to analyze and detect different medical issues by using its hierarchical structure. MedDRA was developed by ICH partners including the WHO. ICH provides a standardized and international terminology MedDRA which can be used to pertain and access all the medically important data and evaluate the same [20]. This platform proves the best for continuously developing and maintaining the MedDRA database for effective and transparent regulation of product which contributes to the global public health [20].

MedDRA contains the following tools under the “Tools” section of the webpage [20].

- I. MedDRA users can access powerful computer tools after subscribing and it helps to get all the information about the database along with the training available on the implementation of the database.
- II. It gives access to commercial software tools like Coding and browsing tools, SMQs (Standardized MedDRA Queries), APIs (application program interfaces), Mapping tools, MVAT (MedDRA Version Analysis Tool).
- III. There are other tools too including clinical data management systems, autoencoders, and safety systems that have applied MedDRA.
- IV. MedDRA allows the users to access the required information through a wide range of options available on the webpage, which are MedDRA hierarchy, Multilingual MedDRA, Standardized MedDRA Queries, Multiaxiality, and Electronic Communication.

## 12.6.6 Medical Subject Headings: MeSH

MeSH is a precise vocabulary by the US National Library of Medicine (NLM) used to organize the database. It is also used for searching the required data on PubMed, and also some other databases where information is fitted into a hierarchy. The MeSH is available on PubMed’s homepage and it allows the users to locate the information for any disease and find its position in the MeSH hierarchy. Some of the subheadings found on the homepage for rare diseases are analysis, classification, metabolism, statistics and numerical data, etc. [21].

## 12.6.7 Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man: OMIM

OMIM is a wide-ranging database consisting of human genes and genetic phenotypes which is available freely. OMIM consists of information about all the known Mendelian disorders and around 15,000 genes [22]. OMIM concerns the relationship between phenotype and genotype. This database was introduced in the 1960s by Dr. Victor A. McKusick as a catalog which consisted of Mendelian traits and disorders, entitled Mendelian Inheritance in Man (MIM). Now it is a project by the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) at Johns Hopkins University (USA) [22].

## 12.6.8 Orphanet Rare Disease Ontology: ORDO

Orphanet has its terminology platform called “ORDO” (Orphanet Rare Disease Ontology) for collecting information about rare diseases obtained from the Orphanet Database and find the relationship between the diseases, genes, and other relevant characteristics. These data are reusable for computational analysis [19]. It is mutually developed by Orphanet and EBI as a multilingual system dedicated to validation and literature of the resources. It resulted from the Orphanet database (www.orpha.net), which is a multilingual database that is inclined toward rare diseases that have been derived from literature and confirmed by the international experts. It incorporates a nosology, i.e., classification of rare diseases, the relationships (epidemiological data, gene–disease relations) and its connections with other terminologies (SNOMED CT, MeSH, UMLS, MedDRA), databases (UniProtKB, OMIM, HGNC, Ensembl, IUPHAR, Reactome, Geantlas), or categorizations (ICD-10).

The ontology will be kept up by Orphanet and will further be added with new data. Orphanet classifications can be browsed in the OLS view. The Orphanet Rare Disease Ontology is updated every six months and it strictly follows the OBO guidelines on disapproval of the terms. It consists of the official ontology of various rare diseases which is produced and kept by Orphanet (INSERM, US14).

## 12.6.9 UMLS: Unified Medical Language System

The Unified Medical Language System (UMLS) is a software that is a collection of many health and biomedical vocabularies and standards to enable access to the required knowledge by a computer system. The three knowledge sources are Metathesaurus, Semantic network, and Specialist Lexicon and Lexical tools. It contains its hierarchy that helps to find out the relationships and attributes of these rare diseases [23].

UMLS provides users with the following features [23]:

- I. Link codes and terms between the pharmacy, doctor, and the insurance company
- II. Coordination of patient care among the various departments in a healthcare facility or hospital
- III. Processing the texts to extract concepts, relationships, or knowledge
- IV. Facilitation of mapping between the terminologies
- V. Develop a system for information retrieval
- VI. Extraction of specific terminologies from the Metathesaurus
- VII. Creation and maintenance of a local terminology
- VIII. Development of a terminology service
- IX. Research terminologies or ontologies [23]

## 12.6.10 SNOMED-CT: Systematized Nomenclature of Human and Veterinary Medicine—Clinical Terms

SNOMED CT is a designated system of electronic exchange of information by the US Federal government. It is used for obtaining the clinical health information and also generates specifications of the US Healthcare Information Standards Panel. SNOMED CT is a very comprehensive and multilingual healthcare database used for rare diseases with scientific validated clinical data. It is used in more than 80 countries and is mapped according to international standards [24]. SNOMED CT is a growing and upcoming platform that supports the development of quality clinical data in electronic form and allows its interpretation.

SNOMED CT supports the following tools and software on its webpage

- I. Change or add to SNOMED CT
- II. SNOMED CT tooling
- III. Software development
- IV. SNOMED CT browser
- V. SNOMED International Managed Service

The webpage also explains Concepts, Descriptions, and Relationships, how to use the tool, and the benefits of the tool to the individuals and population [24].
