This was a very dangerous complacency, as the microbes never went away, they were just out of sight and have now resurfaced again. Since the start of 21st century, we have witnessed many such pathogens which have had an impact on the public health. Some notable ones include the Ebola virus, HIV, Zika virus, and many others, which were either new and less harmful viruses or were just the ones which we have already encountered in the past.

In 2019 an old virus known by the name corona virus resurfaced. This is a type of virus which impacts the respiratory tracts in humans. Coronaviruses are known to cause different diseases some of which include the SARS (severe acute respiratory syndrome), MERS (Middle East respiratory syndrome), and the most recent, coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19).

COVID-19 (shorthand of Corona Virus) was first identified in Wuhan, China in December 2019. It was later declared a global pandemic by the World Health Organization (WHO) on 11 March 2020. At the time of writing, there are 21,450,473 confirmed cases, 805,587 deaths, and 12,34,808 recovered cases. The distribution of these cases across the world can be seen in Figure 14.1. The mortality rate (MR) and the number of cases of this virus is mainly dependent on a few important factors [5]. But the most important of them is the care of the critically affected patients. As the research suggests that nasal cavity is the initial entry point of COVID virus.
