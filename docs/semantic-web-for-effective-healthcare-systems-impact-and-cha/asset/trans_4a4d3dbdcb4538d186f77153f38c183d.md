## 12.5 A Brief Classification

Taking into consideration the importance of rare disease worldwide, the proper classification of rare diseases is immensely significant. Proper classification can help healthcare experts and researchers in preventing, diagnosing, and treating rare diseases. However, there is not any specific classification system but there are various databases, organizations, and machinery tools. These contain information about all the discovered rare diseases. Nowadays, there are many non-profit organizations engaged in rare disease research. Each of these organizations has its classification criteria. For example, the Orphanet classification system is a multi-level classification divided into three levels: barrier group, barrier, and barrier subtype. It helps to diagnose the diseases in a specific manner and is included in the nomenclature of Orphanet data [11], whereas the ICD 10 International Classification of Diseases that uses a codifying arrangement for the classification. Taking into account all the studied available data, codes are assigned to each rare disease. As some rare diseases in ICD 10 were misclassified using the same coding system, improved version ICD 11 was enforced.

Every classification system is comprised of rare diseases occurrence. However, in broader classification, there are many heterogeneities, such as diseases occurring from birth, age-wise, etc. Together with all these factors, rare diseases can be classified into three groups:

- 1. Characterized by a genetic disorder, diseases including genetic bases, extreme rarity, occurrence from birth, prevalence rate lower than 1/10,000, etc.
- 2. Based on known infectious diseases and frequency of occurrence.
- 3. Based on typical heterogeneity like diseases occurring with age, whether widespread, etc.

This classification being undefined is to cover all possible rare diseases and the existing heterogeneity.

## 12.6 Rare Disease Databases and Online Resources

Here are some reference databases by Orphanet that would help to enable the correlation between different information systems and its retrieval.

### 12.6.1 European Reference Network: ERN

The European Reference Network is a virtually developed that includes the healthcare suppliers all over Europe. It is a network for rare diseases to manage access to the required knowledge and information about the rare disease which generally is not available in any health center due to the rarity of the disease. Its main objective is to conduct discussions regarding the complexity of rare diseases and its highly specialized treatment, information, and diagnostic resources. This virtual platform is collaborative in its terms with access given to restricted ERN users only [16]. It does not work as a platform for the exchange of personal or clinical patient data. The main purpose of this network is to deal with the complex rare diseases that require highly specialized treatment and diagnosis. It provides users with all the necessary knowledge and resources [17].

The database has a total of 24 networks where the first ERN was launched in March 2017. It includes over 900 specialized healthcare units from more than 300 hospitals in 26 countries across the European Union [16]. The framework with 18 indicators for monitoring systems will help build an improved system with excellent outcomes to identify the potential limitations and demonstrate its success [17]. It includes video clips consisting of information, stories, and presentations of ERNs, information flyers, and brochures [17].

Following are its characteristics [11]:

- I. To make sure that the treatment facilities are available wherever necessary.
- II. Proper and systematic application of European Union criteria to deal with these rare diseases requiring special care.
- III. Serve as a tool for knowledge, research, and information and a center for treating patients from all countries in Europe [11].
- IV. The ERN network is also classified according to ERN members per network and per country.
- V. The Network subheading on the database page has a “Search tool,” which presents information in a way that contains the ERN names, clinical centers, and details.

### 12.6.2 Genetic and Rare Diseases Information Center: GARD

The Genetic and Rare Diseases Information Center is a program which has been initiated and funded by the National Center for Advancing Translational Sciences (NCATS) and the National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI) in 2002, which are the parts of the National Institute of Health (NIH) [13]. GARD provides the people to access the latest and easily understood information round the clock regarding rare or genetic diseases in either English or Spanish [13]. This database sums up information from the NIH resources, journals, websites, medical textbooks, and databases. Scientists and researchers studying rare or genetic diseases can access information through this database. Even doctors, nurses, and other healthcare providers can obtain the necessary information from this [13].

The GARD webpage has the following components [13]:

- I. The questions are being answered by GARD Information Specialists.
- II. Links to resources have also been provided where you can find more information.
- III. It also contains information about genetic testing and services.
- IV. Contains information about all the scientific conferences that have been sponsored by NIH.
- V. Information about organizations that provide information, support, and research studies and clinical trials.
- VI. Also lists down the FDA-approved medications for rare diseases.

### 12.6.3 International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision: ICD-10

The new National Center for Health Statistics ICD-10-CM Browser tool is an easily accessible web-based query application that permits the users to search for codes from the *International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision, Clinical Modification* (ICD-10-CM) and provides information required to apprehend the usage of ICD-10-CM codes [18]. This application offers access to several fiscal year version sets which are available with real-time wide-ranging results via the search abilities [18]. The International Classification of Diseases (ICD) has been designed to collect, process, classify, and present the mortality statistics and compare them at an international level. It has a specified format that includes death certificates and its cause. The conditions are then reported and decrypted into medical codes by using the standardized grouping system and the rules for selection-modification contained in the valid revision of the ICD, published by the World Health Organization (WHO) [18]. These coding rules are useful to develop the mortality statistics by giving a certain preference to certain categories by selecting the death cause and its sequence [18].

The database consists of the following components [18]:

- I. A tabular list which consists of the codes and the cause of death
- II. Inclusion and exclusion terms for the death cause titles
