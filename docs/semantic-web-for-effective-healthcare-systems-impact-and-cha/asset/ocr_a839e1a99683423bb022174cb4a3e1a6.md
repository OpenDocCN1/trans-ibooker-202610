For LDA model, the number of topics K has to be fixed in prior. It assumes the generative process for a document **w** = (w₁, . . . , w_N) of a corpus D containing N words from a vocabulary consisting of V different terms, w ∈ {1, . . . , V} for all i = {1, . . . , N}. LDA consists of the following steps [12]

+   1. For each topic *k*, draw a distribution over words Φ(*k*) ∼ Dir(α).
+   2. For each document *d*,
+      (a) Draw a vector of topic proportions θ(*d*) ∼ Dir(β).
+      (b) For each word *i*,
+         (i) Draw a topic assignment z<sub>d,i</sub> ∼ Mult(θ<sub>d</sub>), z<sub>d,n</sub> ∈ {1, . . . , K},
+         (ii) Draw a word w<sub>d,i</sub> ∼ Mult(Φ<sub>z<sub>d,i</sub></sub>), w<sub>d,i</sub> ∈ {1, . . . , V}

where α is a Dirichlet prior on the per-document topic distribution, and β is a Dirichlet prior on the per-topic word distribution. Let θ<sub>td</sub> be the probability of topic *t* for document *d*, z<sub>di</sub> be the topic distribution, and let Φ<sub>tw</sub> be the probability of word *w* in topic *t*. The probability of generating word *w* in document *d* is:

$$p(w|d)=\sum_{t=1}^{K}\theta_{td}\Phi_{tw}$$  (1.2)

Equation 1.2 gives the weighted average of the per-topic word probabilities, where the weights are the per-document topic probabilities. The resulting distribution p(w|d) varies from document to document, as the topic weights change among documents. Corpus documents are fitted into LDA model by inferring a collection of hidden variables. These variables are denoted by θ = {θ<sub>td</sub>}, the |K| × |D| matrix of per-document topic weights, and Φ = {Φ<sub>tw</sub>}, the |K| × |N| matrix of per-topic word weights. Inference for LDA is the problem of determining the joint posterior distribution of θ and Φ after observing a corpus of documents, which are influenced by LDA parameters.

Simple LDA model gives term-topic probabilities for all terms under each topic. According to literature [59, 60], only the top 5 or 10 terms under each topic were selected for modeling.
