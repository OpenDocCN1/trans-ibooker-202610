# 14.1 Introduction

Since the beginning of the mankind, humans have faced a large number of pandemics. Pandemic is the term which is used for an epidemic which occurs worldwide or has its effect on a greater part of the world population. According to the World Health Organization, a pandemic is the one which involves the worldwide spread of a new disease whereas an epidemic is the one which remains limited to one region, city or country. A pandemic is said to spreads beyond national borders and possibly worldwide. The main causes of such pandemics can be the introduction of some new bacteria or virus strain or its subtype which easily transmits into the human body and even between them. Some of the deadliest pandemics in the history of mankind have been caused by a single bacterium which is known by Yersinia pestis, and commonly called Plague.

We have recorded many pandemics in the past. Some of the known ones include:

+   - Plague of Justinian (540 AD): estimated to have killed over 40 million (approximately half of the world’s population) [1].
+   - Black Death (1347 AD): same Plague resurfaced after around 800 years killing around 200 million in a span of 3–4 years [2].
+   - Small pox (15th century): it was a constant source of error for almost 3 centuries and was believed to be the cause of death of every third person at its peak. It wiped out 90% of the indigenous population of North American countries like USA and Mexico. Later in 1980, the World Health Organization announced the complete eradication of small pox [3].
+   - Cholera (early 19th century): It is a water borne disease, which is believed to have originated in India and traveled across the world killing countless people over the century [4].
+   - Influenza (early to mid-19th century): This appeared nearly simultaneously across the globe and was the cause of over 70 million deaths.

All these pandemics had a huge impact on world population due to the lack of adequate healthcare facilities. The late diagnosis was one of the main reasons that these diseases were very lethal and also became pandemics.
