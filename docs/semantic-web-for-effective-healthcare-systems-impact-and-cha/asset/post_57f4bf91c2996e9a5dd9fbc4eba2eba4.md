
## A Knowledgebase Model Using RDF Knowledge Graph for Clinical Decision Support Systems

Ravi Lourdusamy and Xavierial J. Mattam*

Department of Computer Science, Sacred Heart College (Autonomous), Tirupattur, Tamil Nadu, India

## Abstract

Clinical Decision Support Systems are used in healthcare to assist in making life-saving decisions. Therefore, such systems have to be efficient and reliable with relevant knowledge that is periodically updated. With vast knowledge available in medical literature and in websites, the challenge is in curating that knowledge on a knowledgebase to be used by the decision support system. In order to curate knowledge in a knowledgebase system the easiest method is to make use of the already well-developed relational database system. But that does not solve problems especially when it comes to representing knowledge having related concepts. Recently, the use of knowledge graphs has become popular for knowledge representation. The use of RDF triples in storing Knowledge Graphs is a convenient method of knowledgebase curation. With a centralized form of RDF Knowledge Graphs in a clinical decision support system, the required presentation of knowledge will become easier to handle. A model for such a knowledgebase is proposed in the chapter that makes use of open information extraction systems to capture relevant knowledge from medical literature and curate that in the knowledgebase of the clinical decision support system.

**Keywords:** Knowledgebase, knowledge graphs, knowledgebase systems, Resource Description Framework, clinical decision support system

## 10.1 Introduction

Knowledgebase systems using relational databases have been popular in recent years especially since the incorporation of artificial intelligence into such a system is an easy task. But the multiple tables and identities linking them have been cumbersome. Even to run artificial intelligence algorithms such as machine learning and deep learning algorithms are found to be difficult on such complicated relational database systems. When it comes to complex decision-making systems such as the clinical decision support systems, relational databases have to be an intricate maze. The information retrieval tasks from the knowledge bases of such a system will be a herculean process beyond the possibility of creating an efficient and reliable system. Graph databases on the other hand make use of the semantic structure of knowledge to link various concepts using its relationship edges. Such a structure is much easier to visualize and so, information retrieval will be more straightforward. It will therefore be less resource-hungry than the relational database systems.

Resource Description Framework or RDF makes use of graph framework to represent information in form of triples. The RDF graphs are directed graphs that consist of the concepts for its nodes and the relationship between the concepts for the edges. RDF graphs make use of semantic networks represented using ontology. The RDF graphs are useful to represent complex information in knowledge bases and the simplicity of its structure makes it highly portable across applications in any system.

Representation of knowledge in any knowledge base is a challenge. The knowledge graphs are pragmatic models of knowledge representation that make use of an ontology to form new knowledge. The interlinking of the entity relationships in a knowledge graph contributes to the search efficiency using knowledge graphs. RDF knowledge graphs are machine-readable and therefore they can be processed in machines. The RDF knowledge graphs are highly interlinked in a logically consistent form permitting machines to reason using it to search and retrieve relevant information. That is why RDF knowledge graphs can be highly efficient in knowledgebase systems that require robust and reliable information retrieval systems such as the Clinical Decision Support Systems.

The efficiency and reliability of information retrieval depend solely on the query language used. SPARQL with its ability to convert natural language queries into a machine-readable format allows users of the system to retrieve relevant information from the RDF knowledge graph efficiently. Since the natural language queries contain semantic information that can be easily processed on the ontological RDF knowledge graph, the SPARQL interpretation of the queries plays the intermediate role. Another advantage of the SPARQL is its portability across multiple programming languages and user interfaces. The difficulty that most knowledgebase systems face is its compatibility with existing systems. With the use of SPARQL on RDF knowledge graph-based systems, the compatibility issue can be reduced to a great extent.

Clinical Decision Support Systems or CDSS are systems that render decision support in healthcare. The knowledgebase CDSS makes use of the knowledge base that is created from multiple sources. The knowledgebase is the backbone of any knowledgebase CDSS. The efficiency, reliability, and robustness of the CDSS depend on the knowledge base. Therefore, the knowledge representation in the knowledge base of the CDSS is the biggest challenge in the creation of a fully functional CDSS. Using RDF knowledge graphs for knowledge representation in the CDSS and using SPARQL for information retrieval has two big advantages over other systems. Firstly, the knowledge representation will be pretty straightforward and visualization of such knowledge will not be difficult. Secondly, such systems will be highly portable. Therefore, the compatibility of such a system with the already existing systems will not become a hindrance to the operation of the systems.

The chapter highlights the use of RDF Knowledge Graph for CDSS and proposes a model of a Knowledgebase CDSS using RDF Knowledge Graph. In the following section, a comparison is made of the traditional relational database and graph database. The section after that will introduce the RDF model of a graph database and elaborate on the technologies associated with it. An elaborate discussion is done in the section following that on knowledgebase systems, Knowledge Graphs, RDF Knowledge Graphs, and SPARQL query language. Methods of information extraction are then discussed together with the proposed knowledge base model and the evaluation measurements generally used. Before the conclusion of the chapter, there is a discussion on the limitations of the knowledge base creation and future possibilities for research and development in knowledgebase systems for CDSS.

## 10.2 Relational Database to Graph Database

There has been a constant evolution in the storage and use of data ever since computers began to gain popularity in the early 1960s. A lot of study and research has been done to make efficient use of the data available. The relational database model that E.F. Codd formulated in 1970 and was commercialized in the 1980s has developed into a well-evolved system in the later years. It has been traditionally considered for any database application [1]. But with the advent of Big Data precipitated by the universal growth of the internet, the well-developed relational database system is left wanting and newer models have taken its place especially in the field of knowledge management.

### 10.2.1 Relational Database for Knowledge Representation

In the 1970s when the relational database was a well-developed form of data storage, logic programming in the form of Prolog evolved. The strong affinity between the two could have been the result of the contemporary development of the two [2]. For decades after that, there has been a lot of research on coupling relational databases with Prolog [3–5]. It is effective as far as linked data is utilized for the knowledgebase system. The significant impetus to the growth in the interest in combining logical programming and relational database is the implementation of the Fifth Generation Computer Systems project that began in April 1982 with the formation of the Institute of New Generation Computer Technology (ICOT) [6]. Research and development on knowledge base systems at ICOT envisages the use of logical programming using Prolog integrated into a conventional database system that stores a huge amount of data [7]. Another momentous impulsion that worked in favor of the coalescing of rule-based programming language with a relational database is the growth in the interest in the use of logical queries by the database communities [8].

A database can be considered for logical programming in two fundamental ways. The first is the model-theoretic approach using first-order logic or predicate logic and the second is the proof-theoretic approach which makes use of rule-based propositional logic or production rules [9]. The creation of a knowledge base using the latter approach is convenient since the relational database is similar to production systems in modularity, uniformity, naturalness, and key-connectedness. Using these analogous concepts and the fourth normal form of the relational database to transform IF conditions to relations, the conversion of the production rules to a relational database is achieved. The conversion of production rules to a relational database removes barriers of logical programming with regards to knowledge representation, storage, and inferencing [10]. The former approach, on the other hand, allows the representation of incomplete knowledge in the sense that not all facts about the knowledge represented need to be in the knowledge base.
