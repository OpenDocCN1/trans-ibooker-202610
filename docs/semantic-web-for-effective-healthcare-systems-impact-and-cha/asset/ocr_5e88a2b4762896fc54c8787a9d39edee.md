While some consider Expert Systems as a type of Knowledgebase system since they are modelled on human cognitive processes and provide functions of Knowledgebase systems such as decision making [74], some others consider Knowledgebase systems as a category of the Expert Systems since knowledge and knowledge base are part of the Knowledgebase systems similar to Expert Systems [75, 76].

The evolution of the early Expert Systems to the current Knowledgebase systems occurred in different stages. The early Expert Systems that were developed in the late 1970s and the early 1980s, which include systems like MYCIN, a clinical decision support system to diagnose bacterial infection, CASNET, to diagnose glaucoma, PROSPECTOR to identify sites for drilling, and Rt that was used to configure computer systems, used the forward chaining and backward chaining techniques with symbolic logic representation to help in inferences and decision making. These systems were limited in their task because the requirements of complex tasks like planning and design could not be met with the inefficient sophistication and inflexible inferencing of these systems. The next stage of evolution that happened in the late 1980s to the late 1990s saw the commercialization of Knowledgebase systems through various applications that were developed such as predicting and forecasting for commercial planning, assigning work for railway crew members and other job scheduling applications, planning power systems and development of rail routes, finance, design and planning in manufacturing sectors, computer-aided process planning, configuring tools and managing a crisis. The development of the knowledge base in these applications lacked sophistication and so the system was neither efficient nor robust although they were helpful to a great extent. The next generation of Knowledgebase systems had more research and development in the building of the knowledge base. The knowledge base was developed as a generic component that could be attached to any system. Together with the development of a formal theory of knowledge, the methodology for the creation of the knowledge base had a more efficient and reliable knowledge acquisition, representing and inferencing technique [77]. Currently, the Knowledgebase systems have reached a certain degree of maturity to the foundation of Computational Intelligence Systems that are built on intricate theories involving engineering economics, operations research, statistical analysis, and decision theory. These systems make use of various recently evolved techniques such as Knowledge Engineering, Natural Language Processing, Machine Learning, Software Agents, Reasoning Engines and so on for the creation of the knowledge base.
