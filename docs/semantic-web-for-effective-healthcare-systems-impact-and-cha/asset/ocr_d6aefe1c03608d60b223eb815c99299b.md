# 10.3 RDF

Big Data is characterized by five elements, namely, volume, velocity, variety, value and veracity, that intrinsically form the huge and complex data-sets that cannot be handled by relational database and the traditional SQL. Big Data, especially in its unstructured form and containing petabytes of data, is analyzed for quick results by leading internet applications like Google, Facebook, Amazon, and the like [42]. In 1995, the Worldwide Web Consortium (W3C) started experimenting with a platform for Internet Content Selection (PICS) that was developed by the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) for rating the content of the web pages for the use of various communities that required it specially to monitor the web content. Due to various limitations in PICS, W3C formed the PICS Next Generation (PICSNG) working group to work on the common problem regarding resource description. The specifications worked out by the group could be applied to many applications which were later consolidated by W3C as the RDF working group. Although a lot of contribution was made by PICSNG in the creation of RDF, the RDF as at its present stage of evolution is shaped by the influence of many W3C member companies and several metadata efforts [43–45]. RDF has the five elements of the Big Data and it allows streaming, that is, continuous changes in the data. So even though, RDF might be inconsistent at times, it is popularly used [46].
