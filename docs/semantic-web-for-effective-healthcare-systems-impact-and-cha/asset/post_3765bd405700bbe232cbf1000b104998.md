### 3.1.1 Basics of Ontology

Ontology learning is a semantic way of knowledge representation which converts the unstructured data sources into structured data. It provides the semantic meaning of classes and gives the relationship between the attributes of the objects, which acts as the knowledge base for the domain. Ontology is the field of identifying the relationship of the data base in the entire domain.

A few scientists are moving in the direction of creating ontology based models for health recommendation system. Ontology helps to find the relationships between various classes and its individuals of selected domain. There are several uses of ontology-based systems related to information retrieval, Artificial Intelligence, machine learning applications and others [3].

The purpose of an ontology is a formal way of knowledge representation of classes called concepts, data and object properties of each class relating various features and attributes of the class. In the era of Artificial Intelligence, Ontology becomes the unavoidable knowledge repository in which group of classes and its attributes along with its relationship creates a knowledge repository. Many researchers started developing standardized ontologies in the field of medicine and used to share and annotate information. The types of ontologies are as follows:

- Top-level ontologies or upper-level ontologies: These kinds of ontologies describe general concepts common to most domains. These ontologies can be reused based on the different purpose of the research. Examples of ontologies are object, purpose, reason, time, space, etc.
- Domain ontologies: These ontologies are developed for a specific closed domain. For example, BASEBALL is an example of a domain ontology.
- Task ontologies: They describe tasks or activities which involve some input, process and output. An ontology is related to any specific task and also helps to make decisions after completing the task.
- Application ontologies: These ontologies are developed for any application which may include the ontologies of domain, task and top level ontology. Examples are banking application or healthcare application.

### 3.1.2 Need of Ontology in Patient Monitoring

In recent days, the necessity of ontology is unavoidable in any domain. The need for ontology is also important in the field of medicine. The reason for developing patient monitoring system ontology is listed below [1]:

- To represent and share knowledge between patient and the medical practitioners.
- To retrieve information based on semantics, unlike syntactic search.
- To apply the reasoning mechanism to validate the correctness of the ontology.
- To define the “N” number of rules.
- To share the knowledge base and its structure of information with different people.
- To support the reusability of information.
- To create the explicit knowledge base.
- To differentiate domain knowledge base from the operational knowledge base.
- To investigate domain knowledge base.

Kumari et al. follow the traditional Term Frequency Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) method and use a dictionary of data for the specific domain. The dictionary defines the synonyms of each word and the semantic similarity between words can be calculated by TF-IDF. It is an information retrieval technique in which the weights of the relevant words of documents can be calculated [6].
