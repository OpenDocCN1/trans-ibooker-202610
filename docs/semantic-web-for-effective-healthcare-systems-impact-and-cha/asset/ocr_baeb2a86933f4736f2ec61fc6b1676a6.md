# 12.6.7 Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man: OMIM

OMIM is a wide-ranging database consisting of human genes and genetic phenotypes which is available freely. OMIM consists of information about all the known Mendelian disorders and around 15,000 genes [22]. OMIM concerns the relationship between phenotype and genotype. This database was introduced in the 1960s by Dr. Victor A. McKusick as a catalog which consisted of Mendelian traits and disorders, entitled Mendelian Inheritance in Man (MIM). Now it is a project by the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) at Johns Hopkins University (USA) [22].

# 12.6.8 Orphanet Rare Disease Ontology: ORDO

Orphanet has its terminology platform called “ORDO” (Orphanet Rare Disease Ontology) for collecting information about rare diseases obtained from the Orphanet Database and find the relationship between the diseases, genes, and other relevant characteristics. These data are reusable for computational analysis [19]. It is mutually developed by Orphanet and EBI as a multilingual system dedicated to validation and literature of the resources. It resulted from the Orphanet database (www.orpha.net), which is a multilingual database that is inclined toward rare diseases that have been derived from literature and confirmed by the international experts. It incorporates a nosology, i.e., classification of rare diseases, the relationships (epidemiological data, gene–disease relations) and its connections with other terminologies (SNOMED CT, MeSH, UMLS, MedDRA), databases (UniProtKB, OMIM, HGNC, Ensembl, IUPHAR, Reactome, Geantlas), or categorizations (ICD-10).

The ontology will be kept up by Orphanet and will further be added with new data. Orphanet classifications can be browsed in the OLS view. The Orphanet Rare Disease Ontology is updated every six months and it strictly follows the OBO guidelines on disapproval of the terms. It consists of the official ontology of various rare diseases which is produced and kept by Orphanet (INSERM, US14).
