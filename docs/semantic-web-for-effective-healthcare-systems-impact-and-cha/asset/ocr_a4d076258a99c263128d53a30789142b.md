NoSQL then was updated to mean “not only SQL.” The rapid expansion of the internet since the early 2000s assisted the growing popularity of NoSQL especially in the web-scale databases that involve the management of an enormous amount of data [17, 19]. The growth of NoSQL was, heavily influenced by Google’s Bigtable and Amazon’s Dynamo systems [20].

The obsession with NoSQL among big data users is primarily its non-relational model that avoids the complex join operations of the relational database. Moreover, NoSQL allows data to be distributed in a different location and is managed using metadata. The distributed nature of NoSQL also allows it to be horizontally scalable as necessary. The freedom from schemas in inserting data makes it flexible in adding data. Being open-source software, the possibility for customization and improvement of the model is highly preferred by data users. The data replication is in NoSQL is effortless ensuring high availability of data. The application programming interface (API) provided by NoSQL is simple and is an attractive component for programmers. The ACID (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability) properties of the relational database are replaced by BASE (basically available, soft-state, and eventual consistency) property of NoSQL [17–28].

CAP (Consistency, Availability and tolerance to network Partitions) theorem, according to Eric Brewer of University of California, Berkeley, first appeared in 1998 and was published first in 1999 and later in 2000. It is considered midway between the ACID property of relational database and BASE property of NoSQL. According to the theorem only any one of the properties, that is, either consistency or availability or partition tolerance have to be compromised in the database. That is because if network partition is present, then either availability (by replication) or consistency (by holding on to only one set of databases) can be guaranteed as shown in Figure 10.1. If network partitioning is not a requirement, then consistency and availability can co-exist. The consistency spoken of in CAP differs from the consistency in ACID in the sense that consistency in CAP is about the physical consistency of data in all the nodes while the consistency in ACID is the logical consistency or integrity of the data in the database [18, 19, 21, 29].
