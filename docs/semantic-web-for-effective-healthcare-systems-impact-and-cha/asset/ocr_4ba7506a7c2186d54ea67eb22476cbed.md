Origins of these data incorporate wearable devices, which follow weight, heart rate, physical activity, nutrition, compliance to prescribed medications, geographical location, and hence, accumulated across duration of moments to ages. The whole received data, without sequencing the germline, return a specific time spot in an individual’s health path [3].

Despite Electronic Medical Records report many data types, they sometimes hinder examining patient-level, molecular or big data along with medical data; that is because of broken, mistaken, an unidentified origin or missing detailed records. Such issues reflect a framework which is more concerned by invoices than reporting clinically crucial patient’s data. Gene-level, wearable devices, behavioral and ecological data are hard to trace and are not consistently gathered or embedded; that is because they are sometimes locked in text files which might be challenging for a computer to analyze in formal research areas. Among these types of challenges, ontologies provide interoperable infrastructure through semantic web technologies to support several statistical aggregations in medical domains.

Advanced contributions of ontologies (UMLS, GO and the EcoCyc Ontology) for biomedical datasets are critical to understand processing of biomedical works. Bioinformatics provides novel insight to deal with upcoming problems using high-level concepts of biology, computer, mathematics and statistical methods to retrieve, analyze, and manage data with effective cost and time frames. Use of bioinformatics tools and applications, with facility of natural language processing, for biomedical data enables managing it according to web semantic analysis for text summarization or document clustering. The patient phenotypic data are sometimes inaccessible or missing details, hence impeding the determination of resemblances and sorting patients into medically relevant categories. These procedures are problematic and crucial in conditions possessing a range of genotypes, biomarkers, signs, and symptoms, which might not be demonstrated in every patient. However, precision medicine aims primarily at applying such data to allocate patients and distinguish resemblances among unique diseases [3]. Therefore, the use of ontologies, with the feature of semantic web technologies, can provide many promising solutions via capturing broad range of concepts in biomedical fields.
