## 5.3 Health Analytics

Healthcare analytics is a type of data analytics used by healthcare professionals that would improve health system management, patient diagnosis, and spending [23]. It is broadly classified into three types as shown in Figure 5.4.

**Descriptive analytics** as the name implies refers to the statistical inference of the data which can be used for other operations in a useful way [9]. ### 5.3.1 Artificial Intelligence/Machine Learning-Based Predictive Analytics

Healthcare Analytics and Artificial Intelligence (AI)/Machine Learning (ML) go hand in hand to reach out to the patients without them even realizing it [18]. Machine Learning can be deployed for various pivotal applications in healthcare such as machine learning based behavior modifications, clinical trial and research, and epidemic prediction. Moreover, machine learning for image analysis is more popular in healthcare (for example, cancer cell drug sensitivity prediction drug targeted interaction strength prediction, etc.).

Currently, image-based diagnostics focus on machine learning based on deep learning and radionics. These aim to extract, structured, reproducible, quantitative, machine-readable information out of medical images, and supplement the prognosis of a medical practitioner.

However, traditional machine learning has some limitations to handle diverse, multifactorial, multi-dimension, highly interrelated healthcare data. This is primarily because

- Artificial intelligence/ML, require a lot of data. This can represent an issue in healthcare when endeavoring to apply a Deep Learning algorithm to disease processes with less predominance. This is further complemented by the fact that clinical information is not common among multiple systems.
- Absence of information sharing framework to healthcare institutions, heterogeneity and deficiency in the assortment of information, and completion between institution/organization.
- This FAIR (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable) guideline ensures that the data is findable, by being assigned a globally unique and persistent identifier [31]. By being accessible the data with metadata should be easily available for free. Interoperable is that feature that the data must use a formal, accessible, shared form of knowledge representation.

It is not able to ensure that the clinical information is transferred and interpreted between multiple systems. Predictive analytics generally get employed by traditional AI/ML techniques.

### 5.3.2 Semantic Technology for Prescriptive Health Analytics

As quoted above, the traditional artificial intelligence (AI)/ML techniques do not address the issue of heterogeneity, interpretability, and sharing of information between multiple systems. However, all these issues can be addressed by semantic technologies and which will also be useful for discovering actionable content from the data. Semantic technology is a tool to establish a semantic link among the various underlined data sources, which will help meaningful ways of a coherent understanding of information that can be converted in terms of actionable information.

Semantic technologies are “meaning-centered.” Semantic Technology develops its languages to express rich self-describing interrelation of the data and linking it in a form that is processable by the machine, despite it being in a long string of characters and index tons of data [32]. It also identifies the meaning and logical relationship of the data and can store manage and restrict its information.
