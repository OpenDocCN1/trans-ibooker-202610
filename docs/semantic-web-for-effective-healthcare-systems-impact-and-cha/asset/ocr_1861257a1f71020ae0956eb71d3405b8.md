# 10.5.1 Curation of Knowledge Base for CDSS

The process of selecting, organizing, and managing entities of a knowledge base is a complex task. The data has to be extracted from structured and unstructured sources and integrated into the existing knowledge base accurately and efficiently capable of creating visualized reports. A manual curation process has the efficiency of producing validated and timely knowledge [112], but the cost and scalability of the manually populated knowledge base make it inefficient. Collaborative curation can mitigate the shortcoming to some extent but it does not solve the problem [113]. Due to the scarcity of human resources for the manual curation process, it is estimated that it takes an average of 1 year to translate the knowledge from the source to the knowledge base [114]. The exiting computational population and curation methods have scalability limitation when it comes to harnessing a huge volume of knowledge available. The computational complexity involved in the deductive process results in proportionally very small knowledge bases [115]. There are studies conducted on the application of RDF Knowledge Graphs for information retrieval from literature or more with other huge sources of clinical data like the Electronic Health Records [116]. There has been a lot of research in building a knowledge base from semi-structured and unstructured data. The traditional ontology-based information extraction tries to populate the existing knowledge base using the canonical identifiers already found in the knowledge base. Information extraction does not include new entities or relationships. The open information extraction includes new entities and relationships that are discovered in the literature. For CDSS, the categorization of information extraction as targeted and non-targeted is more relevant since, the knowledge base is meant only for clinical decision support. Therefore, the method of information retrieval from medical journal only and using only medical jargoned entities is a targeted or focused information extraction, while those that apply to all documents without any distinction is non-targeted or unfocussed [114].
