## Rare Disease Diagnosis as Information Retrieval Task

Jaya Lakkakula, Rutuja Phate, Alfiya Korbu and Sagar Barage*

Amity Institute of Biotechnology, Amity University, Mumbai, Maharashtra, India

## Abstract

Rare diseases are defined as a condition which affects a small percentage of the population. It is also known as orphan diseases due to lack of attention, support, and resources available from the government as well as the private sector to develop therapeutics. The purpose of this chapter is to provide information on rare diseases. It explores the relationship between rare diseases, diagnosis, and information retrieval. In particular, it illustrates the history, characteristics, types, and classification along with databases of rare disease information. We also explore challenges faced by researchers in rare disease information retrieval and how they can resolve by optimizing search query. Specifically, the section with rare disease information database and information retrieval through Google/semantic web search gives a wealth of useful information to clinicians and medical practitioners for rare disease diagnosis. Moreover, a rare disease research institute is useful to a young researcher for career development. Finally, the information on the rare disease is available under a single umbrella useful for academicians, researchers, and medical practitioners.

Keywords: Rare disease, rare disease database, rare disease diagnosis, information retrieval

## 12.1 Introduction

Rare diseases are disorders that are found in an extremely small population of people. This can also be referred to as a disorder. The rareness of the disease varies in different regions and countries. Generally, a disease found in fewer than five people in a population of 10,000 people is termed as a rare disease [1]. It is also called “orphan diseases” and affects a very scarce population worldwide numbering hardly tens to hundreds of a community [1]. They are being studied and regulated worldwide promoting various aids for research and development with around 6000–8000 rare diseases, with mostly 250–280 added annually [2]. However, most of them are less frequent and cannot even be treated competently. Recognition of these rare diseases is a prevailing challenge for global healthcare. For an approximation, 6% to 8% of the population internationally, is suffering from rare diseases. In India, 72 to 96 million people suffering from rare diseases [3]. Up till now, about 450 rare diseases have been documented from tertiary care hospitals in India [3]. Rare diseases can be caused due to inherited mutations, de novo dominant mutations, or mosaic mutations. Some of the examples of rare diseases are cystic fibrosis, IgA nephropathy, rare forms of cancer, X-linked severe combined immunodeficiency, Gaucher disease, etc. [1, 4, 5].

Being affected by a “rare” disease eventually states the fact that it is rare to find the disease as no ancestral records of the disease are found. The whole family is affected somehow relating to each other but the source is unexplained or unknown [5]. Rare diseases often result in impairment of health conditions and abnormal functioning. It is a pathological condition where infections, genetic defects, or other environmental stresses are observed in any part or organ of the body. These symptoms can be grouped as a basis for observation of characterization, mostly identified by its low prevalence [5]. These uncommon diseases tend to create a lot of difficulties and challenges for the affected individuals for proper diagnosis and treatment. The insufficiency of constructive treatment for a maximum of rare diseases and when diagnosed, its expensive treatment, and other scientific interrogations make these cases difficult to settle [2]. Like most, these diseases are asymptomatic or may not show symptoms immediately as they may be genetic or appear in early life, their diagnosis becomes difficult. The majority of the rare diseases are genetic and there exists a wide heterogeneity.

There are various diagnostic web tools, biochemical and genetic tests for diagnosing the atypical symptoms of rare diseases [6]. As these are solely

the common laboratory tests, they might fail to cover all the specific symptoms. Hence, this can lead to a delayed and improper correct diagnosis of rare diseases [6]. Physicians currently are increasingly using the web and internet-based tools for diagnosing and treating rare diseases effectively. Due to the rarity of these disorders, it has been challenging to ensure proper diagnosis until there were initiatives taken towards the use of web bases tools [6]. Advancements in technology opened possibilities to find resources of correct information. Web search engines, semantic web tools, databases, general medical literature platforms, etc. have turned up to be the most popular interfaces for rare diseases diagnosis [6]. These interfaces help us obtain a list of rare diseases, their symptoms, known diagnosis, other specialized and general search about rare diseases, medical repositories, etc. FindZebra, Google, PubMed are some of the widely used search engines [6].

The main objective of this paper is to emphasize web designed systems to ensure proper treatment and diagnosis of these rare diseases. Internet and other web tools which are widely used nowadays can prove a very efficient and excellent means to provide the diagnostic capabilities to medical professionals, clinicians, and non-professionals too. These initiatives can prove useful as a means of knowledge and potential enough for accurate diagnostic aids.

## 12.2 Definition

There is no such specific definition for rare diseases that would be accepted universally. More or less, few of these definitions place reliance on the population size of affected individuals whereas others are focused on other factors like treatments available or severity of the disease [4]. Overall, 1109 organizations or agencies have been searched from around 32 jurisdictions that resulted in identifying 296 rare disease definitions. The most repeatedly defined term was “rare diseases” resulting in 112 definitions. This term was included in about 38% of all identified definitions and has been defined more frequently than “orphan diseases.” Seventy-nine definitions included the term “orphan drug(s)” among those identified [7].

There is currently no standard definition for rare diseases in India [3]. ORDI has suggested a disease to be defined as a rare disease if it is found to affect 1 in every 5000 people roughly, taking into consideration the huge population of India.
