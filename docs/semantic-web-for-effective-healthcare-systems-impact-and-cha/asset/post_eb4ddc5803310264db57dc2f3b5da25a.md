## 7.2 The Necessity for Surgical Robots

Minimally Invasive Surgery has been subjected to various forms, however, Robotic Surgery can be said to be the most advanced of it all. The earliest works of robotics and automation trace back to 400 B.C., however, its application in the field of surgery has only been 20 years old [3].

A major role towards the establishment and development of the field has been a result of the pioneering efforts of surgeons like Archytas of Arentum and visionaries like Vinci, and Jaquet-Droz. They were the first of all to envision the idea of Minimally Invasive Surgery which acted as a founding stone toward the rise of robotic surgery.

Minimally invasive surgery was first performed in 1987. It was a laparoscopic cholecystectomy. As the list of procedures performed laparoscopically grew at an ever-increasing rate, so did the technological advancement and general refinement of the skill of surgeons. Minimally invasive surgery was an idea that the masses immediately bought upon its conception. MIS offered the public and surgeons a great number of advantages. Minimally Invasive Surgery opened a world of smaller incisions, reduced infection risks, and shorter stays in the hospital. It also enables the option to significantly reduce convalescence depending on need.

Though MIS attracted the masses and provided a simplistic system for surgery, it involved a lot of technical and mechanical difficulties. The deficit of haptic feedback, dexterity, and eye-hand coordination posed major difficulties for the surgeons. An example of the above being the movement of laparoscopic instruments in the opposite direction of interest to reach it. This nullified the coordination between hand and eye. This situation is also referred to as the “fulcrum effect.”

There are 7 degrees of freedom in the human hand-wrist, this refers to the fact that the hand can independently move and rotate in a total of seven unique directions. However, the current laparoscopic instruments have only 4 degrees of freedom which again posed difficulties to the surgeons. These instruments also lack the sense of touch which makes procedures regarding manipulation of tissue visualization-dependent. Also, the physiologic tremors that were generated in a surgeon’s hand while the operation was now transmitted readily across rigid instruments’ length.

These limitations accounted for the reason why performing MIS with automated laparoscopic instruments makes the anastomoses and delicate dissections difficult if not impossible. The burning necessity to overcome such limitations laid the founding stones towards the conception of surgical robots.

## 7.4 Inferences Drawn From the Table

Two hundred twenty-one robotic surgeries (112 [50.6%] urology, 53 [23.9%] general surgery, 46 [20.8%] gynecology, and 10 [4.5%] ENT department) were performed during the study period. Patients’ median age was 61 (17–91) years. The youngest patient was operated on in the ENT department and the oldest in the general surgery department. The majority of patients were male, and most (63.3%) had ASA-II scores. Hypertension was the most frequent underlying disease. The distribution of types of surgical procedures is shown in Table 7.1.

Only 10 (4.7%) patients required laparotomy procedures. The mean duration of anesthesia and surgery was the longest in general surgery department operations. Urology department patients had the longest time to discharge. The Trendelenburg position was used only during urological and gynecological operations.

Hypotension was the most frequent intraoperative complication encountered during robotic surgeries, followed by metabolic acidosis, and bradycardia-lingual or buccal nerve damage was seen in any department. Nausea was the most frequent postoperative complication after robotic surgeries, followed by hypoxia and metabolic acidosis. No cardiac or respiratory arrest, laryngospasm, myocardial ischemia, or visual disturbance was seen in any department postoperatively. Both intra- and post-operative complications were most common in the urology department. Neither any intra- nor any post-operative complications were encountered during the ENT operations.
