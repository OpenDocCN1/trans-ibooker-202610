Although there is much progress in knowledge and NLP techniques, the results are not yet significantly satisfactory to use free text without any constraints in clinical documentation. This section presents select research trends from this growing interdisciplinary field to draw the attention of the reader to the complexities of the underlying research problems that makes finding a practical solution difficult and elusive.

# 13.3.1 Semantic Data Analysis

Since the late twentieth century, informaticists and data scientists are tirelessly exploring novel solutions to extract, standardize, and analyze health data locked as narrative free-text in health data repositories. Advances in cognitive computing tools offer exciting opportunities in integrating NLP techniques with everyday clinical care.

For the medical informatics generalist, Nadkarni et al. [25] offer an overview and tutorial of NLP and design of modern NLP systems. Starting with the historical evolution of NLP, the authors describe key NLP sub-problems and draw attention of the reader to selected medical NLP efforts. Velupillai et al. [26] review advances in clinical NLP used to analyze clinical texts semantically. The review focuses on three thematic areas: (1) corpus creation, (2) semantic building blocks, and (3) clinical utility. Nenadic et al. [27] investigate suitability of different types of NLP tasks and their applications when supported by machine learning algorithms. They also explore the needed properties that must be present in the text data used for training the model. They show how data annotation is one of the key impediments in wide spread adoption of clinical machine learning. Deng et al. [28] show a way of addressing the annotation obstacle in medical procedure classification by building a neural network-based processing pipeline for query matching. In particular, they show how adding related concepts to the original input of the semantic network improves semantic matching.

Recognizing the relations between patient problems, clinical treatments, and medical tests is one of the most important capabilities required for cognitive processing of clinical texts. To find the relation between medical concepts and to assign them semantic types, Rink et al. [29] use a single support vector machine classifier.
