TORS offers the surgeons various advantages. Nonrobotic procedures limit the surgical access and therefore restrict Transoral resections’ indications of malignant neoplasms to a restricted area of the tonsillar fossa. TORS, on the other hand, uses the endoscopic optics of high-quality for increased instrument mobility of surgical robot which further facilitates operative and wide-reaching Transoral resection of tonsillar carcinoma. This is done concurrently with decreasing the added indisposition often associated with open surgical processes [13–20]. Today, the use of TORS is not only limited to tonsillectomy. It’s also being used in similar arenas of supraglottic partial laryngectomy, canine model of supraglottic laryngectomy, advanced oropharyngeal carcinoma, and head and neck cancer surgery as well.

# 7.6 Pancreatoduodenectomy

The pancreas is an organ that holds a crucial role in the digestion and regulation of blood sugar. There are mainly three diseases that strike the pancreas—Diabetes, Pancreatitis, and Pancreatic Cancer.

Standing boldly at the fourth position of the foremost root of mortality in the USA is pancreatic cancer [11, 26–30]. The odds of survival further fall low, when its seen statistically that a very small percentage of diagnosed patients (over 50,000 in the year 2019) is operable [12]. These call for the need for automation of the same process and early diagnosis of the same given that pancreatoduodenectomy is itself a challenging procedure for the best of the surgeons. Today as the debate over the advantages between robotic PD and laparoscopic or “open” PD continue, it has been 10 years since surgeons have started using the Da Vinci systems while considering pancreatuoducenomy [2]. A recent study on the literature written about the pancreatoduodenectomy shows that while RPD (Robotic PD) offers theoretical advantages over laparoscopic PD by improved instrument dexterity, better ergonomics, and 3D visualizations practically stand at par with the laparoscopic method [4]. However, this does not deny the fact the advantages it offers weigh out its other aspects.

Currently, it is safe to say that robotic-assisted PD is better suited to patients. This is based on the general observed rate of faster recovery post-operatively alongside the reduced blood loss during the surgery [21]. This also calls the fact that a more comprehensive RCT is badly needed to help us draw more tactful conclusions regarding robotic PD and its manifestations on the patients’ signs post-operation.
