# 12.4.1 Genetic Causes

Rare diseases can be caused by alterations in single or multiple genes. According to experts, the rarest of diseases are genetic in origin, almost more than 80 percent [10]. Generally, a genetic disease is classified into two types: single gene disorder (monogenic) where a specific gene is mutated or permanently altered, and multiple gene disorders where more than one gene is altered. This mutation can be due to failure of repair mechanism, environmental factors, or other reasons. These are inherited depending on their inheritance pattern i.e. autosomal dominant or recessive, X linked or Y linked. Depending on this pattern, rare diseases can be inherited by two carriers (both affected parent) or by a single carrier (single affected parent) from generation to generation. Following are some examples of inherited rare diseases listed below:

+   I) Von Hipple–Lindau syndrome (VHL) [11, 12] affects numerous organ systems in the body by the formation of cysts and benign tumors with potency to transform into malignant form affecting various body parts like kidneys, uterus, pancreas, and nervous system. It is caused by a mutation in a specific tumor suppressor gene (chromosome 3) and is inherited in an autosomal dominant manner. Studies show that 80% of patients are due to heredity and the rest are due to different mutations [12].

+   II) Chronic granulomatous disease (CGD), [11] is a genetic disease inherited by an autosomal recessive X-linked pattern. The affected person loses resistance to various infectious diseases. According to reports, this is a mutation on the X chromosome and defect on chromosome 7 (CYBG gene) and chromosome 16 (NCFI gene). Mutations can interfere with the WBC’s ability to produce oxygen compounds that are essential to kill harmful pathogens. On the contrary, the immune system can produce granulomas in various parts of the body, thereby isolating bacteria [12].
