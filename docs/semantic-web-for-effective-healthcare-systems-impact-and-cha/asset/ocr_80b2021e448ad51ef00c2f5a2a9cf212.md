# 9.1 Introduction

Precision medicine (PM) is a way to treat and prevent diseases that aims to maximize effectiveness by taking into account variation in genes, environmental factors [1]. The PM network also connects patients, cares, clinical laboratories and prospective investigations [2]. With the rapid expansion of personalized medicine, the amounts of heterogeneous clinical data and scientific information are exponentially growing, where valuable knowledge such as functional biomedical units and their semantine relations are inaccessible. One crucial question is how to know and use the knowledge to assist PM. By incorporating, classifying and standardizing these biomedical tools from different resources and establishing relationships between them, terminologies, classifications and ontologies may provide varying degrees of complexity in computational formats, enabling enormous data collections to be analyzed and a patient to be properly categorized [3]. Construction of these knowledge models is necessary for PM studies, and appropriate.

Ontology is a powerful technique for representing pragmatically and understanding biomedicine. In the same contexts precision or personalized medicine is a novel model in the concept of ontologies that endures the outstanding confidence for individualized patient care with incremental manners. The framework of ontologies is emerging to improve patients’ diagnosis and treatment via providing easy tools and methods of medicinal decision support system level for multiple reasoning mechanisms in precision medicines. To deal with this large datasets or data is heterogeneous in nature and collected from individual patients, here we acquired suitable methods to identify the basic knowledge in which every patient potentially different, to handle specifically changing or issues like different signs and symptoms from diseases. The Ontological information has worth to mentioning since the scientific information and clinical data are heterogeneous or hardly to manipulate. Based on classification approaches in the domain of ontologies, it provides the facility for handling disease mechanisms’ in several patients correlating top level information [4].

Terminologies have a long history of recalling information, such as searching for documents or database records that meet certain criteria [5]. Many of the most relevant medical knowledge retrieval tools include Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) for PubMed Indexing and Search; RxNorm [6], a standardized and renamed medication terminology; and UMLS (Unified Medical Language System), which contains over 100 clinical terminology and coding systems [7].
