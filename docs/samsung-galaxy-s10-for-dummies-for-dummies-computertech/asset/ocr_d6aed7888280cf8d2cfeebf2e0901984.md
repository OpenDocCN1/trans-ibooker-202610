# Video Size:

You have the option to get really carried away here. The lowest video option is HD. HD is the minimally acceptable resolution for thoroughly modern folks. However, your phone scoffs at this puny resolution, and can take it to higher settings, as seen in Figure 9-9. The only reason to hesitate to use these higher settings is that, just as with still pictures, videos recorded in higher resolutions take more memory. It is good to know these resolutions are there. You also have the ability to set different setting for the front and back camera.

The main consideration for SD cards has been their rated capacity. When you use your phone for video recording at standard resolution and with the standard frame rate, you're probably fine with a standard SD card. If you want a higher resolution or higher frame rate, you should get a card designed for faster data speeds. These days, the SD card is typically faster and is branded with “SDXC,” but if you're using an older SD card from even a few years ago, it may choke on too much data. The solution is easy: Buy a new SD card that says SDXC.
