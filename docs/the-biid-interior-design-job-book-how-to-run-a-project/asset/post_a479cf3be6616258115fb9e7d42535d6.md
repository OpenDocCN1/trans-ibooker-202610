
## P3 Explanation of ID/10 terms (continued)

It is good practice to issue monthly interim fee accounts (with the proportion due calculated by the designer as set out in clause 5.12) to maintain the designer’s cash flow, to reduce the risk of non-payment, to get the client into the habit of paying monthly and to reduce the temptation for the client not to pay a large sum due which has accumulated over several months.

5.16 Payment shall become due to the Designer on the date of issue of the Designer’s account. The final date for payment of any amount due to the Designer shall be 14 days from the issue of the relevant account.

The Designer shall issue accounts at intervals of not less than one month or as otherwise agreed, setting out any accrued instalments of the fee or other amounts due, less any amounts previously paid and stating the basis of calculation of the amounts due.

Designers would be well advised to observe this clause in order to maintain their cash flow and reduce the risk of non-payment.

The Designer shall submit the final account for fees or other amounts due under any part of the Services Schedule when the Designer reasonably considers the relevant Services have been completed.

The Services are deemed to be completed when the Designer submits such account(s).

This clause is important as it establishes the date from which the time limit for action or proceedings shown in the Project Data is calculated (in accordance with clause 7.1). The designer’s last fee account should be labelled ‘final account’.

### Project Bank Account

Written notification shall be given to the bank or other institution that the money is held on behalf of the Client; and

- 5.17.1 that the money is held on behalf of the Client; and
- 5.17.2 there is no entitlement to combine the account with any other account or to exercise any right of set-off or to counterclaim against it.

Money may only be withdrawn from the account to make a payment to or on behalf of the Client; or on the Client’s specific instructions. Any interest accruing to the account shall be paid to the Client. The Designer shall provide a statement of account at monthly intervals or on reasonable request from the Client.

Clauses 5.17 and 5.18 cover the circumstances where the designer is procuring F&FE as the agent of the client under Annex B2 (i.e. acting in a professional rather than a commercial capacity).

### Payment notices

The Client shall give a written notice to the Designer:

- within 5 days of the date of issue of an account specifying the amount the Client proposes to pay and the basis of calculation of that amount; and
- not later than 5 days before the final date for payment of any amount due to the Designer if the Client intends to withhold payment of any part of that amount stating the amount proposed to be withheld and the ground for doing so or, if there is more than one ground, each ground and the amount attributable to it.

If no such notices are given the amount due shall be the amount stated as due in the account. The Client shall not delay payment of any undisputed part of the account.

Payment notices are required by the Housing Grants Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 (or ‘HGCRA’) if a client intends to not pay or to underpay an invoice. This could be enforceable by the client if, say, there were a mathematical miscalculation of the fees due. However, alleged negligence by the designer is not an acceptable ground.

The failure to issue a payment notice within five days as a reason for enforcing payment would not be applicable with a consumer client unless the clause were agreed with him/her before he/she signed the contract (see ‘Applicable case law’: Domsala v Dyson, page 282). Therefore, this clause must be individually negotiated with a consumer client before he/she enters into the contract.

### Set-off

The Client shall not withhold any amount due to the Designer under this agreement unless the amount has been agreed with the Designer or has been decided by any tribunal to which the matter is referred as not being due to the Designer.

All rights of set-off at common law or in equity which the Client would otherwise be entitled to exercise are expressly excluded.

This is by far the most important clause in ID/10 for enforcing fee payment. It prevents a client unreasonably or misguidedly attempting to withhold fees on the grounds of alleged negligence by the designer. Without this clause the designer would have to notify his/her PI insurer who would take over the matter with a view to dealing with the negligence claim. This could take many months (more likely years) and be very expensive. Fees claimed are usually lost in the wash.

It should be made clear to the client that if they believe they have a genuine claim for negligence then they are free to pursue it but as a separate action. This would then be dealt with by the designer’s PI policy but in the meantime the designer’s charges should be paid promptly to maintain his/her cashflow.

This clause does not cover set-off for reasons other than in common law or in equity. For example, the client is entitled to reduce payment for an obvious mathematical error in calculating the fee.

## P3 Explanation of ID/10 terms (continued)

This clause must be individually negotiated with a consumer client before he/she enters into the contract. Provided the clause is agreed, the relevant statute (UTCCR 1999, clause 5(1)) shows that this clause is fully enforceable.

If a client objects to this clause, then the designer could protect him/herself by requiring all fees to be paid in advance of the design work being carried out. However, if the client makes such an objection, perhaps he/she is not intending to pay – something it is far better for the designer to learn before any resources are spent on the job.

### Late payment

In the event that any amounts are not paid by the Client or the Designer when properly due, the payee shall be entitled to simple interest on such amounts until the date that payment is received at the daily rate equivalent to 8% over the dealing rate of the Bank of England Rate current at the date that payment becomes overdue, together with such costs reasonably incurred and duly mitigated by the payee (including costs of time spent by principals, employees and advisors) in obtaining payment of any sums due under this Agreement.

The payee's entitlement to interest at the specified rate shall also apply in respect of any amounts that are awarded in adjudication, arbitration or legal proceedings.

It should be emphasised that this provides for interest charges at 8% above base rate, which between 1975 and 2008 had historically averaged about 6% pa – giving a total of about 14% pa interest chargeable in normal times. If a potential client were to object to such a rate, the designer should point out that it is mandated in law – The Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Rate of Interest) (No. 3) Order 2002 – for commercial clients. This term should encourage clients to borrow from their bankers, not from their designer.

### Recovery of costs

The Client or the Designer shall pay to the other party who successfully pursues, resists or defends any claim or part of a claim brought by the other:

- such costs reasonably incurred and duly mitigated (including costs of time spent by principals, employees and advisors) where the matter is resolved by negotiation or mediation; or
- such costs as may be determined by any tribunal to which the matter is referred.

Normally, the cost of a claimant's own time spent collecting sums due is not recoverable in a legal action. A legal action can take considerable time, and (along with his/her reputation) the designer's time is all he/she has to sell. This term provides that one party can charge such time (which in the preparation for and participation in a legal action can be considerable) against the other party if:

- the claimant is ultimately successful in defending a claim against him/her by the client, or
- the claimant abandons a claim or part of a claim.
