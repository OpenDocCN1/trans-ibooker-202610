### 2.7 Designer's Representative

The Designer’s Representative shall have full authority to act on behalf of the Designer for all purposes in connection with performance of the Services but not to vary the terms of the Agreement.

Clients often like to know who in the designer’s organisation will be in charge of their project. If this person changes, the designer should remember to notify the client.

### 2.8 Estimates

When the Designer is undertaking the Building Works, Decorations and/or supplying F&E as Principal, any cost or time estimates prepared by the Designer shall not be construed as offers unless submitted as tenders in accordance with Parts 3 and 4 of the Services schedule.

This clause is to distinguish between cost and time estimates of the design which the designer does not warrant and tenders which the designer might submit if he/she is acting as contractor and/or retailer and which are binding if accepted.

### 2.9 Photography and publicity

The Designer shall have the right to take and publish photographs of the Project, and the Client shall give reasonable access to the Project for this purpose for 2 years after practical completion of the Project. The Designer shall obtain the consent of the Client, which consent shall not be unreasonably withheld or delayed, before publication of any other information about the Project, unless reasonably necessary for performance of the Services.

Many clients are understandably concerned to protect their privacy and perhaps not advertise expensive possessions. However, this clause allows the designer to have photographs of the completed project published in his/her portfolio or website and in publications. The designer should obtain the client’s consent before publishing the client’s name or the project address. For large projects the two-year period may have to be extended.

### 2.10 Confidentiality

The Designer shall not disclose to any other person Confidential Information unless:

- 2.10.1 disclosure is necessary for the proper performance of the Services, or in order to take professional advice in relation to this Agreement or the Services, or in order to obtain/maintain insurance cover as required by this Agreement;
- 2.10.2 is in the public domain other than due to wrongful use or disclosure; or
- 2.10.3 disclosure is required by law or because of disputes arising out of or in connection with this Agreement.

Designers should be wary of potential clients attempting to impose stricter confidentiality terms as these may not allow the designer to perform the services effectively.

## OBLIGATIONS AND AUTHORITY OF THE CLIENT

The clauses in this section are important for the smooth running of the project and should be brought to the attention of any inexperienced client at the beginning.

### Client's Representative

The Client's Representative shall have full authority to act on behalf of the Client for all purposes in connection with the matters set out in this Agreement but not to vary the terms of the Agreement. Information, decisions, approvals etc.

It should be clear who in the client organisation is responsible for instructing the designer so as to avoid conflicting instructions being given independently by various, perhaps unauthorised, persons. This person should be named in the Project Data. If this person changes, the client should notify the designer.

The Client shall provide, free of charge, all the information in the Client's possession, or reasonably obtainable, which is necessary for the proper and timely performance of the Services and the Designer shall be entitled to rely on such information.

The designer should not normally be responsible for providing, or for the accuracy of, the information on which he/she will base the design. Otherwise, the designer may be carrying out work for which he/she is not qualified and so may not be covered by his/her PII policy. If this information is unavailable, then the designer can arrange (as an 'Other Service' in the Schedule of Interior Design Services) for this to be provided by others, always ensuring that the client contracts directly with the provider. Alternatively, if the designer is qualified to provide this service, then he/she can provide this and it should be so noted in the designer's appointment letter (see note to clause 2.4.4 regarding surveys).

The Client shall give decisions and approvals and shall take such actions necessary for the proper and timely performance of the Services.

The client must make decisions in time for the designer to be able to carry out his/her work in a timely manner.

The Client shall supply the Brief and shall advise the relative priorities of the Brief, the Project Cost and the Timetable.

It is almost never possible to equally optimise scope, cost and time. Ideally the client should advise the designer of his/her priorities at the beginning of the job, and the designer should refer to this in the designer's appointment letter. Clients often have unrealistic expectations as to what can be achieved for their budget and timescale. Curiously, some clients are reluctant to even tell the designer what their budget is, perhaps because they have not resolved this or are worried that the designer will expend unnecessarily large sums if their budget is too generous. Far too often the desired budget and timetable are substantially less than their brief implies. The designer can help the client concentrate his/her mind on these matters by encouraging him/her to decide what their priorities are.

### Instructions

The Client (or the Lead Consultant, or other Consultant designated by the Client) may issue instructions to the Designer, subject to the Designer's right of reasonable objection.

The client has the right to instruct the designer provided the client is not requiring the designer to, say, contravene the CDM Regulations or to carry out the design work in an impractical or unprofessional way.

Where the Designer, as Lead Consultant or Contract Administrator, has responsibility to direct and/or co-ordinate the work or services of or give instructions to Other Persons, such instructions shall be issued only through the Designer.

If the Client or anyone acting on behalf of the Client, issues instructions to any Other Persons the Designer shall not be responsible for the consequences of such instructions.

It is not uncommon for a client to well-meaningly directly instruct the contractor on site, perhaps without regard to other aspects of the design or even in contravention of building or planning regulations. Designers should draw this clause to the attention of the client before construction starts and point out that the remedy of any problems caused may attract additional fees (see 'J Model letter to client before construction', page 200 and 'J Model letter to contractor before construction', page 198).

### Applications for consent

The Client shall instruct the making of applications for consents under planning legislation, building acts, regulations or other statutory requirements and by others having an interest in the Project. The Client shall pay any statutory charges and any fees, expenses and disbursements in respect of such applications.

When the designer is asked to apply for planning permission, building regulations approval and/or other statutory requirements, he/she should exercise the 'reasonable skill, care and diligence' required in clause 2.1. The designer has the right of reasonable objection to carry out a task beyond the designer's expertise and/or resources and should in these cases advise the client to take specialist advice.

### Appointment of Other Persons

Where work or services, other than those to be performed by the Designer, are required, the Client shall appoint and pay Other Persons under separate agreements to perform such work or services, shall hold such Other Persons responsible for the competence and performance of their work or services, and shall require them to Collaborate with the Designer.

The Client shall confirm in writing to the Designer the services to be performed by Other Persons, their disciplines and the expected duration of their employment.

When it becomes evident that specialist advice is required it is important that consultants are directly appointed by the client. If they are appointed by the designer, he/she becomes responsible for the consultant's work and should obtain evidence of the consultant's PII policy and a written commitment that the policy will be maintained for as long as the time limit for action shown in the appointment letter. The designer should notify his/her own PII insurer of the arrangement and should consider whether to charge an additional fee for this risk.
