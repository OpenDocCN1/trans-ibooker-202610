# # P3 How to use ID/10

# ## Appointment

The appointment contract between client and designer should contain several parts:

+   - appointment letter
+   - Conditions of Appointment
+   - Services Schedule
+   - Schedule of Fees and Expenses
+   - Project Data Schedule

together with the following (if supplied by the client) to be attached to the executed copies of the appointment:

+   - brief
+   - attachments.

# ## Conditions

ID/10 is conveniently bound together in a single booklet containing the Conditions of Appointment, a Services Schedule, notes and model letters. The designer should always tear out the notes and model letters (the centre white pages) and, if the services are scheduled in the appointment letter, remove the Services Schedule as well.

Two copies of ID/10 should be used, one for the client and one for the designer’s file. The box at the bottom of page 1 should be completed in both copies.

Depending on what functions the designer will be performing, the following sections of ID/10 will apply:

+   - when the designer is acting purely as a designer, only the main conditions (clauses 1.1 through 10.4)
+   - when acting as a contractor, also Annex A
+   - when procuring FF&E as an agent, also Annex B, parts B1 and B2
+   - when supplying FF&E as a principal, i.e. as a retailer, also Annex B, parts B1 and B3.

Those parts of the agreement that do not apply should be struck out with a diagonal line.

# ## Negotiated clauses

Consumers are protected by legislation, in particular the Unfair Terms in Consumer Contracts Regulations 1999 (UTCCR 99). If the client is a consumer, then the designer should explain the terms of the proposed design contract, taking the client through the contract, clause by clause, even at the risk of inducing boredom. It should be explained that, as a professional, the designer is looking after the client’s interests in the expenditure of large sums of their money.
