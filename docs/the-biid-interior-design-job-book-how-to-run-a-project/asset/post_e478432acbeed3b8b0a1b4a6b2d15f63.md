
## D Planning applications

### Discussions
Many projects require the consent of the local planning authority. Once a concept design is agreed with the client, it is advisable to contact the local planning authority to ascertain whether planning and/or listed building consent is required, and if so, to discuss the proposals with the neighbourhood planning and/or listed buildings officer, preferably on site. The designer should make a note of the matters discussed and confirm them in writing to the planning officer so that there is no misunderstanding as to what is required. Planning officers are not normally responsible for determining an application (unless it is in regard to minor matters for which they have been given delegated powers) but their recommendations to the Planning Committee can be important in obtaining a favourable decision.

### Outline consent
If the principle of the proposed scheme is in question, it can be useful to test the water without carrying out much design work by making an application for outline planning consent. The application could be accompanied by simple drawings showing the footprint and height of the building relative to any neighbours’ buildings.

### Detailed consent
Normally it is sensible to apply just for a detailed consent, submitting plans showing the disposition of all rooms, and elevations showing the external appearance and materials.

### Listed buildings
If the building is listed, i.e. officially designated as being of special architectural, historical or cultural significance, a listed building consent will be required, even for schemes that do not affect the external appearance of the building.

## D Notes on consents record

### Consents

A record of all statutory and legal consents should be kept in the job file, first as a reminder of what consents remain to be obtained and later as a record of what was decided, in case the designer has to refer to the consents or apply to amend them.

It is important not to proceed with the project until the relevant consents are received (unless the client expressly requests this) as there is a risk that the consents might not be granted and the accelerated work wasted.

Generally, planning consent and, if applicable, listed building consent and landlord's licence, would be applied for after stage D, Design Development (although for simple projects this might be after stage c, Concept), and building regulations approval and party wall negotiations after stage e, Technical Design, has been completed.

### Client's risk

When it is questionable whether one or more of the necessary consents can be obtained, the client must take a risk of commissioning preliminary work to the relevant stage (and paying the application fees) in order to obtain a decision. In these cases, the designer should spell this risk out clearly to the client and remind him/her that under ID/10 clause 2.4, the designer does not warrant that these applications will be successful.

## D Notes on outline specification

### Outline

The outline specification is developed from ‘c Specimen materials schedule’, page 123 and will form the basis of ‘t Specimen Specification’, pages 151 and 152. The purpose of these documents is to provide information for cost estimating and to inform the client of the designer’s intentions as the design of the project progresses. For small jobs, the outline specification may be omitted and the construction specification developed straight from the materials schedule.

The basic materials for the major elements of the project having been shown in the materials schedule, the purpose of the outline specification is to indicate in more detail the materials proposed. There is no need at this stage to indicate details of workmanship unless it has an effect on the appearance or suitability of the material being proposed.

The outline specification should be supported by sample boards/boxes showing actual pieces of fabric and materials proposed.

### Arrangement

Whilst the materials schedule is set out in ci/sfb building elemental form, the outline and construction specifications are organised in the Common Arrangement of Work Sections for Building Works format.
