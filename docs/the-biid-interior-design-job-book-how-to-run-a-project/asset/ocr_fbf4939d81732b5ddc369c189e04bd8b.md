# # £C Notes on invoicing

**Invoice forms** A number of different forms should be used when invoicing:

**Expenses schedule** Generally clients are prepared to accept a summary of expenses when they amount to a relatively small sum, but when the amount is large, e.g. for overseas travel, then these can be shown in a detailed expenses schedule and its totals taken to the fee schedule (see ‘£C Specimen expenses schedule’, page 254).

**Disbursements schedule** This form shows any disbursements (e.g. FF&E items purchased for the client) incurred and its totals also taken to the fee schedule (see ‘£C Specimen disbursements schedule’, page 255).

**Fee schedule** This form summarises the fees, expenses and disbursements incurred and its totals taken to the payment application. Two examples of fee schedules are shown: where fees are calculated on just an hourly basis (see ‘£C Specimen fee schedule (hourly charging basis)’, page 250) or alternatively on various bases, e.g. hourly, percentage, lump sum (see ‘£C Specimen final fee schedule (several charging bases)’, page 256).

**Payment application** This form summarises all the charges due including VAT together with a statement of account noting any unpaid previous applications (see ‘£C Specimen payment application’, page 251). Very straightforward payment applications can sometimes be delivered without supporting material but generally they are accompanied at least by a fee schedule to demonstrate how charges are made up (and sometimes also by an expenses schedule and/or a disbursements schedule if required).

Under clause 5.16 of ID/10, the designer’s services are deemed to have been completed when the final account is submitted for payment. This establishes the date from which the time limit for action or proceedings shown in the appointment letter (clause 7.1) is calculated. The designer’s last fee account should be labelled ‘final application’. Should the designer later be approached by the client to carry out additional work on the building, the designer should make it clear that this is a wholly new project by entering into a separate design contract with the client and by giving the job a new name and new file number.

**Sales invoice** This form is issued after payment is received (see ‘£C Specimen sales invoice’, page 253) and serves as a notice of receipt of payment.

See also ‘£C Charging options’, page 246 and ‘p3 Notes on Schedule of Fees and Expenses’, page 42.

**Numbering** The invoice, application and schedule number refer to the list of all applications/invoices/schedules issued by the practice. The claim number refers to the number of applications/invoices/schedules issued on the project in question.

**VAT** A liability to pay value added tax (VAT) arises when an invoice is issued. As clients sometimes take a long time to pay, a situation can occur where the VAT becomes due before the invoice is paid, thus causing possible cash flow problems for the designer. Therefore the system described above: first issuing a ‘payment application’ (essentially an invoice under a different name) and sending an invoice only after payment has been received, has become established with HM Revenue and Customs approval.

The sum due should be shown in the practice’s VAT accounts only after payment is received and a VAT invoice issued. The date when the VAT invoice is issued then becomes the VAT ‘tax point’.
