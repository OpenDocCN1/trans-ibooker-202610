# E Notes on specifications and schedules

# Specification

The construction specification is developed from ‘d Specimen outline specification’ page 137 and covers three areas:
* Preliminaries: the general conditions that are inserted into the building contract form such as the names of the parties, the site etc. together with the general requirements for mobilisation, running the project and general technical matters.
* Components (sometimes called materials): the elements that are to be used, specified by brand or referenced to an applicable British Standard (bS).
* Execution (sometimes called workmanship): the assembly of components into the building, again, often referenced to a bS.

# NBS

In order to reduce the work necessary to produce a reliable specification, nbs (now part of RIBA Enterprises) developed many years ago what has become the industry standard, the National Building Specification. It is essentially a library of clauses from which the designer can choose those that are applicable.
* The NBS ‘Domestic Specification’ is a loose-leaf paper specification for use on small domestic projects. Unlike their other products, this is not sold on subscription, but can be purchased when required.
* The NBS ‘Building Minor Works’ provides both paper and digital specification clauses, guidance and product information which are updated three times a year. The guidance for each clause appears beside it on screen for speedy referral. It too is updated three times a year.
* The NBS ‘Scheduler’ enables the designer to write work schedules for refurbishment, alteration and small-scale new-build projects without bills of quantities. As the work schedule is compiled, a reference specification is automatically generated. It is also updated three times a year.

# Schedules

Schedules provide the most succinct and comprehensive way of communicating large amounts of related information which cannot easily be contained on the drawings or specification. These can cover finishes, sanitary and electrical fittings, FF&E, doors/ironmongery etc. Sample schedules are shown on pages 153-157.
When it is necessary to show exactly what work is carried out in each location (such as in a refurbishment project), a work schedule is also necessary, referenced to relevant specification clauses and drawings.
