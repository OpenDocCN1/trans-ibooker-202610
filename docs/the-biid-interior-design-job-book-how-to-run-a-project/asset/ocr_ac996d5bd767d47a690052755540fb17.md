# Proposal

A project starts when the designer identifies a possible job, or a potential client contacts a practice asking it to make a proposal. The designer should first consider whether they are professionally competent to carry out the project and whether they have adequate resources in both time and staff.

The designer will wish to describe to the client the services the practice could offer and the relevant experience that qualifies it for the project. Designers who have just recently established their own practices can cite relevant experience gained whilst working in previous employment provided that they make this clear to the potential client. Misrepresenting previous experience is never sensible and could result in legal action if a client believes that they have been deceived.

Practices trading as limited companies or limited liability partnerships (and in today's litigious world, this is highly advisable) should remember that their company accounts are a matter of public record, albeit often a year or two in arrears. Clients may use this information to decide whether the practice has the capacity to carry out the proposed work.

If the potential client is unknown to the designer, the practice might wish to similarly check the client's financial reports, if available, and also the client's credit record. Information on British limited companies can be obtained for a small fee by going to http://www.companieshouse.gov.uk.

# Practice information

Well prepared firms will have general practice information readily to hand together with more detailed project information that can be used to validate its suitability for the project in question. Such printed practice information should be designed to a high standard by a professional graphic designer to reflect positively on the practice, and may include:

+   • curriculum vitae of key staff, emphasising their experience in the proposed project type
+   • images of relevant past projects. (It is essential to have the practice's completed projects professionally photographed – see 'I Model letter to photographer', page 231.)
+   • history of the practice, emphasising past experience in the proposed project type
+   • list of past clients, particularly those who have commissioned similar projects.

# Scope of services

Prospective clients who have never previously experienced the process of designing and then executing a project may have very little idea of what is involved in this process and often imagine that it can be completed for very little cost and in a very short time. It is often useful with such clients to set out in detail the procedure that the designer will follow in carrying out the job (see 'A Specimen proposed scope of services', page 83).
