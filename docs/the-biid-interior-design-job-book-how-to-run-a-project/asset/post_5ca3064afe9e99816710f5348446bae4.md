## C Notes on letters to surveyor tenderers

### Quotations

One of the first tasks on all but the smallest of projects is for the designer to obtain quotations for providing any necessary surveys of the site and any existing building(s).

### Client to contract

As with all services provided by others, the designer should note that the surveyor's contract will be direct with the client, otherwise the designer would become liable for any negligence of the surveyor.

### Measured drawings

The normal specification for measured drawings for the whole of a property is to have drawn all floor plans, all elevations and at least two cross sections. If only a portion of the building is to be affected by the proposed works then the survey can be limited to just these elements.

Profiling of mouldings is useful for detailing but will increase the survey cost and may not be needed unless the building is listed or the new works are to relate closely to existing details.

The information will normally be provided in digital form but if the designer or client requires it, the drawings can also be supplied plotted on drawing film at an appropriate scale.

### Building survey

Building surveys (and party wall advice) is highly specialist and should be carried out by a specialist member of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS).

The designer should make it clear to the client that building surveyors can report only on areas of the building that are easily reached. They do not test service supplies nor do they inspect under furniture or carpets. If a survey report suggests that there might be hidden problems, then allowance for remedying these can be made in the contingency sum included in the contract documents, and inspections can be carried out during construction when suspect areas can be uncovered or fully tested.

### Party wall survey

When a building is semi-detached or terraced, any proposed major works which would affect a wall or floor/ceiling shared by a neighbour are subject to the Party Wall etc. Act 1996. This Act allows building owners to carry out certain works to a party wall whilst protecting others who might be affected by this work. Party wall surveyors, acting on behalf of the client, negotiate with other affected parties and obtain written agreement to a Party Wall Award to allow the client to:

- cut into a wall to take the bearing of a beam
- insert a damp-proof course
- raise, demolish and rebuild and/or underpin a party wall
- weather the junction of adjoining walls or buildings by cutting a flashing into an adjoining building
- excavate foundations: (a) within three metres of a neighbour's structure and lower than its foundations and/or (b) within six metres of a neighbour's structure and below a line drawn down at 45° from the bottom of its foundations
- build a new wall on the boundary between two properties.

The Act does not apply to minor work such as fixing plug sockets, screwing in wall units or shelving, adding or replacing electrical wiring or sockets or replastering walls.
