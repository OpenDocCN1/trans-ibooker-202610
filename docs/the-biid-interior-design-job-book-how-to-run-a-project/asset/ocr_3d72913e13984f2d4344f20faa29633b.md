# The RIBA Plan of Work

The structure of this book is organised around the construction industry's sequence of defined work stages for managing the building design and construction process, the RIBA Plan of Work: Multi-disciplinary Services 2007, revised in 2008. This admirable document, first drafted in 1964, provides a formal procedure for running a building project and was quickly adopted by the whole industry.

It has been amended over the intervening years to include disciplines other than architecture but, oddly, does not include interior design. One of the aims of this book is to show how interior design should be integrated into the overall construction process.

Also missing from the Plan of Work are preliminary stages dealing with making proposals to clients and other pre-appointment matters, in particular negotiating the form of appointment with the client.

The full Plan of Work is sometimes too complex for modest projects that interior designers often carry out. However, it is cited because on larger projects an architect is likely to be the design team leader and the consultant with whom the interior designer works most closely. Following the Plan of Work will ensure that the work of designers in large design teams will mesh with the other participants in a project.

For smaller projects, it can be useful to group stages together, e.g. C Concept, D Design Development and E Technical Design, for the purposes of submissions to the client, invoicing, etc.
