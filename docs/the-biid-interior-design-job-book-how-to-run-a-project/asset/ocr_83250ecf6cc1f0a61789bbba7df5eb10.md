# Acknowledgements

Following their involvement in the publication of the form of interior designer appointment, ID/10, the authors have frequently been asked to write a guide to its use. As the purpose of this form (as with any appointment agreement) is to define the relationship between client and consultant throughout the course of a project, it seemed that a more useful book would be a manual for running a job, from before the point when a designer contemplates making a proposal to a prospective client until after the job is completed.

Having taken this decision, the principal inspiration was the RIBA's Architect's Job Book, various editions of which have been a key reference work in the authors' practice since it was founded in 1974. The recently published eighth edition, thoroughly revised by Robert Dalziel and Nigel Ostime of 3Dreid, is particularly useful. However, in the authors' opinion, the book is better suited to larger projects, and of course, it is written for architects, not interior designers.

This book therefore hopes to provide interior designers, who more often work on projects under £1m in value, with detailed information on best practice for jobs that are typical in their profession.

David Chappell's Standard Letters in Architectural Practice was also an inspiration although again not strictly applicable as it too is written for architects. However, for designers who frequently manage larger projects, it is very useful.
