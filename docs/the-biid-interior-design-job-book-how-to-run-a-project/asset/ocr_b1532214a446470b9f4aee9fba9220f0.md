Normally, if a claimant obtains a favourable judgement on only one part of a claim then the whole burden of costs falls on the defendant, even if all the other parts of the claim are unsuccessful. These costs can often exceed the sum in dispute. Therefore a malevolent client can use this 'costs risk' to bully insurers into settling a complex vexatious claim in his/her favour because of the vast sums required to be spent in legal fees to defend a wide-ranging claim. This clause provides that costs are due only on those parts of a claim that he/she succeeds with and it is a disincentive for clients to exaggerate their claims so as to intimidate designers and their insurers.

This clause is second in importance only to clause 5.20 in enforcing fee payment and is yet another disincentive for a client tempted not to pay.

This clause must be individually negotiated with a consumer client before he/she enters into the contract.

# ## Payment on suspension or termination

If the Designer or the Client suspends performance of any or all of the Services or terminates performance of the Services and/or other obligations, the Designer shall issue an account or accounts on the expiry date of the notice or as soon as reasonably practicable and the Designer shall be entitled to:

+   - payment of any part of the fee and other amounts properly due on the expiry date of the notice; and

+   - reimbursement of any loss and/or damage caused to the Designer by reason of the suspension or the termination, save where the Client gives notice of suspension or termination by reason of the material or persistent breach of the Agreement by the Designer; and

+   - payment of any licence fee due under clause 6.

If the reason for suspension is remedied, the Designer shall be entitled to reimbursement of the reasonable costs of resumption of performance of the Services and other obligations in accordance with clause 8.1.4 (a).

# ## VAT

In addition to the fees, expenses and other amounts due, the Client shall pay any chargeable Value Added Tax.

Work on projects abroad for foreign clients sometimes does not attract var. If in doubt, the designer should clarify this with his/her accountant or HM Revenue and Customs.
