# £C Payment collection

# Description

This section covers the collection of fees and other sums due to the designer.

# General

Cash flow is the lifeblood of any business, particularly an interior design practice, where income is often irregular both in frequency and amount, but where outgoings such as staff salaries are not postponable.

# Appointments

It is therefore essential that appointment agreements provide for regular (ideally, monthly) interim payments that are a reasonable reflection of the work which will be done in each period.

# Invoicing

As designers charge on a number of different bases, even on the same project, it is important when invoicing to cite the payment provisions in the appointment and set out exactly fees and other sums due.

It is a temptation for designers to postpone the boring task of regular invoicing, often preferring to spend their time on more interesting matters. This temptation should at all costs be avoided to ensure the regular income for the practice and to avoid the amount of uninvoiced charges rising to such a level that the client is given an incentive to evade payment.

# Chasing

It is also essential for a practice’s cash flow that payments are received promptly after invoices are delivered. Two clauses in ID/10 will help the designer to receive prompt payment:

+   - ID/10 clause 5.16 provides for payment to become due on the date of issue of an account. The final date for payment is specified as 14 days from the issue of the account
+   - ID/10 clause 5.21 allows the designer to charge 8% above the current bank base rate for sums due, thus hopefully discouraging late payment.

Clients often neglect to promptly pay accounts and tactful reminders issued can usually result in payment being received.
