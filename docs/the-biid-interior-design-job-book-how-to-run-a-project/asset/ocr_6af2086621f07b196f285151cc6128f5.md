# ## Scope of services

Designers’ reputations are (rightly) based on the quality of their design work, and designers therefore tend to emphasise this aspect of their work when proposing for a new project. However, clients (or people advising the client, e.g. a financial director, or spouse) are often concerned that their money should be wisely spent. Building projects famously run over time and budget, and clients are often understandably nervous.

Designers can effectively deal with these concerns by explaining to potential clients the procedures that the designer will follow as he/she carries out the work, which will demonstrate a careful approach to the project incorporating frequent estimates of time and costs.

As with many members of the general public, inexperienced clients often do not understand the many tasks that designers must carry out in order to perform their services. It can sometimes be useful to set out in a scope of services document just what tasks will need to be executed. This can help the designer in negotiating an adequate fee and in explaining why it will take time to produce a complete service.

The basis for the scope of services document could be the tasks listed in the Plan of Work stage descriptions at the beginning of each chapter in this book, customised to suit the project in question.
