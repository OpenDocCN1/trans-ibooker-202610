+     • the designer will not be constrained by legal costs.

# ## Disadvantages

However, designers should be aware of several considerations when using CFAs:

+   • Most CFAs provide that the complainant (in this case the designer) is ultimately responsible for paying his/her solicitor both the legal fee and the uplift if the designer's case is successful but the client cannot pay. It is important therefore for the designer to check the client's solvency before taking any action. Designers may believe that the client's property that was the subject of the design project could represent an asset of the client but such assets can be heavily mortgaged or in different ownership.
+   • Before proceeding with the case, the designer should ascertain the legal fees position if the case is settled on a compromised basis.
+   • If the designer is unsuccessful, he/she will be responsible for paying the client's legal costs. However, this contingency can sometimes be covered by an After the Event (ATE) insurance policy.

If the designer is contemplating a CFA, it is essential to obtain legal advice from an experienced specialist construction law solicitor before proceeding. If the solicitor is convinced that the case has good prospects of success, often a barrister and an ATE insurer will rely on this and also agree to become involved on a conditional basis.

# ## ATE insurance

This type of policy insures against having to pay the other side's legal costs in case the designer loses his/her case. Some policies even allow the cost of the premium for this insurance to be covered under the policy as well and thus payable only if the case is successful. In order to obtain ATE insurance, the designer's solicitors will have to provide insurers with supporting evidence of the case's likelihood of success. Having obtained such insurance provides a strong incentive for the client to settle because the designer is effectively risking nothing by pursuing the case.

# ## Jackson Report

In January 2010 Lord Justice Jackson recommended that ATE premiums and success fees should not be recoverable from defendants. The designer should therefore confirm the current position with his/her solicitor.
