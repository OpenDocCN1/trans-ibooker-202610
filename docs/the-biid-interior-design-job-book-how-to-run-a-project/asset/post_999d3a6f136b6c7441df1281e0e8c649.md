
## Time charges

Where it is stated in the Schedule of Fees and Expenses that this clause 5.5 applies, the time-based fee shall be ascertained by multiplying the time reasonably spent in the performance of the Services calculated to the nearest half hour, by the specified hourly or daily rate for the relevant personnel. Time 'reasonably spent' includes the time spent in connection with performance of the Services and in travelling from and returning to the Designer's office.

## Revision of lump sums and other rates

Every 12 months, lump sums complying with clause 5.4, less any amounts previously claimed, and rates for time charges shall be revised in accordance with changes in the Average Earnings Index and rates for mileage and printing shall be revised in accordance with changes in the Consumer Prices Index.

Each 12-month period shall commence on the anniversary of the Effective Date of this Agreement specified in the Project Data.

If lump sum and/or time charge fees are to be increased every year, it should be shown in the appointment letter or Schedule of Fees and Expenses just how this will be calculated (e.g. stages commenced after the expiry of each 12-month period to be increased in line with the Consumer Price Index). The effect of this clause will generally be to increase the lump sum and/or time charge fees on long jobs.

## Fee adjustment

The Basic Fee:

shall be adjusted including due allowance for any loss and/or expense if:

- a) material changes are made to the Brief and/or the Project Cost and/or the Timetable; and/or

- b) the Services are varied by agreement.

shall, if the Project Cost is reduced during performance of the Services, including any reduction arising solely from deflationary market conditions not prevailing at the Effective Date, be calculated on the basis of the Project Cost current prior to the date of any consequent reduction or the lowest acceptable tender (whichever is applicable).

This subclause deals with the case where a client instructs a reduction in the brief during the performance of the services when a percentage fee applies. It provides that the percentage fee for work before the instruction should be based on the (higher) value of the original brief and the work after the instruction based ultimately on the final construction cost. In deflationary times it prevents percentage fees being reduced.

## Tender not accepted

5.10 Where the Designer is instructed by the Client to invite a tender or tenders for work or services in connection with the Project but no tender is made or accepted, the Designer shall be entitled to fees due based on the Project Cost or that part of it relating to the said work or services current at the date of invitation to tender.

## Supplementary agreements

5.11 If the Designer consents to enter into any supplementary agreement, the terms of which are agreed by the Designer after the date of this Agreement, the Designer shall be entitled to payment of the Designer's reasonable costs of so doing, including but not limited to additional risks, legal advice, and the amount of any additional professional indemnity insurance premium.

If the designer agrees to enter into a third-party agreement (see note to clause 7.7 below) the designer should be paid for the risk, the agreement commits him/her to, the cost of insuring against it and the time spent in negotiating the agreement.

## Incomplete services

5.12 Where for any reason the Designer provides only part of the Services specified in the Services Schedule, fees shall be calculated as described in the schedule of Fees and Expenses for completed work stages and for the balance of work carried out the fees shall be on the basis of the Designer’s estimate of the percentage of completion.

The appointment letter should refer to an enclosed Schedule of Fees and Expenses (see ‘p3 Specimen Schedule of Fees and Expenses’, page 44) which sets out payment at the completion of each work stage.

## Expenses and disbursements

5.13 The Client shall reimburse the Designer for expenses in the manner specified in the schedule of Fees and Expenses.

Expenses in connection with Services under Parts 3 and/or 4 of the Services Schedule shall be deemed to be included in the tender or tenders for such Services.

It is useful to include a percentage uplift (or handling charge) to expenses to remunerate the designer for the administrative time in billing and collecting expenses, for the interest on payments made to suppliers before equivalent sums are collected from the client and for the risk that the client might not pay.

## Maintain records

5.14 The Designer shall maintain records of time spent on Services performed on a time basis for the purpose of verifying charges under clause 5.6 and shall in addition maintain records of any expenses and disbursements to be reimbursed at net cost. The Designer shall make such records available to the Client on reasonable request.

It is important to carefully record time spent on projects, not only the amount of time spent but also what tasks were carried out in that time (see ‘p1 Specimen time sheet’, page 15 and ‘Applicable case law: Munkenbeck v Harold, page 282). Expenses should also be carefully recorded, supported by suppliers’ invoices.
