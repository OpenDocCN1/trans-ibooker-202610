# ## 2.2 Duty to inform

The Designer shall keep the Client informed of progress in the performance of the Services and of any issue that may materially affect the Brief, the Project Cost, the Timetable, or the quality of the Project.

It is important that the designer should notify the client in writing when it becomes apparent that an incompatibility exists in the client's instructions, e.g. between the brief and the project cost. Otherwise the client could reasonably claim that he/she was not made aware of an important issue and was thus prevented from making a timely decision regarding it. Equally, the client should do the same or he/she may be liable for additional fees for additional work under clause 5.8.

# ### Limitation of liability

+   2.4 The Designer does not warrant:

+   2.4.1 that planning permission and other approvals from third parties will be granted at all, or if granted, will be granted in accordance with any anticipated time-scale;

Designers cannot warrant that the project will be granted any approvals; doing so would certainly not be covered by the designer's PI policy. However, it is not uncommon for professionals to agree with the client that they will be paid a supplementary fee contingent on a precisely defined outcome, e.g. that the planning authority will resolve to grant a planning consent for a project to the designer's designs (see 'p3 Model appointment letter to client (contingent)', page 40).
