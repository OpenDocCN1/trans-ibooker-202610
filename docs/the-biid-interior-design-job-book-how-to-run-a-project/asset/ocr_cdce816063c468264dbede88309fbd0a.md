# # L Notes on letter to photographer

Apocryphal stories bound of architectural photographers turning up at a shoot with a pantechnicon full of furniture, which is substituted for the client's. Interior designers, of course, would not have to suffer this indignity.

# ## Project photographs

It is important for the designer to have completed projects photographed to use for practice promotional purposes. ID/10 clause 2.9 gives the designer the right to photograph the project within two years after the date of the agreement (and for larger projects this may need to be extended at the time of entering into the agreement) and to publish these photographs.

# ## Confidentiality

Some clients are particularly concerned to guard their privacy, and the designer may have to agree not to have the client's name and/or address identified when the project is published. These matters should be discussed and agreed when the designer's appointment is being negotiated.

# ## Photographers

The designer should not expect to photograph the project him/herself but should obtain photographs that will meet the high standards necessary for publication. Even ordinary professional photographers generally do not have the necessary skill. For best results the designer should employ a specialist architectural interiors photographer.

Designers should be careful in choosing a photographer. They should visit several photographers' websites and then ask to see the portfolios of those that might be suitable. Designers should look at photographs of interiors; many photographers in this field specialise in architecture, which is subtly different from photographing interiors.

# ## Photography fees

Architectural photographers generally expect to receive an initial fee for taking the photographs plus fees for having the images published. They therefore have experience in knowing which publications will be interested in the images and how they can be taken to maximise the likelihood of publication. The designer may find it useful to see if others such as the contractor, client and/or other consultants might wish to share the photographer's fees.

# ## Preparation

Before the shoot, the designer should:

+   - visit the site with the photographer and agree the views and details of each image
+   - ensure that access to the site is available on the proposed date of the shoot
+   - ensure that all light fixtures are fitted with working lamps
+   - determine whether furniture needs to be moved
+   - ensure that the site is clean and neat on the day(s) of the shoot.

# ## Dressing

Spaces can often look quite different in photographs than they do in real life. They are often taken with wide angle lenses, which can place disproportionate emphasis on the foreground. Therefore an image can benefit from having an interesting object at the front. Some photographers will offer to supply these, but they are best provided by the designer.

# ## Approval

The designer should retain the right to ensure that the images of the project are not published in unsuitable media.

A client might object if his/her project were published in an inappropriate context.
