
## C Notes on letter to previous designer

### Previous designer

Occasionally, clients find that they engaged a designer whom they discovered that they were not in sympathy with, and terminated their employment.

When a designer becomes aware that another designer had been previously employed, it is only courteous to write the predecessor. It can sometimes be worthwhile contacting them to learn if the client has been difficult or slow in payment.

## C Notes on resources projection

### Resource estimating

When practices are contracted with clients to carry out work on a percentage or a lump sum basis, it is important that the cost of carrying out the work is estimated, first in order to calculate the fee, and then updated as each stage is completed, ultimately to show how the cost of the actual time spent compares with the estimate.

### Projection

The specimen resources projection opposite illustrates an approximate rolling estimate of the time costs incurred by the practice as it proceeds through the project. Alternatively, the designer can attempt to forecast more precisely the amount of time to be spent on each task but, as these can vary substantially from job to job, such forecasts can often be quite inaccurate.

The illustration shows a typical job in which the construction cost increases throughout its course, perhaps due to client's instructions or unexpected problems encountered on site. When the designer's fee is on a percentage basis, the fee increases proportionately. However, when the construction cost drops due, say, to a client reduction in the brief, the designer is protected under ID/10 clause 5.7.2.

### Salary cost

Salary cost should be calculated on the basis of each person's yearly salary plus national insurance contributions and other costs directly relating to salary. This should be divided by between 1,600 to 1,750 working hours per year to arrive at the hourly salary cost. A director/partner's yearly salary is assumed to be at a notional level commensurate with their age and experience and salary cost for them similarly calculated.

### Hourly rates

Hourly charge rates for each member of staff should be set as a multiple of their salary cost. A rough assumption is that office overheads are equal to salary costs with a normal multiple of between 2.5 to 3 times salary cost. This represents the total of the salary cost (1.0) plus overheads (±1.0) plus profit (say, 0.5–1.0).

Various directors/partners and staff of appropriate experience are allocated to each project at different stages with a view to the project budget and the available resources in the practice.

### Contingency

It is wise to build in a contingency for each stage and remember that time spent during the construction stage is almost always greater than forecast.

### Future

Building up a record of job costs will aid the designer in estimating fees for future projects after allowing for inflation.

## C Notes on materials schedule

### Materials schedule

Along with the concept drawings which the designer prepares to convey the basic principles of the proposed design, a preliminary materials schedule should also be drawn up in order to:

- submit to the client for discussion and approval
- provide to the quantity surveyor for preliminary cost estimating. Where precise details have not yet been decided, it is useful to indicate the quality range desired
- support a planning application (where relevant)
- become the basis for the outline specification and ultimately the construction specification (see 'd Specimen outline specification', page 137 and 'e Specimen specification', pages 151 and 152), the latter eventually forming part of the construction contract documents.

The materials specification can be supported by mood boards, images of relevant interiors (not necessarily those designed by the designer) to indicate key aspects of the proposed interior design.

In the following example, a significant portion of the choice of materials will be made after consultation with the architect in relation to the new extension that is part of the works.

In a number of areas, the precise material specification will not yet have been determined and the quantity surveyor will have to use his/her judgement to allow provisional sums at this stage.

The materials schedule will later be developed into an outline specification (stage d) and then a construction specification (stage e).

### Arrangement

The materials schedule is arranged under the UK version of the CI/SFB system of building classification established in 1959 that is now widely used in the construction industry. Its name stands for Construction Indexing/Samarbetskommittén för Byggnadsfrågor, the latter being the Swedish organisation that originally developed it.

The elemental section of CI/SFB, showing the building broken down by building element, is used at this early stage.
