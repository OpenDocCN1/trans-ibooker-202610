## Scope of services

Designers’ reputations are (rightly) based on the quality of their design work, and designers therefore tend to emphasise this aspect of their work when proposing for a new project. However, clients (or people advising the client, e.g. a financial director, or spouse) are often concerned that their money should be wisely spent. Building projects famously run over time and budget, and clients are often understandably nervous.

Designers can effectively deal with these concerns by explaining to potential clients the procedures that the designer will follow as he/she carries out the work, which will demonstrate a careful approach to the project incorporating frequent estimates of time and costs.

As with many members of the general public, inexperienced clients often do not understand the many tasks that designers must carry out in order to perform their services. It can sometimes be useful to set out in a scope of services document just what tasks will need to be executed. This can help the designer in negotiating an adequate fee and in explaining why it will take time to produce a complete service.

The basis for the scope of services document could be the tasks listed in the Plan of Work stage descriptions at the beginning of each chapter in this book, customised to suit the project in question.

## A Specimen proposed scope of services

Ployer Enterprises is one of the world’s leading developers and manufacturers of gadgets, widgets and gizmos. Its management wishes to have a strategy prepared to identify suitable sites/buildings with room for future expansion to move office staff into within the next two years.

This brief report outlines how this strategy could be prepared, agreed and then implemented.

The first step will be to assemble key information about buildings/sites being considered including:

- 1. measured drawings and a photographic survey of suitable buildings/sites
- 2. any original building contract documents that may exist with key drawings being digitised
- 3. any building or services surveys that have been carried out since the buildings’ completion.

In addition to Signer Associates, providing interior design services, advice on the following should be sought:

- 1. cost and time estimating
- 2. architectural design of any major proposed building alterations or new extensions
- 3. health and safety advice (appointing a CDM Co-ordinator)

and advice may need to be sought regarding:

- 4. an analysis of existing building mechanical and electrical systems
- 5. sustainability.

Having assembled the design team, the next step will be to define Ployer’s requirements by doing the following:

- 1. identify and evaluate possible sites and/or buildings
- 2. compile a list of spaces to accommodate the required functions and prepare a room schedule
- 3. interview key staff to determine precise requirements for each space and prepare room requirements schedules
- 4. analyse desired adjacencies and prepare an adjacency relationships matrix

![](img/3a2f79b2faeefd4a54f8d2fd202acc56_97_0.png)

- 5. prepare a space relationship diagram based on the adjacency relationships matrix
- 6. prepare a planning diagram based on the space relationship diagram
- 7. provide the planning diagram to the quantity surveyor for the approximation of time and cost
- 8. assist Ployer to determine priorities in the light of the approximated costings and amend floor plans as necessary, perhaps with phasing
- 9. advise Ployer on alternative refurbishment and FF&E procurement strategies
- 10. prepare a corporate interior strategy including mood boards illustrating the general design approach.

*Deliverables: site/building analysis, room schedule, room requirements schedules, adjacency relationships matrix, space relationship diagram, cost and time approximations, procurement strategy options, corporate interior strategy.*

### IMPLEMENTATION

Having determined the client’s requirements, the next steps will be to select the appropriate site/building and prepare designs:

### Design stage (RIBA Work Stages CDE)

- 1. analyse Ployer's requirements and prepare a design showing the layout of the various spaces and preliminary schedules of materials and FF&E
- 2. provide information to discuss proposals with, and incorporate input of, other consultants
- 3. provide information to the quantity surveyor to prepare preliminary cost and time estimates.

*Deliverables: design drawings, preliminary schedule of materials, preliminary schedule of FF&E, cost and time estimates, consultants’ reports.*

### Pre-construction stage (RIBA Work Stages FGH)

- 1. hold discussions with the relevant statutory authorities and make any necessary applications
- 2. prepare technical drawings, specification and contract conditions for the refurbishment work showing type of construction, materials and appearance together with technical details
- 3. obtain quotations from FF&E suppliers and prepare a costed schedule of FF&E; prepare designs for any bespoke items
- 4. provide information to discuss proposals with, and incorporate input of, other consultants
- 5. provide information to the quantity surveyor to prepare detailed cost and time estimates

- 6. agree the form of refurbishment contract with Ployer
- 7. if seeking tenders, advise on and obtain Ployer's approval to a list of tenderers for the refurbishment contract
- 8. provide information to the quantity surveyor to prepare a work schedule in order to obtain competitive tenders or agree a negotiated price (depending on the procurement route chosen) for the refurbishment contract and to prepare a pre-tender estimate
- 9. invite and obtain tenders for the refurbishment contract and submit them to Ployer
- 10. upon acceptance of a tender, prepare construction documentation for execution.

*Deliverables: statutory applications, technical drawings, specification, contract conditions, costed schedule of FF&E, pre-tender cost and time estimates, consultants’ reports, tenders.*

### Construction stage (RIBA Work Stages JK)

- 1. provide to the contractor any further technical information required
- 2. administer the terms of the refurbishment contract
- 3. visit the works to monitor the progress and overall quality of the works and conduct periodic site meetings with the contractor to review progress
- 4. issue to the refurbishment contractor payment certificates based on the quantity surveyor’s periodic valuations
- 5. procure manufactured items of FF&E on a pro forma basis on behalf of Ployer, and monitor the fabrication of bespoke FF&E items
- 6. monitor the delivery and installation of FF&E items
- 7. carry out a final inspection, monitoring the remedying of defects
- 8. provide information to the quantity surveyor to prepare final account
- 9. assist Ployer to acquire and install artworks.

*Deliverables: further technical information, site meeting minutes, payment certificates.*
