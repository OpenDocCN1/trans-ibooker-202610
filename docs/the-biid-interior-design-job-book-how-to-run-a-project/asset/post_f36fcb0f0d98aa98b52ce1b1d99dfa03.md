
## K Notes on FF&E purchase order (as agent)

### As agent

This form is for use where the designer is procuring FF&E as agent under sections B1 and B2 of ID/10. This provides for the contract of supply to be between the supplier and the employer and therefore the risk that the supplier fails to supply goods of the quality specified or otherwise does not comply with the terms of the purchase order is borne by the employer, not the designer.

For purchasing as a principal, see ‘FF&E Notes on purchase order (as principal)’, page 239.

### Purchase orders

The purchase order should be sent to the supplier with copies for the employer and the designer’s file. When the designer has designed an item of bespoke furniture, he/she should ask the client to sign the shop drawings before placing an order for fabrication (see ‘K Model letter to client regarding bespoke FF&E’, page 209).

Where the order is for an item (e.g. fabric) to be incorporated into furniture (e.g. seating upholstery) or when the supplied item is to be fitted by another company (as in the specimen), a copy should also be sent to the fabricator or fitter.

### Fabric samples

When fabric, carpet etc. is part of the order, the designer should attach a sample of each to all copies of the order including the designer’s file copy.

For large orders, supplementary pages can be added.

### Payment

Payment for FF&E can be made

- direct by the client against a pro forma invoice which the supplier has raised following the designer’s issuance of a purchase order
- by the designer from funds provided by the client and held in a project bank account.

See ‘P3 Explanation of ID/10 terms’, page 46, clause 5.17.

## K Notes on letter to client regarding bespoke FF&E

### Bespoke FF&E

Designers often design items of bespoke furniture for clients. As many clients find it difficult to visualise such furniture, it is important for the designer to show the client how the piece will look, often using 3d images. Even so, clients can often reject a piece after fabrication.

Therefore, it is important that the designer obtains the client's signature to the fabricator's shop drawings before proceeding, as evidence that the client has carefully considered the design.

### Fabric samples

As with manufactured pieces, when fabric, carpet etc. is part of the order, the designer should attach a sample to all copies of the order including the designer's file copy.
