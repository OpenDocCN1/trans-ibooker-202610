# P1 Notes on discussion record

# Discussion record

The pertinent details of all informal meetings and telephone conversations should be carefully noted in a discussion record (sometimes called an ‘attendance note’), at the time they happen, and placed in the job file. As with letters, they should not be cryptic but understandable to any third party reading it at some time in the future. Formal meetings involving several participants are best minuted (see ‘k Specimen meeting minutes’, page 213).

The principal reason for writing such a record is to remind the designer (and his/her staff) of key discussions relating to the job but also as a record of matters affecting the designer’s work. It can also serve as a reminder of actions to be taken.

> It is usually too late to write a discussion record when the designer is being sued for negligence.

# Use

Discussion records are written evidence that could be used in the event of a dispute with the client as to contract terms, and may be important in later explaining the designer’s performance. Discussion records written well after the event, based on the designer’s memory, should be recorded as such on the note but are less satisfactory as evidence.

# Client’s instructions

Instructions given by the client in discussions should be confirmed immediately in a client’s instruction (see ‘d Specimen client’s instruction’, page 140). Similarly, verbal instructions given by the designer to a contractor should be confirmed immediately in a designer’s instruction (see ‘k Specimen designer’s instruction’, page 215).
