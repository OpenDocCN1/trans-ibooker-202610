# # £C Payment collection (continued)

# ## Take stock

If the above fails, the designer should take stock of the matter. He/she should understand that going further will almost inevitably destroy whatever relationship exists between the designer and the client. Most designers believe that a client who does not pay is not worth having but there can be other considerations, e.g. the negative effect that pursuing a fee claim can have on other interconnected personal or business relationships. The designer should also balance the sums due against the risk of incurring unrecoverable costs. It is sometimes best to abandon small sums due, before proceeding too far.

If the designer wishes to pursue the matter, he/she should first think carefully and ensure that he/she has all the relevant facts. He/she should evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of his/her position, taking legal advice if necessary.

# ## Communications

It is important to always:

+   - keep communication simple and factual. Remember that the client and his/her solicitor are not experienced in the designer's field, and technical matters and procedures may need explaining
+   - act even-handedly and not make threats nor respond in kind to threats from the client and/or his/her solicitors
+   - avoid communicating in an aggressive or intimidating manner, particularly if the client is a consumer. 'More in sorrow than in anger' is a good rule to observe
+   - not say or write anything that could be remotely construed as defamatory
+   - keep to provable facts, referring as far as possible to written evidence
+   - never give any indication that the unpaid sum will affect the designer's solvency. Such comments will only encourage a malevolent client
+   - avoid emotion. The designer should avoid discussing the matter on the telephone or in meetings unless certain that he/she can speak dispassionately.

# ## Forceful response

Having borne the above in mind, if the designer wishes to continue to pursue payment he/she should, after taking legal advice, draft a letter to the client noting the following points:

+   - Both the designer and the client are bound by the terms of their agreement, ID/10. If the client is a consumer, note that certain terms were explained to the client before he/she entered into the agreement and enclose a copy of the earlier letter confirming this (see 'P3 Model appointment letter to consumer client', page 38).
+   - List the client's criticisms of the designer's services and against each state the designer's precise and detailed reply, referring to supporting written material in the project file. It is often quite useful to discuss this response with a specialist solicitor.
+   - Under clause 5.20 of ID/10, the client is not permitted to set off alleged claims of negligence against sums due. The client is free to pursue such a claim but only as separate matter. In the meantime, he/she must pay the sum due.
