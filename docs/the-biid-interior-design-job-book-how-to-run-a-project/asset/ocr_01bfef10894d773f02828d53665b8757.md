# D Planning applications

**Discussions** Many projects require the consent of the local planning authority. Once a concept design is agreed with the client, it is advisable to contact the local planning authority to ascertain whether planning and/or listed building consent is required, and if so, to discuss the proposals with the neighbourhood planning and/or listed buildings officer, preferably on site. The designer should make a note of the matters discussed and confirm them in writing to the planning officer so that there is no misunderstanding as to what is required. Planning officers are not normally responsible for determining an application (unless it is in regard to minor matters for which they have been given delegated powers) but their recommendations to the Planning Committee can be important in obtaining a favourable decision.

**Outline consent** If the principle of the proposed scheme is in question, it can be useful to test the water without carrying out much design work by making an application for outline planning consent. The application could be accompanied by simple drawings showing the footprint and height of the building relative to any neighbours’ buildings.

**Detailed consent** Normally it is sensible to apply just for a detailed consent, submitting plans showing the disposition of all rooms, and elevations showing the external appearance and materials.

**Listed buildings** If the building is listed, i.e. officially designated as being of special architectural, historical or cultural significance, a listed building consent will be required, even for schemes that do not affect the external appearance of the building.
