# Preface

> 'Design is the transformation of existing conditions into preferred ones'
> Sciences of the Artificial, Herbert Simon (Nobel laureate).

The authors have long felt that British interior designers did not have the benefit of books that explained professional practice, whether in running a firm or in running a job. This book seeks to fill the need for the latter.

The practice of interior design, as with architecture, is mostly a thoroughly enjoyable vocation. Otherwise why would so many gifted and talented designers work long hours for which they are generally underpaid. However, the profession can sometimes be very cruel, as the risks of being in practice can be very great indeed for the unwary.

This book is not, unlike most books on interior design, about design, but about practice. By following longstanding procedures in the construction industry, designers will be communicating in the same terms as other members of a project team.

The first duty of a professional is to place the interests of his/her client before his/her own. By following best practice as illustrated in this book, designers will be doing this and at the same time safeguarding themselves against any suggestion of negligence or incompetence. The designer should always remember that he/she must expend their clients' resources with care and that these sums can often be considerable.
