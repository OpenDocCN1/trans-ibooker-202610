
## FFE Notes on FF&E purchase order (as principal)

### As principal

This form is for use where the designer is procuring FF&E as principal under sections B1 and B3 of ID/10. (See ‘k’ Specimen FF&E purchase order (as agent)’, page 207, when the designer is purchasing FF&E as the client’s agent.) This provides for the contract of supply between the supplier and the designer. (See ‘FFE Specimen FF&E sales order (as principal)’, page 238, for the corresponding contract between the designer and the client.)

This method of procurement places the risk of the supplier failing to supply FF&E of the quality ordered or otherwise not complying with the terms of the purchase order on the designer. If the designer believes that such a problem is the fault of the supplier, then the designer will have to separately pursue the supplier for any remedy sought.

If, for example, audio/visual equipment fails within the manufacturer’s guarantee period but the supplier has in the interim become insolvent, then the designer is left with the burden of rectifying the problem at their own cost. Designers trading in this manner should factor this risk into their business model.

### Purchase orders

The purchase order should be sent to the supplier with copies for the employer and for the designer’s file. When the designer has designed an item of bespoke furniture, the client should be asked to sign off the shop drawings before placing an order for fabrication.

Where the order is for an item (e.g. fabric) to be incorporated into furniture (e.g. seating upholstery) or when the supplied item is to be fitted by another company (as in the specimen), a copy should also be sent to the fabricator or fitter.

### Fabric samples

When fabric is part of the order, the designer should attach samples of the fabric(s) to all copies of the order, including the designer’s file copy.

For large orders, supplementary pages can be added.
