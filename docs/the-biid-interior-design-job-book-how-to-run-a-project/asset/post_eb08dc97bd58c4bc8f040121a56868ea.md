### Description  

This section covers the enforcement of fees and other sums due to the designer when a client is reluctant to pay.  

### General  

Unfortunately, a few unscrupulous clients are sometimes tempted to avoid sums due, often alleging negligence on the part of the designer as a reason for not paying, particularly at the end of a job when they perceive that the designer's services will be no longer needed. This typically manifests itself in complaints by the client that the designer did not follow the client's instructions or did not perform competently.  

When this happens, then the designer should look carefully at the available options as set out in '£C Payment collection', page 258.  

### £E Notes on letter to professional indemnity insurers  

### Circumstance  

An important condition of PII policies is that the insurers must be notified promptly in writing of any ‘circumstance’ that may give rise to a claim against the designer. Such a circumstance could be:  

- an implied or express criticism of the designer’s performance or of one of the designer’s subordinates or of one of the designer’s directly employed consultants, whether justified or not  
- any hint that someone might make a claim against the designer  
- the realisation that the practice has made a mistake in the carrying out of a project  
- an unresolved dispute over fees due  
- where a project has run substantially over time and/or budget  
- when it becomes apparent that remedial works will be required  
- where some form of dispute resolution is mooted.  

### Notifying  

Designers are often reluctant to notify insurers, in the hope that a complaining client will not pursue the matter, or believing that notifying will result in next year’s insurance premium being increased. In fact, insurers are well aware that sometimes clients complain about even the best of practices and often view the occasional notification as evidence of a practice’s diligence. One of the most important benefits of PII is that legal costs incurred in defending the claim are covered by the policy after the policy’s excess has been expended. Designers should never risk losing their policy’s vital safeguards because of tardiness in notifying.  

### Actions  

When a circumstance is identified (even if the designer believes the claim has no merit and regardless of the amount of damages claimed) the designer should promptly take the following actions:  

- Provide the insurers with full details of the allegation(s) together with a copy of relevant documents, a chronology of events leading up to the allegation, including the date when the designer first became aware of the allegation, an explanation of the designer’s involvement and the roles of any other parties in the project.  
- Co-operate fully with insurers while they investigate the matter.  
- If a complaint is made in writing, the designer should note its receipt and reply only that the matter will be addressed.  

The designer should not:  

- make any admissions or concessions  
- offer or agree to make any payment without the prior written agreement of the PII insurers. If the designer believes that a claim is justified or if a claim can be settled, he/she should explain the position clearly to insurers  
- mention the involvement of insurers.  

If the designer believes that a claim is prompted only by the desire to evade paying fees due, he/she could take legal advice. Under ID/10, legal costs can ultimately be recovered from the client unless a claim is upheld. However, such matters should still be notified to the designer’s PII insurers.
