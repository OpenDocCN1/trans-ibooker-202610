## C Notes on letter to client at end of stage

### End of stage

Designers should take the precaution of confirming when the client approves each work stage so that they can proceed to the next stage in the knowledge that the client is happy with the work done to date.

Clients should be warned that a change of mind can result in the expenditure of additional fees and time.

## D Design Development stage

### Stage description

Stage D, Design Development, covers the evolution of the concept into a detailed design, integrating the consultants’ principal recommendations into the scheme and responding to the relevant statutory regulations.

### RIBA Plan of Work

The RIBA Outline Plan of Work 2007 describes the key tasks for this stage as, ‘Development of concept design to include structural and building services systems, updated outline specifications and cost plan. Completion of Project Brief. Application for detailed planning permission.’

Whilst the principal tasks for the design team will be to refine the general arrangement of spaces and integrate the basic structural and services proposals, the designer should be refining furniture layouts and making suggestions for the location of power points, light switch positions, etc.

### ID/10

Stage D is a ‘Design Stage’ on page A of the Schedule of Interior Design Services in ID/10.

### Tasks: Interior designer

When working as the *interior designer* in a design team, the designer will carry out only the following four tasks (deliverables in **bold**):

- Show the general **location of items of FF&E** on the developed design drawings.
- Show general **location of service points** on the developed design drawings.
- Prepare **draft schedules** of internal finishes, sanitary and electrical fittings, doors, FF&E (examples of final schedules shown in stage E, pages 153–157).
- Prepare **sample boards** showing images of typical items of FF&E together with samples of typical fabrics, carpets, paint colours, etc. generally proposed for the project.

### Lead consultant

When acting as *lead consultant*, the designer will carry out all of the above tasks as well as all of the following:

- Develop the concept scheme drawings into **developed design drawings** showing the detailed arrangement of spaces.
- Develop the materials schedule into an **outline specification**.
- Prepare, if necessary, a **draft work schedule**.
- Provide information to, discuss proposals with, and incorporate **preliminary inputs** of, other consultants into the developed design.
- Provide information to the quantity surveyor for the preparation of an **approximate estimate of construction cost and time** including FF&E.
- Present the **developed design** (incorporating the above deliverables) to, and review options with, the client.
- Prepare and submit detailed planning and/or listed building, building regulation and other statutory **approvals applications**.

On smaller projects, stages C, Concept, D and E, Technical Design, are often carried out in the same exercise.
