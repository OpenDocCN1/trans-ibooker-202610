# # £E Dispute resolution

> _‘Discourage litigation. Persuade your neighbours to compromise whenever you can. Point out to them how the nominal winner is often a real loser; in fees, expenses and waste of time.’ Abraham Lincoln._

If a client persists in not paying, there are several alternative methods of dispute resolution provided for in ID/10:

# ## Mediation

Clause 9.1: The courts promote mediation as a pre-court stage for resolving disputes and sometimes will refuse to award costs if the successful party has unreasonably refused to mediate. It can be cheaper and quicker than other forms of dispute resolution and take up less management time. It can also offer the opportunity for the parties to set out their cases and resolve differences before costs escalate. However, mediation is unlikely to resolve a dispute if one or both parties are acting unreasonably.

As with arbitration, mediation is confidential. It is also without prejudice so that information disclosed cannot be used in court if a settlement is not achieved. Even if mediation is unsuccessful in resolving a dispute, it can clarify arguments and thus reduce the time needing to be spent in litigation or arbitration.

# ## Adjudication

Clause 9.2: Adjudication is, by law, used in construction disputes, provided that, if the client is a consumer, for this method of dispute resolution to be available, it has to have been expressly agreed before the contract was entered into. The adjudicator has to reach a decision within 28 days (or such longer period as is permitted by the parties), so this method is not only relatively inexpensive but also quick.

As the timescale is so short, an advantage can be gained by the referring party by carefully constructing a case and then ‘ambushing’ the other party, leaving them with little time to organise a defence. Therefore designers should always keep complete and current files to avoid being disadvantaged.

The costs of adjudication are not usually awarded to the successful party but under ID/10, costs can be awarded by the adjudicator, which allows adjudication to be used with consumer clients, provided it had been previously agreed with them.

Almost all adjudications are enforced: only about 5% of decisions go to court for enforcement and less than 1% are not enforced.

# ## Small claims

(clause 9.3.2(a)) The ‘small claims court’ (i.e. the small claims track litigation procedure in the County Court) can be used for claims not exceeding £5,000 where the litigant can pursue the claim without incurring the cost of a solicitor. However, there is a risk that a client may issue a counterclaim for more than that sum, taking the dispute above the small claims limit. Further, there is also a risk that the judge may decide that the claim is too complex to be dealt with under this simplified procedure.

# ## Arbitration

(clause 9.3) Arbitration has the advantage of being private so that the designer does not have to risk having his/her name attached to a legal case.

A further advantage is that the arbitrator is prevented from making an order for security for costs under clause 9.3.2(c).

However, the cost of the arbitrator and other expenses are charged to the parties.

# ## Litigation

(clause 9.1) This is not provided for specifically in ID/10 save as a way of enforcing adjudication decisions. However, the parties can agree before the appointment is entered into to have disputes resolved by a court.
