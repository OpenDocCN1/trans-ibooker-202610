![](img/3a2f79b2faeefd4a54f8d2fd202acc56_193_0.png)

As you will remember, our agreement with you provides for us, amongst other tasks at this stage, to administer the terms of the building contract and to visit the site to see that the work is proceeding generally in accordance with the building contract. We have allowed for 10 site visits although we will visit the site as necessary depending on the progress of the building work. I would estimate the frequency of these visits normally to be approximately at 14 to 31 day intervals.

Although we will monitor the quality of the building work during our site visits, it is the contractor’s responsibility to ensure that the quality of the work complies with the drawings and specification. Therefore it is conceivable that faulty work could be incorporated into the project and covered up between site visits without us being able to observe it.

If you wish to have more frequent inspections (and our view is that for a project of this scope and complexity, inspections should be carried out every [?] days), we can arrange for you to employ a [part-/full-] time site inspector (clerk of works) to inspect the work more frequently under our direction. We would help you to find a suitable inspector and arrange his/her employment terms. [We would wish to charge an additional hourly fee for managing the site inspector.]

Prior to issuing the final payment certificate to the contractor, we will inspect the completed works and, if they are satisfactory, we will issue a certificate of completion of making good defects.

Yours sincerely

Dee

## H Notes on obtaining tenders

- **Tendering** When the employer has decided to use competitive tendering to select a contractor, the designer should follow a careful procedure to ensure that the tendering process is effective for the employer and is fair to the tenderers.

- **Tenderers** It is generally thought reasonable to ask between three and five contractors to submit tenders. Too few and there is a risk that not enough valid tenders will be received; too many and the designer may find that he/she does not find contractors willing to tender for future work.

- **Cover pricing** Until 2009, it had been common for contractors who were not interested in tendering for a particular project, but did not wish to offend the consultant by declining an invitation, would submit a ‘cover price’. This was determined by obtaining from another tenderer a price that was in excess of the other contractor’s bid, thus ensuring that this tender would not be competitive. This illegal practice was investigated by the Office of Fair Trading which, in that year, penalised 103 large contractors with fines totalling almost £130m. However, the practice still seems to persist and it is therefore helpful for the designer to reassure tenderers that declining an invitation will not prejudice them with regard to the designer’s future projects.

- **Specialist work** When certain items of specialist work are of particular importance, the designer may wish to separately arrange for subcontract tenders to be obtained, nominating the successful subcontract tenderers to the main contractor. If this procedure is to be followed, a list of the subcontract tenderers should be shown in the main contract tender documents so that the main contractor has an opportunity to object.

- **Notification** When tenders are received, it is only fair to the tenderers to advise them of the results. It is prudent to have the lowest tender checked and the second lowest notified of the position so that they remain available if necessary.

## H Model letter to prospective tenderers

123.02
10 August 2007

Dear Sir

### Proposed Alterations and Extension to 42 Supernova Heights for Mr M Ployer

We have been given your firm's name by our client who understands that you might be suitable to be considered for inclusion on the tender list for this project. The quantity surveyor estimates that the construction cost will be in the order of £0.5m and will take approximately 30 weeks. The building contract will be MW 05.

Other consultants involved are Tect Associates Ltd, architects; Sir Veigh Orr & Partners Ltd, quantity surveyors; R S Jay Associates Ltd, structural engineers and The Cannical/Trickle Partnership Ltd, services engineers.

If you are so interested, please send us, before 14 August 2007, information regarding:

- similar projects you have recently completed, providing the names and contact details of the client and the lead consultant
- number of permanently employed staff, broken down into site-based and office-based
- general information about all your firm's contracts currently on site including their value, location and name of employer.

The client's accountant may contact you asking for details of your company accounts.

If you are unable for any reason to prepare a bona fide tender, provided you advise us of this promptly, this will not prejudice you for inclusion in any future tender list for which we consider your firm suitable.

Yours sincerely

Dee Signer
