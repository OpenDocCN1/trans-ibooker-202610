## A Appraisal stage

### Stage description

Most projects commence with stage A. However stage A, Appraisal, is necessary when the client is not clear what his/her requirements are or indeed whether to proceed with a project. Often, the benefits of a project compared with the anticipated cost and time to design and carry out the works need to be determined before the client can make a decision.

Clients are often wildly optimistic about the cost and time necessary to carry out a project to their requirements, and one outcome of this stage is to acquaint the client of the current market conditions and attempt to resolve any concerns.

A proposed scope of services, such as shown in 'A Specimen proposed scope of services', pages 85–85, can support a proposal to a prospective client by indicating the designer's methodology and reassuring the client that important matters such as cost and time will be dealt with professionally. Alternatively, the detailed design stages shown in the Services Schedule of ID/10 (pages B, c and d) can be adapted for a specific project.

### RIBA Plan of Work

The RIBA Outline Plan of Work 2007 describes the key tasks for this stage as, 'Identification of client's needs, business case and possible constraints on development. Preparation of feasibility studies and assessment of options to enable the client to decide whether to proceed.'

### ID/10

Stage A is a 'Design Stage' on page 6 of the Schedules in ID/10.

### Tasks: Interior designer

When acting as the interior designer in a design team, the designer will carry out only the following task (deliverables in bold):

- Contribute a section on the interior requirements to an appraisal report.

### Lead consultant

When acting as lead consultant, the designer will carry out the above task as well as all of the following:

- Receive from client and evaluate initial statement of requirements.

- Advise the client on the need for other consultants' services and involve them as necessary to provide information on engineering, cost, timetable, etc.

- Assist the client in identifying possible site(s)/building(s).

- Visit possible site(s)/building(s).

- Obtain from the quantity surveyor preliminary cost and timescale estimates for preferred option(s).

- Consult relevant statutory authorities and consider preparing outline planning application.

- Appraise alternatives; indicate preferred option(s).

- Present an appraisal report (incorporating the above deliverables) to, and review options with, the client.

On smaller projects, stages A and B, Design Brief, are often carried out in the same exercise.

## A Notes on letter to client re appraisal

Appraisal

When a complex project is contemplated by a client, often he/she knows only the overall need (in the following example new office accommodation) but not precisely its detailed requirements. Important information, such as the cost and timescale, on which the client will base a decision, cannot be estimated with any degree of accuracy at this preliminary stage.

Designers can perform a valuable service by collecting information about the client's general requirements, assembling a design team and investigating alternative options, e.g. converting an existing building or building new space.

Ployer Enterprises: Appraisal

I refer to our recent meeting and I am pleased that you have chosen us to advise you on your proposed offices relocation. You require a preliminary appraisal of Ployer's office space requirements in order to meet the needs of new markets for your products. Following that, you have asked us to set out how we would organise the design and execution of the project.

You imagine that you might require a further 3,000m² of floor area in addition to what you now occupy. You have outgrown your existing offices and wish to find an existing building you can occupy with perhaps a site large enough to later expand into if necessary. Failing this, you are prepared to consider a new-build. Your tentative budget is £600,000 and you would like to see the move completed in 12–18 months’ time.

I enclose a proposed scope of services showing how, if appointed, we would approach this problem. Please note that we would be frequently monitoring the estimated costs and timescale of our proposals as we carry out our work. It may be necessary to carry out substantial building works and so, in analysing our tentative proposals, I may have to seek the advice of an architect and a quantity surveyor. I will suggest firms and then ask them to contact you to agree their terms of employment by you. I will also ask several commercial estate agents to identify suitable existing buildings and sites.

Of course, at this preliminary stage, I cannot comment on whether your proposed budget and timetable are consistent with your requirements (which have not yet been determined in detail). However, I expect that this exercise will allow us to obtain some approximate cost estimates to enable you to take a decision as to whether to proceed to the next stage which would be the detailed analysis of your requirements and the preparation of a design brief.

Yours sincerely
