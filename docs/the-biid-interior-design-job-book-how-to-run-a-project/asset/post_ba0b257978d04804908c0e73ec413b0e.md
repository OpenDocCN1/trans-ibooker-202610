- the limit of the designer's liability (clause 2.4 and section 7)
- appointment, payment and responsibilities of others (clause 3.8)
- no set-off (clause 5.20)
- payment notices (clause 5.19)

After the terms have been explained to a consumer client, the designer should write to the client confirming this (see the first paragraph of ‘p3 Model appointment letter to consumer client’, page 38)

With commercial clients, it can usually be assumed that they understand the terms of any contract they enter into or, if not, have obtained legal advice.

Architects who are carrying out interior design as part of providing architectural design services should ensure that they have contracted with the client using the standard forms ACA SFA/O8, SFA/99 or CE/99 and appending the relevant clauses from ID/10 Annexes A and/or B.

## Right to cancel

Further consumer protection is provided in The Cancellation of Contracts made in a Consumer's Home or Place of Work etc. Regulations 2008. These regulations give a consumer client cancellation rights in respect of a contract for goods and/or services (see ‘p3 Explanation of ID/10 terms’, clause 10, page 69).

## Services Schedule

The Services Schedule can be:

- left bound in ID/10 (the pages entitled ‘Schedule of Interior Design Services’) with the appropriate boxes ticked and the box at the bottom of page A completed in both copies
- torn out and a bespoke version prepared by the designer. The schedule should follow the same format as the printed version in its division and numbering of parts because these are referred to in ID/10’s Conditions and Annexes
- incorporated in the letter of appointment.

When using the Services Schedule, services to be provided should be ticked relating to the various parts:

- Part 1, Design Stages: if the designer is acting as a designer
- Part 2, Installation Management: if the works are to be built by an independent contractor
- Part 2, Installation Management: if the FF&E is to be procured by the designer acting as an agent. Annex B (parts B1 and B2) to the Conditions will apply.

## Project data

This is to be completed by the designer to show all of the information specific to the project.

## Fees and expenses

The designer can either include the Schedule of Fees and Expenses shown on page 'Notes 8' of ID/10 or include his/her own form (see 'P3 Specimen Schedule of Fees and Expenses' on page 44 and 'P3 Specimen alternative payment instalments schedule', page 45) if more applicable. This should include:

- the method of calculating fees
- an interim payment instalments schedule
- the frequency of accounts
- recoverable expenses and any associated handling charges
- disbursements and any associated handling charges.

## Brief

A schedule of the client’s requirements, as far as is provided to the designer at the date of the agreement, should be attached. If the client cannot articulate his/her requirements (including cost or timetable) at this stage, this should be noted in the appointment letter.

## Attachments

Copies of any relevant additional information, e.g. a schedule of client-appointed consultants and/or specialists, a survey of the property which has been supplied by the client, etc. should also be attached to the executed agreement.

## Delivery

The designer should send one copy each of the completed ID/10, the Services Schedule and the brief, together with two copies of the appointment letter, to the client asking him/her to sign both copies of the letter and to return them both. The designer should then sign both copies of the letter and return one copy to client, placing the other copy in the project file along with the other completed copy of ID/10, the Services Schedule and the brief.

## P3 Notes on appointment letters

The designer must determine whether the client is a consumer or a commercial client and send the appropriate letter (see the section on negotiated clauses in ‘P3 How to use ID/10’, page 33). The appointment letter sets out terms specific to the particular project (with optional items in blue) including but not limited to:

- the time limit for action or proceedings when acting in a professional capacity (ID/10 clause 7.1). If this is not stated, the time limit will be six years (or twelve years if the contract is executed as a deed)
- the limit of liability when acting in a professional capacity (ID/10 clause 7.2). This must bear a reasonable relation to the construction value of the works in order to be enforceable (see ‘Applicable case law’: Moores v Yakeley, page 282)
- any desired budget or timetable should be noted. If the client has not defined his/her requirements at this stage this should be noted. Clause 3.4 should be brought to the client’s attention
- other consultants whose advice may be required
- the client’s representative who is to instruct the designer on behalf of the client
- if the client is a consumer: confirmation that certain clauses have been agreed by the client
- if the designer is acting as contractor: the agreed contract sum (clause A2), the agreed completion date (clause A4), the period of liability (clause A8)
- if the designer is procuring FF&E as an agent under Annex B: part B2: the fee for doing this
- if the designer is acting as retailer directly supplying FF&E under Annex B, part B3: the basis on which this will be charged to the client and the liability period
- fees and expenses to be charged (or note if this is covered in an enclosed schedule of fees and expenses)
- jurisdiction: when Scottish law is to apply, clauses 9.3.3, 9.3.4, and 1.2 should be struck out, and replaced with clause 9.3.3 from the RIBA Standard Conditions of Appointment for an Architect 2010
- the default method of ultimate dispute resolution is shown as arbitration which provides privacy and protection against a security for costs application. If litigation is preferred, this should be stated in the letter
- any other relevant points specific to the project.

The designer should ensure that the letter is addressed to the correctly identified client (if it is a company, ensure that the name is exactly correct), enclose two copies and request that the client:

- sign each of the two copies of the letter in the appropriate signature blank at its foot (blanks to be shown for both the client and the designer) and return one to the designer
- sign both copies of the agreement, return them both for the designer to sign and to return one copy to the client.
