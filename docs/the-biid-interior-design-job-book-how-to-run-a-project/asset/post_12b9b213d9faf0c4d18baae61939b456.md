## J Mobilisation stage

Stage description
Stage J, Mobilisation (the period during which the chosen contractor is mobilising resources), covers the preparation and execution of the building contract documents (which should be done before the contractor takes possession of the site) and the issuance to the contractor of copies of Production Information necessary to construct the works.

### RIBA Plan of Work
The RIBA Outline Plan of Work 2007 describes the key tasks for this stage as, 'Letting the building contract, appointing the contractor. Issuing of information to the contractor. Arranging site handover to the contractor.'

### ID/10
Stage J is an 'Installation Management Stage' on page A of the Schedule of Interior Design Services in ID/10.

### Tasks: Interior designer/ lead consultant
When acting as the interior designer in a design team or when acting as lead consultant, the designer will carry out only the following seven tasks (deliverables in bold):

- **Advise the client** on the appointment of the contractor and on the responsibilities of the parties and the contract administrator (CA) under the building contract.
- Assist the quantity surveyor to prepare the **building contract** and arrange execution.
- Provide **construction information** to the contractor and specialists, including sufficient detailed drawing and specification information for the construction of the project.
- Receive **insurance policies** submitted by the contractor; advise the client of any need to insure.
- Receive a **programme** submitted by the contractor.
- Receive a **health and safety plan** submitted by the contractor.
- Arrange for client to employ a **site inspector**, if required.

### Contractor
When acting as contractor under Annex A of ID/10, the designer will carry out only the following tasks:

- **Mobilise resources** to carry out the building works.
- Enter into any necessary specialist **subcontracts**.
- Advise the client of any need to insure; take out/amend and submit to the client copies of any necessary **insurance policies**.

- Prepare a **programme**.
- Prepare a **health and safety plan**.

On smaller projects, stages J, K, Construction to Practical Completion, and L, Post Practical Completion, are often carried out in the same exercise.

## J Notes on letters before construction

### To contractor
When a tenderer has been selected, the designer should write to the tenderer:

- enclosing two copies of the contract documents, asking the tenderer to execute and return them
- requesting copies of the tenderer's relevant insurance policies and evidence that associated premiums have been paid. If MW 05 is the contract form then policies are required under the following clauses:

5.3: Insurance for personal injury or death; injury or damage to property

together with:

5.4A: Insurance of the works by the contractor in the joint names of the contractor and the employer (where the works are not an extension to, or an alteration of, an existing structure)

or

5.4B: Insurance of existing structures and the works by the employer in the joint names of the contractor and the employer (where the works are an extension to, or an alteration of, an existing structure and the employer can obtain the insurance in joint names)

or

5.4C: Insurance of existing structures by the employer in the employer's own name (where the works are an extension to, or an alteration of, an existing structure and where the employer is a residential occupier and cannot obtain the insurance in joint names). If this clause is chosen, clause 5.4A should also remain operative so that the contractor is required to insure the works in joint names

- requesting copies of the construction programme
- requesting copies of the anticipated payments schedule.

As clients are often tempted to directly instruct the builder, it is important to explicitly warn the contractor that work not the subject of the contract or a designer's instruction will not be paid for (and perhaps also that the cost of removing it will be deducted).

It is also important that it be made clear to the builder that quality control is his/her responsibility, not the designer's.

### To client
When the builder has correctly executed the contract documents and returned them to the designer, the designer should send both copies to the client for execution and request that both copies be returned to the designer. The designer should then return one copy to the tenderer and store the other in the designer's project file.

Just prior to construction commencing, the designer should write to the client reminding him/her to:

- insure the works under clause 5.4B (or clause 5.4C, if either is a provision of the building contract)
- enter the building works area only with the permission of the contractor
- provide on-site services to the contractor
- observe safety precautions
- not directly instruct the contractor
- make payment against designer's certificates within the contractual time (14 days in MW 05)
- not deduct sums from that shown in the certificate without giving proper notice
- not interfere with the designer's certifying function.
