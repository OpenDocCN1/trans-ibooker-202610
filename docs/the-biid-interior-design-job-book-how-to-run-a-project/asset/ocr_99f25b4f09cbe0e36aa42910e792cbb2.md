# E Drawing standards

# Drawings

Drawings are essentially a method of communicating complex, annotated pictorial data. Early drafts are used to convey the concept to the client and are then developed stage by stage as necessary to inform planners, building regulation officials and finally the contractor.

At all stages, it is important that the drawings are clear and consistent. It is helpful therefore if the format is standardised as far as possible to make them easily accessible for all the parties who will read them.

> 'Back roll' drawings so that the printing is on the outside – that way they will stay unrolled when they are laid out.

# Size

Most construction drawings are prepared on A1 sized sheets, 840mm wide by 594mm high. This has an area of half a square metre in the proportion of 1 to 1.414, the 'root two' ratio. This means that dividing the sheet in half vertically gives an A2 sized sheet in the same proportion and so on through A3 (420 x 297mm) and A4 (210 x 297mm).

Small projects can be drawn on A2 or A3 sheets.

# Layout

It is useful to divide the A1 sheet into eight A4 size vertical panels (or an A3 sheet into two A4 panels), using various combinations of the A4 panels as necessary for various sized drawings.

Rather than conventionally showing a title block in the lower right-hand corner, locating the title information along the bottom margin, and revisions boxes along the top, leaves the majority of the sheet available for drawing.

> 'God is in the details.' Ludwig Mies van der Rohe.

# Symbols

There are a number of symbols that are universally understood by all people who read building drawings and these should be used.
