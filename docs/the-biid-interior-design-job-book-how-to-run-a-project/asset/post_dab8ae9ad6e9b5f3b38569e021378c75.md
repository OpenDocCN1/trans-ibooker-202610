
## P3 Explanation of ID/10 terms

The following is a list of the terms of ID/10 (in blue italics) together with explanations (in black) of those that are not self-evident:

## 1 GENERAL

### Interpretation

The headings and notes to the Conditions are for convenience only and do not affect interpretation. Words denoting natural persons include corporations and firms and vice versa.

### Applicable law

This Agreement is subject to the law of England unless otherwise agreed and specified in the Project Data and subject to clause 9.3 the parties submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of the specified jurisdiction.

This clause is very useful to avoid disputes with foreign clients or projects being heard in foreign courts.

### Communications

Any notice or other document required under this Agreement shall be in writing and given or served by any effective means to the address of the recipient specified in this Agreement or such other address, including a postal address or fax number notified to the other party.

Communications between the Client and the Designer that are not such notices or documents may be sent to any other address, including an e-mail address, notified by the other party as an appropriate address for specific communications. Communications take effect on receipt, but if not in writing are of no effect unless and until confirmed in writing by the sender or the other party.

Communications sent by special delivery or recorded delivery shall be deemed (subject to proof to the contrary) to have arrived at the appropriate address on the second working day after posting.

All but trivial communications between the designer and any other parties involved in a project should be in writing, or confirmed in writing. Emails are beginning to be accepted by the courts as acceptable evidence but the designer should remember to make and file a paper copy when they are sent or received.

### Public holidays

Where under this Agreement an action is required within a specified period of days from a specified date, that period commences immediately after that date. The period shall include Saturdays and Sundays but shall exclude any day that is a public holiday.

### Duration

The provisions of this Agreement are without prejudice to the respective rights and obligations of the parties and continue in force as long as necessary to give effect to such rights and obligations.

## 2 OBLIGATIONS AND AUTHORITY OF THE DESIGNER

### Duty of care

The Designer:

when performing the Services under Parts 1 and 2 of the Services Schedule, shall exercise reasonable skill, care and diligence in accordance with the normal standards of the Designer’s profession in performing the Services and discharging all the obligations under this clause 2.

This is the key protection given to clients in respect of work carried out by the designer working in a professional capacity. The designer should not change this wording without consulting his/her PI insurer and perhaps solicitor.

- 2.1.2 when performing Services under Parts 3 and 4 of the Services Schedule, shall do so in accordance with the terms of Annex A and/or Annex B of that schedule.

When the designer is working in a commercial capacity, e.g. as a contractor and/or retailer, he/she is held to a lesser standard as, for these tasks, the designer is in a commercial relationship with the client (see 'p3 Designer's responsibilities to clients', page 26).

### 2.2 Duty to inform

The Designer shall keep the Client informed of progress in the performance of the Services and of any issue that may materially affect the Brief, the Project Cost, the Timetable, or the quality of the Project.

It is important that the designer should notify the client in writing when it becomes apparent that an incompatibility exists in the client's instructions, e.g. between the brief and the project cost. Otherwise the client could reasonably claim that he/she was not made aware of an important issue and was thus prevented from making a timely decision regarding it. Equally, the client should do the same or he/she may be liable for additional fees for additional work under clause 5.8.

- 2.3 The Designer shall inform the Client upon becoming aware of:

- 2.3.1 a need to appoint Other Persons, other than those named in the Project Data, to perform work or services in connection with the Project; and/or

- 2.3.2 any information, decision or action required from the Client or others in connection with performance of the Services.

### Limitation of liability

- 2.4 The Designer does not warrant:

- 2.4.1 that planning permission and other approvals from third parties will be granted at all, or if granted, will be granted in accordance with any anticipated time-scale;

Designers cannot warrant that the project will be granted any approvals; doing so would certainly not be covered by the designer's PI policy. However, it is not uncommon for professionals to agree with the client that they will be paid a supplementary fee contingent on a precisely defined outcome, e.g. that the planning authority will resolve to grant a planning consent for a project to the designer's designs (see 'p3 Model appointment letter to client (contingent)', page 40).

- 2.4.2 compliance with the Project Cost and/or the Timetable, which may need to be reviewed for such matters as, but not limited to:

- a) approved variations arising from design development or requested by the Client;

- b) delays caused by any Other Person; and/or

- c) any other factors beyond the control of the Designer;

Designers acting in a purely professional capacity should not warrant that the project will be built within any budget or to any timetable as the responsibility for achieving this is mainly outside the designer’s control. Again, doing so would probably not be covered by the designer’s PII policy.

However, if acting as a contractor (under Annex A) and/or retailer (under Annex B), the terms of the designer’s agreement may provide for completion within agreed costs and time.

- 2.4.3 the competence, performance, work, services, products or solvency of Other Persons appointed by the Client

Where the designer is practising in a professional capacity, he/she is not responsible for the performance of other consultants, contractors, etc. unless they are employed directly by the designer. Therefore it is important to ensure that these are employed by the client.

- 2.4.4 the accuracy of any survey made by the Designer unless the survey is specially commissioned by the Client as part of the Services.

Designers are not liable for surveys (including measured drawings) which they prepare for their own use in designing the project. However, if the work to be surveyed is extensive then the designer would be sensible to advise the client to employ professional building surveyors (whom the designer can recommend) under clause 3.2 below. It will be cheaper for the client than paying the designer to do it, and the quality of the survey is likely to be higher.

### Collaboration

The Designer shall Collaborate with Other Persons named in the Project Data, or whose appointment is foreseeable and, as applicable, shall co-ordinate and integrate the information received into the Designer’s work.

### Designer's authority

The Designer shall act on behalf of the Client in the matters set out or necessarily implied in this Agreement or in project procedures agreed with the Client from time to time, but has no authority, without the Client’s prior approval:

- 2.6.1 to make or cause to be made any material alteration to or addition to or omission from the Services or the approved design.

- 2.6.2 to enter into any contractual or other commitment on behalf of the Client; or

- 2.6.3 to terminate the employment of Other Persons appointed by the Client.

- 2.6.4 In the event of an emergency, the Designer may issue instructions to a contractor to prevent danger to persons or material damage to the Project without the Client’s prior approval, and shall confirm such action in writing to the Client without delay.
