# # E Notes on contract documents

# ## Contract documents

The principal documents that make up the contract between the client and the contractor are:

+   - Building contract form: examples of standard forms are:
+     - Annex A of ID/10 if the designer is acting as contractor
+     - JCT Minor Works Building Contract (MW 05) if the designer is not acting as contractor and there is no bill of quantities
+     - JCT Intermediate Building Contract (IC 05) if there is a bill of quantities.

+   - Drawings: these are divided into:
+     - location (general arrangement)
+     - component
+     - assembly (detail).

Thus a window’s location would be shown in plan and elevation general arrangement drawings (say, at 1:50 scale), its overall appearance as a fabricated element would be shown in a component drawing (say, 1:20) and the details of its incorporation into the building fabric would be shown in detail drawing(s) (say, 1:5).

+   - Specification and schedule(s): as shown in ‘E Notes on specifications and schedules’, page 150, specification clauses are divided into:
+     - preliminaries (contract conditions)
+     - components
+     - execution.

+   - Bill of quantities (optional on small projects).

# ## Conflicts

Due to the complexity of most projects, it is not unusual for conflicts to occur between (or even within) contract documents, e.g. an element being noted in a drawing as being fabricated by one manufacturer and shown in the specification or a schedule as by another. To reduce the likelihood of such conflicts, the designer should:

+   - avoid repeating information. For example, product names or suppliers of an element should be shown only in the specification or schedules, and the element’s location, shape, size, etc. shown only in the drawings. Items on the drawings should be cross-referenced by clause number to the specification

+   - include a clause in the specification preliminaries to require the contractor, upon discovering such a conflict, to obtain an instruction from the designer resolving the matter before proceeding.

# ## Legal interpretation

The general rule is that where a term has been specifically drafted for a contract, e.g. in the drawings, specification or schedules, then that term will take precedence over a standard term such as in the building contract.

However, this rule can be overridden by an express term of the building contract. For example, clause 1.2 of MW 05 states that ‘nothing contained in the Contract Drawings, the Contract Specification or the Work Schedules ... shall override or modify the Agreement or these Conditions.’ If the designer wishes to amend this provision, clause 1.2 (or its equivalent) should be amended in the building contract form itself.
