
Normally, if a claimant obtains a favourable judgement on only one part of a claim then the whole burden of costs falls on the defendant, even if all the other parts of the claim are unsuccessful. These costs can often exceed the sum in dispute. Therefore a malevolent client can use this 'costs risk' to bully insurers into settling a complex vexatious claim in his/her favour because of the vast sums required to be spent in legal fees to defend a wide-ranging claim. This clause provides that costs are due only on those parts of a claim that he/she succeeds with and it is a disincentive for clients to exaggerate their claims so as to intimidate designers and their insurers.

This clause is second in importance only to clause 5.20 in enforcing fee payment and is yet another disincentive for a client tempted not to pay.

This clause must be individually negotiated with a consumer client before he/she enters into the contract.

## 6 INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY: USE OF INFORMATION

### Intellectual property

The Designer shall own all intellectual property rights including copyright in the drawings and all other work produced in the performance of the Services and generally asserts the Designer's moral rights and all other rights to be identified as the author of such work.

Designers should advise clients that it is prudent to ensure that copyright issues are properly dealt with, say, in connection with selling on a project with the benefit of the designer's scheme.

Copyright can be a powerful safeguard for the designer. The best way to evidence authorship is to ensure that all preparatory documentation that goes into the creation of a work is signed by the designer, dated and then stored in a safe place. Such documentation could include sketches, drafts, plans etc. at significant stages in the design process.

Once the work is complete, filing a copy with a recognised third party such as a solicitor, bank or independent organisation will help provide independent evidence of a latest possible date of creation. ACID (Anti Copying in Design), acid.eu.com, offers such a service through the ACID Design Data Bank.

### 6.2 No part of any design by the Designer may be registered or used by the Client without a written assignment of rights by the Designer.

Copyright is a non-registrable right; it arises automatically. However, if a designer has created a three-dimensional design, an application for its registration can be made to the UK Intellectual Property Office, www.ipo.gov.uk, or for a registered Community design (rcd) at OHIM (The Office for Harmonisation in the Internal Market) www.oami.europa.eu. Design registration offers protection in 27 member states for 25 years.
