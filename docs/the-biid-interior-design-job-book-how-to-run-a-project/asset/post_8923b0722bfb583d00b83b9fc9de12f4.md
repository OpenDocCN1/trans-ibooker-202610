
## Introduction

## Letters and forms

The book adopts a very hands-on approach to running a job, and contains model letters and specimen forms (for both in-house and external use) which the authors have developed in over 40 years of practice, together with notes on how to use them. Designers are encouraged to use them, not necessarily exactly as set out but adapted to suit each practice and project as necessary.

## Illustrative projects

In order to clearly illustrate how the procedures in this book should be followed, two imaginary projects have been created. The first is an office refurbishment which illustrates how a designer can:

- prepare a proposed scope of services to explain to a lay client how the designer will go about carrying out a project. This can be used in support of a proposal in order to demonstrate to clients that the designer has the organisational capacity to satisfactorily safeguard the client’s interests, particularly cost and time
- carry out a detailed space planning exercise to accommodate a complex organisation, which will form the basis of the design brief.

On this imaginary project, the designer is working as part of a multidisciplinary design team. This covers the RIBA Plan of Work Preparation stages A and B.

> Designers should be process orientated, not product driven.

As interior designers are sometimes the first professional that clients approach, it is assumed for the purposes of the second imaginary project in this book that this commission, whilst mostly the interior renovation of an existing dwelling, also involves a new external extension which is designed by an architect reporting to the interior designer who is also acting as lead consultant and contract administrator (ca). This covers all the RIBA Plan of Work Design, Pre-construction, Construction and Use stages, c through l.

## ID/10

A proportion of this book is devoted to explaining ID/10, the BIID Agreement for Interior Design Services, produced in association with the RIBA, which sets out in detail the most important relationship in the process: between designer and client.

## MW 05

It is assumed that the building contract will be the Joint Contracts Tribunal’s (JCT) Minor Works Building Contract 2005 Revision 2 2009 (MW 05). There are a number of other contract forms which can be used but MW 05 is well known by consultants and contractors and is generally satisfactory for small projects where a bill of quantities has not been prepared.

## Drawings

A section on technical drawing standards and presentation is also included to indicate how complex drawn information can be made easily accessible.

## Scope

No book such as this can hope to foresee all occurrences that might arise in the course of carrying out a design project; thus the authors have concentrated on the most common situations. Not all of the items in this book will apply to each individual project – the designer should choose just those that are appropriate for the job in hand. It is not suggested that examples should always be used exactly as shown but adapted as necessary to suit the circumstances of any particular job or client.

## Legal advice

It is strongly suggested that on any matters with which the designer is unfamiliar, he/she takes advice from a specialist construction law solicitor. Even though such a lawyer will probably charge considerably more per hour than most designers, it will be cheaper and safer than getting into trouble.

## Diana Yaley PPBIID FCSD

Diana Yaley trained at the Inchbald School and began practising interior design in 1981. Soon after, she joined Yaley Associates, an architectural practice, to establish its interior and garden design department. She became a member of the British Institute of Interior Design (BIID) in 1984 and was elected a Fellow in 1994. She joined the BIID Council in 2003 and chaired the CPD Committee.

In 2004/05 she chaired a joint committee with the Royal Institute of British Architects (RIBA) to draft the first British standard form of appointment for interior design services, ID/05. In 2005 she was voted president-elect and served as president in 2005/06. After a gap of a year, she returned to Council in 2008 with responsibility for development, and was instrumental in the BIID’s being granted ‘Institute’ status by Companies House. In 2009 she was made a Fellow of the Chartered Society of Designers.

In 2008 she was granted the BIID Award of Merit for lifetime service to the profession, only the third such award ever given. In 2009 she was instrumental in the Construction Industry Council (CIC), the umbrella body for consultants in the construction industry, accepting the BIID as full members. She serves on the CIC as the BIID’s representative.

Her work has been the subject of interior design and garden design awards and has been published in many books, professional journals and television programmes in the UK and abroad. She serves regularly as a judge on awards juries and is a frequent contributor to design-related magazines.

She has written two books: Indoor Gardening published in 2003 and Conservatories in 2009.

## Stephen Yaley BArch AIA RIBA ACA

Stephen Yaley graduated from Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute in New York in 1963. He became a member of the American Institute of Architects (AIA) in 1970 and of the RIBA in 1971. ## Cast of characters

It has been assumed for the purpose of most of the model letters and specimen forms in this book that they refer to a substantial residential project for a consumer client. This has been chosen only because such projects are a common type and can often be quite expensive and complex, involving several consultants and specialist contractors. It is hoped that the reader will forgive the rather literal names given to the dramatis personae, who are:

- **Designer** Signer Associates Ltd, an interior design practice run by Dee Signer. On this project Dee is the interior designer, but also acts as design team leader, contract administrator (ca), specialist contractor and Furniture, Fixtures and Equipment (FF&E) supplier. Dee is a sensible professional who tempers her flair for design with good business sense. Long experience has taught her how to run projects capably and this has kept the practice profitable and out of trouble.

- **Employer (Client)** Mike Ployer first came across Dee when she was a member of the design team renovating the offices of his business premises and he subsequently asked her to alter his house. Mr Ployer, who had been entirely reasonable throughout most of the project, unfortunately became involved near the end of construction with Phyllis Tyne, who it quickly transpired was not in sympathy with Dee’s designs and was frustrated by not being able to put her own stamp on the project.

- **Architect** Generally architects are the design team leaders on construction projects but where their role is a relatively minor one, as in the second example in this book, they can work under the direction of the interior designer. Archie Tect of Tect Associates Ltd, with whom Dee had worked before and whom she knew to be a team player, was brought in by Dee to design ancillary building works under her direction.

- **Quantity Surveyor** Consultants with the high reputation of Sir Veigh Orr & Partners Ltd would not normally wish to be involved in modest projects. However, Sir Veigh is an old friend of Dee’s family and has agreed to advise. Quantity surveyors often offer to write the specification as part of their service but Dee prefers to do this herself as she likes to keep careful control over the quality of materials and workmanship. William (Bill) O’Quant is Sir Veigh’s able assistant.

- **Engineers** Mr Ployer’s brief includes structural alterations to existing interior load-bearing walls and so Dee sensibly arranges for the client to be advised by Roland Jay of RS Jay Associates Ltd.

  Similarly, the project involves heating and electrical work and so Dee arranges for The Cannical/Trickle Partnership Ltd, led by Mick Cannical and Eli Trickle, to advise.

- **Main Contractor** Although Signer Associates will carry out certain items of decorative work with her own specialist tradesmen, she has arranged for most of the new and refurbishment works to be built by Durr Construction Ltd, run by William (Bill) Durr. Durr specialises in high quality residential refurbishment projects. As with many building firms, they employ mostly foreign labour although the work is directed on site by Cyrus (Cy) Taygent.

- **FF&E Suppliers** Dee will be procuring most of the FF&E for the Ployer house, acting as an agent and paying for it from a client account established by Mr Ployer. However, Dee has a sideline in supplying specialist fabrics and will be supplying these to the cushion and curtain makers, acting in these instances effectively as a retailer.
