Similarly, the probability of having successes on the first k attempts followed by failures on the remaining n − k attempts is Π^k * (1 − Π)^(n − k). In fact, this is the probability of any specific ordering of k successes and n − k failures; what remains is to determine the number of ways that these successes and failures can be rearranged. Think of this set of successes and failures as n slips of paper, k of which have a 1 written on them and n − k of which have a 0 written on them. Ignoring the 0s and 1s, there are n * (n − 1) * (n − 2) * ... * 2 * 1 = n! ways to arrange these slips of paper, as there are n ways to select the first slip, n − 1 ways to select the second slip, and so on. For any specific arrangement of the slips of paper, rearranging the ones or zeroes won’t really change anything, so we have to account for lots of identical arrangements.
