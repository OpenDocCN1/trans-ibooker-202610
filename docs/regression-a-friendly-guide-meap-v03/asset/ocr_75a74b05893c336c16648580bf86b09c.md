nearest inch, we predict his height to be 69.77 inches; this prediction is unlikely to be off by more than 4.98 inches or so. These predictions are better than those of the null model in two important ways:

+   - the *point estimate*— the single best guess for the son’s height— is more accurate for each group, and
+   - the *margin of error* is now smaller for each prediction, since the groups have smaller standard deviations than all of the sons considered together.

Getting improved predictions is great, but doing it this way is awfully tedious. Fortunately, we are close to a much more streamlined method for making better predictions, for any father’s height, so let’s keep going, adding a black point at the average height of the sons for each group of fathers. Doing so reveals something absolutely remarkable: most of the black points are very close to being on the same line! This is the *line of averages*, shown below in black.
