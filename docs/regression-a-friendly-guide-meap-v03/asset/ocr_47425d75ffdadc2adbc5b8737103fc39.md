When working with two random variables, $A$ and $B$, we often want to know how they interact; we care about more than just their individual averages and variances. The *covariance* of $A$ and $B$, denoted $\mathrm{Cov}(A, B)$, does just what the name says it does—it measures how $A$ and $B$ vary together:

The covariance therefore measures how $A$ and $B$ simultaneously relate, on average, to their individual averages; note that $\mathrm{Cov}(A, A) = \mathrm{Var}(A)$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(B, B) = \mathrm{Var}(B)$. (Also note that we did something similar when we defined the correlation coefficient back in Chapter 2, even though we didn’t use the word covariance at the time.)
