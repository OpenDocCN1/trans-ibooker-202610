If the distributions of the features are unknown, we assume that a sample of _n_ vector time series generated by each state, of the form (_s<sub>i</sub>_, **_z_**<sub>_i_</sub><sup>T</sup>(_i_)), where _s<sub>i</sub>_ indicates the state and \(\mathbf{z}_1^T(i)\) a vector of time series that is transformed into the sample (_s<sub>j</sub>_, **_x_**<sub>_i_</sub>), where **_x_**<sub>_i_</sub> is the vector of features. With this information we estimate the joint distribution of the features given the state, _f_(**_x_**|_s<sub>j</sub>_), that is often assumed to be multivariate normal. In order to simplify the exposition in the next sections, we consider the parameters \(\mu_j\) and **_V_**<sub>_j_</sub> as known, although often they are estimated in the usual way.

# ## 5.5.1 Linear Discriminant Function

Suppose that the prior probabilities and the covariance matrices are the same in all states, that is, **_V_**<sub>_j_</sub> = **_V_** , and consider first the case _c_ = 2. Assuming normality, the vector of features **_x_**<sub>0</sub> is classified in the first state if

\((\mathbf{x}_0 - \mu_1)'\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_0 - \mu_1) < (\mathbf{x}_0 - \mu_2)'\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_0 - \mu_2).\)

Let
\(D_i = (\mathbf{x}_0 - \mu_i)'\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_0 - \mu_i), \quad i = 1, 2,\quad (5.48)\)

be the Mahalanobis distance between **_x_**<sub>0</sub> and the vector of means, the output series will be classified in the first population if _D_<sub>2</sub> > _D_<sub>1</sub>. A well-known alternative interpretation of this rule can be obtained by writing the equation as

\((\mu_1 - \mu_2)'\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_0 > \frac{1}{2}(\mu_1 - \mu_2)'\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\mu_1 + \mu_2),\)

implying that the features are combined to form a linear indicator, _v_ = **_w_**\('\)**_x_**<sub>0</sub>, where **_w_** = **_V_**<sup>-1</sup>(\(\mu_1 - \mu_2\)), and if this indicator is larger than _v_<sub>0</sub>, given by
\(v_0 = \frac{1}{2}(\mu_1 - \mu_2)'\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\mu_1 + \mu_2),\)

the first state is selected. This is the well know Fisher’s linear discriminant function (LDF). It is easy to see that this rule minimizes the sum of the classification errors \(p(\mathbf{z} \in s_2|s_1) + p(\mathbf{z} \in s_1|s_2)\), where the notation \(\mathbf{z} \in s_2\) means the series is classified in state _s_<sub>2</sub>.
In the general case of _c_ states, assuming that the covariance matrix is the same, the extension of the previous procedure is to compute the Mahalanobis distances (5.48) for _i_ = 1, ... , _c_ and classify the series in the state whose mean vector is closer to the observed vector of features. This rule can also be expressed as finding _c_-1 separating hyperplanes into _c_ regions.

When the parameters are unknown and estimated from a training sample of size _n_ with _n<sub>i</sub>_ elements from the _i_th state and \(n = \sum_{i=1}^c n_i\), the mean vectors are estimated by the sample means and the covariance matrix by the pooled covariance matrix
\(\widehat{\mathbf{V}} = \sum_{i=1}^c \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (\mathbf{x}_{ij} - \bar{\mathbf{x}}_i)(\mathbf{x}_{ij} - \bar{\mathbf{x}}_i)'/(n-c).\quad (5.49)\)
