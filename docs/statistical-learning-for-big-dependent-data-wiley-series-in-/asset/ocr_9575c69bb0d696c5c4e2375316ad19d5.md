This provides a direct link between the EDFM and the work of Box and Tiao (1977). As explained in Chapter 3, Tiao and Tsay (1989) introduced simplification of VARMA models via the scalar component models that are linear combinations of the observed time series with specific dynamics. In particular, an SCM(0,0) is a white noise process and under the EDFM, the number of linear combinations that are white noise, or number of SCM(0,0), is $k - r$. Thus, the SCM(0,0) model is also linked to the EDFM.

Peña and Box (1987) proved that if the series $z_t$ follows a DFM in Eq. (6.1) with factors given by a VARMA($p_1, q_1$) process, then the series also follows a VARMA($p_2, q_2$) process with $p_1 = p_2$ and $q_2 = \max(p_1, q_1)$. However, the parameter matrices of the VARMA($p_2, q_2$) representation of the series $z_t$ are not identified, which can make the estimation of the VARMA model difficult and unstable.

# 6.2 FITTING A STATIONARY DFM TO DATA

Given the data $\{z_1, \dots, z_T\}$, where $z_t = (z_{t1}, \dots, z_{tk})'$, we assume, for simplicity, that each series $z_{it}$ is mean-adjusted. Let $\mathbf{Z}$ be the $T \times k$ data matrix, where $\mathbf{Z}' = [z_1, \dots, z_T]$. Then $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_z(h) = \sum_{t=h+1}^T z_t z_{t-h}' / T$ and, in particular, $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_z(0) = \mathbf{Z}'\mathbf{Z} / T$. In this section, we discuss different procedures for estimating the $k \times r$ loading matrix $\mathbf{P}$, the $T \times r$ matrix $\mathbf{F}$ of common factors, and the parameters of the models for the factors and the noises. We start with the case that the number of common factors $r$ is known, then discuss methods to select $r$.

In this section, we assume that $T > k$. The case of $T \le k$ will be discussed in Section 6.10, where we adopt a different formulation and use projected PCA to improve the efficiency in estimating the common factors.

# 6.2.1 Principal Components (PC) Estimation

Under certain conditions, consistent estimation for both, the EDFM and ADFM, can be obtained via the eigenvalue-eigenvector analysis of the sample covariance matrix $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_z(0)$. With $r$ given, the loading matrix $\mathbf{P}$ is estimated by the normalized eigenvectors of $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_z(0)$ corresponding to the largest $r$ eigenvalues. Denote the estimate by $\hat{\mathbf{P}}$ and since $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_z(0)$ is symmetric and positive definite for $T > k$, we have $\hat{\mathbf{P}}'\hat{\mathbf{P}} = \mathbf{I}$. The latent factors are then estimated by $\hat{f}_t = \hat{\mathbf{P}}' z_t$, which are the first $r$ PCs of the data matrix $\mathbf{Z}$. Note that pre-multiplying in (6.1), by $\hat{\mathbf{P}}'$, we have that

```
$\hat{\mathbf{P}}' \mathbf{z}_t = \hat{\mathbf{P}}' \mathbf{P} \mathbf{f}_t + \hat{\mathbf{P}}' \mathbf{n}_t$
```

and, for fixed $k$, $\hat{\mathbf{P}}$ would converge to $\mathbf{P}$ as $T \to \infty$. Therefore, we have, approximately, $\hat{\mathbf{P}}'\hat{\mathbf{P}} = \mathbf{I}$. For the EDFM, the noises $n_t$ are white noise and the linear combinations $\hat{\mathbf{P}}' n_t$ are zero mean variables and their sample mean would converge to zero as $T$ increases via the law of large numbers. For the ADFM, the proper conditions on the structure of the noises and $f_t$ are imposed so that the eigenvectors of $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_z(0)$ continue to provide consistent estimates of the loadings and the factors $\hat{f}_t = \hat{\mathbf{P}}' z_t$. See, Bai and Ng (2002, 2013).
