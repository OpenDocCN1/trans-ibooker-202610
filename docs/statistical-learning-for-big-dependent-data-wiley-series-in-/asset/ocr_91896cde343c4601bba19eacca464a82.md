# 9.3.4 Estimation of Variogram

For a second-order stationary spatial process $Z(oldsymbol{s}_j) = Y(oldsymbol{s}_i) + \epsilon(oldsymbol{s}_i)$ with data $oldsymbol{Z}$, one often uses method-of-moments to estimate covariogram or variogram. Matheron (1962) proposed the estimator

$$2\hat{\gamma}(oldsymbol{h}) = \frac{1}{|N(oldsymbol{h})|} \sum_{N(oldsymbol{h})} [Z(oldsymbol{s}_i) - Z(oldsymbol{s}_j)]^2, \quad oldsymbol{h} \in R^d, 	ag{9.19}$$

where

$$N(oldsymbol{h}) = \{(oldsymbol{s}_i, oldsymbol{s}_j)|oldsymbol{s}_i - oldsymbol{s}_j = oldsymbol{h}; i, j = 1, \dots, m\},$$

and $|N(oldsymbol{h})|$ is the number of distinct pairs in $N(oldsymbol{h})$.

When data are irregularly spaced in $R^d$, the variogram estimator in Eq. (9.19) is often smoothed by using instead

$$2\gamma^+(oldsymbol{h}) = 	ext{ave}\{[Z(oldsymbol{s}_i) - Z(oldsymbol{s}_j)]^2|(i,j) \in N(oldsymbol{h}), oldsymbol{h} \in U(oldsymbol{h}(\ell))\}, 	ag{9.20}$$

where the region $U(oldsymbol{h}(\ell))$ is some specified tolerance region in $R^d$ around $oldsymbol{h}(\ell)$ for $\ell = 1, \dots, g$ with $g$ being a positive integer. The choice of tolerance region is discussed in Journel and Huijbregts (1978). Following the time series literature, one can also employ the estimator

$$\widehat{C}(oldsymbol{h}) = \frac{1}{|N(oldsymbol{h})|} \sum_{N(oldsymbol{h})} [Z(oldsymbol{s}_j) - ar{Z}][Z(oldsymbol{s}_j) - ar{Z}], 	ag{9.21}$$

where $ar{Z} = \sum_{i=1}^m Z(oldsymbol{s}_i)/m$ and $N(oldsymbol{h})$ is defined as before in Eq. (9.19). For the case of $d=1$, asymptotic properties of the estimators $\widehat{C}(oldsymbol{h})$ and $2\hat{\gamma}(oldsymbol{h})$ have been studied in the time series literature.

# 9.3.5 Testing Spatial Dependence

Given the spatial data $oldsymbol{Z} = [Z(oldsymbol{s}_1), \dots, Z(oldsymbol{s}_m)]'$, one important question to ask is whether the data have spatial dependence. That is, we are interested in testing $H_0 : Y(oldsymbol{s})$ has no spatial dependence versus $H_a : Y(oldsymbol{s})$ is spatially dependent. Assume that the variogram is isotropic, i.e. $2\gamma_y(oldsymbol{h}) = 2\gamma^*(\|oldsymbol{h}\|)$, and that the mean of $Z(\cdot)$ (and of $Y(\cdot)$) is constant, Cressie and Wikle (2011) consider an estimate of the variogram

$$2\hat{\gamma}_z^*(oldsymbol{h}) = 	ext{ave}\{[Z(oldsymbol{s}_i) - Z(oldsymbol{s}_j)]^2|\|oldsymbol{s}_i - oldsymbol{s}_j\| \in N(h); i,j = 1, \dots, m\}, 	ag{9.22}$$

where $N(h)$ is a tolerance region around $h$ such as $h \pm \Delta$ for some small $\Delta > 0$. This estimate is referred to as the *empirical variogram*. It is similar to the smoothed version in Eq. (9.20). These authors propose the test statistic

$$Q = \frac{\hat{\gamma}_z^*(h_1)}{\hat{\sigma}_z^2}, 	ag{9.23}$$

where $\hat{\sigma}_z^2 = \sum_{i=1}^m [Z(oldsymbol{s}_i) - \hat{\mu}_z]^2/(m-1)$, $\hat{\mu}_z = \sum_{i=1}^m Z(oldsymbol{s}_i)/m$, and $h_1$ is the smallest lag from all possible lags $h_1, \dots, h_L$.
