In what follows, for a $k$-dimensional random vector $z$, the $q$th norm is defined as $\|z\|_q = (E|z|^q)^{1/q}$ for $1 \le q \le k$.

Consider the linear regression model (7.25). Let $\{\epsilon_t\}$ be an i.i.d. sequence of random vectors and $F_t = \sigma\{\epsilon_i | i \le t\}$ be the $\sigma$-field generated by $\epsilon_i$ with $i \le t$. We assume that the components of $x_t$ and the error $e_t$ are well-defined real-valued measurable functions of $F_t$, that is,

$x_t = (g_1(F_t), \dots, g_k(F_t))'$ and $e_t = g_e(F_t)$.

For simplicity, one can think of $x_{it}$ and $e_t$ are linear functions of $F_t$. To quantify the serial dependence of time series data, we adopt the functional dependence measure of Wu (2005). Specifically, let

$\delta_{t,q,i} = \|x_{it} - x_{it}^*\|_q = \|g_i(F_t) - g_i(F_t^*)\|_q, \quad \text{(7.27)}$

$\delta_{t,q,e} = \|e_t - e_t^*\|_q = \|g_e(F_t) - g_e(F_t^*)\|_q, \quad \text{(7.28)}$

where $x_t^* = (g_1(F_t^*), \dots, g_k(F_t^*))'$ and $e_t^* = g_e(F_t^*)$ with $F_t^* = \sigma[\{\epsilon_i | i \le t \cap i \neq 0\} \cup \epsilon_0^*\}],$ where $\epsilon_0^*$ is a random vector independent of $\{\epsilon_i\}$ but follows the same distribution as $\epsilon_0$. Roughly speaking, for a given time index $t$ and positive integer $q$, $\delta_{t,q,i}$ of Eq. (7.27) quantifies the impact of changing $\epsilon_0$ to $\epsilon_0^*$ on $x_{it}$ as measured by the $q$th norm. Similarly, $\delta_{t,q,e}$ of Eq. (7.28) quantifies the impact of changing $\epsilon_0$ to $\epsilon_0^*$ on the error term $e_t$. These two dependence measures play a similar role as the impulse response weight $\psi_t$ of the moving-average representation of a linear time series model.

For a positive integer $m$, define the cumulative dependence measures as

$\Delta_{m,q,i} = \sum_{l=m}^\infty \delta_{t,q,l}, \quad \text{(7.29)}$

$\Delta_{m,q,e} = \sum_{l=m}^\infty \delta_{t,q,e}. \quad \text{(7.30)}$

These two measures quantify the cumulative impacts on $x_{it}$ and $e_t$, respectively, when one change $\epsilon_0$ to $\epsilon_0^*$. Again, one can think of these two measures as cumulative sums of the $\psi$-weights of a linear ARMA model. We say that the series $x_{it}$ and $e_t$ are short-range dependence if $\Delta_{m,q,i} < \infty$ and $\Delta_{m,q,e} < \infty$ for some fixed $m$. From the definition, it is seen that the impact of changing $\epsilon_0$ to $\epsilon_0^*$ on a time series is bounded if the series is short-range dependence, because $\delta_{t,q,i}$ and $\delta_{t,q,e}$ should approach zero under short-range dependence.

The cumulative dependence measures can be used, under the short-range dependence, to define dependence-adjusted norm (DAN) as follows:

$\|x_i\|_{q,a} = \sup_{m \ge 0} (m+1)^\alpha \Delta_{m,q,i}, \quad \alpha \ge 0,$

$\|e\|_{q,a} = \sum_{m \ge 0} (m+1)^\alpha \Delta_{m,q,e}, \quad \alpha \ge 0.$

These DANs are used to derive consistency of Lasso estimator for dependent data.
