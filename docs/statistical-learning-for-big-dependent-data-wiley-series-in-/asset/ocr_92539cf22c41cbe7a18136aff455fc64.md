$f_{2t}$ is a seasonal factor with period $s$ and dimension $r_2$ such that

$$
\Phi_2(B^s)\nabla_s^D f_{2t} = \theta_2(B^s)a_{2t}, \quad (6.52)
$$

where $\nabla_s = (1 - B^s)$ and $D \ge 0$, and $f_{3t}$ consists of stationary factors with dimension $r_3$ and follows the model

$$
\Phi_3(B)f_{3t} = \theta_3(B)a_{3t}, \quad (6.53)
$$

where the AR and MA matrix polynomials satisfy the identifiability conditions discussed in Chapter 3. In fact, we assume that, for each $i = 1, 2, 3$, $\{a_{it}\}$ is a Gaussian white noise process with mean $\mathbf{0}$ and diagonal covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ and all parameter matrices are diagonal. Here, $r_1 + r_2 + r_3 = r$ and we write $\mathbf{P} = [\mathbf{P}_1 \mathbf{P}_2 \mathbf{P}_3]$, where the submatrix $\mathbf{P}_i$ is of dimension $k \times r_i$, and $f_{it} = (f_{i1t}, \dots, f_{iri,t})'$, where $i = 1, 2, 3$. The innovation processes $\{a_{1t}\}$, $\{a_{2t}\}$, $\{a_{3t}\}$ and $\{e_t\}$ are mutually uncorrelated. We assume $\mathbf{P}'\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{I}_r$, implying that $\mathbf{P}'_i\mathbf{P}_i = \mathbf{I}_{r_i}$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$.

Assume, for simplicity, that $d = D = 1$. We define the sample generalized seasonal autocovariance (SGCV) matrices $C_{z,s}(h)$ as

$$
C_{z,s}(h) = \frac{s^{2d}}{T^{2d}} \sum_{t=h+1}^T z_t z'_{t-h}. \quad (6.54)
$$

The normalization constant in $C_{z,s}(h)$ is equal to $1/(T/s)^{2d}$, where $T/s$ is the number of seasons in the sample, whenever $T$ is a multiple of $s$. It can be shown, see Nieto et al. (2016), that when $h$ is fixed and $T \to \infty$, then, the sequence $\{C_{z,s}(h)\}$ converges weakly to a random matrix $\Gamma_{z,s}(h)$ that has the following properties: (a) for $h = js$ with $j$ being a positive integer, $\Gamma_{z,s}(h)$ almost surely has $r_1 + r_2$ positive eigenvalues and $k - r_1 - r_2$ zero eigenvalues; (b) for $k \neq js$, $\Gamma_{z,s}(h)$ has, almost surely, $r_1$ positive eigenvalues and $k - r_1$ zero eigenvalues. Let $\lambda_1(h), \dots, \lambda_k(h)$ be the eigenvalues of $\Gamma_{z,s}(h)$. Then, if $h = js$, the eigenvectors corresponding to the $r_1 + r_2$ positive eigenvalues form a basis of the column space of the submatrix $[\mathbf{P}_1 \mathbf{P}_2]$ and, if $h \neq js$, the eigenvectors corresponding to the $r_1$ positive eigenvalues form a basis for the column space of the submatrix $\mathbf{P}_1$.

Thus, we expect that the sample covariance matrices $C_{z,s}(h)$ have two disjoint subsets of eigenvalues: one contains relatively large positive values and the other consists of small ones close to zero. For the ordered eigenvalues, the small ones would occur at $i = r_1 + 1, \dots, k$, when $h \neq js$, and at $i = r_1 + r_2 + 1, \dots, k$, when $k = js$. A procedure based on these results to fit the model has been proposed by Nieto et al. (2016).
