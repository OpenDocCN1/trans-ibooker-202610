## 4.7 HETEROGENEITY FOR PARAMETER CHANGES

Another form of heterogeneity in time series analysis is caused by changes on the model parameters. Their effects can be observed gradually throughout the sampling period. There is a large literature on monitoring parameter changes in univariate time series models. First, mean and variance shifts in AR process have been studied from the Bayesian point of view by McCulloch and Tsay (1993). Changes in AR parameters have been considered by Lee et al. (2003), Gambay and Serban (2009), and Chan et al. (2014), among many others. Second, changes in the marginal variance were studied by Inclán and Tiao (1994), who proposed an iterative procedure based in a cumulative sum of squares statistics and by Bai (1994) and Bai and Perron (1998). Much less work has been carried out for multivariate time series analysis. Galeano and Peña (2007) have studied the detection of step changes in the variance and in the correlation structure of the components of a VARMA model using both the LR and cusum approaches. In what follows, we present procedures for detecting parameter changes in a univariate times series and covariance changes in a multivariate time series.

### 4.7.1 Parameter Changes in Univariate Time Series

Consider a univariate AR(p) times series that follows the model \(z_t = \boldsymbol{\phi}' \mathbf{Z}_{t-1} + a_t\) with \(1 \le t \le T\), where \(\boldsymbol{\phi} = (\phi_0, \phi_1, \dots, \phi_p)'\) and \(\mathbf{Z}_{t-1} = (1, z_{t-1}, \dots, z_{t-p})'\). Suppose that the series suffers m parameter changes at time indexes \(\tau_1 < \tau_2 < \dots < \tau_m\) with \(\tau_1 > 1\) and \(\tau_m < T\). The changes can be both in the AR coefficients and in the noise variance. For simplicity, we consider Var(a_t) = 1 to avoid any confusion in variance parameterization. Thus, when \(1 \le t < \tau_1\) the series follows the AR(p) model \(z_t = \boldsymbol{\phi}'_1 \mathbf{Z}_{t-1} + \sigma_1 a_t\), but when \(t = \tau_1\) the model changes to \(z_t = \boldsymbol{\phi}'_2 \mathbf{Z}_{t-1} + \sigma_2 a_t\). This second model applies to data in the interval \(\tau_1 \le t < \tau_2\). Again, at time \(t = \tau_2\), the AR parameter shift to \(\boldsymbol{\phi}'_3\) and the variance of the noise to \(\sigma_3\), and so on. Defining \(\tau_0 = 1\) and \(\tau_{m+1} = T\), this model can be written as

\(z_t = \sum_{i=1}^{m+1} (\boldsymbol{\phi}'_i \mathbf{Z}_{t-1} + \sigma_i a_t) I_{[\tau_{i-1} \le t < \tau_i]},\) (4.39)

where I(A) = 1 if A is true and 0 otherwise. Chan et al. (2014) proposed to write this model in a more compact form using as new parameters the vectors of parameter changes. Define at time t the vector \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_t\) of parameter changes and take \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_1 = \boldsymbol{\phi}_1\).

Then, for \( t < \tau_1 \), the parameters are unchanged so that \( \beta_t = \mathbf{0} \). However, at \( t = \tau_1 \), the parameters become \( \phi_2 \), so that \( \beta_{\tau_1} = \phi_2 - \phi_1 \). In general, \( \beta_t = \mathbf{0} \) if \( t \) does not belong to the set of change points \( (\tau_1, \tau_2, \dots, \tau_m) \), and \( \beta_t = \phi_{i+1} - \phi_i \) when \( t = \tau_i \). Thus, we can write Eq. (4.39) as

\( Y = XB + U, \) (4.40)

where \( \mathbf{Y} = (z_1, \dots, z_T)' \), \( X \) is the \( T \times T(p+1) \) matrix of lag values of the variable defined as

\( X = \left[ \begin{array}{ccccc} Y_0' & 0' & 0' & \dots & 0' \\ Y_1' & Y_1' & 0' & \dots & 0' \\ Y_2' & Y_2' & Y_2' & \dots & 0' \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ Y_{T-1}' & Y_{T-1}' & Y_{T-1}' & \dots & Y_{T-1}' \end{array} \right], \)

with \( \mathbf{Y}_i = (1, z_i, z_{i-1}, \dots, z_{i-p+1})' \) and \( \mathbf{0} \) is a \( p+1 \) vector of zeros, \( B = (\beta_1', \dots, \beta_T') \) is a \( T(p+1) \) vector of parameter changes and \( U = (\sigma_1 a_1, \dots, \sigma_T a_T) \).

Chan et al. (2014) proposed to estimate this model that has only \( m+1 \) coefficients \( \beta_t' \) different from zero by the group Lasso estimation, that will be explained in more detail in Chapter 7. In this case, we have group structure in the model parameters, that is, we are interested in the \( T \) vectors \( \beta_t \) and not in the individual values of the \( T(p+1) \) parameters \( \beta_{ij} \), where \( \beta_t = (\beta_{t0}, \beta_{t1}, \dots, \beta_{tp}) \). The standard Lasso minimization attempts to make many \( \beta_{ij} \) equal to zero, whereas here we are interested in making all the coefficients in the group \( \beta_t \) equal to zero. To this end, Yuan and Lin (2006) proposed the Group Lasso in which the estimation equation is

\( \hat{B} = \arg \min \frac{1}{T} \| \mathbf{Y} - \mathbf{XB} \|^2 + \lambda_T \sum_{t=1}^T \| \beta_t \|, \)

where \( \lambda_T \) is a penalty parameter and \( \| . \| \) is the Euclidean or \( \ell_2 \) norm. Note that if we use in the penalty the \( \ell_1 \) norm we will be back to the standard Lasso. However, by using the \( \ell_2 \) norm all the coefficients at time \( t \) are considered together. Thus, if \( \hat{\beta}_t \neq \mathbf{0} \) a parameter change is detected at time index \( t \), and the estimated number of changes, \( \hat{m} \), is equal to the number of non-zero elements \( \hat{\beta}_t \) minus one. Let \( (\hat{\tau}_1, \hat{\tau}_2, \dots, \hat{\tau}_{\hat{m}}) \) be the time indexes of these changes. The AR parameters are estimated by

\( \hat{\phi}_1 = \hat{\beta}_1 \quad \text{and} \quad \hat{\phi}_j = \sum_{t=1}^{\hat{j}} \hat{\beta}_t, \quad j = 2, \dots, \hat{m} + 1. \)

Chan et al. (2014) proved that the procedure is consistent in terms of prediction error, but overestimates the number of change points. They also propose to apply a model selection criterion to select the best set of change points among the \( \hat{m} \) found by the Group Lasso estimation. Finally, there are some works focusing on nonparametric approach to detecting change points of multivariate data. See, for instance, Matteson and James (2014).

\( \Phi(B)z_t = \Theta(B)e_t, \)
