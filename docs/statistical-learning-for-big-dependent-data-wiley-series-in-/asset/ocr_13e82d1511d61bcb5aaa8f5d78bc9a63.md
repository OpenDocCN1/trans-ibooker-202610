[ψ₁(s), ..., ψₖ(s)]V. It is easy to see that {ψᵢ*(s)} are also orthonormal and ξᵢₜ* ~ (0, λᵢ*) for t = 1, ..., T and i = 1, ..., K, are mutually uncorrelated. Consequently, we have
Y(s, t) = [ψ₁*(s), ..., ψₖ*(s)]ξₜ* = Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ ξᵢₜ*ψᵢ*(s),  s ∈ D. (9.90)

This expansion is known as the Karhunen-Loève expansion of Y(s, t) with K non-zero eigenvalues, where ψᵢ*(s) is the ith eigenfunction of Cᵧ(·, ·) with λᵢ* being the corresponding eigenvalue. See Karhunen (1947) and Loève (1978).

The observed data Z_t = [Z(s₁, t), ..., Z(sₘ, t)]' can then be written as
Z_t = Y_t + ε_t = Ψξₜ + ε_t,   t = 1, ..., T,   (9.91)

where ε_t denotes the vector of independent measurement errors with ε_t ~ (0, σ²I), Y_t consists of the latent S-T process [Y(s₁, t), ..., Y(sₘ, t)]', Ψ = [ψ₁, ..., ψₖ] is an m × K matrix with (i, j)th element being ψⱼ(sᵢ), and ξₜ and ε_t are uncorrelated. The goal here is to find the first p ≤ K dominant patterns ψ₁, ..., ψ_p with large λ₁*, ..., λ_p* and to estimate the covariance function Cᵧ(·, ·) for spatial prediction. Equation (9.91) is in the form of a latent factor model, and PCA provides a way to achieve our goal. However, if the sample size T is not large compared with the dimension m, the PCA estimates of the latent factor and loadings would vary substantially. To overcome the difficulty, Wang and Huang (2017) propose to use regularization and impose certain smoothness constraints in estimation. It turns out from the theory of smoothing splines (Green and Silverman, 1994) that the estimates Ψ̂(s) is a natural cubic spline when d = 1 and a thin-plate spline when d = 2 or 3 with nodes at {s₁, ..., sₘ}. More specifically, let Z = (Z₁, ..., Z_T)' be the T × m data matrix with the understanding that the mean of Z_t is zero. The sample covariance matrix as S = Y'Y / T. Wang and Huang (2017) estimate Ψ as follows

Ψ̂_τ₁,τ₂ = arg min_{Ψ:Ψ'Ψ=I_K} ||Z - ZΨΨ'||_F² + τ₁ Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ ψᵢ'Ωᵢψᵢ + τ₂ Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ Σⱼ₌₁ᵐ |ψ_{ji}|,   (9.92)

subject to the constraints ψ₁'Sψ₁ ≥ ψ₂'Sψ₂ ≥ ... ≥ ψ_K'Sψ_K, where τ₁ and τ₂ are penalty parameters with values determined by cross-validation, and ||M||_F = (Σ_{i,j} m_{ij}²)^{1/2} is the Frobenius norm of the matrix M = [m_{ij}]. Furthermore, to estimate the covariance function Cᵧ(·, ·, ...), we also need to estimate σ² and Λ. To this end, Wang and Huang apply a regularized least squares method of Tzeng and Huang (2015)

(σ̂², Λ̂) = arg min_{σ², Λ | σ² ≥ 0, Λ ≥ 0} { 1/2 ||S - Ψ̂ΛΨ̂' - σ²I||_F² + γ ||Ψ̂ΛΨ̂'||_* },   (9.93)

where γ ≥ 0 is a tuning parameter and ||A||_* = tr[(A'A)^{1/2}] is the nuclear norm of A. It turns out closed-from solutions are available for both Λ̂ and σ̂². See Wang and Huang (2017, proposition 1) for details. Finally, the analysis discussed in this section can be carried out via the R package SpatPCA.
