the estimation of \(\boldsymbol{\omega}_m\) must verify

\(\sum_{t=1}^n \sum_{v=1}^K (y_{vt} - \boldsymbol{h}'_t \boldsymbol{\beta}_v) \beta_{vm} p_m \mathbf{x}_t = \mathbf{0}\),

and the weighted residuals must also be orthogonal to the input variables.

Given the derivatives, a gradient descent update from the \(i\)th to the \((i+1)\)th iteration has the form

\(\boldsymbol{\beta}_{vm}^{(i+1)} = \boldsymbol{\beta}_{vm}^{(i)} - \gamma_i \sum_{t=1}^{n_1} \frac{\partial L_t}{\partial \boldsymbol{\beta}_{vm}^{(i)}},\tag{8.17}\)

\(\mathbf{w}_{m\ell}^{(i+1)} = \mathbf{w}_{m\ell}^{(i)} - \gamma_i \sum_{t=1}^{n_1} \frac{\partial L_t}{\partial \mathbf{w}_{m\ell}^{(i)}},\tag{8.18}\)

where \(\gamma_i\) is the *learning rate*, which must satisfy certain condition to ensure the convergence of the estimates. For instance, \(\gamma_i \rightarrow 0\) as \(i \rightarrow \infty\). Some discussions on \(\gamma_i\) can be found in Hastie et al. (2001). In practice, one may try different values of the learning rate and choose the one that attains the minimum loss function. To implement the update, one can rewrite Eqs. (8.13) and (8.14) for each weight as the *backpropagation equations*:

\(\frac{\partial L_t}{\partial \beta_{km}} \equiv \delta_{vt} h_{mt},\tag{8.19}\)

\(\frac{\partial L_t}{\partial w_{m\ell}} \equiv s_{mt} x_{\ell t},\tag{8.20}\)

where

\(\delta_{vt} = -2(y_{vt} - \boldsymbol{h}'_t \boldsymbol{\beta}_v),\)

and

\(s_{mt} = p_m \sum_{v=1}^K \beta_{vm} \delta_{vt}.\tag{8.21}\)

For instance, suppose \(K=1\) so that we have only an output variable and call \(e_t^{(i)} = y_t - \boldsymbol{h}'_t \boldsymbol{\beta}_v^{(i)}\). The updating equations are

\(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(i+1)} = \boldsymbol{\beta}^{(i)} - \gamma_i \sum_{t=1}^{n_1} \mathbf{e}_t^{(i)} \mathbf{h}_t^{(i)},\tag{8.22}\)

\(\mathbf{w}_m^{(i+1)} = \mathbf{w}_m^{(i)} - \gamma_i \beta_m^{(i)} p_m^{(i)} \sum_{t=1}^{n_1} \mathbf{e}_t^{(i)} \mathbf{x}_t,\tag{8.23}\)

and have a straightforward interpretation: the \(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(i)}\) weights are updated depending on orthogonality of the forecast errors and the intermediate explanatory variables, \(\boldsymbol{h}_t^{(i)}\); the \(\mathbf{w}_{m\ell}^{(i)}\) weights in neuron \(m\) are updated depending on the orthogonality of the prediction errors and the input variables, the importance of this neuron to the response, as measured by \(\beta_m^{(i)}\), and the effect of changes in the \(\mathbf{w}_m^{(i)}\) on the \(\boldsymbol{h}_t^{(i)}\), as measured by the derivative of the activation function, \(p_m^{(i)}\).
