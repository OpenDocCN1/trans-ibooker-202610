**Pre-whiting:** Multiplying Eq. (3.5) by \(\frac{\phi_x(B)}{\theta_x(B)}\), we have

\(\frac{\phi_x(B)}{\theta_x(B)}y_t = v(B)\frac{\phi_x(B)}{\theta_x(B)}x_t + \frac{\phi_x(B)}{\theta_x(B)}n_t.\)

Let \(\eta_t = \frac{\phi_x(B)}{\theta_x(B)}y_t\) and \(\tilde{n}_t = \frac{\phi_x(B)}{\theta_x(B)}n_t\). The prior equation then becomes

\(n_t = v(B)b_t + \tilde{n}_t = v_0 b_t + v_1 b_{t-1} + v_2 b_{t-2} + \cdots + \tilde{n}_t, \quad (3.6)\)

where \(b_t\) is a white noise series and independent of \(\tilde{n}_t\), and \(\eta_t\) is called the pre-whitened series of \(y_t\). Multiplying Eq. (3.6) by \(b_{t-j} (j \ge 0)\) and taking expectation, we have

\(\gamma_{\eta b}(j) = v_j \gamma_{bb}(0), \quad j \ge 0, \quad (3.7)\)

where, again, \(\gamma_{\eta b}(j) = \text{cov}(\eta_t, b_{t-j})\) and \(\gamma_{bb}(j) = \text{cov}(b_t, b_{t-j})\). Eq. (3.7) provides a consistent estimator of the impulse response weight \(v_j\). Specifically, we have

\(\rho_{\eta b}(j) = v_j \sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{bb}(0)}{\gamma_{\eta\eta}(0)}}, \quad j = 0, 1, 2, \dots, \quad (3.8)\)

where \(\rho_{\eta b}(j)\) is the correlation between \(\eta_t\) and \(b_{t-j}\). In other words,

\(v_j = c \times \rho_{\eta b}(j), \quad j \ge 0, \quad (3.9)\)

where \(c\) denotes the ratio of the standard error of \(\eta_t\) to that of \(b_t\). In practice, \(b_t\) is estimated by the residual series of \(x_t\), and \(\eta_t\) is the pre-whitened series of the output variable \(y_t\) via the model of \(x_t\).
