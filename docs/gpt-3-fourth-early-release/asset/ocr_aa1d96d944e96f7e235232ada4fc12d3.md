# Data Privacy and Leaks

GPT-3, given its extremely large numbers of parameters, models its training data very closely and can be prompted to output specific personally identifiable information. The model may thus be susceptible to attacks aiming to extract such information. Building training data out of publicly available documents exacerbates this risk. Even though such information was publicly available on the internet to begin with, collecting it and making it available to discover via GPT-3 outputs creates great potential for additional harm.

As discussed in chapter 5, corporations are deeply concerned about data privacy and security while using API-based AI models. When private data needs to leave the corporation’s internal ecosystem, there is always some potential that confidential information could be leaked. To tackle this issue, OpenAI has come up with elaborative data privacy and security policy; all users must prove that their use cases comply with this policy before going live with their products. The idea is to create a secure bubble for end-users, shielding them from any potential harm. You also saw in chapter 5 how the Azure OpenAI service is making it easier for enterprises to use API-based AI models in a secure environment.
