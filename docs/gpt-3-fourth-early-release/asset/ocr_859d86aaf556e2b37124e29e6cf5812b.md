# Generative models

Generative modeling is a branch of statistical modeling. It is a method for mathematically approximating the world. In this section we will discuss two key statistical models: generative and discriminative. *Generative* models can generate new data. *Discriminative* models discriminate between different kinds of data.

What does that mean in practice? Generator generates as realistic images as possible from the training dataset and tries to fool Discriminator pretending the images were originals. Discriminator analyzes the images by comparing with knowledge gained from the dataset and tries to debunk the fakes and to reject them.

The iterative co-activity between Generator and Discriminator creates realistic images, where Discriminator approves the generated image as a real one.

For example, A generative model can generate new photos of animals that look like real animals, while a discriminative model can differentiate a dog from a cat.

We are surrounded by an incredible amount of easily accessible information — both in the physical world and the digital one. The tricky part is to develop intelligent models and algorithms that can analyze and understand this treasure trove of data. Generative models are one of the most promising approaches to achieve this goal.
