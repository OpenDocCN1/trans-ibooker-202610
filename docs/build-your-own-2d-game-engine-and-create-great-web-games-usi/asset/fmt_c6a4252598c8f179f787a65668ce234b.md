## Relative Velocity with Rotation

Recall from the previous section that the definitions of relative velocity from before and after a collision between objects `A` and `B` are defined as follows:

*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{AB}1}={\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{A}1}-{\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{B}1} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq86.png)
*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{AB}2}={\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{A}2}-{\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{B}2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq87.png)

These velocities are analyzed based on components in the collision normal and tangent directions in Equations (1) and (2) and are relisted for convenience in the following:

*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{AB}2}\bullet \hat{\mathrm{N}}=-\mathrm{e}\left({\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{AB}1}\bullet \hat{\mathrm{N}}\right) $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq88.png) (1)
*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{AB}2}\bullet \hat{\mathrm{T}}=\mathrm{f}\left({\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{AB}1}\bullet \hat{\mathrm{T}}\right) $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq89.png) (2)

These equations are derived without considering rotation and the formulation assumes that the velocity is constant over the entire shape. In order to support rotation, these equations must be generalized and solved at the point of collision, `P`.

*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{ABP2}\bullet \hat{\mathrm{N}}=-\mathrm{e}\left({\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{ABP1}\bullet \hat{\mathrm{N}}\right) $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq90.png) (13)
*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{ABP2}\bullet \hat{\mathrm{T}}=\mathrm{f}\left({\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{ABP1}\bullet \hat{\mathrm{T}}\right) $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq91.png) (14)

In this case, ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{ABP1} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq92.png) and ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{ABP2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq93.png) are relative velocities at collision position `P` from before and after the collision. It is still true that these vectors are defined by the difference in velocities for objects `A` and `B` from before, ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{AP1} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq94.png) and ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{BP1} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq95.png), and after, ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{AP2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq96.png) and ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{BP2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq97.png), the collision at the collision position `P` on each object.

*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{ABP1}={\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{AP1}-{\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{BP1} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq98.png) (15)
*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{ABP2}={\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{AP2}-{\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{BP2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq99.png) (16)

You are now ready to generalize the Impulse Method to support rotation and to derive a solution to approximate the linear and angular velocities: ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{A}2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq100.png), ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{B}2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq101.png), ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\upomega}}_{\mathrm{A}2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq102.png), and ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\upomega}}_{\mathrm{B}2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq103.png).

## Impulse Method with Rotation

Continue with the Impulse Method discussion from the previous section, that after the collision between objects `A` and `B`, the Impulse Method describes the changes in their linear velocities by an impulse, ![$$ \overrightarrow{\mathrm{J}} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq104.png), scaled by the inverse of their corresponding masses, `m[A]` and `m[B]`. This change in linear velocities is described in Equations (3) and (4), relisted as follows:

*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{A}2}={\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{A}1}+\frac{\overrightarrow{\mathrm{J}}}{{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathrm{A}}} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq105.png) (3)
*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{B}2}={\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{B}1}-\frac{\overrightarrow{\mathrm{J}}}{{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathrm{B}}} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq106.png) (4)

In general, rotations are intrinsic results of collisions and the same impulse must properly describe the change in angular velocity from before and after a collision. Remember that inertial, or rotational inertia, is the rotational mass. In a manner similar to linear velocity and mass, it is also the case that the change in angular velocity in a collision is inversely related to the rotational inertia. As illustrated in Figure [9-29](#334805_2_En_9_Chapter.xhtml#Fig29), for objects `A` and `B` with rotational inertia of `I[A]` and `I[B]`, after a collision, the angular velocities, ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\upomega}}_{\mathrm{A}2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq107.png) and ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\upomega}}_{\mathrm{B}2} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq108.png), can be described as follows, where ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{R}}}_{AP} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq109.png) and ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\mathrm{R}}}_{BP} $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq110.png) are the positional vectors of each object:

*   ![$$ {\overrightarrow{\upomega}}_{\mathrm{A}2}={\overrightarrow{\upomega}}_{\mathrm{A}1}+\left({\overrightarrow{\mathrm{R}}}_{AP}\times \frac{\overrightarrow{\mathrm{J}}}{{\mathrm{I}}_{\mathrm{A}}}\right) $$](images/334805_2_En_9_Chapter/334805_2_En_9_Chapter_TeX_IEq111.png) (17)
