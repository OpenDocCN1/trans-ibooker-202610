Now, the first major group of elementary particles is known as the *fermions*. They have been given rather exotic names, such as “charm,” “strange,” and “bottom.” These are sometimes known as *flavors*. The fermions are divided into *quarks* and *leptons*; all fermions have a spin value of ½. The second major group of elementary particles, the bosons, carry a spin value of 1, save for the rather recently discovered Higgs boson which has a spin of 0 (zero).

The fermions are also often categorized by their generation. Each generation in general has more mass than the previous one (e.g., up quarks have less mass than top quarks).

Black holes aren’t just a sci-fi trope. For one, they potentially offer incredible sources of energy ripe for harvesting as soon as the technology is there. If our scientists and engineers one day manage to leverage the power of a black hole, we would basically enter a new golden age of technology.
