Now, sub-atomic particles simply exhibit two different behaviors: that of a particle or that of a wave. An experiment known as the *double-slit experiment* demonstrated this. Devised in 1801 by physicist **Thomas Young** (1773–1829), this experiment at first dealt with light-related properties only. It was further refined by numerous scientists a century or so later to include electrons, atoms, and molecules. Basically, in the double-slit experiment, a beam of light (say, a laser) is directed at a plate with two holes (see Figure 9-2). The light is then observed on a screen behind this plate. The wave properties in light cause these waves passing through the two slits an interference, producing bands of various brightness on the rear screen; this wouldn’t happen if purely particle-based physics were at play (see Figure 9-3). However, each individual photon still (i.e., light particle) passes through a single slit like it would in classical particle physics instead of both holes. This seems to be the behavior with other types of particles as well, including electrons. Again, larger entities, such as molecules, also exhibit this behavior in the experiment.
