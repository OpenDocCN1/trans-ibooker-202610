# The Planck Constant

The *Planck’s constant* defines the amount of energy a photon (i.e., an elementary light particle) can carry; it defines the smallest unit for energy. These constants are whole number multiples of base number labeled *h*. This means particles vibrate at, say, base *h* or *4h*, but not *2.4h* or *1.6h*.

The exact value of the Planck constant has been refined and updated over the decades. As of 2019, the value of this constant was fixed at **6.62607015x10⁻³⁴ Joule seconds** according to the *National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)*.

# I. Planck Length

The unit used to measure the distance light travels during one unit of Planck time is known as the *Planck length*. It’s defined using three fundamental constants of physics, namely, the speed of light in a vacuum, Newton’s gravitational constant, and the previously mentioned Planck constant.
