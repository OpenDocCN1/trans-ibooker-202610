# # Moth-Pollinated Flowers

Moths nectar on many of the same plants that attract butterflies and bees. The most enticing moth-pollinated flowers often have a strong, sweet aroma only at night. Color does not matter as much, and flowers tend to be pale white, pale pink, dull red, or purple. There are no nectar guides, as they would not be visible at night.

The flower structure often offers clusters of bloom or landing platforms. Like butterflies, moths have long tongues and appreciate plentiful nectar in flowers. Most moths do not intentionally gather pollen, so it may be more limited in moth-pollinated plants.
