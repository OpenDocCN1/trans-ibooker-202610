# Ten Tips for a Thriving Pollinator Victory Garden

There is a lot to consider as you create and manage your Pollinator Victory Garden. On occasion you may need some quick refresher tips, or you might want to share some succinct yet usable information with others. Here are ten tips for a thriving Pollinator Victory Garden:

+   1. Plant for a succession of bloom throughout the growing season. Different pollinator species emerge at various times of year and have differing lifespans and periods of activity. Create an ongoing pollinator buffet throughout the growing season by planting a succession of overlapping bloom. In most parts of North America, the growing season is early spring through late fall.

+   2. Skip double-flowered plants—they have little, and sometimes no, nectar or pollen. What is beautiful to the human eye may be a source of starvation for a bee or other pollinator. Find the beauty in what a plant does, not just how it looks. Emphasize plants that have naturally occurring forms that pollinators can recognize and use.

+   3. Emphasize native plants to support native pollinators and your ecosystem. Evolution matters! Native pollinators have evolved with native plants and may excel at pollinating those species. Some research shows that local native pollinators have a strong preference for local native plants. In some cases, pollinators and plants are dependent on each other. Specialist pollinators may depend upon a small group of plants or a single native plant species. Native plants perform more ecological functions in your garden than you might realize. Let Mother Nature be your guide in plant selection.
