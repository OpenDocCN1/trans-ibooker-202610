leads to the desired extensibility. It is something we will experience multiple times throughout this book, in particular when we talk about design patterns. Thus it stands to reason that *SRP* and *OCP* are related or even the same.

On the other hand, in this example, we have seen that there are some specific, architectural considerations about *OCP* that we did not take into account while talking about *SRP*. Also, as we will experience in “Guideline 15: Design for the Addition of Types or Operations”, we will often have to make explicit decisions about what we want to extend and how we want to extend it. That decision can significantly influence the way we design our software. Therefore *OCP* seems to be more about the awareness of extensions and conscious decisions about extensions than the *SRP*. As such it is perhaps a little more than just an afterthought of *SRP*. Or perhaps it just depends.³⁷

Either way, this example indisputably demonstrates that extensibility should be explicitly considered during software design and that the desire for extending our software in a specific way is an excellent indication for the need to separate concerns. It is important to understand how software will be extended, to identify such *customization points* and to design such that this kind of extension can be performed easily.

# Compile-time Extensibility

The `Document` example may give the impression that all of these design considerations apply to runtime polymorphism. No, absolutely not: the same considerations, the same arguments also apply to compile time problems. In order to illustrate this, I now reach for a couple of examples from the standard library. Of course it is of utmost interest that you are able to extend the standard library. Yes, you are supposed to *use* the standard library, but you are also encouraged to build on it and to add your own pieces of functionality. For that reason, the standard library is designed for extensibility. But interestingly it is not using base classes for that purpose, but primarily builds on function overloading, templates, and (class) template specialization.
