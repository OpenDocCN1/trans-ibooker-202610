However, a loyal follower might not always agree with the leader, but when s/he does disagree, it is done in a constructive manner and with discretion (e.g., in private). As such, the follower is demonstrating the type of political savvy (e.g., social astuteness) that was discussed in Chapter 2. In contrast, a non-loyal follower might undermine the leader by, for example, publicly disagreeing (either overtly or in a passive aggressive manner) with the leader. As another example, the follower might attempt to do an “end around” by speaking with other coworkers or even the leader’s boss regarding a matter of disagreement between the follower and leader. This latter behavior involves going around the chain of command. In some extreme cases of unethical or highly improper behavior on the part of one’s boss, it may be necessary to “whistleblow” by going around the chain of command. But in general, such behavior shows a high degree of disloyalty, and thus, poor followership. More will be said about followership and whistleblowing later in this chapter.

> ## conformist
> 
> A follower who is highly agreeable and ingratiating toward his or her leader, and one who does not “rock the boat.”

Given these three dimensions of follower effectiveness, we now overview three common types of followers: (1) conformist, (2) alienated, and (3) effective. The conformist is in line with the stereotypical view of a follower as being highly agreeable and ingratiating toward his or her leader, and one who does not “rock the boat.” The individual’s level of engagement in the job or organization may vary. Some conformists will be highly engaged, while others will be more withdrawn. Regardless, the conformist follower is likely to be very loyal to the leader. If this is the case, then the leader may be lulled into thinking of the follower as highly effective. After all, the conformist does his or her job in an unquestioning or reliable manner and can be counted on by the leader. In other words, the conformist does not rock the boat in any manner. However, in the long-run, if the leader is surrounded by conformists, nagging problems might not be solved, innovative ideas may not be pursued, and necessary change in the work setting may not occur. Indeed, the problem of groupthink, which we will consider in more detail in Chapter 8, becomes highly likely when a group of followers is composed largely of conformists.

> ## alienated
> 
> A follower who is a critical and independent thinker, but not highly engaged.

In a different way, the alienated follower is much more of a critical and independent thinker, which means that s/he at least has the potential to be a problem-solver and innovator. The problem is that the individual is not engaged, and instead may shy away from increased responsibilities, his or her leader, or even coworkers. A number of reasons could account for the individual's passiveness. For example, the person's ideas may have been routinely “shot down” in the past by the leader or conformist coworkers. Then over time, the alienated follower will become conditioned to not being fully engaged. This can obviously be a problem, since this sort of individual could have much to offer to the leader, the team, and the organization.

On the other hand, the alienated follower might have “made his/her own bed” by previously providing ideas in a very pushy or even disloyal manner. This can happen by, for example, the follower questioning the leader's knowledge or competence in an open forum (i.e., in a public sort of venue). It can also happen when the follower goes around the leader to that person's boss (i.e., the 2nd level manager). Either sort of action can help cause the follower to ultimately be in the leader's out-group, which only serves to alienate the follower. We should further note that it is always possible for a follower to be loyal to the leader, but nevertheless disengaged. It could be that reasons aside from the leader might be causing the follower to disengage, such as personal problems or organizational policies with which the follower disagrees.

> ## effective
> 
> A follower who is a critical and independent thinker, highly engaged, and loyal to the leader.

Obviously, both conformist and alienated followers are problematic for leaders, the organization, and even the followers themselves. So what exactly is effective followership all about?
