# Outcomes to Date

In addition to the accomplishments and changes suggested above, to date, there have been a number of more financially or numerically-based outcomes that can be associated with Crow’s presidency. For example, in 2006, ASU passed the $200 million in sponsored research funding and by 2020, it had reached about $673 million, making it one of the fastest-growing research enterprises in the country. ASU’s current research funding puts it at number 6 in the country with regard to research-oriented universities that lack a medical school.[77] Along with sponsored funding, the university’s endowment level has grown at rates equaling or surpassing almost any university in the country. In essence, this means that the university has been able to secure large amounts of funds from rich donors. Furthermore, as compared to when Crow took office, university scientists have engaged in increased technology transfer efforts. For example, in 2020, ASU ranked #10 worldwide in terms of patent awards at universities.[18] Moreover, both faculty and the university have gained from revenue streams that are the result of licensing/commercializing the intellectual property associated with faculty research.

The stature of ASU among universities has also gone up. For example, since 2016, ASU has been ranked #1 every year by the U.S. News & World Report in terms of “most innovative schools”, ahead of famous institutions like Stanford and MIT.[79] As a specific example of innovativeness, by the spring of 2020, the Biodesign Institute at ASU had built and deployed a high-speed robotic system capable of processing 2,000 COVID-19 test kits a day. Indeed, during Crow’s tenure, ASU has begun to shed its top party school image for one of academic excellence and research. Renowned researchers are prevalent in ASU’s many nationally recognized departments and programs, including a Nobel Prize winner in economics. Under Crow, ASU has also been able to increase its ability to attract top students, including National Merit Scholars. Further, four-year graduation rates have greatly increased under Crow’s tenure.

Furthermore, ASU has become a more inclusive university. Specifically, while growing ASU to be by far the largest university in North America, it has simultaneously increased percentages of Hispanics and other minorities. Here are some examples:

+   - ASU’s colleges and programs have frequently been ranked in the top 25 for Hispanic students in the United States by Hispanic magazines.
+   - In the spring of 2014, ASU received an award from the Greater Phoenix Urban League for engendering a more ethnically and racially diverse university.[20]
+   - More than half of ASU freshmen students in the fall of 2017 who were from the state of Arizona were minority students.
+   - By 2017, ASU was graduating more Native Americans than all of the University of California system campuses combined (even though California has more Native American residents than any other state).
+   - Fifty percent of students in 2017 were from families at or below the median income level. In 1990, this number was 3 percent. In both 2016 and 2017, ASU also ranked #1 public research university chosen by international students (and number 4 overall among U.S. universities).

As an additional example of inclusiveness and innovativeness, Crow and ASU struck a deal with Starbucks in 2014 to provide online undergraduate degrees to its benefits-eligible employees (i.e., “partners”). As stated by Howard Schultz, founder of Starbucks, the deal was struck largely because of the online capabilities that ASU had developed over the years. The idea was to include Starbucks employees who have started college, but were unable to complete their degrees.
