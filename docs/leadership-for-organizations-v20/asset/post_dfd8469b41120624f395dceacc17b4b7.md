In short, the ongoing inertia in existing reward systems largely do not make lower-level administrators accountable for change, and only serve to provide obstacles or impede individuals' empowerment to act on Crow's vision. Furthermore, little training has ensued for either administrative staff or faculty members in terms of what it means to be more entrepreneurial within a university context, or how aspects of the bureaucracy may be preventing such change.

Fifth, and in line with the early New Times articles, Crow has shown at least some tendency to be highly confrontational or dismissive of those who do not share his views; for example, views pertaining to the rate of growth of the student body. This tendency has manifested itself in various venues, such as town hall-style meetings with faculty and students. He has been known to either intentionally or unintentionally embarrass or even lash out at people (sometimes in public) who pose challenges to his own ideas or authority—even when those challenges are constructive in nature. One journalist claimed that this behavior has created a “culture of retaliation” at ASU.[90]

Sixth, in more recent years, Crow has been noticeably absent in terms of visits to units/colleges or the various campuses. During the earlier years of his tenure, he had more of a physical presence throughout the university (e.g., town hall meetings with faculty, students, and staff). In later years, this presence seems to have tapered off significantly. Instead, he appears to have adopted a more laissez-faire approach to leading (at least in terms of physical, on-campus presence), and some of the more favorable aspects of his leadership depicted in Exhibit 3 are not so apparent anymore. Moreover, with the exception of some notable initiatives (e.g., the Starbucks initiative and Thunderbird acquisition), there seems to be little leadership effort to keep the pace of change moving. Could it be that there is little need for additional change?

## Exhibit 1 — ASU Vision and University Goals

### ASU VISION

To establish ASU as the model for a New American University, measured not by who we exclude, but rather by who we include; pursuing research and discovery that benefits the public good; assuming major responsibility for the economic, social, and cultural vitality and health and well-being of the communities that it serves.

### ASU GOALS

### Access and Quality for All

- Expand university access to match Arizona diversification and growth
- Improve freshmen persistence to 90 percent
- Enhance university graduation rate to 70–75 percent as soon as possible
- Develop ASU culture that represents a commitment to quality and community outreach
- Enroll 100,000 continuing education and degree completion students (including our contribution to AZUN)
- Enhance linkages with community colleges so as to expand baccalaureate degree production
- Enhance student development and individual student learning

### National Comprehensive University

- Become a leading center for interdisciplinary science and technology discovery and development
- Become a leading center for discovery and scholarship in the social sciences, arts, and humanities
- Enhance research and discovery competitiveness to more than $800 million in annual research expenditures by 2025 (currently around $673 million)
- Enhance regional economic competitiveness through research and discovery and value-added education programs

### Establish National Standing in Academic Quality and Impact of Colleges and Schools in Every Field

- Attain national standing in academic quality for each school
- Attain national standing in the value added to our graduates in each school
- Become the leading university academically (faculty, discovery, research, creativity) in at least one core subject within each school or college

### Enhance our Local Impact and Social Embeddedness

- Enhance linkage to local and regional social and community development groups
- Establish/develop/enhance linkages and partnerships with local, regional and national NGO's, governments and public agencies, and private sector firms with a focus on community development
- Undertake applied sustainability research that impacts the social, environmental and economic evolution of the southwest
- Provide an objective and ongoing monitoring role for the region's progress through the ASU Indicators Project

## Exhibit 2—Crow’s Vision for ASU: Elements, Accomplishments, and Challenges or Setbacks

| Elements of the New Vision | Implementation Efforts and Accomplishments | Implementation Challenges |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Interdisciplinary research | Establishment of new Institutes and interdisciplinary colleges | Entrenched attitudes, culture, and structure favoring mono-disciplinary units; little change in reward structures (e.g., promotion and tenure procedures for faculty members) |
| Entrepreneurial enterprise and innovative sources of funding | Steady increases in sponsored research funding; increases in technology transfer efforts (and accompanying revenue streams) | Highly bureaucratic procedures and organizational culture; little incentive to be “entrepreneurial” |
| College-centric and non-tiered structure | Unified accreditation; elimination of campus-level Provost positions; non-duplication of programs | Geographical (i.e., spread out metropolitan area) hindrances to integration for both students and faculty (e.g., lack of available coursework for students at campuses other than Tempe) |
| Inclusiveness | Steady growth in student numbers, including minorities and economically disadvantaged students; increased financial aid to low-income students | Faculty and student values sometimes favoring exclusiveness, rather than inclusiveness; some disparity in student qualifications across ethnic groups |
| Expansion into medical education | Attempt to partner with the University of Arizona with a downtown Phoenix campus; recent attempts to partner with the Mayo Clinic | Dissolution of joint venture (to establish a medical school) between ASU and University of Arizona in the spring of 2010 |

## Exhibit 3: Crow's Leadership Approach

| Generally Favorable Aspects | Controversial or Problematic Aspects |
| :--- | :--- |
| Highly visionary, articulate, brilliant, and persuasive. | Seems more concerned with "his" vision or imposing his will, as opposed to building shared vision. |
| Shows determination when accomplishing goals. | Tends to not widely share key information about planned changes until they are broadly announced to the public and media |
| Highly energetic and a role model for hard work. | Can be highly confrontational with those who do not share his views. Seems to have faded from view in more recent times. |
| Had high standards for university employees and units. | Shows lack of empathy for the effects of rapid change on administrators, faculty, and students. |
| A willingness to personally take on some of the ingrained problems in the university's system. | Has not significantly dented the widespread bureaucracy of ASU that has always existed. |
| Action and quick-change orientation. | Has shown a tendency to "shoot first" before aiming or carefully considering the ramifications of actions (e.g., implementation challenges of planned initiatives) |
| Avoidance of decision-making or actions based on internal political concerns. | Lack of co-opting and coalition building, shows little concern for the necessity of involvement of others (e.g., students and faculty members) at lower levels in the implementation of changes. |
