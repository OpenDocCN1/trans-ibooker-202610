1. This notion of currencies of exchange was considered by Dienesch, R.M., & Liden, R.C. (1986). Leader member exchange model of leadership: A critique and further development. Academy of Management Review, 11, 618-634.

2. Note that contractual work relationships can involve actual written, formal contracts. However, they also can refer to unwritten or informal expectations.

3. We say “likely to occur” here for a reason. Specifically, in-group vs. out-group formation is a natural sort of occurrence for a leader vis-à-vis followers. With that said, and as argued further below, it is not an ideal circumstance. Leaders can take steps to try to have all followers in his or her in-group, which is more ideal. For more information about in-groups vs. out-groups, see Dulebohn, J.H., Bommer, W.H., Liden, R.C., Brouer, R. L., & Ferris, G. R. (2012). A meta-analysis of antecedents and consequences of leader-member exchange: Integrating the past with an eye toward the future. Journal of Management, 38, 1715-1759.

4. For considerations of person-organization fit, see Kristof-Brown, A., & Guay, R. P. (2011). Person-environment fit. APA handbook of industrial and organizational psychology, 3, 3–50.
