A great deal of research has addressed cognitive and skill-based attributes of effective global leaders, although much less attention has been devoted to more emotional or affective qualities.[15] Here, we suggest that both know-how and emotional factors are required for global leaders. But what are such qualities all about?

# Global Orientation Factors

Based on emerging research, we identify three key factors that are associated with a global orientation on the part of leaders: (1) global business know-how, (2) cross-cultural know-how, and (3) cross-cultural engagement. Together, these factors represent both the cognitive and emotional make-up that is essential for a leader attempting to influence in a global context. We address each of these factors separately.

**Global business know-how.** Business tends to be conducted differently in different countries around the world. Accordingly, the global leader needs to possess knowledge in such industry-level areas as customers, competitors, supply chains, and risks of doing business. A leader will also need know-how regarding how government and government regulations affect doing business in different countries, as well as the extent to which doing business coincides with non-business, cultural norms.

A classic example is the extent to which business and socializing, especially through the intake of alcohol, might mix. In cultures such as that of the U.S., there is the common cultural norm of “not mixing business and pleasure.” In other words, business pursuits are largely kept separate from personal or social activities. However, in many other cultures (e.g., Asian cultures) business and socializing are heavily intertwined. That is, the norm is that business largely gets accomplished through social activities that might involve the consuming of alcohol.

**Cross-cultural know-how.** As mentioned earlier, it is important for a leader to first have knowledge of his or her own culture. Based on that knowledge, the leader can form a better understanding of other cultures, which is essential for effectiveness in a global leader role. Cross-cultural know-how pertains to a variety of issues including politics, religion, art, and history of cultures. Oftentimes in other cultures, these issues end up getting intertwined with how business is done.[16] For example, given that business and social pursuits can blend together, discussions of things like local politics and history can naturally arise in the course of discussing business matters with customers, suppliers, government officials, and even local employees. While no global leader is expected to be an expert on the cultures in which he or she is doing business, a rudimentary knowledge of basic issues will give the leader more credence. And perhaps more importantly, asking questions and showing curiosity may be even more important than a high level of expertise.

**Cross-cultural engagement.** Global leaders are likely to face challenges that might be emotionally taxing. They need to not only have knowledge of other cultures. In addition, they must continually engage people and groups from other cultures, and then adapt to the multicultural environments in which they are immersed.[17] To engage means to show emotional enthusiasm, excitement, curiosity, and passion about people and ways of doing things in different parts of the world.[18] Note that it also means that these positive emotions will outweigh more negative ones, such as fear and anxiety. Engagement further implies having a high degree of openness towards diversity and life as it is experienced in different countries.
