Second, feedback reports might be delivered to recipients without appropriate follow up coaching, which can potentially be delivered by one's supervisor, or outsiders (e.g., HR specialists, consultants, and executive coaches). A key advantage of 360-degree feedback is that it provides data that is systematically collected, rather than feedback that is based on hearsay. Accordingly, these types of processes can provide very tangible data upon which coaching can proceed. Yet, there is no guarantee that coaching will indeed accompany a 360-degree feedback process. For example, one's supervisor might deliver the feedback report without actually helping the recipient to work through the feedback to determine what might be learned and changed, as well as why such change is important (e.g., to learn from the feedback in order to obtain higher level leadership positions). Since leadership and coaching represent a two-way street, if the feedback recipient has a negative attitude toward the process, it will be that much harder for the supervisor/coach to help guide the recipient. So for the process to be successful, a coach needs to help the recipient to learn from the feedback, and the recipient needs to be open to change based on the feedback.

Finally, even if coaching accompanies the feedback, if the feedback recipient does not take follow-up steps to improve, then the 360-degree feedback process is liable to be all for naught. One possible approach is for the feedback recipient to take just three or four items or issues from the data, and have a meeting with those who provided the feedback. The goal is to learn more about why those items/issues were rated relatively low, or how the recipient can improve. Obviously, the goal should not be to identify who said what in the survey, or to debate the validity of the survey process. Instead, the feedback recipient should simply listen, take notes, and ask constructive questions regarding how improvements might be made. In our own experience with 360-degree feedback, this follow-up step is crucial if feedback recipients are to learn and change their behavior.

In sum, while 360-degree feedback can help develop leaders in terms of the receiving aspect of leader communication, its implementation has challenges. The feedback can reveal blind spots that the leader did not realize, and if improved, could greatly enhance the leader's development. On the other hand, aspects of the leader, as well as the context within which the feedback is administered, all work to determine whether or not 360-degree feedback will be successful.
