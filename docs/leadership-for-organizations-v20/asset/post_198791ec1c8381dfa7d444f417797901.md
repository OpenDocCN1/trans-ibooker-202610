Questions 1–4 reflect an avoidant orientation; questions 5–8 reflect a performance prove orientation, and questions 9–12 reflect a learning orientation. ## Team and Shared Leadership

> "If you want to go fast, go alone. But if you want to go far, go together." —old African proverb

> "A genuine leader is not a searcher for consensus, but a molder of consensus." —Martin Luther King, Jr.

### Learning Objectives

- 1. Explain the key elements of team-oriented leadership.
- 2. Compare how leadership is necessary at the various stages of a team's development, and how problems can develop if leadership is lacking.
- 3. Describe the role and importance of shared leadership in teams.
- 4. Identify the nature and relevance of virtual teams, as well as how they can be effectively led.

Leadership is all about influencing people toward common goals or visions. Sometimes that influence occurs one-on-one, as was emphasized in Chapter 3 of this book. Through relationship-building, leaders are able to influence individuals. However, a leader's influence may also be directed toward an entire group or team of individuals. A group is simply a collection of individuals performing similar tasks that when added together, will help to further the overall goals of the group. But a team is more than just a collection of individuals who do similar tasks. It involves individuals who work collaboratively and cooperatively to achieve commonly-held goals or vision.

Teams can come in different sizes, purposes, duration, and locations. For example, some teams are formed to be relatively ongoing, while other teams are formed simply to solve specific problems, and then they are disbanded. Some teams are mostly or totally in the same geographic proximity, while other teams are more geographically distributed and must operate largely through virtual media (e.g., email and video conferencing).

A high performing team is cohesive, but not so cohesive that it fails to adapt to changing information or circumstances (e.g., new technology or ways to serve customers) in its environment. In addition, a high performing team is able to take advantage of the diverse backgrounds, motivations, and abilities of its members, while simultaneously dealing with conflicts and disagreements that arise. In short, while teams have potential to perform beyond simple collections of individuals with similar tasks, they are not without their challenges. It is the job of leaders to ensure that the needs of teams are met. These include making sure that the team has clear vision and task direction, supportive and relations-oriented (i.e., socio-emotional) needs are recognized and dealt with, and change-oriented needs are addressed when necessary.
