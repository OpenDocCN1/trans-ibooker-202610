
## 6.6 Appendix C: Mountainside Fitness (A Case Study in Entrepreneurial Leadership in a Pandemic)

> "Who knew you could build a company on a small towel and a big dream?" —Tom Hatten, Founder and CEO of Mountainside Fitness

Tom Hatten founded Mountainside Fitness in the Phoenix, AZ area in 1991 while he was still a junior at Arizona State University. In a market that was increasingly dominated by "corporate" gym chains (e.g., LA Fitness, Lifetime Fitness, etc.), Hatten’s basic idea was to make his gym friendlier and more personal for clients. His first gym was located in Ahwatukee, AZ. As an example of his approach to leadership and customer service, he showed appreciation to his members by providing free towels, and he would put on monthly socials at the club that included pizza, beer, and soft drinks. By the spring of 2020, Mountainside Fitness had grown to eighteen locations and over 90,000 members. Hatten recently wrote an autobiography about the evolution of his business, entitled *Dream On*.

## The Pandemic of 2020

In the middle of March 2020, the COVID-19 virus pandemic ensued around the world. In the United States, businesses deemed to be "essential" (e.g., grocery stores) were allowed by government executive orders to remain open. However, other businesses that were deemed to be "non-essential" were closed. Gyms and health clubs were among these latter businesses. This classification was controversial from the start, since gyms and health clubs obviously contribute to physical and mental health of their members, thus potentially making people less susceptible to COVID-19 or other illnesses. Indeed, some gym owners around the country have vehemently protested against the closure of their businesses.

In compliance with the executive order from Governor Ducey of Arizona, Mountainside Fitness shut down its operations and furloughed most of its employees for almost two months. Toward the middle of May 2020, the governor’s office of Arizona allowed gyms and health clubs to reopen, but only in accordance with social distancing guidelines established by the U.S. Centers for Disease Control (CDC). Mountainside Fitness diligently attempted to adhere to those guidelines, and it reopened its facilities. However, toward the end of June 2020, COVID-19 cases in Arizona appeared to be "spiking" in terms of numbers. It was not clear as to why the increased numbers were occurring although it was probably due to host of factors, such as increased testing to identify existing cases. Despite the increase in cases, hospital ICUs were not yet close to maximum capacity, the fear of which was a key reason originally for the shutdown of "non-essential" businesses.

In response to the "spike" in cases, at approximately 3 pm on June 29, 2020, Governor Doug Ducey ordered a return to the shutdown by 8 p.m. that evening for targeted businesses, including gyms/health clubs, bars, movie theaters, and water parks. The order was to be in effect until at least July 27, 2020. Tom Hatten initially decided to ignore the new executive order by keeping his establishments open. This resulted in multiple citations of $2,500 each at his facilities, as well as the possibility of Hatten’s arrest.

Hatten immediately sued the governor's office in an attempt to get an injunction against the executive order. He did a press conference, explaining his actions. However, on Tuesday July 7, 2020, the circuit court of Arizona ruled against him by deciding to not overturn the governor's executive order. The same day, Hatten announced that he would follow the court's decision and shut down operations by 2 p.m. He also announced that at least through July 27, 2020, he would continue to pay his employees.

But simultaneously, Hatten appealed to the Superior Court of Arizona. On Aug. 4, 2020, that court made the following ruling: “The Executive Orders, as implemented, violate procedural due process . . . fitness centers must be provided a prompt opportunity to apply for reopening. The process for doing so must be in place within one week from the date of entry of this Order.” In essence, Hatten won his case, and he promptly announced that he would reopen his gyms a week later on August 11, 2020. In reality, as of August 12, 2020, he had not reopened because of a lack of clear guidelines from the governor's office. Hatten describes his situation in an interview with a local news affiliate.

In a new court filing after August 12, 2020, Hatten appeared to be taking up a broader cause beyond his own organization or even his industry, health clubs. Specifically, he said, “We're right in the end, and I think I speak for all small business owners, whether it's health clubs, water parks or other businesses. The abuse of power and what he's doing to small businesses under the guise of coronavirus, has to be stopped. We all certainly understand the serious nature of the virus, but we all have to be allowed to move forward together and keep our businesses open. Otherwise, we will continue to see businesses close their doors and entire industries wiped out.”
