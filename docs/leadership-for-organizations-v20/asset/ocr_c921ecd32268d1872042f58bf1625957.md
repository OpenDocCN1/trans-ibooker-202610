# 8.4 Leading Virtual Teams

**virtual team**
A team that is geographically distributed, oftentimes relatively temporary in nature, and largely dependent on virtual media for communication purposes.

Increasingly, people work in jobs that both allow and even require geographical dispersion, i.e., distally remote. Various forces are responsible for this trend. Most recently, the COVID-19 pandemic forced individuals and teams to work remotely using virtual technology. During the pandemic, university students clearly saw this happen through the use of such technologies as Zoom.[22] But even beyond educational settings, it is likely that after the pandemic, distally remote work will continue to increase.[23] Even before the pandemic, there were trends toward remote work, including globally-oriented business, individuals’ desires to work at least partially from their homes, and virtual technology that allows for dispersions. The upshot is that teams are, and will increasingly be, geographically distributed. In other words, members are spread out over widely diverse regions of the country or world. Oftentimes, such teams are relatively temporary in nature, perhaps focusing on a specific problem or project. Because of the dispersion, teams of this nature are forced to communicate through virtual media, such as email, chat systems, and video conferencing. Accordingly, the label **virtual team** is often used.

There are advantages and potential disadvantages when leading virtual teams. Oftentimes, virtual teams are formed on a temporary basis. In such cases, team members are brought together from different localities of an organization who have the right mix of skills, know-how, and motivation to accomplish the team’s goals. Accordingly, from the get-go, the leader is presented with a situation in which he or she may not have to worry about having the complete set of skills and expertise that is necessary to accomplish the team’s mission. There is a high likelihood that such a mix has been designed into the team at time of selection.

On the other hand, there can be challenges when it comes to leading virtual teams – both temporary and more permanent teams. Research has shown that less transformational leadership tends to occur, or at least be perceived, in virtual as compared to face-to-face teams.[24] As we will discuss in more detail in Chapter 11, transformational leadership is an especially effective form of leadership that stresses inspiration, change, and development. However, it may be difficult for leaders to show such leadership in a virtual setting.

In highly virtual teams, there is also the issue of “out of sight, out of mind”. That is, virtual teams may be especially prone to the type of free-riding/social loafing problems discussed earlier in this chapter. Since members have little or no face-to-face contact with either the leader or other team members, they could become demotivated or simply “slip through the cracks.” This problem is accentuated by the fact that virtual teams are often subject to a matrix structure, whereby team members have permanent membership in another unit or team, while being assigned more temporary membership in the virtual team. When this occurs, the virtual team member may attempt to free-ride at times when more pressing matters occur in his or her permanent team.[25]

As yet another example, in Chapter 7, we considered some of the problems that can result from communication through virtual media. In teams that are essentially not highly virtual (i.e., geographically proximal), communication such as email may still be used, even though face-to-face contact may be readily possible. However, virtual teams by definition rely predominantly on virtual media for communication. It follows that they are highly prone to the type of communication issues that were considered in Chapter 7. Thus, communication that is complex, interpersonal, or emotionally-charged may be especially difficult and problematic in virtual teams. Overall, problems of free-riding/social loafing and poor communication foster tendencies for virtual teams to have group process (i.e., group dynamics) difficulties.[26]
