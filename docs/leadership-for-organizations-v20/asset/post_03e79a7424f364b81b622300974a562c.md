After such an initial face-to-face encounter, it should be more feasible to rely on virtual communication.

Second, leaders should encourage communication that includes videoconferencing as much as possible, rather than only relying on less “rich” media like email, texting, or chat. Over the past decade, videoconferencing has become highly efficient and cost-effective. With its use, the typical problems mentioned above that are associated with less rich media can be minimized, at least to a degree. These problems might include emotionally-based conflict and lack of clarity regarding goals, mission, and individuals’ responsibilities. While video conferencing is not a panacea for such issues, it can nevertheless help.

Third, leaders need to remind members regarding communication ground rules for highly sensitive (e.g., emotionally sensitive) or complex information. Specifically, information of such nature should be communicated through either phone, videoconferencing, or the occasional opportunities of face-to-face meetings.

Finally, perhaps more so than many other types of teams, highly dispersed, virtual teams often times do not have formal leaders. Accordingly, the shared leadership processes that we considered earlier in this chapter may be especially relevant and necessary. In other words, perhaps especially in virtual teams, it behooves all members to share in the leadership role.

## Conclusion

The goal of this chapter has been to highlight the importance, and nature, of leading teams. Although there are some common elements regarding leading individuals vs. leading teams, there are also some unique aspects of team leadership. In this chapter, we have laid out the basic elements of team leadership, how such leadership occurs at various stages of team development, and the negative things that can happen if leadership is missing in teams. Further, we described shared leadership and the reason for its importance. Finally, we examined the growing relevance and ways of leading in a virtual environment.
