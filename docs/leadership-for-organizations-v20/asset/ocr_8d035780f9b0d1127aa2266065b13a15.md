# Reactive Change

A first type of change occurs when, in the face of some performance threat (e.g., declining revenues and profits, new competitors stealing customers, changing technologies that make existing products less attractive), firms change after the threat has affected the existing business. For example, as low-cost airlines like Southwest began to attract customers, the initial response by the major U.S. airlines (e.g., United, Delta, and American) was to do nothing. It was only after they saw significant financial losses and several bankruptcies that the major airlines began to respond aggressively by cutting costs, increasing efficiencies, and, in some cases, starting their own low-cost airlines. More recently we have seen brick and mortar stores like Walmart begin to react to the threat of online sales by aggressively trying to beef up their own online capabilities.
