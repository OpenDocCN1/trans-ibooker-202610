The topic of ethical and moral leadership (EML) has grown in importance in recent years. We are frequently exposed to stories in the media of leaders at various levels of management who have acted in an unethical or irresponsible manner. Some of these individuals have suffered a great personal price. As an example, Jeff Skilling, ex-CFO and ex-CEO of Enron, served over 10 years in prison for his role in that scandal. Such individuals create problems not only for themselves, but also for their organizations, as well as the image of the greater management profession. Overall, it should not be so surprising that the public image of organizational leaders, especially those at higher levels of large corporations is not very favorable. Indeed, the public tends to lack confidence in these individuals, and they are largely not trusted to tell the truth.

Nevertheless, research shows that organizations that are guided by leaders with a stronger ethical/moral compass do exist, and they tend to outperform their competitors. These organizations do so by first establishing an organizational climate that stresses such things as **procedural justice**, or the establishment of fairness and consistency in how employees are treated. Organizations that have a climate of this nature are characterized by decision-making that is free of biases, favoritism, and personal interests. Top-level leaders are the ones who establish and reinforce such climates.

**procedural justice**
The establishment of fairness and consistency in how employees are treated.
