Accordingly, the findings are relevant to locations around the world, not just firms in Western countries.

+   • Economic values are important, but should not be predominant. There is nothing in the research by Sully de Luque and colleagues that would suggest that leaders should not be concerned with profits and controlling costs. Without positive financial outcomes, a firm cannot survive. However, if economic values become too predominant, and concern for stakeholders (other than shareholders) becomes too secondary, in the long-run the firm is liable to suffer in terms of profits. Part of the title of this research was “Unrequited Profit.” Ironically, it may be that the leaders who over-stress profits are actually the ones who are less likely to consistently achieve those profits. In other words, their values and resulting efforts go “unrequited.”
