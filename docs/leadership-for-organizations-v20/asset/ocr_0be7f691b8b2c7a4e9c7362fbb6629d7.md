# Communication Media

Gradually over time, the options of communication media have grown in number and complexity. Over a hundred years ago, leaders had one simple option: face-to-face communication. Then with the invention of the telephone, leaders had an additional means of communicating with followers. In most recent times, the options have grown even further. Less than thirty years ago, the telephone was restricted to landline capabilities, but now cell phones are extremely common around the world, making phone communication much more available and convenient. Moreover, email, texting, video conferencing, and "chat" pose additional, communication media possibilities. Accordingly, in any given instance in which a leader feels the need to communicate or give feedback to one or more followers, the logical question becomes: which medium should be chosen?

That is not an easy question to answer. The COVID-19 pandemic oftentimes restricted the face-to-face option and made more virtual modes of communication unavoidable in many instances. But even under more normal circumstances, a leader will mistakenly use an electronic, virtual medium, such as email or texting, simply because it is expedient. In other words, the leader may be experiencing a busy day, and feels the need to send a follower a quick message, so he or she simply sends an email or text message. However, the appropriate medium depends on the nature of the communication or the feedback. The key factor to consider is the "richness" of the communication medium, and whether that richness matches the nature of the feedback to be given. In essence, richness refers to the extent to which communication not only conveys information, but also properly takes into account emotions—on the part of both the sender and the receiver. As mentioned in Chapter 2, emotions are not ignored by effective leaders; instead, they are embraced.

Electronic or virtual media such as email, chat, or text messaging can effectively and efficiently send information, sometimes to thousands or even millions of people in a single message.[7] But those media may not adequately deal with emotions or potentially contentious matters. In other words, the emotions of the communication sender may not come through clearly, and perhaps more importantly, the emotions of the communication receiver may not be properly understood or handled by electronic media. In contrast, media such as phone or face-to-face contact are much better in terms of effectively dealing with emotions. The communicator can adequately get his or her emotions across, and the receiver can adequately show the emotions that result from the communication. Overall, if the leader is the sender of feedback, he or she is in a much better position to deal with emotions effectively through either phone or, even more so, face-to-face communication.

With all of that in mind, here are a number of recommendations to which leaders should adhere as givers of feedback when it comes to electronic and other communication media:

+   - Think carefully about the nature of the feedback that is given. If it is controversial, laden with potentially negative emotions (either in terms of the emotions being portrayed by the sender, or the emotions that are likely to be generated on the part of the receiver), or highly complex, then electronic forms of communication are not best. Instead, the leader should simply pick up the phone or communicate face-to-face.
+   - If using email: (a) use an appropriate subject line to grab the receiver's attention, and (b) keep the message relatively short and to the point.
+   - If either email or text messaging does not seem to be working well in terms of the results that are expected by the feedback sender, then just pick up the phone and call the feedback receiver. The call might serve to quickly clear up the confusion or quell negative emotions.
+   - Be careful not to send information in email messages that you might not want to be distributed widely. Even though you may have appropriately restricted an email message to just one or a few people, email has a way of finding itself in the hands of an unintended audience.
