# ### 13.1 Why the Leading of Change Has Become So Important

What do the following firms have in common: IBM (computers), Ingram (electronics distribution), Johnson & Johnson (pharmaceuticals); Nokia (mobile phones), Vivendi (media), and Toyota (automobiles)? The answer may not be so obvious because these firms come from different countries and compete in different industries. But there is something that ties them all together: these are all old companies (average age is 135 years old) that are alive today only because in the face of the need for dramatic change, they had leaders who helped them move into new businesses. Each of these firms began in a different business and now competes in a new one. Now think about the following companies: Polaroid, DEC, U.S. Steel, Smith Corona, Kodak, and GM. These were well-known companies in the past that, in the face of change, have failed and either gone bankrupt or been acquired
