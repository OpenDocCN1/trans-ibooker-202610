# ## 9.1 Challenges for Women Who Attempt to Lead

**glass ceiling**
An invisible, but nevertheless real, barrier that keeps women from rising up to higher managerial levels in organizations.

Diversity presents challenges for women who attempt to lead in work settings where the traditional control or power has been centered in men. Largely, these challenges involve two metaphors: (1) the glass ceiling, and (2) the glass cliff. The **glass ceiling** pertains to an invisible, but nevertheless real, barrier that keeps women from rising up to higher managerial levels in organizations.[3] Numerous factors can cause such barriers, such as “old boy” clubs or networks from which managers might be chosen or promoted.

**glass cliff**
A phenomenon whereby once a woman is able to break through the glass ceiling, that individual may end up in positions or circumstances in which they fail.

The **glass cliff** is a newer concept, but equally as problematic. The idea is that once a woman is able to break through the glass ceiling, that individual may end up in positions or circumstances in which they fail— or metaphorically fall off the slippery glass cliff. It could be that the individual may have been purposefully put into a higher-level position where the likelihood of failure was high,[4] or alternatively simply experience discrimination, sexual harassment, or lack of support once in a leadership position.[5] But regardless of the reason, the individual ends up failing, and falling off the glass cliff.

It is interesting to note that a woman may face multiple glass ceilings and glass cliffs throughout her career. For example, a woman may break through a glass ceiling into middle management, is able to avoid the glass cliff at that level, but then face another glass ceiling or glass cliff at higher levels of management. Next, we address specific challenges that are associated with glass ceilings and cliffs.

# ## Attributions of Being a Diversity Hire or Promotion

**reverse discrimination**
Occurs when favorable actions (e.g., hiring, promotion and work assignments) toward group members who are in the minority are perceived to be unfair or unwarranted by group members who are in the majority.

When a woman is able to break through the glass ceiling, that person may be surrounded by others (e.g., followers, fellow leaders, and so forth) who either subtly or overtly suggest that the reason for the individual’s promotion or placement was simply the person’s sex. In other words, these others make the attribution (see Chapter 7) that the reason for this individual breaking through the glass ceiling is **reverse discrimination**. **Reverse discrimination** occurs when favorable actions (e.g., hiring, promotion and work assignments) toward group members who are in the minority are perceived to be unfair or unwarranted by group members who are in the majority. In other words, there could be the perception that there were more qualified men for a position, but the only reason that the woman was promoted was because of her sex. If the new leader picks up on these perceptions, the likely outcome is defensiveness, lack of confidence, or the proverbial “chip on the shoulder.”
