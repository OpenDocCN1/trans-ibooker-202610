Impact networks also provide participants with multiple channels to connect with each other directly so that information can flow freely between them, unrestrained from the bottleneck of a central hub. Most networks set up an online communication system where members can share resources, opportunities, and requests. To create feedback loops for learning, it’s also good practice to establish simple network-wide practices for sharing real-time learnings related to the work that people are doing.

The IMPT enables the flow of information across the MPT field by weaving connections between people and organizations who should know each other but do not. They do so by identifying people who are conducting complementary research and then actively making formal introductions between them, going so far as helping to schedule an initial call. Coordinators also organize meetings ranging in size from small workshops to large conferences and regularly convene a collaborative group of funders to prioritize funding needs and fill funding gaps.

# Network Coordination

The people who specialize in developing the network’s ability to share information, capture learnings, and coordinate actions are called *network coordinators*. Coordinators provide the operational support that impact networks need to sustain their activities over an extended period of time. In particular, coordinators have four primary responsibilities:

+   - Keep the network on track
+   - Establish and maintain network operations
+   - Provide knowledge management
+   - Assist network teams
