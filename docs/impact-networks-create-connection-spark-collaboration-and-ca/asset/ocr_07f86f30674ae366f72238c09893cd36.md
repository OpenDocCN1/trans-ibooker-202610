In hierarchical systems, decisions are typically made by those at the top and instructions for implementation are passed down. Not only does this structure minimize input, but it normalizes power imbalances that eliminate the potential for people to make decisions about things that directly affect their lives. Simply stated, “Power is participation in decision-making,” writes Cyndi Suarez in *The Power Manual.*¹⁰

In impact networks, decision-making—like leadership—is distributed. This does not mean that everyone will agree with every decision—in many cases, that simply isn’t realistic. It does mean, however, that all participants are invited to shape proposals and share perspectives, while demonstrating a genuine consideration of each other’s circumstances, benefits, and burdens. As long as decision-making is inclusive, transparent, and intended to serve the well-being of the whole, people are usually able to live with a decision, even if they disagree—and they are grateful for a process that results in forward motion.

In situations where the network needs to make a collective decision, such as defining its participation agreements, achieving full consensus demonstrates coherence within the system. Yet consensus decision-making has major limitations—it can be slow, it can drain the network’s energy when action is stalled, and it can restrict experimentation.

We prefer an alternative approach: consent-based decision-making, which allows actions in the best interest of the whole to proceed without unanimous agreement. This approach is rooted in the “range of tolerance” principle borrowed from ecosystem science: when organisms find themselves in contexts that are too hot or too cold, some parts of the system die because the temperature is out of their range of tolerance. With consent-based decision-making, we find a decision that everyone can live with, even if it’s not their top preference. As questions or objections arise, participants take the time to hear each other’s honest concerns, and they work together to refine the final proposal so that it lands within everyone’s range of tolerance.
