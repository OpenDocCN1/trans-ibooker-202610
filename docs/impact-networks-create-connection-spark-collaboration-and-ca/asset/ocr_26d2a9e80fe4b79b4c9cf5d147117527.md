+   - Weaving the network by acting as liaisons between leaders, project teams, and the full network, including being available to hear and consider ideas and concerns raised by participants
+   - Representing the network in public settings
+   - Supporting the selection and onboarding of new participants
+   - Advancing efforts to resource the network

# ### Project Teams

Project teams (sometimes called *working groups, task forces,* and *action circles*) are organized by participants to advance a body of work related to the network’s purpose. When it comes to forming project teams, what participants think should happen is less important than what they will do. As Marvin Weisbord and Sandra Janoff advise, set priorities “based on willing actors rather than good ideas.”4

Early on in a network’s formation, project teams are most often focused internally to build the network itself. Internally focused project teams might be tasked with determining decision-making processes, securing funding, clarifying communications strategies, or developing an asset map. As the network evolves, project teams increasingly focus externally, advancing work in the community or broader system.

Project teams aim to break down the complexity that the network is trying to address into discrete, short-term tasks that can be deliberately planned and advanced. Accordingly, they are the perfect place to flex project management muscles. To balance deliberate and emergent strategy, project teams can develop plans of action only from one convening to the next, or over the course of a few months, whichever is shorter. Then, when they’ve completed their task, they can assess what they’ve learned, report their progress back to the network, and plan out the next phase of work, if necessary. This cyclical process allows project teams to assign responsibilities and make consistent progress, while also staying emergent and open to input and support from the network.
