Some funders have the impression that networks are especially expensive or difficult to fund, but the truth is, they don’t need to be—not if you’re investing in the right things, and not if you simply get out of the way and let the network get to work. To get started, “find a partner you trust who can help manage the network and hold a mirror up to you if you are impeding the network’s development,” suggests Jennifer Husbands.<sup>24</sup> We also encourage funders to focus on investing in dedicated network leadership, with all the roles it entails—including catalyzing, facilitation, weaving, and coordination. Under-resourced leadership is one of the primary factors we have seen that cause impact networks to fail to live up to their potential.

Ultimately, an investment in networks is an investment in our ability to work across silos and sectors to get things done. To tackle our most pressing challenges, we must collaborate—and impact networks provide the essential infrastructure that enables individuals and organizations to connect, learn, and work together for a better future. “Take a learning-by-doing approach, and take the first step,” encourages Liz Wilson.<sup>25</sup> It’s time for funders to embrace the way of the network.
