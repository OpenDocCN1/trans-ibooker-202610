The process of gathering information into a network begins with a clear and consistent practice of collecting relevant resources and data from participants, as well as from outside sources. That information can then be organized and made available to participants through tools such as asset maps, searchable directories, and online databases. An interactive asset map, for instance, provides members with an easy way to identify the available resources, areas of expertise, and geographic regions present across the network—while also helping them to immediately connect with each other to follow through on what they learned. Specifically, the IMPT gathers information from its members through its online resource center. The resource center collects a wide range of resources from beyond the network, including articles, presentations, events, and funding opportunities related to the network’s purpose. Network coordinators help to organize the resources gathered from participants so they are up-to-date and easy to find. Coordinators also attend conferences, subscribe to listserv groups and alerts, and track all MPT products in development in order to collect information from the broader field in addition to its members.

# Information Out

Once collected, information is sent back out to participants through webinars, calls, emails, and other means of distribution. Electronic newsletters, when curated, can be effective at providing participants with a broad overview of what’s happening across the network in only a few minutes.

In addition to its monthly newsletter, full of information relevant to the field, the IMPT hosts regular webinars, presents at conferences, and provides opportunities for participants to share their work and answer questions. The network also hosts a website, theimpt.org, to make information about MPTs available to the public.
