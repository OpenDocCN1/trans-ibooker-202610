We have found it useful to offer four primary levels of engagement to choose from: lead, partner, follow, and honor. These levels of participation can apply to the network as a whole, as well as to specific project teams:

+   - **Lead:** “I will take responsibility for making this network/team happen.”
+   - **Partner:** “I will actively work with the leaders to make this network/team happen.”
+   - **Follow:** “I would like to stay informed about how this network/team is progressing.”
+   - **Honor:** “Although I appreciate what you are proposing, this network/team is not of particular interest to me at this time.”

When a network is just getting started, we ask participants to share their preferred level of engagement with the network as a whole, to get a better sense of who would like to attend future convenings and be kept in the loop with network communications. *Leaders* are those who would like to be particularly engaged in the work of cultivating the network—for instance, by helping to coordinate the network, participating on the core team, or leading a project team. Network *partners* are actively engaged in the work of the network and committed to attending convenings and participating in network activities. *Followers* want to be kept in the loop but cannot be actively engaged at the present time. People who choose *honor* are respectfully declining to participate and are therefore removed from network communications. As the network evolves, we ask participants to clarify their intended level of engagement annually to help leaders with planning, coordination, and budgeting.

Be sure not to overlook the followers. Anyone who has seen Derek Sivers’s YouTube video “First Follower: Leadership Lessons from Dancing Guy” knows the power that followers have to inspire and advance the work of others. “If the leader is the flint,” he says, “the follower is the spark that really makes the fire.”⁶ Followers should be kept in the loop with updates on future opportunities to engage with and support the work of the network.
