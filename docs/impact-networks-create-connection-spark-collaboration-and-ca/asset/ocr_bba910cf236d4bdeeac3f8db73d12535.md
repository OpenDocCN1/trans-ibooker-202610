Convening people doesn’t mean inviting them into “your thing”; it means inviting them into co-creating what is possible, now that they are part of the group. Every person who joins an impact network has the opportunity, and the responsibility, to contribute to its development.

# Identifying Who to Convene

Who to invite into an impact network depends on its purpose, context, timing, and scope. When considering who to engage, we include people “for a lot of the wrong reasons—obligation, guilt, representation . . . even love,” notes adrienne maree brown. Instead, bring together those who

+   - are directly impacted by the issue,
+   - are doing compelling work on the issue, and
+   - can move the work forward.¹

Make it a priority to convene a diverse set of participants from the very beginning, not later after the effort has already been defined. Whoever is invited to join a network first is likely to significantly impact the makeup of the resulting group, as the social structures that are present at a group’s founding are likely to be retained over time.² This may require seeking out people who have different backgrounds, disciplines, ways of thinking, and experiences. A design team that includes people from many different parts of the system can play a central role in crafting a well-considered selection process or invite list for the launch of a new network.
