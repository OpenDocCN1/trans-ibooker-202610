Coordinators led discussions and webinars to share vital information about how member organizations could help protect the large number of public health professionals and worker-safety trainers they worked with. Public health professionals in the network shared the best scientifically based information available so that members could share that information with the at-risk communities and frontline workers they supported. And an emergency response fund organized by CFEN quickly distributed $250,000 into the hands of organizations to help them make up for canceled fundraising events, support their move to virtual operations and services, and provide direct COVID-related support for frontline workers and for constituents who found themselves in dire financial situations due to the pandemic.⁵ With its foundation of trust and established channels for coordination already in place, CFEN was able to respond quickly and effectively to support its members and those they serve.⁶

The critical importance of being able to coordinate actions quickly was also evident in the Santa Cruz Mountains region during the height of the 2020 wildfires. The CZU Lightning Complex wildfire took over a month to contain, during which time 86,000 acres burned in San Mateo and Santa Cruz counties.⁷ As the fire raged, more forest was burning in Santa Cruz County than in San Mateo County. Because of this discrepancy, the San Mateo Resource Conservation District (RCD), a government agency usually constrained by jurisdictional boundaries, had a bit of extra capacity and immediately sent their staff across county lines to support Santa Cruz’s postfire recovery work. Recalling that experience, Kellyx Nelson, the executive director of the San Mateo RCD and a founding member of the Santa Cruz Mountains Stewardship Network (SCMSN), remembers thinking, “We’ll figure out the problems with county boundaries later; we’ll just find a way.”

Meanwhile, the Santa Cruz RCD, another organizational member of the SCMSN, was so busy responding to the fire that they didn’t have the bandwidth to raise resources for their efforts.
