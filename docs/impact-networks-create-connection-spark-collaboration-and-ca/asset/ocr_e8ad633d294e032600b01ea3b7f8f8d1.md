Given the emergent nature of networks, longer-term impacts often cannot be well defined at the beginning. In fact, determining long-term outcomes before participants have had a chance to think and work together is likely to stifle their ability to self-organize around what they think is most important. Therefore, efforts to assess long-term impacts require a thoughtful evaluation approach that allows for this flexibility over time; approaches that rely largely on predetermined and static metrics are generally not a good fit.

At the same time, healthy impact networks share a set of short-term and intermediate-term outcomes that are definable, including markers of network connectivity and demonstrated value for participants. These are important to document, in terms of actionable data to inform network development, as well as to demonstrate forward motion to funders and participants alike. Because the aspects of network health are relatively universal, they can be identified in advance and assessed with fairly standardized (yet customizable) tools. This is why we are able to offer the following structured evaluation approach, which will be useful for almost any impact network.

# Four Fundamentals of Network Evaluation

We propose four basic components of network evaluation, explained in more detail below, to help ensure that your network is continually learning and evolving in a positive direction:

+   - Collaborative infrastructure
+   - Network connectivity
+   - Participant experience
+   - Emergent activity
