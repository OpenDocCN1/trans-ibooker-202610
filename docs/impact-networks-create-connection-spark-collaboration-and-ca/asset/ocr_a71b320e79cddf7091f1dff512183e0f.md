Over time, they may shift to become partners or leaders as their interests and capacity change—it's common for people to move between different levels of engagement over the course of a network's life cycle.

One of the responsibilities of network leaders is to provide many different opportunities for leadership, partnership, and followership to emerge, and to allow people to decline altogether. Trust that participants will self-organize to place their energy where they are most inspired to contribute and where they are most needed.

# Establishing Participation Agreements

Network participation is not a one-size-fits-all proposition. Participation in networks may be open (anyone can join), closed (participants agree to fulfill certain criteria to join and stay engaged), or tiered (the network provides multiple ways for participants to engage, each with distinct criteria and responsibilities). Networks will need to decide what participation agreements are necessary to maintain continuity as they evolve.

While it may be tempting to keep participation open to avoid complications around defining criteria and excluding some people, open participation comes with its own challenges. In The Art of Community, Charles Vogl identifies “clear boundaries” as one of the core principles of belonging. Vogl notes that for a strong community to develop, people often need to know that being a part of the community *means something* and that not just anyone can join in on a whim. In other words, boundaries are less about exclusion and more about fostering greater inclusion within the network.^7

The Enspiral network learned this lesson the hard way. Enspiral is a community of more than 150 freelancers and activists located primarily in Europe and New Zealand who work collaboratively to create positive social impact through their ventures. At first, Enspiral was an open network, with few boundaries on participation. While this worked at first, over time the lack of boundaries created an unhealthy work environment when some people abused the high-trust atmosphere.
