# CHAPTER 8

# Coordinate Actions

> *What happens to one happens to us all.*
> *We can starve together or feast together.*
> *All flourishing is mutual.*

> — ROBIN WALL KIMMERER, *Braiding Sweetgrass*

People are often inspired by the big new idea they have yet to try—the moonshot investment that will make all the difference. Large-scale change is, after all, why many impact networks form in the first place. But the process of creating large-scale change is not fast or easy. It usually means engaging with the slow work of understanding what is, what we want, and how we will build something different together. It can take a substantial amount of time, energy, and trusting relationships to persevere despite disagreements and roadblocks and to shift persistent mindsets, structures, and habits.

It’s common for people to want to jump immediately into ambitious plans to change the system. In their eagerness to “get to action,” they launch new projects before building on the work that’s already underway. And when they encounter inevitable challenges or failures, they become demoralized, and their enthusiasm for the network fades. When this happens, participants might question whether the time they’re putting into the network is worth their while, and many will choose to disengage. As a result, the network’s full potential is abandoned before it has a chance to mature.
