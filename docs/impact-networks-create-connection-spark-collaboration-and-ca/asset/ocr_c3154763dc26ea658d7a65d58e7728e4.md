# Enabling Information to Flow In, Out, and Across

Impact networks can accelerate the flow of information in three primary ways: by bringing *information in* from the network as well as from outside sources, organizing and sending *information out* to participants and others who are interested, and creating pathways for participants to easily share *information across* the network with one another.

The Fire Adapted Communities Learning Network (FAC Net) has advanced a variety of strategies to accelerate the exchange of information between communities and organizations to enhance the practice of fire adaptation around the United States. The network forms learning circles, organizes and creates resources, and maintains a communication system that each one of its 150 members engages in.

The fruits of its labor paid off during the devastating 2020 wildfire season. “Getting through these wildfire crises immediately came down to relationships and our ability to share information,” reflects Emily Troisi, one of the network’s coordinators. “People were using our communication system to share requests, donation opportunities, and connections to outside resources. Every person was texting, calling, and emailing each other to say, ‘I’m here, let me know how I can support you.’”³ Thanks in large part to the work of FAC Net since its launch in 2013, what was once a fragmented field is now a vibrant learning ecosystem.

Another example of a network with an information-rich ecosystem is the IMPT, a global learning network focused on advancing multipurpose prevention technologies (MPTs) for women, described in chapter 1. The IMPT has developed some specific techniques that coordinators can use to accelerate the flow of information in, out, and across a network, as described in more detail below.
