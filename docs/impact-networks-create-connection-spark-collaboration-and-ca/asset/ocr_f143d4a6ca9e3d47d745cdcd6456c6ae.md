# Core Team

The leadership function for the network as a whole is served by a core team (sometimes called a *leadership team, network council, governance circle,* or *steering committee*). Core teams coordinate the work, activities, and resources of the network in partnership with other network leaders. Because they are not always necessary, core teams should be created only if a network is sufficiently complex that an additional layer of governance is needed to support greater contributions and aligned action among participants.

Core teams may appear to be an unusually hierarchical feature of impact networks at first glance, and indeed they do tend to have some influence on the network’s decisions. The primary difference from a traditional power hierarchy, however, is that core teams do not hold formal authority over other network members. Core teams make recommendations, but final decisions ultimately rest with the network as a whole. Another difference from traditional hierarchies is that core teams rotate their members regularly. They usually consist of four to seven people, each representing a different participating organization or part of the network, who volunteer for the core team for a specific period of time (often from one convening to the next).

Core teams have wide-ranging roles, depending on what the network needs most at any given time, including but not limited to the following:

+   - Providing advisory support to other network leaders, such as those leading project teams and learning circles
+   - Working to ensure that the network’s stated principles are infused in the network’s practices
+   - Making recommendations on important decisions for the network to consider
