I called the hiring manager and asked for an example of his team advocating for such a change, and he confirmed that it would require jumping in slow motion through layers of hoops.

Prior to that call, I had nearly accepted the offer. But, hearing about all the big corporate red tape I’d have to wade through, I realized that taking that job would make me miserable. I couldn’t get myself to leave the high-flying start-up atmosphere where I had the freedom to move the needle in quantum leaps, not increments.

It’s a big decision to choose to share your talents with a start-up versus a large corporate incumbent. Both options include benefits and risks. But if you have an entrepreneurial mind-set, you will discover, like I did, that start-ups can offer huge benefits. Here are what I see as the top six.

# Fewer Barriers to Entry

While big corporations often choose to hire the candidates who went to Ivy League schools, are well-connected, or have loads of experience at higher-level positions, start-ups are interested in something else. They choose to hire people who think creatively, show a willingness to work hard, and demonstrate raw leadership qualities that, once cultivated, can help the company (and the individual) achieve breakthrough success. Remember, this is the ground floor—every early hire plays a vital part in the company’s construction and development.
