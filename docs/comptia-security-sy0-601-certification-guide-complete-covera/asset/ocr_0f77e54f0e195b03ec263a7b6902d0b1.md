b. **Lack of Vendor Support:** There is a danger that a vendor has won so many contracts that they do not have sufficient management or people to fulfil them all and have spread themselves too thinly. This means that they cannot commit enough support for the contract.

c. **Supply Chain:** A company outsources to a contractor who, in turn, may subcontract to a third party. Both are part of the supply chain. If one of these is vulnerable, it poses a risk to you. In 2013, the company called Target was a victim of a supply chain attack in which the admin account maintaining their HVAC system was used to gain access to their network.

- **Outsourced Code Development:** A lot of companies do not have the skillset to write code and, instead, rely upon a third party to do so. If this is the case, the first thing that you need to do is to find a reputable provider. You should look at the company's reputation as well as the quality of their work; otherwise, you may receive improperly written code that may leave your company vulnerable.

- **Data Storage:** Companies might outsource their data storage to a third party such as a cloud provider. In this case, you must ensure that their protection of the data is at least equal to the encryption that you currently use, that you have a secure means of transferring data, and that you are able to monitor who has access to the data. Additionally, ensure that the cloud provider has resilience built in so that, even if the storage area fails, your data is still available.

- **Improper or Weak Patch Management:** Companies need to ensure that all software and applications are fully patched to mitigate any vulnerabilities. It is best practice to make an inventory of all your computer systems along with their operating systems and applications so that, when a patch comes out, you know which computers to update.
