Container systems as we know them can be traced to seeds in the 1970s with the addition of the *chroot* system⁹ In 2000, FreeBSD’s Jail¹⁰ system emerged as a way of allowing multi-tenant hosts to further separate what was shared and what was unique to each user. This drove interest and development in Linux control groups many years later leading to the development of Linux Containers (LXC) in 2008. Google and CloudFoundry had some offerings along the way, but it was ultimately Docker being released in 2013 that really got the ball rolling.

Overall this containerization approach allowed finer-level granularity around the virtualization than the heavier weight images of the previous generation. More of the infrastructure could be pre-defined so we could focus on installing the right versions of our shared libraries and applications.
