# Observations

+   1) The rates of both the horizontal and vertical spreading depend on atmospheric conditions.
+      a) Atmospheric conditions depend on
+         i) incoming short wave radiation, e.g. sunlight
+         ii) outgoing long wave radiation, mainly affected by degree of cloudiness
+         iii) terrain surface roughness
+         iv) terrain surface moisture
+         v) wind speed

+   2) Buoyancy affects the plume centerline to produce a change in the height depending on the difference in temperature between the plume and the surrounding atmosphere.

The idealized concentration at any point downwind of an elevated source at height *H* is given by “emission rate of pollutant, wind speed, distance, and plume standard deviations in the horizontal and vertical directions.”

**Point Source at Elevation H**

- Assumes no interference or limitation to dispersion in any direction

\[
C(x,y,z)=\frac{Q}{2\pi u\sigma_y\sigma_z}\exp\left[-\frac{(y-y_0)^2}{2\sigma_y^2}\right]\exp\left[-\frac{(z-z_0)^2}{2\sigma_z^2}\right]
\tag{D.2}
\]

*x₀* and *z₀* are locations of centerline of plume  
*y₀* is taken as the base of the stack  
*z₀* is *H*  
*Q* = emission strength of source (mass/time) – g/s  
*u* = average wind speed through the plume – m/s  
*C* = concentration – g/m³ (notice this is not ppm)  
*σᵧ* and *σ𝓏* are horizontal and vertical standard deviations in meter
