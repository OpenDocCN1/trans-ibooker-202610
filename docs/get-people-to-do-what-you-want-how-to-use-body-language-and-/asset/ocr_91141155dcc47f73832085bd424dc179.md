With jihadists, there are elements of the bonding process that are treasured and vital parts of normal Muslim life. Similar to how Hitler used valued cultural rituals to create belonging, jihadists take the noble and usurp or pervert it. The connection that a young man or woman might feel to the jihadists is therefore ennobled by rituals and beliefs that are shared by at least 1.5 billion people. A good example is the salat, the obligatory Muslim prayers performed five times each day. This devotion sets the rhythm for a devout Muslim's daily life, and both spiritually and energetically reinforces the primary attachments in the person's life. For 1,400 years, the prayer ritual has linked Muslims around the world on a daily basis.

The shift toward radical behavior is not a small step; it is a journey. Like Charles Manson, the leaders reward what they want and punish what they do not want. If a sense of devotion can be maintained while someone is mentored and incentivized to go toward it, however, then the person being radicalized can continue to feel as though the effort—no matter how much violence it entails—allows her to maintain spiritual integrity.

Thomas Hegghammer, author of *Jihadi Culture: The Art and Social Practices of Militant Islamists*, explains that the militancy of brutal killers like Mohammed Emwaze (Jihadi John) “is about more than bombs and doctrines. It is also about rituals, customs, and dress codes. It is about music, films, and storytelling. It is about sports, jokes, and food.”¹⁴ He goes on to talk about their culture as a romantic one, “insofar as they see themselves as historical heroes, knights in shining armour, every one of them.”¹⁵
