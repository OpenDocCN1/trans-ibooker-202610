# The Dynamic of Bonding

These stories point to the two primary reasons of why people bond:

+   1. Common enemy
+   2. Common cause

The Arab saying “The enemy of my enemy is my friend” points to the number-one way to get people to bond: give them a common enemy, whether you find one or create one.

Common cause may have less clarity in people minds, and different people may view the cause very differently (democracy), but it still operates as a unifying principle.

Even if there is no authentic common enemy, by creating artificial stress, you can draw someone closer to you. You see this in offices all the time, even if the effect lasts only for a single meeting or a single day, when someone points out that the boss is in a bad mood.

Years ago, Greg worked for a guy who had a volatile temperament. On any given day, he would reverse his normal behavior patterns and suddenly disagree with anything a person said. Greg and his colleagues developed a simple code: Opposite Day. The first person to interact with the boss would send a note to the others: Opposite Day. The unpredictable, and often disagreeeable, boss unknowingly created a bond—one of sanity preservation—among his team members.
