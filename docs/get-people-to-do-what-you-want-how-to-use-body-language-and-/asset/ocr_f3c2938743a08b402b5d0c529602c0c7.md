Then move the conversation about those interests to the office: “Do people at the office know you do the pre-concert narrations for the city symphony?” After that, you find out her preparation for those gigs sometimes digs into office time. You might respond, “Bill strikes me as the kind of CEO who encourages employees to do what's important to them.” This comment might elicit raised eyebrows in surprise; a knit brow that says, “Are you kidding?”; or maybe a big smile and nod that broadcast “Absolutely.” Regardless of the subject of the conversation, if you find out whether or not Bill treats his executive assistant with respect and support, then you find out a lot about his workplace priorities. Does he care about having her feel as though she belongs? Does he do anything to give her a sense of status? Does he support her efforts toward self-actualization through this very important “night job”?

Knowing how he tunes into the needs of employees will give you clues as to where he himself sits in the hierarchy of needs. More importantly, if he is doing a poor job, and you can identify his needs and fulfill them, then you bond with him—and information flows freely as he forgets who you are.
