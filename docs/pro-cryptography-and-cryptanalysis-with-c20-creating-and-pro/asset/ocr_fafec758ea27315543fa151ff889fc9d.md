There’s no need to keep the encryption key e secret; it can be made public. Every individual can then send encrypted messages to Bob, which can be decrypted only by Bob. Figure 2-7 illustrates the idea, where A₁, A₂, and A₃ represents different entities. Remember if A₁ destroys the message m₁ after encrypting it to c₁, then even A₁ is in the position of not being able to recover m₁ from c₁.

Let’s take the following analog example to make it simple. Consider a metal box with the cover secured by a lock with a particular combination. Bob is the only one who knows how to open the lock. If the lock stays open and is made accessible to the public for different purposes, someone can put a message inside and lock the lock.
