# Internet of Things (IoT)

The term IoT, or Internet of Things, refers to the collective network of interconnected devices and the technology that facilitates communication between devices and the cloud, as well as between the devices themselves. Thanks to the advent of inexpensive computer chips and high bandwidth telecommunication, we now have billions of devices connected to the internet. This means everyday devices like toothbrushes, vacuums, cars, and machines can use sensors to collect data and respond intelligently to their users.

The IoT has evolved from the convergence of wireless technologies, microelectromechanical systems (MEMSes), microservices, and the internet. This convergence helped tear down the silos that existed between operational technology (OT) and information technology (IT), enabling machine-generated, unstructured data to be analyzed for insights to drive improvements.

Although the concept of IoT has been around since the 1990s, it has only truly become practical. Both consumers and businesses are leveraging IoT to improve everything from health care to supply chain management.

Although the term is similar to the IoT, IIoT is the Internet of Things as it applies to the industrial sector. The IIoT uses smart sensors and actuators to enhance manufacturing and industrial processes. Also known as the industrial internet or the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT), it uses the power of the smart machines and real-time analytics to take advantage of the data that "dumb machines" have produced in machine logs on factory floors for years.

The driving philosophy behind the IoT is to make the surrounding elements—parts of the environment that are relevant to the user, their role, machine, or task—actionable to create a common operating picture (COP) that enables an unprecedented ability to make decisions.

# An IoT ecosystem

An IoT ecosystem consists of web-enabled smart devices that use embedded systems, such as processors, sensors, and communication hardware to collect, send, and act on the data they acquire from their environments. IoT devices share the sensor data they collect by connecting to an IoT gateway or other edge device where data is either sent to the cloud to be analyzed or analyzed locally. Sometimes, these devices communicate with other related devices and act on the information they get from one another. The devices do most of the work without human intervention, although people can interact with the devices—for instance, to set them up, give them instructions, or access the data.
