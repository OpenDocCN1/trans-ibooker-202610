When working with mathematical objects, you must be careful in mixing algebraic meanings of operations with structural interpretations. Mathematically, you cannot say when one UniPoly polynomial is less than another. For some subsets such as constants, you can decide this, but it does not generally work. Structurally, we could compare the polynomials lexicographically by tuples of their exponents and coefficients.

Given

p = x² + 7x + 1   and   q = 4x² − 7x + 3

look at the tuples of the highest degree. For p, this is (2, 1), and for q, it is (2, 4). If one exponent were smaller, that polynomial would be “less than” the other. In this case, the exponents are equal, so we compare the coefficients. Since 1 < 4, p is “less than” q. Had they been equal, we would have moved to the terms of the next lower degree.
