When we have two points, say (2.0, 2.0) and (8.0, 5.0), it is trivial to compute the line through them. In this case, $m = 0.5$ and $b = 1.0$, and Figure 15.11 shows the plot.

![](bbox=[0.299, 0.156, 0.721, 0.415])

Figure 15.11: Simple linear regression on two points

What happens when we add the point (4.0, 5.0)? There is no line that passes through all three points, so we seek a line that somehow represents the data better than any other line.

![](bbox=[0.299, 0.532, 0.721, 0.733])

Figure 15.12: Simple linear regression on three points

I’ve labeled the line we want as $L$ in Figure 15.12. The three initial points are the black-filled circles. We need to compute the equation of $L$ so that we minimize the sum of the squares of the vertical distances from our points to $L$. The white circles are the points on $L$ closest to our initial points, and their vertical distances are $e_1$, $e_2$, and $e_3$.
