# 6.5. Visualizing to communicate

Concepts related to data visualization are often rooted in the lexical field of language: from the semiology of graphics of Jacques Bertin (1973) to the grammar of graphics of Leland Wilkinson (2006 [1999]), via the “languages of art” of Nelson Goodman (1968) and the “visual explanations” of Edward Tufte (1997), many researchers find in visualization characteristics similar to those of the language. Without going into detail, we can nevertheless retain a strong common point in terms of usage: like a language, visualization is used – or at least allows us – to communicate information. By pointing out the importance of visualization in an interdisciplinary modeling process, it has already been shown that visualization makes it possible to communicate with an audience of experts involved in a common project.
