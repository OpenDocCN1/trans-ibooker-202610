# 4.33

If there are CCCs, description criterion DC6 requires that fact to be disclosed in the description of the entity's system. In addition, because the practitioner does not examine the controls implemented by customers, disclosure of that information in the practitioner's report is often necessary to enable report users to understand the limitations on the subject matter being examined. Such disclosure is consistent with paragraph .63c of AT-C section 205, which requires that the practitioner's report include an identification or description of the subject matter being examined. To help intended users understand the subject matter being examined, the practitioner may include in his or her report a statement that the practitioner has not evaluated the effectiveness of the CCCs and that the entity can achieve its principal system objectives based on the applicable trust services criteria only if the CCCs are effective, along with the related controls at the entity. A paragraph that includes such disclosures, which would normally follow the scope paragraph, is illustrated here:

The description indicates that effective complementary customer controls are necessary, along with controls at [name of entity], to provide reasonable assurance of achieving [name of entity]'s principal system objectives based on the applicable trust services criteria. The description presents [name of entity]'s controls, the applicable trust services criteria, and the complementary customer controls assumed in the design of [name of entity]'s controls. Our examination did not include such complementary customer controls and we have not evaluated their effectiveness.

# 4.34

In addition, when there are CCCs, the practitioner would modify the paragraph containing the opinion on control effectiveness by referring to the CCCs. An example of language that might be added to the opinion paragraph is shown in boldface italics in the following paragraph:

The controls stated in the description, which are necessary to provide reasonable assurance that [name of entity]'s principal system objectives were achieved, were effective throughout the period [date] to [date] based on the applicable trust services criteria if complementary customer controls assumed in the design of [name of entity]'s controls were effective throughout that period.

# 4.35

When there are CCCs, entity management would also modify its assertion to reflect the same modifications made to the practitioner's report by

+   a. adding to its assertion a paragraph similar to that in paragraph 4.33 (without the final sentence) and
+   b. using the same language (illustrated in boldface italics in paragraph 4.34) to revise its statement about control effectiveness.

# Reporting When There Are Complementary Supplier Controls

# 4.36

Supplier controls are discussed beginning at paragraph 2.134. When controls performed by the supplier are necessary, in combination with the entity's controls, to achieve the entity's principal system objectives, such complementary supplier controls (CSCs) are required to be disclosed in the description.
