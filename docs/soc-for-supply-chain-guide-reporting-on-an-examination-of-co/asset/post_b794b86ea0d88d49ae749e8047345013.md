## Modifications to the Practitioner’s Opinion

4.42 Paragraph .68 of AT-C section 205 requires the practitioner to modify the opinion when either of the following circumstances exist and, in the practitioner's professional judgment, the effect of the matter is or may be material:

- a. The practitioner is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence to conclude that the subject matter is in accordance with (or based on) the criteria, in all material respects.
- b. The practitioner concludes, based on evidence obtained, that the subject matter is not in accordance with (or based on) the criteria, in all material respects.

4.43 In applying paragraphs .68–.69 of AT-C section 205 to the SOC for Supply Chain examination, the practitioner's opinion should be modified, and the practitioner's report should include a description of the matters giving rise to the modification, if any of the following apply:

- a. The practitioner concludes, based on the evidence obtained, that the description, taken as a whole, does not present the system designed and implemented throughout the period in accordance with the description criteria, in all material respects.
- b. The practitioner concludes, based on evidence obtained, that controls were not effective throughout the specified period to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria, in all material respects.
- c. The practitioner is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence.

4.44 The objective of including a description of each of the matters giving rise to the modification is to enable intended users to consider the effects of identified description misstatements, control deficiencies, and control deviations when making their own risk assessments. Materiality considerations related to the description are discussed beginning in paragraph 3.63, and considerations related to the effectiveness of controls are discussed beginning in paragraph 3.143.

4.45 If a modified opinion is appropriate, the practitioner determines whether to issue a qualified opinion, an adverse opinion, or a disclaimer of opinion. Based on paragraph .A103 of AT-C section 205, the decision regarding which type of modified opinion is appropriate depends on the following:

- a. The nature of the matter giving rise to the modification (that is, whether the description is presented in accordance with the description criteria or whether controls were effective to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria), in all material respects, or in the case of an inability to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence, whether either may be materially misstated
- b. The practitioner's professional judgment about the pervasiveness of the effects or possible effects of the matter on the description or the effectiveness of controls

4.46 When determining the type of modified opinion to be issued, the practitioner evaluates whether identified (a) description misstatements (including omissions) or (b) deficiencies in the effectiveness of controls are material.

4.47 Table 4-4 identifies the types of modified opinions that may be issued depending on the nature of the matter giving rise to the modification and the practitioner's professional judgment about the materiality and pervasiveness of its effects (or possible effects) on the opinion on the description and on the effectiveness of controls.

4.48 Paragraph .69 of AT-C section 205 states that, when the practitioner modifies the opinion, the practitioner should include a separate paragraph in the practitioner's report that provides a description of the matters giving rise to the modification. Examples of separate paragraphs that describe the matters giving rise to a modification are provided beginning in paragraph 4.68.

4.49 When determining whether to modify the practitioner's report, the practitioner considers the effect of identified description misstatements, individually and in the aggregate, on the description of the entity's system taken as a whole. The practitioner also considers the effect of identified control deficiencies, individually and in the aggregate, on the effectiveness of the controls to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria. Chapter 3 discusses materiality, including the quantitative and qualitative factors the practitioner considers, in further detail.

### Qualified Opinion

4.50 The practitioner expresses a qualified opinion in the following circumstances:

- a. The practitioner, having obtained sufficient appropriate evidence, concludes that description misstatements or deficiencies in the effectiveness of controls are, either individually or in the aggregate, material but not pervasive to the description or the effectiveness of controls.
- b. The practitioner is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence on which to base the opinion but the practitioner has concluded that the possible effects on the subject matter of undetected description misstatements or control deficiencies, if any, could be material but not pervasive to the description or the effectiveness of controls.

This section discusses qualifications because of material misstatements. The section beginning in paragraph 4.57 discusses qualifications because of scope limitations.

4.51 When the practitioner has determined that a qualified opinion is appropriate, the practitioner's report would be modified by doing the following:

- a. Including, in a separate paragraph before the opinion paragraph, a clear explanation of the matters giving rise to the qualified opinion. When the modification is due to a deficiency in control effectiveness, the practitioner usually identifies the effect of the deficiency on the entity's ability to achieve one or more principal system objectives or to meet one or more of the applicable trust services criteria used to evaluate the achievement of those objectives. Determining which approach to take is usually based on the practitioner's professional judgment related to which disclosure is likely to provide more useful information to the intended users of the report.
- b. Stating in the opinion paragraph that, except for the effects of the matters giving rise to the modification, the description is presented in accordance with the description criteria and controls were effective to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria, in all material respects.
- c. Amending the practitioner's responsibility paragraph to state that the practitioner believes that the evidence obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for the practitioner's qualified opinion.
