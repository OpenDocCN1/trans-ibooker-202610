4.64 If the practitioner decides to disclaim an opinion and, based on the limited procedures performed, has concluded that (a) certain aspects of the description do not present the system designed and implemented in accordance with the description criteria or (b) certain controls were not effective to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria, the practitioner should include in the practitioner's report a separate paragraph containing a clear description of the matters that led the practitioner to those conclusions.

4.65 Other situations in which the practitioner should disclaim an opinion may include the following:
- a. Entity management refuses to provide a written assertion (after initially agreeing to do so), and law or regulation does not allow the practitioner to withdraw from the engagement (see paragraph 4.66).
- b. Entity management refuses to provide a representation reaffirming its written assertion included in or attached to its description or a representation stating that it has provided the practitioner with all relevant information and access agreed to.

## Entity Management Will Not Provide a Written Assertion but Law or Regulation Does Not Permit the Practitioner to Withdraw From the Engagement

4.66 Paragraph .82 of AT-C section 205 states that, if entity management is both the responsible party and the engaging party and refuses to provide a written assertion, the practitioner is required to withdraw from the engagement. However, if the practitioner is required by law or regulation to accept or continue an engagement to report on controls at an entity and entity management refuses to provide a written assertion, the practitioner may conduct the engagement and, ultimately, should disclaim an opinion.

4.67 The following is an example of a separate paragraph that might be added to the practitioner's report in that situation:

> Attestation standards established by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants require that we request a written assertion from entity management of Example Entity that its description of its [type of system] throughout the period [date] to [date] is presented in accordance with the description criteria and that the controls stated in the description, which are necessary to provide reasonable assurance that Example Entity achieved its principal system objectives, were effective throughout the period based on the applicable trust services criteria. We requested that assertion, but Example Entity management did not provide such an assertion.

## Illustrative Separate Paragraphs When There Are Material Misstatements in the Description

4.68 Chapter 3 presents several situations in which the practitioner determines that the description is not presented in accordance with the description criteria, in all material respects. In practice, if the practitioner makes such a determination, the practitioner would discuss the matter with entity management and work with entity management to make the necessary changes to the description. If entity management refuses to amend the description, the practitioner may decide to withdraw from the engagement. If the practitioner decides to continue with the engagement, the practitioner would ordinarily express an adverse opinion on the description.

4.69 Examples of separate paragraphs that would be added to the practitioner’s report if entity management is unwilling to amend a description that is not presented in accordance with the description criteria, in all material respects, are presented beginning at paragraph 4.70.

### Description Includes Controls That Have Not Been Implemented

4.70 The following is an example of a separate paragraph that would be added to the practitioner’s report when the description includes controls that have not been implemented:

The accompanying description states that Example Entity’s system is protected against unauthorized logical access through the use of operator identification numbers and passwords. Based on inquiries of staff personnel and observation of activities, we determined that operator identification numbers and passwords are used in applications A and B but not in application C.

### Description Includes Information That Cannot Be Objectively Evaluated

4.71 The following is an example of a separate paragraph that would be added to the practitioner’s report when the description of the entity’s system includes subjective information that is not measurable:

On page XX of the accompanying description, Example Entity states that its quality inspection for raw materials and subassemblies provided by suppliers is the industry’s best system and is staffed by the most talented IT personnel. Because there are no criteria against which these attributes can be measured, these statements cannot be measured or objectively evaluated within the scope of this examination.

### Description Omits Relevant Changes to Controls

4.72 The following is an example of a separate paragraph that would be added to the practitioner’s report when the description does not address relevant changes to the entity’s controls:

The accompanying description states that the information security group evaluates detected security anomalies and determines whether they are security events. However, on July 1, 20XX, the company engaged a third-party security service to perform the evaluations. The description does not disclose this significant change to the system during the period.

### Description Does Not Disclose That Entity Uses a Supplier

4.73 The following is an example of a separate paragraph that would be added to the practitioner's report when the entity has not disclosed the existence of a supplier, the functions it performs, and other related matters:

Example Entity uses a cloud service provider to host the software used in its production line; however, the system description omits disclosure of the use of this third-party provider and the functions it performs.

## Illustrative Separate Paragraph: Material Deficiencies in the Effectiveness of Controls

4.74 Chapter 3 presents several situations in which the practitioner would determine that controls were not effective, in all material respects. Examples of separate paragraphs that may be added to the practitioner's reports are presented in the subsequent paragraphs of this section.

4.75 The following is an example of a separate paragraph that may be added, before the opinion paragraph, to the practitioner's report if the practitioner concludes that controls were not effective due to a design deficiency:

The accompanying description of ABC Entity's system states on page 8 that ABC Entity's system supervisor makes changes to the software system that controls heat treatment of castings only if the changes are authorized, tested, and documented. The procedures, however, do not include a requirement for approval of a change before the change is placed into operation. As a result, controls were not suitably designed throughout the period [date] to [date] to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives related to physical specifications were achieved based on CC8.1, The entity authorizes, designs, develops or acquires, configures, documents, tests, approves, and implements changes to infrastructure, data, software, and procedures to meet its objectives.

4.76 The practitioner may conclude that controls, although appropriately designed, were not operating as designed. The following is an example of a separate paragraph that could be added to the practitioner's report when the practitioner determines that controls were not operating as designed:

ABC Entity states in the description of its system that the director of IT may approve emergency changes to the system without receiving a written request for such changes, if the changes are documented within 48 hours after implementation into production. However, as noted on page 155 of the description of tests of controls and the results thereof, controls related to the authorization of emergency changes to the environmental control system were not consistently performed throughout the period [date] to [date]. As a result, controls were not effective in providing reasonable assurance that the principal system objective "finished products requiring cold storage are stored at specified temperatures until shipped" was not achieved.
