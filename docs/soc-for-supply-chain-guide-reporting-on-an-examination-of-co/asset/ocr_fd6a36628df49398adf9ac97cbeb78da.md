**2.77** When an entity has a more formal process for preparation, an understanding of the entity's process and controls for preparing the description may assist the practitioner in

+   a. identifying possible sources of material misstatement,
+   
+   b. determining the likelihood of such misstatements, and
+   
+   c. designing procedures to evaluate the description and the effectiveness of controls.

**2.78** However, when an entity has an informal preparation process, the practitioner is unlikely to perform extensive procedures to obtain an understanding of that process; instead, the practitioner is likely to focus on procedures to obtain evidence about whether the description presents the system that was designed and implemented in accordance with the description criteria.

# Effectiveness of Controls

**2.79** Entity management would usually be unable to make an assertion about the effectiveness of controls without the design, implementation, and operation of activities to monitor control effectiveness. An entity's monitoring activities, and the reports generated from those activities, enable entity management to periodically or continuously monitor control effectiveness. An understanding of the entity's process for identifying and assessing the risks that would prevent the entity from achieving its principal system objectives and for designing and implementing controls to address those risks also may assist the practitioner in identifying deficiencies in the design of controls. Therefore, the practitioner usually focuses on understanding entity management's process for assessing the effectiveness of controls.

**2.80** In addition to the performance of monitoring activities, entity management may consider the results of the internal audit function's control evaluations when assessing control effectiveness. Entity management may also periodically perform specific procedures to assess the effectiveness of controls through self-assessment programs and functions that are responsible for testing the effectiveness of controls. In most cases, entity management will use a combination of the various assessment techniques. It will also document the performance of such monitoring activities, which permits the practitioner to inspect the documentation as part of obtaining an understanding of the effectiveness of control within the system. The practitioner's procedures to obtain the understanding may also assist the practitioner in identifying deficiencies in the design of controls.

# Procedures to Obtain the Understanding

**2.81** The practitioner's procedures to obtain an understanding of the entity's system may include the following, usually in some combination:

+   a. Making inquiries of entity management, those charged with governance, and others within the entity who, in the practitioner's judgment, may have relevant information
+   
+   b. Observing operations and inspecting documents, reports, and printed and electronic records of transaction processing
