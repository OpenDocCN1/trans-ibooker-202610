Consideration of whether entity management uses a recognized security framework for managing its system processes and controls (for example, the National Institute of Standards and Technology's "Framework for Improving Critical Infrastructure Cybersecurity" [NIST cybersecurity framework], the Consortium for Information and Software Quality [CISQ] standard for information and software reliability and security, or International Organization for Standardization and International Electrotechnical Commission [ISO/IEC] Standards 27001 and 27002) as part of its assessment and entity management process

+   iv. Consideration of whether the risk assessment also takes into account the risk of fraud, such as entity management override of identified controls at the entity, misappropriation of assets by entity personnel, creation by entity personnel of false or misleading documents or records, and inappropriate physical and logical access to the system and the underlying infrastructure through social engineering attacks or similar measures

+   b. Assesses the completeness and accuracy of entity management's identification of risks that threaten the achievement of the entity's principal system objectives

+   c. Evaluates the linkage between the controls stated in the description and those risks

+   d. Evaluates the adequacy of precision and reliability of the controls as designed, based on the risks they are meant to mitigate

# 3.74 When evaluating whether controls are suitably designed, the practitioner may consider the following:

+   a. The frequency or timing of the occurrence or performance of the control

+   b. The authority and competence of the individual responsible for performing the control

+   c. The tasks within the control being performed and the precision and sensitivity of those tasks (for example, the results of reviews and related follow-up activities)

+   d. Evidence that contradicts the assertion that the control is functioning as designed, such as the rate of system incidents identified related to the control

+   e. Whether the control, when considered with other controls, addresses each of the risks that threaten the achievement of the principal system objectives based on the trust services criteria to which it relates
