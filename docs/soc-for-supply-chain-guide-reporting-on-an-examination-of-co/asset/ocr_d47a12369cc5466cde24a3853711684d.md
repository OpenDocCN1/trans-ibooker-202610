3.100 In addition to procedures performed to directly test the operating effectiveness of a control, the practitioner may also perform procedures to obtain evidence that might contradict management's assertion that one or more controls functioned to prevent or detect errors or fraud. For example, the practitioner might obtain a listing of the system incidents identified throughout the period and compare the vulnerabilities exploited to the controls implemented to identify control deficiencies in the related control activities. This testing may identify deficiencies in specific controls designed to prevent or detect those incidents in a timely manner, permitting the practitioner to evaluate the effectiveness of the specific controls.

# ## Testing Review Controls

3.101 As part of evaluating and testing the design of a review control, the practitioner may need to obtain a sufficient understanding and document conclusions about the following matters:

+   - How the control is performed, including the precision with which it is performed and the specific steps involved in executing the review
+   - What the control owner considered when performing the review
+   - The criteria or thresholds used to trigger further investigation or other follow-up
+   - The steps involved in investigating and resolving matters identified by the review

3.102 The practitioner's test of the precision of each review control may include evaluating the same aspects of the control to determine whether the control operated the same way each time it was tested. The practitioner may need to determine whether the evidence gathered through the tests performed demonstrates that the review control consistently identified appropriate items for follow-up and that matters identified for investigation were resolved in a timely manner. Without documented instances of the review control identifying appropriate items for follow-up, the practitioner may not be able to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence that the review control operated as designed.
