
## Other Matters Related to the Practitioner’s Report

### Emphasis-of-Matter Paragraphs and Other-Matter Paragraphs

4.77 The practitioner may consider it necessary to draw users' attention to the following:

- a. Matters appropriately presented or disclosed by entity management's description, assertion, or other accompanying information that, in the practitioner's professional judgment, are of such importance that they are fundamental to users' understanding of the system (emphasis-of-matter paragraph)

- b. Matters other than those presented or disclosed by entity management that are relevant to users' understanding of a SOC for Supply Chain engagement, the practitioner's responsibilities, or the practitioner's report (other-matter paragraph)

4.78 The practitioner may draw users' attention to such matters by including an emphasis-of-matter paragraph or other-matter paragraph, as applicable, in the practitioner's report. The practitioner may adapt and apply the guidance in AU-C section 706, Emphasis-of-Matter Paragraphs and Other-Matter Paragraphs in the Independent Auditor's Report.6 The following is an example of an emphasis-of-matter paragraph for a situation in which the entity experienced a significant operating disruption after the examination period:

As described on page X of "Other Information Provided by Example Entity," after the period covered by the examination, Example Entity's data center system was flooded and rendered inoperable for a period of two weeks by a severe storm in January 20XX.

### Distribution of the Report by Management

4.79 When engaged by the entity, the practitioner provides the report to entity management, and entity management distributes the report to the parties to whom use of the report is restricted. A practitioner is not responsible for controlling a client's distribution of a restricted-use report.

4.80 When establishing the terms of the engagement, the practitioner's understanding with the engaging party may include the fact that the use of the SOC for Supply Chain report will be restricted to the parties identified in the report. In addition, the practitioner may consider informing the engaging party that restricted-use reports are not intended for distribution to nonspecified parties. In addition, the practitioner may obtain from the engaging party an agreement that the engaging party and the specified parties will not distribute the report to parties other than those identified in the report.

### Practitioner’s Recommendations for Improving Controls

4.81 Although it is not the objective of a practitioner's engagement, a practitioner may develop recommendations to improve an entity's controls. The practitioner and entity management agree on whether and how such recommendations will be communicated. Typically, the practitioner includes this information in a separate written communication provided only to entity management.

### Other Information Not Covered by the Practitioner's Report

4.82 Entity management may include, in a separate section of the report or as an attachment to the description, other information that is not covered by the practitioner's report. Typically, this is information that is beyond the scope of the examination but that entity management wishes to communicate to intended users. Such information may be prepared by entity management or by another party. For example, entity management may want to include other information, such as the following, in the SOC for Supply Chain report:

- a. Future plans for new systems or system conversions
- b. Other services provided by the entity that are not included in the scope of the engagement
- c. Qualitative information, such as marketing claims, that may not be objectively measurable
- d. Entity management's expected future plans to correct deviations identified by the practitioner
- e. A report comparing the entity's performance to its commitments to customers per service level agreements or a newsletter containing information about events at the entity
- f. A description of a subsequent event that does not affect the functions and processing performed by the entity during the period covered by the practitioner's report but that may be of interest to intended users

4.83 Paragraph .57 of AT-C section 205 indicates that if, prior to or after the release of the practitioner's report, the practitioner is willing to permit the inclusion of the practitioner's report in a document that contains the description of the entity's system or entity management's assertion and other information, the practitioner should read the other information to identify the following:

- a. Material inconsistencies with the description of the entity's system, entity management's assertion, or the practitioner's report
- b. A material misstatement of fact in the other information, the description of the entity's system, entity management's assertion, or the practitioner's report (Other information may bring to light a material misstatement of fact in the description, assertion, or in the practitioner's report that the practitioner did not identify when evaluating whether
    - i. the description presents the system that was designed and implemented throughout the period in accordance with the description criteria,
    - ii. controls were suitably designed, or
    - iii. controls were operating effectively.)

4.84 Paragraph .57 of AT-C section 205 indicates that if a material misstatement of fact or a material inconsistency exists, the practitioner should discuss the matter with entity management. The practitioner would ordinarily request that entity management correct or delete the other information.

4.85 If entity management refuses to correct or delete the other information containing a material inconsistency or a material misstatement of fact, paragraph .A67 of AT-C section 205 identifies the following examples of further actions the practitioner may take:

- a. Requesting the appropriate party or parties to consult with a qualified third party, such as the appropriate party's legal counsel
- b. Obtaining legal advice about the consequences of different courses of action
- c. If required or permissible, communicating with third parties (for example, a regulator)
- d. Describing the material inconsistency in the practitioner's report
- e. Withdrawing from the engagement, when withdrawal is possible under applicable laws and regulations

4.86 The following is an example of a separate paragraph that would be added to the practitioner's report to identify other information provided by the entity and to disclaim an opinion on it:

The information attached to the description entitled "Other Information Provided by Example Entity" is presented by Example Entity management to describe the entity's optional maintenance services and is not a part of the entity's description of its server manufacturing system during the period June 1, 20X0, to May 31, 20X1. Information about Example Entity's optional maintenance services has not been subjected to the procedures applied in the examination and, accordingly, we express no opinion on it.

### Illustrative Report

4.87 Although this guide specifies the information to be included in a description of an entity's system, it does not prescribe a specific format for a SOC for Supply Chain report. Entities and practitioners may organize and present the required information in a variety of formats.

4.88 Appendix D presents an illustrative report that includes an unmodified opinion on the description and on control effectiveness. The headings in that report are optional.

### Preparing a SOC for Supply Chain Report in a Design-Only Examination

4.89 Paragraph 1.41 discusses certain aspects of a design-only examination. When preparing a report on a design-only examination, certain of the elements in table 4-3 would be tailored to refer specifically to the subject matters addressed in that examination. Specifically, the practitioner's opinion would be revised to address whether

- a. the description of the entity's system as of the specified date was presented in accordance with the description criteria and
- b. implemented controls stated in the description were suitably designed to achieve the entity's principal system objectives as of the specified date, if the controls operated effectively.

4.90 Because a design-only report is for a specified date rather than for a period of time, the practitioner would refer to that specified date in the report.
