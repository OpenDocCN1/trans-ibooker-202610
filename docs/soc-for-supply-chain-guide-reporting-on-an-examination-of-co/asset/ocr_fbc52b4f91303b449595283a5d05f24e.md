# Using Sampling to Select Items to Be Tested

3.121 If a control operates frequently, the practitioner may consider whether to use audit sampling when testing the operating effectiveness of the control. When determining the extent of testing needed and considering whether sampling is appropriate, the practitioner should consider (a) the characteristics of the population of control to be tested, including the nature of the control; (b) whether the population is made up of homogenous items; (c) the frequency of the control's application; and (d) the expected deviation rate. AICPA Audit Guide Audit Sampling may be useful to the practitioner when performing sampling.

3.122 Before deciding to use sampling in a SOC for Supply Chain examination, the practitioner should consider whether sampling is an appropriate strategy for testing a control. The following are examples of situations in which the practitioner might decide that sampling would not be appropriate:

+   a. Due to the design of one or more systems, it may not be possible to give every item in the population a chance of being selected for the sample.
+   b. The practitioner may determine that a 100% test of the control using data analytics is necessary because even a one-time failure of the control could result in a material deficiency in the effectiveness of controls.
+   c. The practitioner may conclude that it is more efficient and more effective to perform a 100% test of the data evidencing the effective operation of the control than selecting and testing a sample.

3.123 In such circumstances, the practitioner needs to consider whether the assumptions for sample-based testing have been met in applying professional judgment regarding the extent of testing.

# Selecting Items to Be Tested

3.124 For tests of controls using sampling, the practitioner determines the tolerable rate of deviation and uses that rate to determine the number of items to be selected for a particular sample.

3.125 When selecting sample items, the practitioner's selection should result in a sample that is representative of the population covering the reporting period, as addressed in paragraph .31b of AT-C section 205. Random selection of items represents one means of obtaining such samples.
