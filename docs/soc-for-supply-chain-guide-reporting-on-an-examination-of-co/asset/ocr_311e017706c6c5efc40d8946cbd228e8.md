+   a. identifying the controls designed to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria.
+   b. assessing the effectiveness of controls, which are necessary to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria.

2.72 Among other things, obtaining an understanding of the entity's system and relevant controls assists the practitioner in the following:

+   a. Determining the appropriateness of the subject matter, including the system being examined, the boundaries of the system, and how the system interfaces with other entity systems
+   b. Assessing whether the description of the entity's system presents the system that has been designed and implemented in accordance with the description criteria

2.73 As required by paragraph .15 of AT-C section 205, the practitioner should also obtain an understanding of the entity's processes and procedures for preparing the description of the entity's system in accordance with the description criteria. This includes evaluating the design of controls relevant to the achievement of the entity's system objectives and determining whether they have been implemented by performing procedures in addition to inquiry of the entity personnel responsible for the preparation of the description.

2.74 If the entity has an internal audit function, that function may be a key component of internal control over the system being examined. In such situations, the practitioner's understanding of the entity's system should include the following:

+   a. The nature of the internal audit function's responsibilities and how the internal audit function fits into the entity's organizational structure
+   b. The activities performed or to be performed by the internal audit function as it relates to the entity

2.75 The practitioner's responsibilities when an entity has an internal audit department are discussed further beginning in paragraph 2.112.

# Description of the System

2.76 The processes and controls an entity uses to prepare the description may vary widely and are usually affected by the size and complexity of the entity. For this reason, entities may have either informal or formal processes to prepare the description. A small entity that prepares only one description per year, for example, is likely to have an informal process in which a few employees with personal knowledge of the system's operation are assigned responsibility for drafting the description and the draft is reviewed by senior management. A larger entity with many interrelated products and multiple descriptions that address manufacturing systems that span many functional units is likely to have a more formal process. Such a process is likely to include a project manager who coordinates preparation of the description by the different functional areas and the review of the description by key executives across the organization.
