# Materiality Considerations When Evaluating Deficiencies in the Effectiveness of Controls

**3.143** The practitioner considers materiality when evaluating whether the effects of identified control deficiencies, individually or in the aggregate, are material with respect to the opinion on control effectiveness. As discussed in chapter 1, the practitioner considers the effect of identified control deficiencies on the entity’s ability to provide reasonable assurance of achieving the principal system objectives based on the applicable trust services criteria. If the practitioner concludes that the effects of identified control deficiencies are likely to be material to the achievement of the principal system objectives based on the applicable trust services criteria, the practitioner would modify the opinion on control effectiveness.

**3.144** When evaluating the materiality of a control deficiency, materiality is considered in the context of qualitative factors and, when applicable, quantitative factors. Qualitative factors the practitioner may consider include the following:

+   a. *Relevance of a control to the achievement of a specific principal system objective based on the applicable trust services criteria.* Not all controls that have been implemented need to be considered if the applicable trust services criteria are met through the application of other controls. As an example, assume an entity mirrors data to a data center located in another city and creates tapes of the data as a secondary backup. These tapes are stored at a third location. Data written to the backup tapes is encrypted. The entity has identified the encryption of the tape as a control; however, the entity has not identified physical security controls over the tape storage location in its description because entity management concluded the following:

+   i. The risk that both the primary data center and the mirror site are simultaneously destroyed is remote.
+   
+   ii. Encryption of the data on the tapes, in conjunction with effective controls over the encryption process by entity management, is sufficient.

In this example, physical access controls over the tape storage location are unlikely to be material or relevant because controls over the encryption of the tapes prevent unauthorized access. Accordingly, a control deficiency in physical access controls is likely to be immaterial to the practitioner's conclusions about whether backup controls are suitably designed and operating effectively.

+   b. *Alignment between the processes and controls stated in the description and the underlying system controls implemented by the entity.* If the description includes a particular control, it is likely that intended users will presume that the control is material for the purposes of the SOC for Supply Chain examination. Similarly, users are likely to expect that such controls, individually or in combination with other controls, support the processes and controls stated in the description. For this reason, users would ordinarily expect the practitioner to test and evaluate those controls as part of the evaluation of control effectiveness.
