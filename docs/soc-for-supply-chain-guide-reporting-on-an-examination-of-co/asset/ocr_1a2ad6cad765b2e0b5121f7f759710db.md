+   e. State that the engaging party has disclosed to the practitioner all known events subsequent to the period (or point in time) of the subject matter being reported on that would have a material effect on the subject matter or assertion.
+   f. Address other matters as the practitioner deems appropriate.

# Subsequent Events and Subsequently Discovered Facts

3.184 Events or transactions may occur after the period of time covered by the examination, but prior to the date of the practitioner’s report, that could have a significant effect on the description or on the effectiveness of controls. In such circumstances, disclosure of those events and transactions in the description or in entity management’s assertion may be necessary to prevent intended users from being misled.

3.185 The following are examples of events that could affect the description of the entity’s system or entity management’s assertion:

+   a. After the period covered by the examination, entity management discovered that, during the last quarter of that period, the IT security director provided all the programmers with access to the production data files, enabling them to modify data.
+   b. After the period covered by the examination, entity management discovered that a security breach occurred during the period covered by the practitioner’s report.

3.186 Paragraph .48 of AT-C section 205 requires the practitioner to inquire of entity management (and, if different, the engaging party) about whether it is aware of any such events. If such events exist, the practitioner should apply appropriate procedures to obtain evidence regarding the events. For example, the practitioner may obtain evidence by inquiring about and considering information regarding the operating effectiveness of controls by inspecting the following:

+   a. Relevant internal auditors' reports issued during the subsequent period
+   b. Other practitioners' reports issued during the subsequent period
+   c. Relevant regulatory agencies' reports issued during the subsequent period
+   d. Reports on other professional engagements for that entity

3.187 Paragraph .48 of AT-C section 205 does not require the practitioner to perform any procedures regarding the description or the effectiveness of controls after the date of the practitioner’s report. However, paragraph .49 of AT-C section 205 clarifies that the practitioner is responsible for responding appropriately to facts that become known after the date of the practitioner’s report that, had they been known as of the report date, may have caused the practitioner to revise the report.
