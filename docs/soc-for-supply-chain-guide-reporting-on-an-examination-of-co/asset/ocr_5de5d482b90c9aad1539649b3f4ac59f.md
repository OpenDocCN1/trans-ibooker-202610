3.30 When evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about risks that may have a significant effect on the entity's ability to achieve its principal system objectives, the practitioner may perform a combination of the following procedures:

+   - Obtaining an understanding of management's risk assessment process as it relates to the trust services criteria
+   - Obtaining the results of management's risk assessment process and comparing it to the disclosures about such risks
+   - Comparing management's evaluation of such risks to the practitioner's risk assessment

# Disclosures About Inputs to and Components of the System

3.31 Description criterion DC5 requires entity management to include in the description relevant information about the system the entity uses to produce, manufacture, or distribute the products, including the (a) components of the system, (b) significant inputs used by the system (raw materials and other inputs), and (c) boundaries of the system, when necessary to prevent users from misunderstanding the system being described. Description criterion DC5 also contains implementation guidance that is intended to assist practitioners when evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about inputs to and components of the system.

3.32 When evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about inputs to and components of the system, the practitioner may perform a combination of the following procedures:

+   a. Reading sample contracts with suppliers (for example, contract templates or a selection of contracts) and associated performance or service level agreements and other documentation to understand the nature of the inputs provided by suppliers
+   b. Reading policy and procedure manuals, system documentation, flowcharts, narratives, asset management records, and other system documentation to understand the entity's use of technology, including its embedded software, robotic devices, applications, infrastructure, network architecture, use of mobile devices, use of cloud technologies, and the types of external party access or connectivity to the system to understand IT policies and procedures and to understand controls over data loss prevention, access provisioning and deprovisioning, user identification and authentication, data destruction, system event monitoring and detection, and backup procedures
