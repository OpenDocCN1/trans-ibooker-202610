3.66 Paragraph .45 of AT-C section 205 requires the practitioner to accumulate description misstatements identified during the engagement, other than those that are clearly trivial, to evaluate whether, individually or in the aggregate, those misstatements are material when forming the practitioner's opinion.

3.67 If the practitioner determines that the effects of identified description misstatements, individually or in the aggregate, are material with respect to the description, based on consideration of materiality as discussed beginning in paragraph 3.60 the practitioner should modify the opinion on the description. When modifying the opinion, the practitioner's understanding of the nature and cause of the description misstatements enables the practitioner to determine how to appropriately modify the opinion. Chapter 4 discusses modifications of the practitioner's report.

# Considering Whether the Description Is Misstated or Otherwise Misleading

3.68 Paragraph .60 of AT-C section 205 requires the practitioner to evaluate whether the description is misleading within the context of the engagement based on the evidence obtained. Because a description in accordance with the description criteria is designed to meet the common informational needs of intended users, this paragraph focuses on whether there are additional disclosures, beyond those called for by the description criteria, that are necessary to prevent intended users from being misled about the system described in the description. For example, if a processing facility was destroyed by fire shortly after the examination but before the issuance of the practitioner's report, disclosure of that event may be helpful to intended users.

3.69 If the practitioner believes additional disclosures beyond those required by the criteria are necessary, the practitioner may ask the responsible party to amend the description. Such additional disclosures may be present in the description (in which case, they are subject to the practitioner's examination procedures) or as other information. The practitioner's responsibility for other information presented in a SOC for Supply Chain report is discussed beginning at paragraph 4.82. If the responsible party refuses to add the disclosures, the practitioner should consider the effect on the practitioner's report, including modifying the opinion on the description, adding explanatory language to the practitioner's report, restricting the use of the practitioner's report to those with more comprehensive understanding of the issue, or withdrawing from the engagement.
