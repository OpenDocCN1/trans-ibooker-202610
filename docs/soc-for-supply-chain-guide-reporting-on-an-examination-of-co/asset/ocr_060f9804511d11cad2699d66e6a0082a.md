Consequently, the practitioner's evaluation of the suitability of the criteria depends on whether the entity's principal system objectives are reasonable under the circumstances. The practitioner should understand the preliminary principal system objectives identified by entity management. The principal system objectives are those that are relevant to the trust services category or categories addressed by the examination and that could reasonably be expected to influence the relevant decisions of the intended users of the report. In addition, early evaluation can help the practitioner identify potential risks to the successful completion of the engagement and establish a more thorough understanding of the engagement scope and risks prior to engagement acceptance.

2.46 An entity's principal system objectives generally include those that address customers' needs and expectations and usually relate to (a) meeting the product's performance specifications and requirements for production, manufacturing, and distribution processes and (b) conforming with other matters, including standards and contractual or other commitments related to a product, such as its availability. For example, entity management may identify a principal system objective for commitments made to customers about the availability of its products in the quantities and at the times agreed on with customers, when such commitments are common to most customers. If, however, entity management makes a different commitment about product availability to an individual customer, that system objective ordinarily would not be disclosed in the description because information about that specific customer commitment is unlikely to be relevant to intended users.

2.47 When considering whether the principal system objectives established by management, and identified in the description, are reasonable in the circumstances, the practitioner may consider the following questions:

+   - Do the principal system objectives relate to the types of risks that the system presents to intended users to which controls at the entity could reasonably be expected to relate (for example, cybersecurity risks to the user that are affected by logical access controls to prevent or detect unauthorized access to the system)?
+   - Are the principal system objectives complete? An examination based on a complete set of principal system objectives can provide intended users with the information they need to assess the effect of the entity's controls on the risks associated with doing business with the entity. However, the practitioner ordinarily will not be able to determine how the entity's controls specifically address the risks specific to each intended user; accordingly, the practitioner cannot consider completeness from the perspective of individual intended users. Instead, individual intended users are expected to assess whether the principal system objectives disclosed in the description included those that are relevant to them.

2.48 If the practitioner believes the principal system objectives identified by entity management and stated in the description do not appear reasonable, the practitioner may need to discuss the matter with entity management. If entity management is unwilling to revise the description of its principal system objectives, the practitioner may refuse to accept the engagement.
