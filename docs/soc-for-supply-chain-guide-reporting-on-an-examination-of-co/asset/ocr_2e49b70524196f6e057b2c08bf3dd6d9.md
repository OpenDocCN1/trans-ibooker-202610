# Modifications to the Practitioner’s Opinion

4.42 Paragraph .68 of AT-C section 205 requires the practitioner to modify the opinion when either of the following circumstances exist and, in the practitioner's professional judgment, the effect of the matter is or may be material:

+   a. The practitioner is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence to conclude that the subject matter is in accordance with (or based on) the criteria, in all material respects.
+   b. The practitioner concludes, based on evidence obtained, that the subject matter is not in accordance with (or based on) the criteria, in all material respects.

4.43 In applying paragraphs .68–.69 of AT-C section 205 to the SOC for Supply Chain examination, the practitioner's opinion should be modified, and the practitioner's report should include a description of the matters giving rise to the modification, if any of the following apply:

+   a. The practitioner concludes, based on the evidence obtained, that the description, taken as a whole, does not present the system designed and implemented throughout the period in accordance with the description criteria, in all material respects.
+   b. The practitioner concludes, based on evidence obtained, that controls were not effective throughout the specified period to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria, in all material respects.
+   c. The practitioner is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence.

4.44 The objective of including a description of each of the matters giving rise to the modification is to enable intended users to consider the effects of identified description misstatements, control deficiencies, and control deviations when making their own risk assessments. Materiality considerations related to the description are discussed beginning in paragraph 3.63, and considerations related to the effectiveness of controls are discussed beginning in paragraph 3.143.
