3.23 The practitioner may obtain information that causes him or her to believe that the principal system objectives stated in the description are misstated, either because a principal system objective is omitted or because the disclosed objectives are inaccurate or misleading. Because the principal system objectives stated in the description are necessary for intended users to understand how control effectiveness was evaluated, a misstatement of one or more principal system objectives is likely to affect the practitioner's opinion on control effectiveness.

3.24 Assume, for example, that an entity states in its principal system objectives that goods are produced on a production line that meets specific security requirements. However, only half of the product models are produced on that line, and therefore, only half of the product models require that level of secure production. Because the entity did not identify the differing system objectives for the different product models, and because the entity did not design, implement, and operate controls on the second production line, the practitioner may conclude that the entity's principal system objectives are misstated. In such a situation, the practitioner would discuss the matter with entity management. If entity management is unwilling to revise the principal system objectives to address the practitioner's concerns, the practitioner should evaluate the effect on his or her opinion on whether the description is presented in accordance with the description criteria. In such circumstances, it is likely that the practitioner would express a qualified opinion on the description.

## Disclosures About System Incidents

3.25 Description criterion DC3 requires entity management to include in the description certain information related to system incidents that were the result of controls that were ineffective or otherwise resulted in a significant failure in the achievement of one or more principal system objectives. Accordingly, the description would include the following information about each incident:

- a. Nature of each incident
- b. Timing surrounding the incident
- c. Extent (or effect) of the incident and its mitigation and remediation

3.26 Description criterion DC3 contains implementation guidance that is intended to assist practitioners when evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about system incidents. The following is an example of disclosures about an identified system incident:

During the period under examination, the Company experienced an incident in which an online intruder gained access to the server used to store and configure the embedded software used in its widgets. Through a previously unknown operating system vulnerability in the server used to update a supplier's software, the intruder made unauthorized changes to the software and configuration parameters to be loaded in the widgets. The attack was detected approximately 66 hours after the unauthorized access was obtained and was remediated within 5 days of detection. The Company ceased the manufacturing of widgets during the 5-day period and recalled all widgets that were loaded with the software from the time of initial unauthorized access through the remediation of the incident. The Company reconciled serial numbers of all recalled and unshipped widgets to manufacturing records without exception. Based on this reconciliation, management believes that all widgets with the unauthorized software have been accounted for. All of these widgets were subsequently destroyed under controlled conditions.

As part of the remediation, the Company reinstalled the operating system and applications from a backup made prior to the incident and applied the software patch provided by the operating system supplier.

3.27 When evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about system incidents, the practitioner may perform a combination of the following procedures:

- Understanding entity management's process for identifying and evaluating the significance of system incidents
- Obtaining the listing of system incidents and their resolution for the examination period from the incident ticketing system and reperforming an independent evaluation of the significance of such incidents
- Inquiring of entity management and inspecting documentation about the resolution of significant incidents that occurred during the prior period
- Inspecting documentation related to the remediation of significant system incidents

3.28 If entity management includes in the description disclosures about identified system incidents as defined in description criterion DC3, such incidents are an indicator that controls were not effective. Accordingly, the practitioner would evaluate the need to modify the opinion on control effectiveness.

## Disclosures About Risks That May Have a Significant Effect on the Entity’s Production, Manufacturing, or Distribution

3.29 Description criterion DC4 requires entity management to include in the description certain information related to risks that may have a significant impact on the entity's ability to achieve its principal system objectives. Description criterion DC4 contains implementation guidance that is intended to assist practitioners when evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about such risks.

3.30 When evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about risks that may have a significant effect on the entity's ability to achieve its principal system objectives, the practitioner may perform a combination of the following procedures:

- Obtaining an understanding of management's risk assessment process as it relates to the trust services criteria
- Obtaining the results of management's risk assessment process and comparing it to the disclosures about such risks
- Comparing management's evaluation of such risks to the practitioner's risk assessment

## Disclosures About Inputs to and Components of the System

3.31 Description criterion DC5 requires entity management to include in the description relevant information about the system the entity uses to produce, manufacture, or distribute the products, including the (a) components of the system, (b) significant inputs used by the system (raw materials and other inputs), and (c) boundaries of the system, when necessary to prevent users from misunderstanding the system being described. Description criterion DC5 also contains implementation guidance that is intended to assist practitioners when evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about inputs to and components of the system.

3.32 When evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about inputs to and components of the system, the practitioner may perform a combination of the following procedures:

- a. Reading sample contracts with suppliers (for example, contract templates or a selection of contracts) and associated performance or service level agreements and other documentation to understand the nature of the inputs provided by suppliers
- b. Reading policy and procedure manuals, system documentation, flowcharts, narratives, asset management records, and other system documentation to understand the entity's use of technology, including its embedded software, robotic devices, applications, infrastructure, network architecture, use of mobile devices, use of cloud technologies, and the types of external party access or connectivity to the system to understand IT policies and procedures and to understand controls over data loss prevention, access provisioning and deprovisioning, user identification and authentication, data destruction, system event monitoring and detection, and backup procedures
