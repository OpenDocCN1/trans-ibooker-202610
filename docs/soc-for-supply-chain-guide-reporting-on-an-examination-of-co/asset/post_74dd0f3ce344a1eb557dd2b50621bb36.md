## Performing Risk Assessment Procedures

2.70 Paragraph .14 of AT-C section 205 requires a practitioner to obtain an understanding of the subject matter and other engagement circumstances sufficient to

- a. enable the practitioner to identify and assess the risks of material misstatement in the subject matter and
- b. provide a basis for designing and performing procedures to respond to the assessed risks and to obtain reasonable assurance to support the practitioner's opinion on the description and on control effectiveness.

### Obtaining an Understanding of the Description of the Entity's System and Control Effectiveness

2.71 The practitioner should obtain an understanding of the subject matter of the examination, specifically the entity's system and the related processes and procedures for

- a. identifying the controls designed to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria.
- b. assessing the effectiveness of controls, which are necessary to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria.

2.72 Among other things, obtaining an understanding of the entity's system and relevant controls assists the practitioner in the following:

- a. Determining the appropriateness of the subject matter, including the system being examined, the boundaries of the system, and how the system interfaces with other entity systems
- b. Assessing whether the description of the entity's system presents the system that has been designed and implemented in accordance with the description criteria

2.73 As required by paragraph .15 of AT-C section 205, the practitioner should also obtain an understanding of the entity's processes and procedures for preparing the description of the entity's system in accordance with the description criteria. This includes evaluating the design of controls relevant to the achievement of the entity's system objectives and determining whether they have been implemented by performing procedures in addition to inquiry of the entity personnel responsible for the preparation of the description.

2.74 If the entity has an internal audit function, that function may be a key component of internal control over the system being examined. In such situations, the practitioner's understanding of the entity's system should include the following:

- a. The nature of the internal audit function's responsibilities and how the internal audit function fits into the entity's organizational structure
- b. The activities performed or to be performed by the internal audit function as it relates to the entity

2.75 The practitioner's responsibilities when an entity has an internal audit department are discussed further beginning in paragraph 2.112.

### Description of the System

2.76 The processes and controls an entity uses to prepare the description may vary widely and are usually affected by the size and complexity of the entity. For this reason, entities may have either informal or formal processes to prepare the description. A small entity that prepares only one description per year, for example, is likely to have an informal process in which a few employees with personal knowledge of the system's operation are assigned responsibility for drafting the description and the draft is reviewed by senior management. A larger entity with many interrelated products and multiple descriptions that address manufacturing systems that span many functional units is likely to have a more formal process. Such a process is likely to include a project manager who coordinates preparation of the description by the different functional areas and the review of the description by key executives across the organization.

2.77 When an entity has a more formal process for preparation, an understanding of the entity's process and controls for preparing the description may assist the practitioner in

- a. identifying possible sources of material misstatement,
- b. determining the likelihood of such misstatements, and
- c. designing procedures to evaluate the description and the effectiveness of controls.

2.78 However, when an entity has an informal preparation process, the practitioner is unlikely to perform extensive procedures to obtain an understanding of that process; instead, the practitioner is likely to focus on procedures to obtain evidence about whether the description presents the system that was designed and implemented in accordance with the description criteria.

### Effectiveness of Controls

2.79 Entity management would usually be unable to make an assertion about the effectiveness of controls without the design, implementation, and operation of activities to monitor control effectiveness. An entity's monitoring activities, and the reports generated from those activities, enable entity management to periodically or continuously monitor control effectiveness. An understanding of the entity's process for identifying and assessing the risks that would prevent the entity from achieving its principal system objectives and for designing and implementing controls to address those risks also may assist the practitioner in identifying deficiencies in the design of controls. Therefore, the practitioner usually focuses on understanding entity management's process for assessing the effectiveness of controls.

2.80 In addition to the performance of monitoring activities, entity management may consider the results of the internal audit function's control evaluations when assessing control effectiveness. Entity management may also periodically perform specific procedures to assess the effectiveness of controls through self-assessment programs and functions that are responsible for testing the effectiveness of controls. In most cases, entity management will use a combination of the various assessment techniques. It will also document the performance of such monitoring activities, which permits the practitioner to inspect the documentation as part of obtaining an understanding of the effectiveness of control within the system. The practitioner's procedures to obtain the understanding may also assist the practitioner in identifying deficiencies in the design of controls.

### Procedures to Obtain the Understanding

2.81 The practitioner's procedures to obtain an understanding of the entity's system may include the following, usually in some combination:

- a. Making inquiries of entity management, those charged with governance, and others within the entity who, in the practitioner's judgment, may have relevant information
- b. Observing operations and inspecting documents, reports, and printed and electronic records of transaction processing
