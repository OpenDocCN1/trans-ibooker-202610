# Accepting a Change in the Terms of the Examination

**2.60** After the engagement agreement is executed but prior to the com- pletion of the engagement, entity management may communicate a desire to change the scope of the engagement. For example, a change in scope may oc- cur when there is a change in the trust services category or categories to be addressed by the examination; in the product, production line, or production facility to be addressed by the examination; in the boundaries of the system be- ing examined; or in the components of the system covered by the examination.

**2.61** When entity management requests a change in the scope of the en- gagement, paragraph .29 of AT-C section 105 states that the practitioner should not agree to the change in the terms of the engagement unless there is reason- able justification for the change. Examples of situations in which there may be reasonable justification for a change include the following:

+   * *a.* Misunderstanding concerning the nature and scope of the exami- nation originally requested.
+   
+   * *b.* Change in the informational needs of intended users.
+   
+   * *c.* Identification of additional system components, principal system objectives, or expansion of the boundaries of the system to be in- cluded in the description to enhance the presentation of the descrip- tion.
+   
+   * *d.* Determination that certain system components, which were pre- liminarily identified as part of the system, are not relevant to the products produced, manufactured, or distributed.
+   
+   * *e.* Determination that the system the entity uses to manufacture cer- tain products addressed in the description is not relevant to in- tended users.
+   
+   * *f.* For an initial engagement, management's discovery, as a result of the practitioner's initial planning, that it has not been retaining evidence supporting the functioning of several key controls. As a result, management requests that the starting date of the exam- ination period be shifted back by three months in order for it to correct its evidence-retention practices.
