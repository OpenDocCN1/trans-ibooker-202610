# Description Does Not Disclose That Entity Uses a Supplier

4.73 The following is an example of a separate paragraph that would be added to the practitioner's report when the entity has not disclosed the existence of a supplier, the functions it performs, and other related matters:

Example Entity uses a cloud service provider to host the software used in its production line; however, the system description omits disclosure of the use of this third-party provider and the functions it performs.

# Illustrative Separate Paragraph: Material Deficiencies in the Effectiveness of Controls

4.74 Chapter 3 presents several situations in which the practitioner would determine that controls were not effective, in all material respects. Examples of separate paragraphs that may be added to the practitioner's reports are presented in the subsequent paragraphs of this section.

4.75 The following is an example of a separate paragraph that may be added, before the opinion paragraph, to the practitioner's report if the practitioner concludes that controls were not effective due to a design deficiency:

The accompanying description of ABC Entity's system states on page 8 that ABC Entity's system supervisor makes changes to the software system that controls heat treatment of castings only if the changes are authorized, tested, and documented. The procedures, however, do not include a requirement for approval of a change before the change is placed into operation. As a result, controls were not suitably designed throughout the period [date] to [date] to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives related to physical specifications were achieved based on CC8.1, The entity authorizes, designs, develops or acquires, configures, documents, tests, approves, and implements changes to infrastructure, data, software, and procedures to meet its objectives.

4.76 The practitioner may conclude that controls, although appropriately designed, were not operating as designed. The following is an example of a separate paragraph that could be added to the practitioner's report when the practitioner determines that controls were not operating as designed:

ABC Entity states in the description of its system that the director of IT may approve emergency changes to the system without receiving a written request for such changes, if the changes are documented within 48 hours after implementation into production. However, as noted on page 155 of the description of tests of controls and the results thereof, controls related to the authorization of emergency changes to the environmental control system were not consistently performed throughout the period [date] to [date]. As a result, controls were not effective in providing reasonable assurance that the principal system objective "finished products requiring cold storage are stored at specified temperatures until shipped" was not achieved.
