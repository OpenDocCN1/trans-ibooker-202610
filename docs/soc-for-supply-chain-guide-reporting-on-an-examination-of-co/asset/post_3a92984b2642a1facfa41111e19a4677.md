## Characteristics That May Indicate Whether the Description Is Presented in Accordance With the Description Criteria

| Characteristics That May Indicate the Description Is Presented in Accordance With the Description Criteria | Characteristics That May Indicate the Description Is Not Presented in Accordance With the Description Criteria |
|---|---|
| The description includes the significant components of the system the entity has designed and implemented (placed into operation) to produce, manufacture, or distribute the products. | The description states or implies that certain production components exist when they do not. |
| The description does not inadvertently or intentionally omit or distort information that is likely to be relevant to intended users' decisions. | The description states or implies that certain processes and controls were implemented when they were not being performed. |
| The description includes information about each description criterion, to the extent it is relevant to the system being described, without using language that omits or distorts the information. | The description contains statements that cannot be objectively evaluated (for example, unsubstantiated advertising claims). |
| The description is prepared at a level of detail likely to be meaningful to intended users. | The description is prepared at such a high level that it omits significant amounts of information relevant for decision-making by intended users. |
| The characteristics of the presentation, such as the format, are appropriate, given that the description criteria allow for variations in presentation. | Certain characteristics of the presentation of the description are confusing and obscure critical information about the system. |

3.13 Implementation guidance for each criterion, which is presented in supplement A, is intended to assist management and the practitioner when evaluating whether the description is presented in accordance with the description criteria. The guidance presents factors to consider when making judgments about the nature and extent of disclosures called for by each criterion. The implementation guidance does not address all possible situations; therefore, entity management and the practitioner may need to consider other facts and circumstances of the entity and its environment when applying the description criteria.

3.14 In addition to the more specific procedures described in the remainder of this section, the practitioner may perform any of the following procedures, or a combination thereof, to obtain evidence about whether the description presents the system that was designed and implemented in accordance with the description criteria:

- a. Discussing with entity management and other entity personnel the content of entity management's assertion and the description
- b. Reading documents (such as the annual report, board minutes, organization charts, and communications about the security, availability, and processing integrity of the system and the confidentiality or privacy of the information it uses) to understand the entity's risk governance structure and processes, including
  - i. the involvement of board members,
  - ii. the organizational structure that supports the entity's system,
  - iii. the types of threat and vulnerability assessments the entity performs (both internal and external), and
  - iv. the types and frequency of communications made to executive entity management and others about the security, availability, and processing integrity of the system and the confidentiality or privacy of the information it uses
- c. Reading internal audit reports, third-party assessments, audit committee presentations, and other documentation related to the entity's monitoring activities, system incidents, or investigative activities
- d. Reading documents describing laws, regulations, or industry standards relevant to the entity's principal system objectives

## 3.15

In certain circumstances, additional disclosures may be necessary to supplement the description. Entity management's decisions about whether such additional disclosures are necessary, and the practitioner's evaluation of entity management's decisions, involve consideration of whether the disclosures may affect information that is likely to be relevant to the decisions of intended users. Examples of additional disclosures that may be necessary include the following:

- a. Significant interpretations made in applying the description criteria in the specific circumstances of the examination (for example, what constitutes a security event or incident)
- b. Events, depending on their nature and significance, subsequent to the "as of" date or period covered by the description but prior to the delivery of the practitioner's report

## 3.16

If the practitioner identifies description misstatements (including omissions) that the practitioner believes may be material, the practitioner usually asks entity management to revise the description. In most situations, entity management makes the appropriate modifications. If, however, entity management does not appropriately modify the description to address the practitioner's concerns, the practitioner should evaluate whether the misstatements are material and determine the effect of the misstatements on his or her opinion on the description.

## Disclosures Related to the Types of Goods Produced, Manufactured, or Distributed

## 3.17

Description criterion DC1 requires entity management to include in the description certain disclosures about the types of goods produced, manufactured, or distributed by an entity. Description criterion DC1 also contains

implementation guidance that is intended to assist practitioners when evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about the types of goods produced, manufactured, or distributed.

3.18 When evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about the types of goods produced, manufactured, or distributed by an entity, the practitioner may perform a combination of the following procedures:

- a. Reading the entity's annual report, if there is one, to understand the nature of the entity's operations and the types of products produced, manufactured, or distributed for customers and business partners
- b. Reading contracts with customers and business partners (such as performance or service level agreements), marketing materials distributed to customers and business partners or posted on the entity's website, and other available documentation to better understand the specific products produced, manufactured, or distributed to customers
- c. Reading other documents related to the goods produced, manufactured, or distributed by an entity

## Disclosures About the Entity’s Principal System Objectives

3.19 As discussed in chapter 1, "Introduction and Background," entity management is responsible for designing, implementing, and operating a system and related controls to obtain reasonable assurance of achieving the entity's system objectives. A SOC for Supply Chain examination addresses the principal system objectives that are relevant to one or more of the trust services categories.

3.20 When using a SOC for Supply Chain report, intended users need to know the principal system objectives identified by management; therefore, description criterion DC2 requires entity management to disclose the principal system objectives in the description. An entity's principal system objectives generally address the system's ability to support its product performance specifications, commitments, and requirements, and production, manufacturing, or distribution commitments and requirements. The principal system objectives are those that are relevant to the trust services category or categories addressed by the description and likely to affect relevant decisions of intended users of the report.

3.21 Description criterion DC2 contains implementation guidance that is intended to assist practitioners when evaluating the nature and extent of disclosures about principal system objectives. An example of disclosures about an entity's principal system objectives is included in appendix E, "Illustrative SOC for Supply Chain Report (Including Entity Management's Assertion, Accountant's Report, and Illustrative Description of the System)."
