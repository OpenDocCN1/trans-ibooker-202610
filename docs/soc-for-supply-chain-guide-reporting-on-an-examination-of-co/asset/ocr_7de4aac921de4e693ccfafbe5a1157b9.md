2.42 If controls at third parties are necessary, in combination with the entity's controls, to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives are achieved, entity management identifies and assesses the risks of doing business with those third parties. If those risks represent a significant threat to the achievement of the entity's principal system objectives (for example, because of the nature of those parties' access to the system, the provision of critical raw materials or components, or the controls they operate on behalf of the entity), third-party risk and control assessment activities addressing the third parties' activities are necessary to enable entity management to determine whether the processes and controls performed by those third parties effectively mitigate the identified risks. For example, third-party risk and control assessment activities for a supplier may include a combination of the following:

+   - Quality control testing of raw materials, components, or other inputs received
+   - Testing of controls at the supplier by entity personnel or third parties
+   - Holding periodic discussions with supplier personnel and evaluating supplier performance against established service-level objectives and agreements
+   - Making site visits to the supplier to evaluate its processes
+   - Inspecting attestation reports (such as type 2 SOC 2® reports) on the supplier's system
+   - Monitoring external communications, such as complaints from customers, entity communications about system events, communications from regulators, and regulatory activities that relate to the products or services provided by the supplier

When such third-party risk and control assessment activities do not exist or they appear inadequate, it may be difficult for entity management to conclude that it has a reasonable basis for its assertion.

2.43 If the practitioner believes the subject matter is not appropriate (because entity management does not have a reasonable basis for its assertion) or expects that evidence needed to support the opinion is unlikely to be available, AT-C section 105 indicates that the practitioner should not accept the examination.

# Assessing the Suitability and Availability of Criteria

2.44 As discussed in chapter 1, two distinct sets of criteria are used in the SOC for Supply Chain examination: description criteria and trust services criteria. The description criteria in supplement A and the trust services criteria in supplement B were promulgated by the Assurance Services Executive Committee. As discussed in chapter 1, such criteria meet the requirements in paragraph .25bii of AT-C section 105 for criteria that are both suitable and available for use in the examination discussed in this guide.
