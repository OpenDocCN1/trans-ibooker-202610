**3.189** Specific actions to be taken at that point depend on a number of factors, including the time elapsed since the date of the practitioner's opinion and whether issuance of a subsequent report is imminent. Depending on the circumstances, the practitioner may determine that notification of persons currently using or likely to use the practitioner's report is necessary. This may be the case, for example, when

+   a. the SOC for Supply Chain report is not to be relied on because the description, entity management's assertion, or the practitioner's report needs revision or the practitioner is unable to determine whether revision is necessary; and
+   b. issuance of a subsequent practitioner's report is not imminent.

**3.190** If the practitioner believes the event is of such a nature and significance that its disclosure is necessary to prevent intended users from being misled, the practitioner should determine whether information about the event is adequately disclosed in the description or in entity management's assertion. For example, assume that, after the period covered by the examination but prior to the date of the practitioner's report, entity management learns of a security breach. After investigation, entity management determines that the incident stemmed from an otherwise unknown vulnerability in its system; furthermore, that vulnerability existed during the examination period. In this example, the practitioner ordinarily would expect management to disclose the matter in the description and assertion. If it is not, the practitioner's course of action depends on the practitioner's legal and ethical rights and obligations, and the practitioner may consider seeking legal advice before deciding on a course of action. Appropriate actions may include

+   a. disclosing the event (including a description of the nature of the event and its effect on the description, assertion, or report) in the practitioner's report and modifying the related practitioner's opinion; and
+   b. withdrawing from the engagement.

# Subsequent Events Unlikely to Have an Effect on the Practitioner’s Report

**3.191** The practitioner may have determined that the event discovered subsequent to the period covered by the examination would likely have had no effect on the description or on the effectiveness of controls because the underlying situation did not exist until after the period addressed by the SOC for Supply Chain examination. However, the matter may be sufficiently important to warrant disclosure by entity management in its description and, potentially, emphasis by the practitioner in the practitioner's report. The following are examples of such events:

+   a. The entity was acquired by another entity.
+   b. The entity experienced a significant operating disruption or other extraordinary event such as an event caused by weather or other natural disaster.
