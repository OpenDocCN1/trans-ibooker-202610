+   a. The description of the entity's system was not presented in accordance with the description criteria.
+   
+   b. The controls were not effective to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's system objectives stated in the description of the entity's system were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria.

# Defining a Material Misstatement

**2.87** Paragraph .10 of AT-C section 105 defines a misstatement as follows:

A difference between the measurement or evaluation of the subject matter by the responsible party and the proper measurement or evaluation of the subject matter based on the criteria. Misstatements can be intentional or unintentional, qualitative or quantitative, and include omissions. In certain engagements, a misstatement may be referred to as a *deviation*, *exception*, or *instance of noncompliance*.

**2.88** In this guide, the following terms are used when discussing misstatements:

+   - The term *description misstatement* is used when describing differences between the description and the description criteria or omissions in the description.
+   - The term *control deficiency* is used to identify misstatements resulting from controls that were not suitably designed or did not operate effectively.
+   - The term *deviation* is used to identify misstatements resulting from the failure of a control to operate in a specific instance. A deviation may, individually or in combination with other deviations, result in a deficiency.

# Identifying and Assessing Inherent Risks

**2.89** The practitioner's risk assessment procedures begin with the identification and assessment of inherent risks that may affect the description of the system and the effectiveness of controls. Paragraph .A10a of AT-C section 105 defines *inherent risk* as the susceptibility of the subject matter to a material misstatement before consideration of any related controls. Inherent risks related to a practitioner's engagement may include those related to new or changed controls, system changes, significant changes in processing volume, new personnel or significant changes in key management or personnel, new types of transactions, or new products or technologies.

**2.90** Inherent risks may also arise from suppliers with whom the entity does business, fraud, or noncompliance with laws or regulations. For example, fraud risks might include management override of entity controls, misappropriation of customer or supplier assets by entity personnel, and creation by entity personnel of false or misleading documents or records.
