+   b. Assessing the likelihood and magnitude of those risks.
+   c. Understanding the processes and controls the entity has designed, implemented, and operated to mitigate those risks to an acceptable level based on the applicable trust services criteria.
+   d. Designing tests of controls that focus on controls necessary to mitigate the risks that threaten the achievement of the entity's principal system objectives.
+   e. Developing a materiality threshold for evaluating control deviations. (Although there is no requirement in the attestation standards to develop a threshold below which deviations would be considered immaterial, doing so may assist a practitioner in the development of appropriate procedures and in the evaluation of the results of the procedures.)

2.106 For example, the practitioner may believe that, because the entity updates software applications frequently, controls are unlikely to operate effectively if application software changes are not tested prior to implementation in the production environment. Therefore, the practitioner may determine that the extent of evidence needed to support a conclusion that controls over testing software application changes are effective is greater than that to be obtained to support the effectiveness of other controls that are not as significant to the achievement of the entity's principal system objectives (and the practitioner's opinion). In that case, when designing tests of controls over software application change controls, the practitioner would be likely to establish a low acceptable rate of error for such testing.

# Considering Entity-Level Controls

2.107 Entity management designs, implements, and operates controls at the entity level that are necessary to support the achievement of its principal system objectives and affect the operation of system-level control activities. Controls that address the trust services criteria for the control environment component of internal control (CC1.1–1.5) are examples of entity-level controls. Although entity-level controls can also address the achievement of principal system objectives based on the applicable trust services criteria for the communication and information (CC2.1–2.3), risk assessment (CC3.1–3.4), and monitoring (CC4.1–4.2) components of internal control, entity management often addresses those criteria by designing, implementing, and operating controls that are performed at the system level. As an example, assume that the entity performs an enterprise-wide risk assessment and assesses its information security risk and its infrastructure risk at the system level. Because the latter two assessments are likely to be more relevant to the SOC for Supply Chain examination, the practitioner ordinarily devotes more time and attention to obtaining an understanding of those assessments than to understanding the enterprise-wide risk assessment.

2.108 Nevertheless, effective entity-level controls, particularly those designed and implemented to meet the trust services criteria related to the control environment, may enable the practitioner to place greater confidence in the processes and controls the entity has designed, implemented, and operated to provide reasonable assurance that its principal system objectives were achieved.
