# Qualified Opinion

4.50 The practitioner expresses a qualified opinion in the following circumstances:

+   a. The practitioner, having obtained sufficient appropriate evidence, concludes that description misstatements or deficiencies in the effectiveness of controls are, either individually or in the aggregate, material but not pervasive to the description or the effectiveness of controls.

+   b. The practitioner is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence on which to base the opinion but the practitioner has concluded that the possible effects on the subject matter of undetected description misstatements or control deficiencies, if any, could be material but not pervasive to the description or the effectiveness of controls.

This section discusses qualifications because of material misstatements. The section beginning in paragraph 4.57 discusses qualifications because of scope limitations.

4.51 When the practitioner has determined that a qualified opinion is appropriate, the practitioner's report would be modified by doing the following:

+   a. Including, in a separate paragraph before the opinion paragraph, a clear explanation of the matters giving rise to the qualified opinion. When the modification is due to a deficiency in control effectiveness, the practitioner usually identifies the effect of the deficiency on the entity's ability to achieve one or more principal system objectives or to meet one or more of the applicable trust services criteria used to evaluate the achievement of those objectives. Determining which approach to take is usually based on the practitioner's professional judgment related to which disclosure is likely to provide more useful information to the intended users of the report.

+   b. Stating in the opinion paragraph that, except for the effects of the matters giving rise to the modification, the description is presented in accordance with the description criteria and controls were effective to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria, in all material respects.

+   c. Amending the practitioner's responsibility paragraph to state that the practitioner believes that the evidence obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for the practitioner's qualified opinion.
