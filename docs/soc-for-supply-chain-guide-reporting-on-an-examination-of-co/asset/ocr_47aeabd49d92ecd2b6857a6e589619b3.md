2.91 Paragraph .32 of AT-C section 205 states that the practitioner should also consider whether risk assessment and other procedures related to understanding the subject matter indicate the risk of material misstatement due to fraud or noncompliance with laws or regulations. For example, internal fraud risks might include management override of production controls in order to meet production schedules, misappropriation of customer or business partner assets by entity personnel, and creation, by entity personnel, of false or misleading documents or records of production.

2.92 Inherent risks may also include risks derived from customers and suppliers that threaten the achievement of the entity's principal system objectives if not addressed by effective complementary customer controls and complementary supplier controls. Complementary customer controls are discussed further beginning at paragraph 2.127, and complementary supplier controls are discussed beginning at paragraph 2.134.

# Identifying and Assessing Control Risk

2.93 Control risk is the risk that a material misstatement will not be prevented, or detected and corrected, on a timely basis by the entity's controls. After assessing inherent risk, the practitioner should consider the controls the entity has designed and implemented to mitigate those risks at a high level. This enables the practitioner to focus further procedures on those controls that are necessary for the entity to achieve its principal system objectives.
