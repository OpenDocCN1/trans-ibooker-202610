Such tests ordinarily would include determining that changes to the program are not made without being subject to the appropriate program change controls, that the authorized version of the program is used for processing transactions, and that other relevant IT general controls are effective. In instances in which the automated control is configurable, the practitioner should consider what additional evidence, if any, is needed to support his or her opinion and perform any procedures needed to evaluate the configuration. Such procedures may include obtaining an understanding of the configuration process, performing procedures to test the completeness and accuracy of the configuration parameters, and evaluating the controls over access to alter the configuration. If the control is tested in an environment other than the production environment, the practitioner may need to assess the risk that the functionality of the control in the production environment differs from that in the nonproduction environment and perform procedures to determine that the environment being tested matches that of the production environment.

3.118 Automated application controls may be tested only once or a few times if effective IT general controls are present. In such situations, the practitioner considers whether changes to a control made after the testing, but prior to the end of the examination period, would change his or her conclusion regarding the effectiveness of the control and performs additional testing as deemed necessary.

# Testing Superseded Controls

3.119 If (a) the entity makes significant changes to controls during the period, (b) the superseded controls are relevant to the achievement of the entity's principal system objectives based on the applicable trust services criteria, and (c) the practitioner believes that deficiencies in the superseded controls would be relevant to the practitioner's opinion, the practitioner should obtain sufficient appropriate evidence about the design and operating effectiveness of those controls. Such procedures may include, if possible, testing the controls to be superseded before the change. Assume, for example, that during the examination period June 1, 20X0, to May 31, 20X1, an entity replaced a manual control with an automated control on December 15, 20X0. The practitioner would test the manual control for the period from June 1, 20X0, to December 14, 20X0, and the automated control for the period from December 15, 20X0, to May 31, 20X1, taking into consideration the nature of the control and the frequency of its operation.

3.120 If (a) the practitioner is unable to test the superseded controls (for example, because the control does not leave evidence of its operation after a period of time or because the practitioner was engaged after the control was superseded) and (b) the controls are significant to the achievement of the entity's principal system objectives based on the applicable trust services criteria, the practitioner discloses that fact in the description of tests performed and results thereof. In such circumstances, the practitioner should determine the effect on the practitioner's report. If the circumstances result in a material scope limitation, the practitioner should modify the opinion. (See the relevant paragraphs within paragraphs .68–.81 of AT-C section 205 for reporting requirements when the practitioner is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence.)
