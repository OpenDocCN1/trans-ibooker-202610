2.98 When considering materiality, the practitioner typically considers whether identified misstatements in the description or deficiencies in control effectiveness could reasonably be expected to influence decisions made by intended users based on the subject matter. However, if the examination has been designed to meet the informational needs of a specific subset of such intended users (and the practitioner’s report is restricted to those specific users), the practitioner considers the possible effect of such description misstatements or control deficiencies on the decisions that may be made by that specific subset of report users.

2.99 If the practitioner becomes aware, during the conduct of the examination, of information that would have caused the practitioner to have initially determined a different materiality, paragraph .17 of AT-C section 205 requires the practitioner to reconsider materiality. Chapter 3 of this guide discusses materiality considerations during the performance of the examination in further detail.

# Description of the System

2.100 Because the description of the system is a narrative presentation, considering materiality during planning involves some unique considerations. The practitioner needs to understand that the initial description prepared by entity management is ordinarily revised several times during the examination, as the practitioner’s procedures provide further insight into the nature and extent of appropriate disclosures. Therefore, the initial draft of the description of the system often does not reflect what the final description will look like. Nevertheless, during the planning stage, the practitioner may consider certain factors that may affect the nature, timing, and extent of further procedures.

2.101 For instance, a practitioner may determine that any of the following would be considered a misstatement in the description of the system:

+   - Inclusion of misleading or inappropriate information (for example, information that obscures the information required by the description criteria)
+   - Omission of information required by the description criteria (for example, inadequate or incomplete information)
+   - Changes to disclosures made in a previous period without reasonable justification
+   - Misstatements of fact

2.102 When reading the initial draft of the description, the practitioner may compare it to certain expectations related to the nature and extent of disclosures that the practitioner may have developed based on the understanding of the entity’s system. For example, if the entity purchases a significant component from a supplier and the supplier’s controls are necessary, alone or in combination with the entity’s controls, for the entity to achieve its principal system objectives based on the applicable trust services criteria, the practitioner would expect the description to include the appropriate disclosures required by description criterion DC8. Conversely, if there have been no significant changes to the entity’s system and controls for the specified period, the practitioner would not expect to see disclosures required by description criterion DC10.
