## 3.104 The primary types of information produced by the entity and obtained by the practitioner are as follows:

- a. Information used by the entity in the execution of a control (for example, a user access list used by entity personnel in an access review control)
- b. Information generated by the entity in response to ad hoc requests from the practitioner (for example, a request for a population list, such as a population of software changes that the practitioner uses to select a sample of items for testing)

## 3.105 The practitioner may identify information produced by the entity during the practitioner's evaluation of the description of the entity's system or during the evaluation of the effectiveness of the controls included in the description.

## 3.106 Information produced by the entity may be produced

- a. manually or generated by a system.
- b. one time (for example, a population list provided to the practitioner for use in selecting a sample of items to be tested) or may be produced on a recurring basis (for example, a manually prepared exception report prepared on a weekly basis).

## 3.107 Questions to be asked when assessing the reliability of the information produced by the entity may include the following:

- a. Where is the information produced or generated? (For example, the entity's applications or systems, physical devices on the production line, the end product's use in the field, other entity sources such as manually produced reports, or suppliers outside the entity)
- b. How is the information used?
- c. What effect could the information have on customers or business partners?
- d. Is the information located in a controlled IT environment, an ad hoc reporting database, or data warehouse?
- e. Is the information highly structured and complex or relatively straightforward?
- f. Does the information originate from a system already subject to the practitioner's procedures or a system beyond the scope of the practitioner's examination?
- g. What is the basis for the entity's comfort with the reliability of the information?
- h. Were any classes or ranges of data excluded from the information provided by the entity? If so, were those exclusions appropriate?

## 3.108 Determining the nature and extent of evidence needed to assess the reliability of information produced by the entity is a matter of professional judgment. The practitioner may obtain evidence about the reliability of such information when testing controls or may develop specific procedures to address this information. As the importance of the information or the control increases, the persuasiveness of the evidence about the reliability of the information may need to increase.

## 3.109 The following factors are relevant when assessing the reliability of information used in the execution of controls:

- a. The importance of the control in mitigating risks
- b. The risk that one or more principal system objectives would not be achieved if the information produced by the entity is not reliable
- c. The degree to which the effectiveness of the control depends on the completeness and accuracy of the information
- d. The degree to which the control depends on other controls
- e. The precision with which the control is performed (for example, precision of review controls)

## 3.110 The following are examples of procedures the practitioner may perform when evaluating the reliability of various types of information produced by the entity:

Example 1: Information provided by the entity to the practitioner in response to an ad hoc request from the practitioner

The entity provides the practitioner with a system-generated list of new accounts set up during the period. In evaluating the accuracy and completeness of the list of new accounts set up during the period, the practitioner may do the following:

- a. Observe the generation of the list of new accounts set up during the period, confirm that the correct source was queried and that the date range and type of account parameters were accurately entered, and determine whether any exclusions are listed.
- b. Inspect the list for any new accounts with a "created on" date that is outside the date range specified.
- c. Test the IT general controls supporting the system.

Example 2: Information used in the execution of a control

The description of the entity's system states that a list of terminated employees is automatically produced by the Human Resources Management System (HRMS) application on a weekly basis and that access to supporting business applications by terminated employees is removed on the date of termination. In evaluating the accuracy and completeness of the termination report, the practitioner may do the following:

- a. Observe the human resources manager enter the date range and termination parameter into the reporting tool within the production environment of the HRMS application.
- b. Inspect the report for any termination dates outside the date range specified.
- c. Test the IT general controls supporting the HRMS.

Example 3: Population of incidents

The incident recordkeeping application generates a report of all incidents during a period. Before testing a sample of such incidents, the practitioner may inspect the query logic used to generate the report

## Extent of Tests of Controls

### 3.113 The extent of the practitioner's testing refers to the size of the sample tested or the number of observations of a control activity. The extent of testing is based on the practitioner's professional judgment after considering factors such as the following:

- a. The tolerable rate of deviation
- b. The frequency with which the control operates
- c. The relevance and reliability of the evidence that can be obtained to support the conclusion that controls are operating effectively
- d. The length of the testing period
- e. The significance of the control to the achievement of the entity's principal system objectives based on the applicable trust services criteria
- f. The extent to which audit evidence is obtained from tests of other controls that support the achievement of the principal system objectives based on the applicable trust services criteria

### 3.114 The practitioner expresses an opinion on the operating effectiveness of the controls throughout the period covered by the examination; consequently, the sufficiency of the evidence regarding the effectiveness of controls should support that opinion. For example, if a control operates daily, the practitioner would test the operation of the control for a sufficient number of days throughout the period covered by the examination to determine whether the control operated effectively throughout the entire period. The shorter the period over which the control is performed, the less likely the practitioner will be able to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence to express an opinion on the effectiveness of controls.

### 3.115 Evidence about the satisfactory operation of controls in prior periods does not provide evidence of the operating effectiveness of controls during the current period. The practitioner expresses an opinion on the effectiveness of controls throughout the specified period addressed by each examination; therefore, sufficient appropriate evidence about the effectiveness of controls throughout the specified period addressed by the current examination is required for the practitioner to express an opinion.

### 3.116 The practitioner's knowledge of modifications to the practitioner's report or deviations observed in prior engagements may, however, be considered when assessing the risks of material misstatement. Such knowledge may lead the practitioner to increase the extent of testing in the current period. For example, if the practitioner's opinion in the prior year's report was qualified because of a control deficiency arising from the inexperience of the person authorizing user access, the practitioner may decide to increase the number of sample items tested during the current examination to determine whether the control deficiency was effectively corrected.

### 3.117 Generally, IT processing is inherently consistent; therefore, the practitioner may be able to limit the testing of automated controls to one or a few instances of the control's operation. An automated control usually functions consistently unless the program, including the tables, files, or other permanent data used by the program, is changed.
