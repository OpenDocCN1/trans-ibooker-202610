+   a. Discussing the matter with senior entity management (and the engaging party, if different) and other appropriate parties, unless senior entity management is suspected to have committed the fraud
+   b. If the practitioner suspects fraud involving senior entity management, communicating those suspicions to those charged with governance and discussing with them the nature, timing, and extent of procedures necessary to complete the examination
+   c. Requesting that senior entity management (and the engaging party, if different) consult with an appropriately qualified third party, such as the entity's legal counsel or a regulator
+   d. Considering the implications of the matter in relation to other aspects of the engagement, including the practitioner's risk assessment and the reliability of written representations from entity management (and the engaging party, if different)
+   e. Obtaining legal advice about the consequences of different courses of action
+   f. Communicating with third parties (such as a regulator)
+   g. Withdrawing from the engagement

3.165 The actions noted in paragraph 3.164 may also be appropriate in response to identified or suspected noncompliance with laws or regulations relevant to the examination. In addition, the practitioner may decide to describe the matter in a separate paragraph in the practitioner's report, unless the following apply:

+   a. The practitioner is precluded by entity management (or the engaging party, if different) from obtaining sufficient appropriate evidence to evaluate whether noncompliance that may be material to the conclusion about the effectiveness of controls has, or is likely to have, occurred. In this situation, there is a scope limitation that precludes the practitioner from expressing an opinion on control effectiveness. Accordingly, the practitioner would disclaim an opinion.
+   b. The practitioner concludes that the noncompliance results in the failure of the entity's controls to provide reasonable assurance that the entity's principal system objectives were achieved based on the applicable trust services criteria. In this situation, the practitioner expresses a modified opinion.

# Communicating Incidents of Known or Suspected Fraud, Noncompliance With Laws or Regulations, Uncorrected Misstatements, or Internal Control Deficiencies

3.166 In addition to responding to known and suspected fraud and noncompliance with laws or regulations, the practitioner should communicate information regarding those matters, along with information regarding any uncorrected description misstatements or material deficiencies, to the appropriate levels of entity management (and to the engaging party, if different). The practitioner may also consider whether to communicate other matters.
