The MVDR estimator is obtained by minimizing the variance

$$\min_{\mathbf{h}} J=\mathbf{h}'\mathbf{R}_{yy}\mathbf{h}$$

subject to the constraint that $\mathbf{V}'(\Omega_m)\mathbf{h}=1$. This estimator can be derived using the Lagrange multiplier method of transforming the constrained problem to an unconstrained by augmenting the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ into the cost function and minimizing [25]

$$\min_{\mathbf{h}} J=\mathbf{h}'\mathbf{R}_{yy}\mathbf{h}+\lambda(1-\mathbf{V}'(\Omega_m)\mathbf{h})$$

Performing the minimization and substituting for the constraint yields the filter weights as

$$\mathbf{h}_{\mathrm{MVDR}}=\frac{\mathbf{R}_{yy}^{-1}\mathbf{V}(\Omega_m)}{\mathbf{V}'(\Omega_m)\mathbf{R}_{yy}^{-1}\mathbf{V}(\Omega_m)}$$

Substituting this expression into $J$ gives the $MVDR$ spectral estimator at $\Omega_m$ as

$$S_{\mathrm{MVDR}}(\Omega_m)=\frac{\Delta T}{\mathbf{V}'(\Omega_m)\mathbf{R}_{yy}^{-1}\mathbf{V}(\Omega_m)}=\Delta T\mathbf{h}'\mathbf{R}_{yy}\mathbf{h}$$

which implies that the spectral estimate at a given frequency is obtained by scaling the output covariance appropriately at the desired frequency. In practice, MVDR exhibits more resolution than the classical correlation/periodogram estimators, but less than the AR (MEM) estimator. We also note that the peak of MVDR spectral estimate for a narrowband process is proportional to the power, while for the MEM it is proportional to the square of the power. In fact, it can be shown that the MVDR and MEM spectra are related by Burg’s formula: [18]

$$\frac{1}{S_{\mathrm{MVDR}}(\Omega)}=\frac{1}{N_a}\sum_{i=1}^{N_a}\frac{1}{S_{\mathrm{AR}}(\Omega,i)}$$

where $S_{\mathrm{AR}}(\Omega,i)$ is the MEM PSD corresponding to the $i$th-order AR model. Thus, we see that the MVDR spectral estimate averages all of the lower order (less resolution) and higher order AR models to obtain its estimate, thus explaining its lower resolution.
