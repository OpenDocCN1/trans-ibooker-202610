$$x \sim \Pr(x(t)\mid x(t-1),\theta(t-1))$$

$$\theta \sim \Pr(\theta(t)\mid\theta(t-1),x(t-1))$$

$$y \sim \Pr(y(t)\mid x(t),\theta(t)) \tag{5.40}$$

Here, we separate the state transition function into the individual vectors for illustrative purposes, but in reality (as we shall observe), they can be jointly coupled. The key idea is to develop the PF technique to estimate the joint posterior $\Pr(x(t),\theta(t)\mid Y_t)$ relying on the parametric posterior $\Pr(\theta(t)\mid Y_t)$ in the Bayesian decomposition. We will follow the approach outlined in [17, 25] starting with the full posterior distributions and proceeding to the filtering distributions.

Suppose it is possible to sample $N_p$-particles, $\{X_t(i),\Theta_t(i)\}$ for $i=1,\ldots,N_p$ from the joint posterior distribution where we define $X_t:=\{x(0),\ldots,x(t)\}$ and $\Theta_t:=\{\theta(0),\ldots,\theta(t)\}$. Then the corresponding empirical approximation of the joint posterior is given by

$$\widehat{\Pr}(X_t,\Theta_t\mid Y_t)\approx\frac{1}{N_p}\sum_{i=1}^{N_p}\delta(X_t-X_t(i),\Theta_t-\Theta_t(i)) \tag{5.41}$$

and it follows directly that the filtering posterior is given by

$$\widehat{\Pr}(x(t),\theta(t)\mid Y_t)\approx\frac{1}{N_p}\sum_{i=1}^{N_p}\delta(x(t)-x_i(t),\theta(t)-\theta_i(t)) \tag{5.42}$$

Unfortunately, it is not possible to sample directly from the full joint posterior $\Pr(X_t,\Theta_t\mid Y_t)$ at any time $t$. However, one approach to mitigate this problem is by using the importance sampling approach.

Suppose we define a (full) importance distribution, $\mathcal{I}(X_t,\Theta_t\mid Y_t)$ such that $\Pr(X_t,\Theta_t\mid Y_t)>0$ implies $\mathcal{I}(\cdot)>0$, then we define the corresponding importance weight (as before) by

$$W(X_t,\Theta_t)\propto\frac{\Pr(X_t,\Theta_t\mid Y_t)}{\mathcal{I}(X_t,\Theta_t\mid Y_t)} \tag{5.43}$$

From Bayes’ rule, we have that the posterior can be expressed as

$$\Pr(X_t,\Theta_t\mid Y_t)=\frac{\Pr(Y_t\mid X_t,\Theta_t)\times\Pr(X_t,\Theta_t)}{\Pr(Y_t)} \tag{5.44}$$

Thus, if $N_p$-particles, $\{X_t(i),\Theta_t(i)\};\,i=1,\ldots,N_p$, can be generated from the importance distribution

$$\{X_t(i),\Theta_t(i)\}\longrightarrow \mathcal{I}(X_t,\Theta_t\mid Y_t) \tag{5.45}$$
