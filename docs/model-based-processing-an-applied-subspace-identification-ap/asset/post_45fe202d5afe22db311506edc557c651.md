
Analogously, the orthogonal complement projection can be expressed in terms of the oblique projection operator as

$$P_{A \mid B}^{\perp} = P_{A \mid C\otimes B}^{\parallel} \times P_{B}^{\perp} \quad (B.18)$$

Numerically, the oblique projection can be computed again using the LQ-decomposition (see Appendix C).

$$
\begin{bmatrix} B \\ --- \\ C \\ --- \\ A \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} L_{11} & 0 & 0 \\ --- & --- & --- \\ L_{21} & L_{22} & 0 \\ --- & --- & --- \\ L_{31} & L_{32} & L_{33} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} Q'_1 \\ --- \\ Q'_2 \\ --- \\ Q'_3 \end{bmatrix} \quad (B.19)
$$

where the oblique projection of the Row(A) onto Row(C) along the Row(B) is given by

$$P_{A \mid C\otimes B}^{\parallel} = L_{32} \times L_{22}^{-1} \times C = L_{32} \times L_{22}^{-1} \times (L_{21} Q'_1 + L_{22} Q'_2) \quad (B.20)$$

### C.1 Singular-Value Decomposition

The singular-value decomposition (SVD) is an integral part of subspace identification methods [1–4]. It is characterized by an orthogonal decomposition of a rectangular $n \times m$ matrix $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$

$$Z = U \times \Sigma \times V' \quad (C.1)$$

where the matrices $U \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ and $V \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times m}$ are orthogonal such that $U'U = I_n$, $V'V = I_m$ along with the diagonal matrix, $\Sigma = \operatorname{diag}[\sigma_1 \cdots \sigma_N]$ with $N < \min(n,m)$. The set of ordered singular values are given by $\{\sigma_i\}$; $i = 1, \ldots, N$ with the property that $\sigma_1 > \sigma_2 > \cdots > \sigma_N$. The singular values of $Z$ and the respective column vectors of $U$ and $V$, $u_i$ and $v_i$, are the ith left/right singular vectors such that [1]

$$Zv_i = \sigma_i u_i$$

$$Z'u_i = \sigma_i v_i$$

which follows directly from the SVD, since $ZV = U\Sigma$ and $Z'U = V\Sigma'$. Also premultiplying Eq. (C.1) by $U'$ and postmultiplying by $V$ and applying the orthogonality properties gives the relation

$$U' \times Z \times V = \Sigma = \operatorname{diag}[\sigma_1 \cdots \sigma_N] \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times m} \quad \text{for } N = \min(n,m) \quad (C.2)$$

It also follows that for a rank $r$ matrix, the SVD can be expressed as

$$Z = U\Sigma V' = [U_r \mid U_{n-r}]\begin{bmatrix}\Sigma_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}V_r' \\ V_{m-r}'\end{bmatrix} = U_r\Sigma_rV_r' \quad (C.3)$$

Numerical errors occurring in this approximation are quantified in terms of the singular values as

$$
E_{\mathrm{abs}} = \sqrt{ \sum_{i=r+1}^{N} \sigma_i^2 } \quad \text{(Absolute Error)}
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{rel}} = \sqrt{ \frac{ \sum_{i=r+1}^{N} \sigma_i^2 }{ \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sigma_i^2 } } \quad \text{(Relative Error)} \quad (C.4)
$$

The SVD of a matrix provides various properties:

- The set of singular values of $Z$ are equal to the positive square roots of the eigenvalues of $Z'Z$: $\sigma_i = +\sqrt{\lambda_i}$.

- For $Z$ of rank $r$, then the singular values satisfy

  $$\sigma_1 \ge \cdots \ge \sigma_r > \sigma_{r+1} = \cdots = \sigma_N = 0$$

- The rank condition gives

  $$\rho(Z) = r$$
  $$\mathrm{null}(Z) = \mathrm{span}\{v_{r+1}, \ldots, v_m\}$$
  $$\mathrm{range}(Z) = \mathrm{span}\{u_1, \ldots, u_r\} \quad (C.5)$$

- The matrix decomposition can be expressed directly in terms of the singular values as

  $$Z = U_r \Sigma_r V_r' = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i u_i v_i' \quad (C.6)$$

- The norms are also expressed in terms of the singular values as

  $$\| Z \|_F = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N} \sigma_i^2} \quad N < \min(n, m) \quad \text{(Frobenius Norm)}$$
  $$\| Z \|_2 = \sigma_1 \quad \text{(2-Norm)} \quad (C.7)$$

- The pseudo-inverse of a matrix ($Z^{\#} = (Z'Z)^{-1} Z'$ (row space) or $Z^{\#} = Z'(ZZ')^{-1}$ (column space)) is easily determined by

  $$Z^{\#} = (U \Sigma V')^{\#} = V \Sigma^{-1} U' \quad (C.8)$$

Associated with the SVD of Eq. (C.3) is the idea of projections, that is, projecting a vector onto a line or plane [4]. In terms of the SVD we have the following orthogonal projection matrices [5]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{Z \mid U} &= U_1 \times U_1' = Z \times Z^{\#} \\
P_{Z \mid U}^{\perp} &= U_2 \times U_2' = I - Z \times Z^{\#} \\
P_{Z \mid V} &= V_1 \times V_1' = Z^{\#} \times Z \\
P_{Z \mid V}^{\perp} &= V_2 \times V_2' = I - Z^{\#} \times Z
\end{aligned}
\tag{C.9}
$$

Finally, the SVD simultaneously diagonalizes the projection, Gramian matrix, and pseudo-inverse [2]

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{Z \mid U} &= U \times \begin{bmatrix} I & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \times U' \quad \text{(Projection)} \\
G_Z &:= Z' \times Z = V \times \Sigma^2 \times V' \quad \text{(Gramian)} \\
Z^{\#} &= V \times \Sigma^{-1} \times U' \quad \text{(Pseudo-Inverse)}
\end{aligned}
\tag{C.10}
$$

### C.2 QR-Decomposition

The QR-decomposition of a matrix is the matrix implementation of the well-known Gram–Schmidt vector orthogonalization procedure [4]. It is defined by the product of an orthogonal matrix $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ with $m \ge n$ and an upper triangular matrix $R_q \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times m}$, that is,

$$R = Q \times R_q \quad (C.11)$$

where $Q' \times Q = I_n$ such that $Q' R = R_q$, with $Q'$ the orthogonal matrix transforming $R \to R_q$. It is numerically implemented using the Householder transform, $T = I - 2 u u'$ with the vector $u$ defined by

$$u = \pm \frac{v_1 - v_2}{\| v_1 - v_2 \|} \quad \| u \| = 1 \quad (C.12)$$

### C.3 LQ-Decomposition

The LQ-decomposition is the dual of the QR-decomposition: $L = R'$, $Q = Q'$ where

$$Z = L \times Q \quad (C.13)$$

with $L$ lower block triangular and $Q$ orthogonal.

or equivalently the relationship of the basis vectors of $Z$ with those of $Q$. In fact from this viewpoint, we can think of partitioning $Q = [Q_Z \mid Q_Z^{\perp}]$ such that a rank-deficient data matrix $Z$ can (hypothetically) be decomposed as

$$Z = LQ = [L_Z \mid 0]\begin{bmatrix}Q_Z\\Q_Z^{\perp}\end{bmatrix} = L_Z \times Q_Z$$

The *projection matrix* associated with the decomposition is specified by [4]

$$P = Q \times Q' \quad \text{(Projection Matrix)}$$

In this appendix, we address the subspace identification approach for the constrained “output-only” realization problem where *no* input excitation data are available in contrast to the developments of Chapters 6 and 7.
