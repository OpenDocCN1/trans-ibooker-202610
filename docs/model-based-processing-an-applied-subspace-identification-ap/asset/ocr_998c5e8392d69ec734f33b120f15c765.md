The resulting posterior is then estimated by

P̂r(x(t)|Y_t) = Σ_{i=1}^{N_p} W_i(t) × δ(x(t) − x_i(t)) (4.104)

Note that it can also be shown that the estimate converges to the true posterior as

lim_{N_p→∞} P̂r(x(t)|Y_t) → Pr(x(t)|Y_t) (4.105)

implying that the MC error variance decreases as the number of particles increase [32].

There are a variety of PF algorithms available based on the choice of the importance distribution [33–36]. The most popular choice for the distribution is the state transition prior:

I(x(t)|X_{t−1}, Y_t) ⇒ Pr(x(t)|x(t−1)) (4.106)

This prior is defined in terms of the state-space representation A(x(t−1), u(t−1), w(t−1)), which is dependent on the known excitation (u(·)) and process noise (w(·)) statistics. For this implementation, we draw samples from the state transition distribution using the dynamic state-space model driven by the process uncertainty w_i(t) to generate the set of particles, {x_i(t)} for each i = 1, …, N_p. So we see that in order to generate particles, we execute the state-space simulation driving it with process noise {w_i(t)} for the i th particle.

Substituting this choice into the expression for the weights gives

W_i(t) = W_i(t−1) × [Pr(y(t)|x_i(t)) × Pr(x(t)|x_i(t−1))]/I(x(t)|X_{t−1}, Y_t) = W_i(t−1) × Pr(y(t)|x_i(t)) (4.107)

which is simply the likelihood distribution, since the priors cancel.

Note two properties for this choice of importance distribution. First, the weight does not use the most recent observation, y(t) and second, it does not use the past particles (x_i(t−1)), but only the likelihood. This choice is easily implemented and updated by simply evaluating the measurement likelihood, Pr(y(t)|x_i(t)); i = 1, …, N_p for the sampled particle set.

This particular choice of importance distribution can lead to problems since the transition prior is not conditioned on the measurement data, especially the most recent. Failing to incorporate the latest available information from the most recent measurement to propose new values for the states leads to only a few particles having significant weights when their likelihood is calculated. The transition prior is a much broader distribution than the likelihood, indicating that only a few particles will be assigned a large weight.
