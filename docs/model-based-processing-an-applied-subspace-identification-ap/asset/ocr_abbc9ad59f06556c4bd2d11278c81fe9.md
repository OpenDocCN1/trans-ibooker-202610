and therefore, the output is given by

$$y(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_p}R_i v_i(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_p}R_i x_i(t),\qquad i=1,\ldots,N_p$$

Expanding these relations over $i$, we obtain

$$\begin{bmatrix}x_1(t)\\x_2(t)\\\vdots\\x_{N_p}(t)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}p_1&0&\cdots&0\\0&p_2&\cdots&0\\\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\0&0&\cdots&p_{N_p}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_1(t-1)\\x_2(t-1)\\\vdots\\x_{N_p}(t-1)\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\\\vdots\\1\end{bmatrix}e(t-1)$$

$$y(t)=\begin{bmatrix}R_1&R_2&\cdots&R_{N_p}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_1(t-1)\\x_2(t-1)\\\vdots\\x_{N_p}(t-1)\end{bmatrix}$$

Thus, the general decoupled form of the *normal state-space model* is given by

$$\mathbf{x}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}p_1&0&\cdots&0\\0&p_2&\cdots&0\\\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\0&0&\cdots&p_{N_p}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}(t-1)+\mathbf{b}e(t-1)$$

$$y(t)=\mathbf{c}'\mathbf{x}(t)$$

for $\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_p\times1}$ with $\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{1}$, an $N_p$-vector of unit elements. Here $\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times N_p}$ and $\mathbf{c}'=[R_1\ R_2\ \cdots\ R_{N_p}]$.

# 3.8 Nonlinear (Approximate) Gauss–Markov State-Space Models

Many processes in practice are nonlinear, rather than linear. Coupling the nonlinearities with noisy data makes the signal processing problem a challenging one. In this section, we develop an approximate solution to the nonlinear modeling problem involving the linearization of the nonlinear process about a “known” reference trajectory.
