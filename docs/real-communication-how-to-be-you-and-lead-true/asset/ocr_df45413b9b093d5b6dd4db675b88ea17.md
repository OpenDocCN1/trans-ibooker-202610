# # Say that again

Babbel is one of the worlds most popular learning language apps. In 2018,
they asked representatives from international chambers of commerce in the
United States about country-specific jargon and reported it in Babbel magazine.

Here are a few of the more unusual ones.

From Indonesia:

+   - Phrase: ‘Asal bapak senang’
+   - Literal translation: ‘Keeping Father happy’
+   - Meaning: Hiding bad news from the boss; being a ‘yes-man’

Belgium:

+   - Phrase: ‘De kogel is door de kerk’
+   - Translation: ‘The bullet is through the church’
+   - Meaning: The decision has been made

In the future, when we say ‘you need a skintervention’ only the four of us
will know exactly what it means because of our joint experience.²

I have seen this kind of process play out in the workplace on many occasions.
For example, in one company that had adopted the agile manufacturing
methodology, someone was complaining about another colleague who had
said they were not implementing changes fast enough. With that another
peer responded with, ‘They are just agile shaming us’. The team loved it
and started to use ‘agile shaming’ as a joke, but I noticed after time the
terms had become part of their everyday language.

This is exactly the problem when it comes to business.

> Teams have their own jargon. Companies and industries have their
> own jargon. Even countries have their own jargon.

² Well, except you now (and everyone else reading this) because I have just explained it
to you.
