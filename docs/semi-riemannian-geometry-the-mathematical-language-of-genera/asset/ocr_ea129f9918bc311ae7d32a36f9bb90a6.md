$$\frac{d(H_j\circ\zeta)}{dt}(0)=\sum_k\frac{d\zeta^k}{dt}(0)\frac{\partial H_j}{\partial r^k}\bigg|_q=\frac{\partial H_j}{\partial r^i}\bigg|_q.$$

It follows from Theorem 10.1.17 and Theorem 11.2.8 that $H_j\circ\varphi^{-1}$ is smooth. We have

$$D_{H_i\circ\varphi^{-1}}(H_j\circ\varphi^{-1})\big|_p
=\frac{d(H_j\circ\varphi^{-1}\circ\lambda)}{dt}(0)
=\frac{d(H_j\circ\zeta)}{dt}(0)

=\sum_k\Gamma^k_{ij}(q)H_k\big|_q+\vartheta_{ij}(q)G_q

=\left(\left[\sum_k\Gamma^k_{ij}H_k+\vartheta_{ij}G\right]\circ\varphi^{-1}\right)\bigg|_p.$$

Since $p$ was arbitrary,

$$D_{H_i\circ\varphi^{-1}}(H_j\circ\varphi^{-1})=\left(\sum_k\Gamma^k_{ij}H_k+\vartheta_{ij}G\right)\circ\varphi^{-1},$$

from which the result follows.
