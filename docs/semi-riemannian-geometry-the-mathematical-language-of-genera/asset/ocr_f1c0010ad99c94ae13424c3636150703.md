where
\[\gamma'' = \gamma\gamma'(1 + \beta\beta')\quad\text{and}\quad\beta'' = \frac{\beta + \beta'}{1 + \beta\beta'}.\]

It is easily shown that $\gamma'' \ge 1$, and that $\gamma''$ and $\beta''$ satisfy (22.1.2). Thus, $A \circ A'$ is a boost. This demonstrates that $\mathrm{B}_m$ is closed under multiplication. However,

\[\begin{bmatrix} I_{m-2} & O_{(m-2)\times 2} \\ O_{2\times(m-2)} & \begin{bmatrix} \gamma & -\beta\gamma \\ -\beta\gamma & \gamma \end{bmatrix} \end{bmatrix}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} I_{m-2} & O_{(m-2)\times 2} \\ O_{2\times(m-2)} & \begin{bmatrix} \gamma & \beta\gamma \\ \beta\gamma & \gamma \end{bmatrix} \end{bmatrix}.\]

Since the matrix on the right-hand side is missing the necessary minus signs to make it a boost, $\mathrm{B}_m$ does not contain inverses (except in the case of the identity map). Thus, $\mathrm{B}_m$ is not a group.

# Theorem 22.1.8. $\mathrm{B}_m$ is a subset of $\mathrm{L}_m$.

*Proof.* Let $A$ be a map in $\mathrm{B}_m$. We have from $\mathfrak{g}_\mathcal{E} = \operatorname{diag}(1,\dots,1,-1)$ and the identity in (22.1.3) that

\[[A]^\mathrm{T}\,\mathfrak{g}_\mathcal{E}\,[A] = \begin{bmatrix} I_{m-2} & O_{(m-2)\times 2} \\ O_{2\times(m-2)} & \begin{bmatrix} \gamma & \beta\gamma \\ -\beta\gamma & -\gamma \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \gamma & -\beta\gamma \\ -\beta\gamma & \gamma \end{bmatrix} \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} I_{m-2} & O_{(m-2)\times 2} \\ O_{2\times(m-2)} & \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \end{bmatrix} = \mathfrak{g}_\mathcal{E}.\]

By Theorem 4.4.2, $A$ is a linear isometry and is therefore in $\mathrm{L}_m$.

# Theorem 22.1.9. $\mathrm{R}_m \cap \mathrm{B}_m = \{\mathrm{id}_V\}$.

*Proof.* If $A$ is in $\mathrm{R}_m \cap \mathrm{B}_m$, then, in the above notation,

\[\begin{bmatrix} [A]^1_1 & O_{(m-1)\times 1} \\ O_{1\times(m-1)} & 1 \end{bmatrix} = [A] = \begin{bmatrix} I_{m-2} & O_{(m-2)\times 2} \\ O_{2\times(m-2)} & \begin{bmatrix} \gamma & -\beta\gamma \\ -\beta\gamma & \gamma \end{bmatrix} \end{bmatrix},\]

where, by definition, $[A]^1_1$ is in $\mathrm{SO}(m-1)$, $\gamma \ge 1$, and $\beta$ is computed using (22.1.2). It follows that $\gamma = 1$, and then from (22.1.2) that $\beta = 0$. Thus, $[A] = I_m$.
