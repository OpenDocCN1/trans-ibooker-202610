ℋ. Then

```
ℓ(𝒜) = ℓ(∑_{ij} 𝒜_j^i h_i ⊗ θ^j) = ∑_{ij} 𝒜_j^i ℓ(h_i ⊗ θ^j)
= ∑_{ij} 𝒜_j^i h_i(θ^j)  [(5.4.1)]
= ∑_i 𝒜_i^i = tr([𝒜_j^i])
= tr([ℛ^{-1}(𝒜)]_ℋ^ℋ)  [Th 5.3.2(c)]
= tr(ℛ^{-1}(𝒜)).  [(2.5.1)]
```

Since 𝒜 was arbitrary, ℓ = tr ∘ ℛ^{-1}.

# Theorem 5.4.2. Let V be a vector space, let ℋ be a basis for V, let 𝒜 be a tensor in 𝒯_1^1(V), and let 𝒜_j^i be the components of 𝒜 with respect to ℋ. Then

```
C_1^1(𝒜) = ∑_i 𝒜_i^i.
```

Proof. This was shown as part of the proof of Theorem 5.4.1.

Theorem 5.4.2 explains why in the literature “contraction” is sometimes called “trace”. The next result is a generalization of Theorem 5.4.1.
