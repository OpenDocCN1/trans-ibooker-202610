# ## 20.5 Curl

Let \((M,g,\mathcal{D})\) be an oriented semi-Riemannian 3-manifold of index \(\nu\), and let \(\Omega_M\) be its volume form. Curl is the map

\[\operatorname{Curl}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)\]

defined by

\[\operatorname{Curl}(X)^\mathrm{F}=\star d(X^\mathrm{F}) \tag{20.5.1}\]

for all vector fields \(X\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\). Since \(X^\mathrm{F}\) is a covector field in \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)=\Lambda^1(M)\), it follows that \(d(X^\mathrm{F})\) is a form in \(\Lambda^2(M)\), hence \(\star d(X^\mathrm{F})\) is a vector field in \(\Lambda^1(M)\), so the definition makes sense. We refer to \(\operatorname{Curl}(X)\) as the curl of \(X\).

# **Theorem 20.5.1.** *With the above setup,*

\[i_{\operatorname{Curl}(X)}(\Omega_M)=d(X^\mathrm{F}).\]

*Proof.* We have

\[i_{\operatorname{Curl}(X)}(\Omega_M)=(-1)^\nu\star(\operatorname{Curl}(X)^\mathrm{F})\qquad [\mathrm{Th\ 20.1.2}]\]
\[\phantom{i_{\operatorname{Curl}(X)}(\Omega_M)}=(-1)^\nu\star^2d(X^\mathrm{F}).\qquad [(20.5.1)]\]

Since \(d(X^\mathrm{F})\) is a form in \(\Lambda^2(M)\), setting \(m=3\) and \(s=2\) in Theorem 20.1.1(c) yields

\[\star^2(d(X^\mathrm{F}))=(-1)^\nu d(X^\mathrm{F}).\]

The result follows.

Despite the unfamiliar appearance of definition (20.5.1), the following example shows that the Curl and classical curl operators are identical in \(\mathbb{R}^3_0\).

# **Example 20.5.2.**

Let \((x,y,z)\) and \((d(x),d(y),d(z))\) be the standard coordinates and corresponding standard dual coordinate frame on \(\mathbb{R}^3_0\), respectively, and let \(X=(\alpha^1,\alpha^2,\alpha^3)\) be a vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{R}^3_0)\). From the manifold version of Theorem 3.3.2,

\[X^\mathrm{F}=\alpha^1d(x)+\alpha^2d(y)+\alpha^3d(z).\]

Then (16.1.2) gives

\[
d(X^\mathrm{F})=\left(\frac{\partial\alpha^3}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial\alpha^2}{\partial z}\right)d(y)\wedge d(z)+\left(\frac{\partial\alpha^1}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial\alpha^3}{\partial x}\right)d(z)\wedge d(x)
\]
\[
\qquad+\left(\frac{\partial\alpha^2}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial\alpha^1}{\partial y}\right)d(x)\wedge d(y).
\]
