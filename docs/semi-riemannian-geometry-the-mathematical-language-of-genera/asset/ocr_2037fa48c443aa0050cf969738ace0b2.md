∂/∂r^{m-1}) is the corresponding standard coordinate frame. By Theorem 15.8.4,

i_{-∂/∂r^m}(Ω_{ℍ^m})|_{∂ℍ^m} (∂/∂r^1, ..., ∂/∂r^{m-1}) = (-1)^m,

hence

Since i_{-∂/∂r^m}(Ω_{ℍ^m})|_{∂ℍ^m} induces the Stokes orientation, it follows from Theorem 16.6.3 that ((-1)^m ∂/∂r^1, ..., ∂/∂r^{m-1}) has the Stokes orientation.

# 17.4 Stokes’s Theorem

In this section, we prove one of the central results of differential geometry—Stokes’s theorem. In one sweeping statement it unifies numerous core theorems of vector calculus, as illustrated in Section 21.2.

Although details will not be presented here, the theory of integration of differential forms on smooth manifolds given in Section 16.7 can be extended to include the integration of differential forms on smooth manifolds with boundary.

Theorem 17.4.1 (Stokes’s Theorem). Let (M,𝔇) be an oriented smooth m-manifold with boundary, and suppose ∂M has the Stokes orientation. If ω is a form in Λ^{m-1}(M) that has compact support, then

∫_M d(ω) = ∫_{∂M} ω, (17.4.1)

where the integrand on the right-hand side is interpreted as ω|_{∂M}.

Proof. We consider only the case M = ℍ^m. Let (r^1, ..., r^m) be standard coordinates on ℍ^m, and let (d(r^1), ..., d(r^m)) be the corresponding standard dual coordinate frame. Suppose ℍ^m has the standard orientation and that ∂ℍ^m has the corresponding Stokes orientation.

Step 1. Compute ∫_{ℍ^m} d(ω), the left-hand side of (17.4.1).

Since ω has compact support, it follows from Theorem 9.4.2 that there are real numbers c1, ..., cm > 0 such that supp(ω) is properly contained in the closed cell

C = [-c1, c1] × ⋯ × [-c_{m-1}, c_{m-1}] × [0, c_m].
