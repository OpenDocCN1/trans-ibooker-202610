
## Theorem 8.4.13. Let $(V,\mathfrak{g})$ be a scalar product space of dimension $m \geq 2$, let $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ be orthonormal bases for $V$, and let $\times$ and $\boxtimes$ denote the corresponding vector product operations. Let $U$ be a subspace of $V$ of dimension $m-1$ on which $\mathfrak{g}$ is nondegenerate, and let $(u_1,\dots,u_{m-1})$ be a basis for $U$. Then:

(a)
$$u_1 \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes u_{m-1} = \det\big([\mathrm{id}_V]_{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathcal{F}}\big)\, u_1 \times \cdots \times u_{m-1}.$$

(b)
$$\frac{u_1 \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes u_{m-1}}{\|u_1 \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes u_{m-1}\|}= \pm \frac{u_1 \times \cdots \times u_{m-1}}{\|u_1 \times \cdots \times u_{m-1}\|},$$
where the positive (negative) sign is chosen if $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ are consistent (not consistent).

Remark. By Theorem 8.4.9, the denominators in part (b) are nonzero.

Proof. (a): Let $\mathcal{E} = (e_1,\dots,e_m)$, and let
$$P = [\mathrm{id}_V]_{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathcal{F}} = \big[[e_1]_{\mathcal{F}}\,\cdots\,[e_m]_{\mathcal{F}}\big],$$
where the second equality comes from (2.2.7). Let
$$Q = \big[[u_1]_{\mathcal{E}}\,\cdots\,[u_{m-1}]_{\mathcal{E}}\big],$$
and let
$$R_i = \big(P^{(i)^c}\big)^{\mathsf{T}}$$
for $i=1,\dots,m$. By Theorem 2.1.6(b),
$$R_i^{(j)^c} = \big(P_{(j)^c}^{(i)^c}\big)^{\mathsf{T}}$$
for $j=1,\dots,m$. It follows from (2.2.5) that
$$\big[[u_1]_{\mathcal{F}}\,\cdots\,[u_{m-1}]_{\mathcal{F}}\big] = [\mathrm{id}_V]_{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathcal{F}}\big[[u_1]_{\mathcal{E}}\,\cdots\,[u_{m-1}]_{\mathcal{E}}\big] = PQ,$$
and then from Theorem 2.1.6(c) that
$$\big[[u_1]_{\mathcal{F}}\,\cdots\,[u_{m-1}]_{\mathcal{F}}\big]^{(i)^c} = P^{(i)^c}Q.$$

Then (8.4.1) gives
$$u_1 \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes u_{m-1} = \sum_i \varepsilon_i (-1)^{i-1} \det\Big(\big[[u_1]_{\mathcal{F}}\,\cdots\,[u_{m-1}]_{\mathcal{F}}\big]^{(i)^c}\Big) e_i$$
$$= \sum_i \varepsilon_i (-1)^{i-1} \det\big(P^{(i)^c}Q\big) e_i.$$

By Theorem 2.4.10(a),

$$\det(P^{(i)c}Q)=\det(R_i^TQ)=\sum_j\det(R_i^{(j)c})\det(Q^{(j)c})$$

$$=\sum_j\det\left(P_{(j)}^{(i)c}\right)\det\left(Q^{(j)c}\right).$$

We have

$$P_{(j)}^{(i)c}=\left(\left[e_1\right]_F\ \cdots\ \left[e_m\right]_F\right)_{(j)c}^{(i)c}$$

$$=\left(\left[e_1\right]_F\ \cdots\ \widehat{\left[e_j\right]_F}\ \cdots\ \left[e_m\right]_F\right)^{(i)c},$$

where $\widehat{\phantom{x}}$ indicates that an expression is omitted. The preceding three identities combine to give

$$u_1\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes u_{m-1}$$

$$=\sum_i\varepsilon_i(-1)^{i-1}\left(\sum_j\det\left(P_{(j)}^{(i)c}\right)\det\left(Q^{(j)c}\right)\right)e_i$$

$$=\sum_j\det\left(Q^{(j)c}\right)\left(\sum_i\varepsilon_i(-1)^{i-1}\det\left(\left[\left[e_1\right]_F\ \cdots\ \widehat{\left[e_j\right]_F}\ \cdots\ \left[e_m\right]_F\right]^{(i)c}\right)e_i\right)$$

$$=\sum_j\det\left(Q^{(j)c}\right)e_1\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\widehat{e_j}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes e_m,$$

where the last equality follows from (8.4.1). By Theorem 8.4.12,

$$e_1\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\widehat{e_j}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes e_m=\varepsilon_j(-1)^{j-1}\det(P)e_j$$

for $j=1,\ldots,m$. Thus,

$$u_1\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes u_{m-1}=\det(P)\sum_j\varepsilon_j(-1)^{j-1}\det\left(Q^{(j)c}\right)e_j$$

$$=\det(P)\sum_j\varepsilon_j(-1)^{j-1}\det\left(\left[\left[u_1\right]_E\ \cdots\ \left[u_{m-1}\right]_E\right]^{(j)c}\right)e_j$$

$$=\det(P)u_1\times\cdots\times u_{m-1},$$

where the last equality follows from (8.4.1).

(b): We have from Theorem 8.4.12 and Theorem 8.4.9 that $\det(P)$, $\|u_1\times\cdots\times u_{m-1}\|$, and $\|u_1\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes u_{m-1}\|$ are each nonzero. Then Theorem 8.3.3 and part (a) give

$$\frac{u_1\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes u_{m-1}}{\|u_1\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes u_{m-1}\|}=\frac{\det(P)}{|\det(P)|}\frac{u_1\times\cdots\times u_{m-1}}{\|u_1\times\cdots\times u_{m-1}\|}$$

$$=\operatorname{sgn}(\det(P))\frac{u_1\times\cdots\times u_{m-1}}{\|u_1\times\cdots\times u_{m-1}\|}.$$

## 8.5 Hodge Star

In this section, we define a map that assigns to a given multivector another multivector that “complements” the first. The methods that result add to our growing armamentarium of techniques for computing with multivectors.

### Theorem 8.5.1. Let $(V,g,\mathcal{Q})$ be an oriented scalar product space of dimension $m$, let $\Omega_V$ be its volume multivector, and let $\eta$ be a multivector in $\Lambda^s(V)$, where $s\le m$. Then there is a unique multivector $\star(\eta)$ in $\Lambda^{m-s}(V)$ such that

$$\eta\wedge\zeta=\langle\star(\eta),\zeta\rangle^\Lambda\Omega_V$$

for all multivectors $\zeta$ in $\Lambda^{m-s}(V)$, where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle^\Lambda$ is the scalar product defined in Section 7.5.

**Proof.** *Existence.* By Theorem 7.2.12(d), $(\Omega_V)$ is a basis for $\Lambda^m(V)$, so

$$\eta\wedge\zeta=f_\eta(\zeta)\,\Omega_V$$

for some real number $f_\eta(\zeta)$. The assignment $\zeta\mapsto f_\eta(\zeta)$ defines a function $f_\eta:\Lambda^{m-s}(V)\to\mathbb{R}$. Evidently, $f_\eta$ is linear, so $f_\eta$ is a multivector in $\Lambda^{m-s}(V)^*$. Recall the flat map and sharp map introduced at the end of Section 7.5:

$$F^\Lambda:\Lambda^{m-s}(V)\longrightarrow\Lambda^{m-s}(V)^*\qquad\text{and}\qquad S^\Lambda:\Lambda^{m-s}(V)^*\longrightarrow\Lambda^{m-s}(V).$$

Let us define $\star(\eta)=(f_\eta)^{S^\Lambda}$. Then $\star(\eta)$ is a multivector in $\Lambda^{m-s}(V)$ and $f_\eta=(\star(\eta))^{F^\Lambda}$. According to (7.5.12), $f_\eta(\zeta)=\langle\star(\eta),\zeta\rangle^\Lambda$, so

$$\eta\wedge\zeta=\langle\star(\eta),\zeta\rangle^\Lambda\Omega_V \tag{8.5.1}$$

for all multivectors $\zeta$ in $\Lambda^{m-s}(V)$. Thus, $\star(\eta)$ satisfies the desired property.

*Uniqueness.* Suppose $\zeta$ is a multivector in $\Lambda^{m-s}(V)$ satisfying the specified property; that is,

$$\eta\wedge\zeta=\langle\xi,\zeta\rangle^\Lambda\Omega_V \tag{8.5.2}$$

for all multivectors $\zeta$ in $\Lambda^{m-s}(V)$. Since $(\Omega_V)$ is a basis for $\Lambda^m(V)$, we have from (8.5.1) and (8.5.2) that $\langle\xi,\zeta\rangle^\Lambda=\langle\star(\eta),\zeta\rangle^\Lambda$, hence $\langle\xi-\star(\eta),\zeta\rangle^\Lambda=0$. Because $\zeta$ was arbitrary and, by Theorem 7.5.3(a), $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle^\Lambda$ is nondegenerate, $\xi-\star(\eta)=0$.

**Hodge star** is the family of linear maps

$$\star:\Lambda^s(V)\longrightarrow\Lambda^{m-s}(V)$$

defined for $s\le m$ by the assignment

$$\eta\longmapsto\star(\eta)$$

for all multivectors $\eta$ in $\Lambda^s(V)$. Let us denote

$$\star\circ\star\qquad\text{by}\qquad\star^2.$$

### Theorem 8.5.2. Let $(V,\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{O})$ be an oriented scalar product space, let $(e_1,\dots,e_m)$ be an orthonormal basis for $V$ that is positively oriented with respect to $\mathfrak{O}$, let $(\xi^1,\dots,\xi^m)$ be its dual basis, and let $I$ be a multi-index in $\mathcal{I}_{s,m}$. Then

$\star(\xi^I) = \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_{(I,I^c)})\langle\xi^{I^c},\xi^{I^c}\rangle^{\Lambda}\xi^{I^c}$,

where $\sigma_{(I,I^c)}$ is the permutation defined in Section B.2.

Proof. We have from Theorem 7.5.3(b) that $(\xi^I : I \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m})$ is an orthonormal basis for $(\Lambda^s(V),\mathfrak{g}^{\Lambda})$, and then from Theorem 4.2.7 that

$\star(\xi^I) = \sum_{J \in \mathcal{I}_{m-s,m}} \langle\xi^J,\xi^J\rangle^{\Lambda}\langle\star(\xi^I),\xi^J\rangle^{\Lambda}\xi^J. \quad (8.5.3)$

According to Theorem 8.5.1,

$\langle\star(\xi^I),\xi^J\rangle^{\Lambda}\Omega_V = \xi^I \wedge \xi^J.$

For $J = I^c$, we have

$\xi^I \wedge \xi^{I^c} = \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_{(I,I^c)})\xi^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \xi^m \quad [\text{Th 7.2.10}]$

$= \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_{(I,I^c)})\Omega_V, \quad [\text{Th 8.3.4}]$

hence $\langle\star(\xi^I),\xi^{I^c}\rangle^{\Lambda}\Omega_V = \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_{(I,I^c)})\Omega_V.$

Since $(\Omega_V)$ is a basis for $\Lambda^m(V)$, $\langle\star(\xi^I),\xi^{I^c}\rangle^{\Lambda} = \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_{(I,I^c)}).$

On the other hand, for $J \neq I^c$, we have from Theorem 7.2.6 that $\xi^I \wedge \xi^J = 0.$ In summary, $\langle\star(\xi^I),\xi^J\rangle^{\Lambda} = \begin{cases}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_{(I,I^c)}) & \text{if } J = I^c \\ 0 & \text{if } J \neq I^c.\end{cases}$

Substituting into (8.5.3) gives the result.

Theorem 8.5.2 provides a way to compute with $\star$ on an orthonormal basis for $\Lambda^s(V)$. Computations are then extended to all of $\Lambda^s(V)$ by the linearity of $\star$.

### Theorem 8.5.3. Let $(V,\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{O})$ be an oriented scalar product space of dimension $m$ and index $\nu$, let $\Omega_V$ be its volume multicovector, and let $\eta,\zeta$ be multicovectors in $\Lambda^s(V)$. Then:

- (a) $\star(1) = (-1)^{\nu}\Omega_V$.
- (b) $\star(\Omega_V) = 1$.
- (c) $\star^2(\eta) = (-1)^{s(m-s)+\nu}\eta$.
- (d) $\eta \wedge \star(\zeta) = (-1)^{\nu}\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle^{\Lambda}\Omega_V$.
