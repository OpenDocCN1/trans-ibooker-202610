It was remarked in the introduction to this section that if a smooth manifold has an orientation, then it has at least two of them. The next result gives a condition under which there are two orientations, and no more.

# Theorem 16.6.8. A connected orientable smooth manifold has precisely two orientations.

Proof. Let M be a connected orientable smooth manifold. By Theorem 16.6.5, M has an orientation form ϖ. Each orientation form in [ϖ] induces the same orientation, and each orientation form in [−ϖ] induces its opposite. Let ϑ be another orientation form on M. We have from Theorem 16.6.1(a) that there is a uniquely determined nowhere-vanishing function f in C^∞(M) such that ϑ = f ϖ, and then from Theorem 16.6.1(b) that ϑ is either in [ϖ] or [−ϖ].

It follows from Theorem 14.1.3 that a smooth manifold has countably many connected components, each of which is an open set in the smooth manifold. Let M be a smooth manifold that has a finite number of connected components C1, …, Ck. Suppose Ci, viewed as an open submanifold of M, is orientable for i = 1, …, k. Then Ci can be given an orientation 𝔇i independently of the other connected components. Taken together, the 𝔇i give an orientation of M, which we denote by (𝔇1, …, 𝔇k). It follows from Theorem 16.6.8 that by taking all combinations of signs in (±𝔇1, …, ±𝔇k) we obtain the 2^k possible orientations of M.

Let (M̄, 𝔇̄) be an oriented smooth m̄-manifold, where m̄ ≥ 2, and let M be a submanifold of M̄. In general, there is no mechanism for constructing an orientation of M from 𝔇̄. However, when M is a hypersurface of M̄, this may be possible, as the next result shows.

# Theorem 16.6.9. Let (M̄, 𝔇̄) be an oriented smooth m̄-manifold, where m̄ ≥ 2, let M be a hypersurface of M̄, and suppose there is a nowhere-tangent vector field V in 𝔛_M(M). Then:

+   (a) There is a unique orientation 𝔇_M of M, called the orientation induced by V, such that for all points p in M, (v1, …, v_{m̄−1}) is a basis for T_p(M) that is positively oriented with respect to 𝔇_M(p) if and only if (V_p, v1, …, v_{m̄−1}) is a basis for T_p(M̄) that is positively oriented with respect to 𝔇̄(p).

+   (b) If ϖ is an orientation form on M̄ that induces 𝔇̄, then i_V(ϖ)|_M is an orientation form on M that induces 𝔇_M.

+   (c) 𝔇_M is independent of the choice of orientation form on M̄ that induces 𝔇̄.

Proof. This is the manifold version of Theorem 8.2.5, but we provide a detailed proof.

(a), (b): Let ϖ be an orientation form on M̄ that induces 𝔇̄. According to Theorem 16.6.3, by definition, ϖ_q is an orientation multicovector on T_q(M̄) that induces 𝔇̄(q) for all q in M̄. It follows from Theorem 8.2.5(b) that (i_V(ϖ)|_M)|_p = i_{V_p}(ϖ_p)|_{T_p(M)} is an orientation multicovector on T_p(M) for all p in M. Thus, i_V(ϖ)|_M is a nowhere-vanishing form on M. For vector fields X1, …, X_{m−1} in 𝔛(M), we have i_V(ϖ)|_M(X1, …, X_{m−1}) = ϖ(V, X1, …, X_{m−1}).
