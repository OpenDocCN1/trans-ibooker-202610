# 12.8 Gauss Curvature in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\)

In this section, we describe a way of measuring the “curvature” of a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\).

As part of the discussion of hyperquadrics \(Q^2\) in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), in Section 12.2, we observed that \(S^2\) is the set of (spacelike) unit vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^3_0\), \(P^2\) is the set of spacelike unit vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^3_1\), and \(H^2\) is the set of timelike unit vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^3_1\). In fact, more than just being sets, according to Theorem 12.2.12(a), \(S^2\) is a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_0\), and \(P^2\) and \(H^2\) are regular surfaces in \(\mathbb{R}^3_1\).

Let \((M,\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{N},\mathcal{D})\) be an oriented regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), let \(\mathcal{N}:M\to\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\) be the Gauss map, and let \(p\) be a point in \(M\). Since \(\mathcal{N}_p\) is a unit normal vector at \(p\), it follows from (12.2.3) that \(q(\mathcal{N}_p)=\langle\mathcal{N}_p,\mathcal{N}_p\rangle=\epsilon_M\), where \(q\) is the quadratic function corresponding to \(\mathfrak{g}\). Thus, \(\mathcal{N}_p\) is in the same hyperquadric for all \(p\) in \(M\). Denoting the hyperquadric by \(Q^2\), we can now say that \(\mathcal{N}_p\) is in \(Q^2\) for all \(p\) in \(M\). Thus, \(\mathcal{N}\) can be expressed more precisely as

\[\mathcal{N}:M\longrightarrow Q^2.\]

The situation for \(S^2\) is depicted in Figure 12.8.1, where \(\mathcal{N}_i\) stands for \(\mathcal{N}_{p_i}\), for \(i=1,2,3\).

The differential of \(\mathcal{N}\) at \(p\) is \(d_p(\mathcal{N}):T_p(M)\to T_{\mathcal{N}_p}(Q^2)\). By definition, \(\mathcal{N}_p\) is in \(T_p(M)^\perp\). On the other hand, since \(\mathcal{N}_p\) is in \(Q^2\), we have from Theorem 12.2.12(c) that \(\mathcal{N}_p\) is also in \(T_{\mathcal{N}_p}(Q^2)^\perp\). Since \(T_p(M)^\perp\) and \(T_{\mathcal{N}_p}(Q^2)^\perp\) are both 1-dimensional, it follows that \(T_p(M)^\perp=T_{\mathcal{N}_p}(Q^2)^\perp\), and then from Theorem 4.1.2(c) that

\[T_p(M)=T_{\mathcal{N}_p}(Q^2).\]

We can therefore express the differential of \(\mathcal{N}\) at \(p\) as

\[d_p(\mathcal{N}):T_p(M)\longrightarrow T_p(M).\]
