Since $\eta(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_s)$ has $v_i$ in (at least) two positions, $\eta(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_s)=0$ for $i=2,\ldots,s$. Thus, $\eta(v_1,\ldots,v_s)=0$.

$(d')\Rightarrow(c)$: If two (or more) of $v_1,\ldots,v_s$ are equal, then they are linearly dependent, so $\eta(v_1,\ldots,v_s)=0$.

We now introduce a way of associating a multivector to a given tensor. Let $V$ be a vector space. **Alternating map** is the family of linear maps

$$\operatorname{Alt}:\mathcal{T}^0_s(V)\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}^0_s(V)$$

defined for $s\ge 0$ by

$$\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A})=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\sigma(\mathcal{A}) \tag{7.1.4}$$

for all tensors $\mathcal{A}$ in $\mathcal{T}^0_s(V)$.

**Theorem 7.1.3.** Let $V$ be a vector space, let $\mathcal{A}$ be a tensor in $\mathcal{T}^0_s(V)$, let $\sigma$ be a permutation in $S_s$, and let $\eta$ be a multivector in $\Lambda^s(V)$. Then:

(a) $\sigma(\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A}))=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A})=\operatorname{Alt}(\sigma(\mathcal{A}))$.

(b) $\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A})$ is a multivector in $\Lambda^s(V)$.

(c) $\operatorname{Alt}(\eta)=\eta$.

(d) $\operatorname{Alt}(\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A}))=\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A})$.

(e) $\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{T}^0_s(V))=\Lambda^s(V)$.

**Proof.** (a): We have

$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma(\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A}))
&=\sigma\left(\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\rho\in S_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\rho)\,\rho(\mathcal{A})\right) &&\text{[(7.1.4)]}\\
&=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\rho\in S_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\rho)\,\sigma(\rho(\mathcal{A}))\\
&=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\rho\in S_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\rho)\,(\sigma\rho)(\mathcal{A}) &&\text{[Th 7.1.1]}\\
&=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\rho\in S_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma\rho)\,(\sigma\rho)(\mathcal{A}) &&\text{[Th B.2.2]}\\
&=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A}), &&\text{[(7.1.4)]}
\end{aligned}$$

where the last equality follows from the observation that as $\rho$ varies over $S_s$, so does $\sigma\rho$. We also have

$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Alt}(\sigma(\mathcal{A}))
&=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\rho\in S_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\rho)\,\rho(\sigma(\mathcal{A})) &&\text{[(7.1.4)]}\\
&=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\rho\in S_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\rho\sigma)\,(\rho\sigma)(\mathcal{A}) &&\text{[Th 7.1.1, Th B.2.2]}\\
&=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A}).
\end{aligned}$$
