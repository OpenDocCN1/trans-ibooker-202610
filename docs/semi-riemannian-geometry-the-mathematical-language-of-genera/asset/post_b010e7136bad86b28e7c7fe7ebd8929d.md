
Relabeling indices $(i_{n+1},\ldots,i_{r+1})$ as $(i_n,\ldots,i_r)$, and $(j_{k+1},\ldots,j_{s-1},q,j_l,\ldots,j_{s-1})$ as $(j_k,\ldots,j_{l-2},q,j_{l-1},\ldots,j_{s-2})$, gives the result.

**(b):** We have

$$C_k^n\circ S_k^n(\mathcal A)^{i_1\ldots\widehat{i_k}\ldots i_{r+1}}_{j_1\ldots\widehat{j_k}\ldots j_{s-1}}=\sum_p S_k^n(\mathcal A)^{i_1\ldots i_{n-1}\,p\,i_{n+1}\ldots i_{r+1}}_{j_1\ldots j_{k-1}\,p\,j_{k+1}\ldots j_{s-1}}$$

$$=\sum_p\left(\sum_q g^{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots\widehat{i_n}\ldots i_{r+1}}_{j_1\ldots j_{k-1}\,q\,j_{k+1}\ldots j_{s-1}}\right)$$

$$=\sum_{pq}g^{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots\widehat{i_n}\ldots i_{r+1}}_{j_1\ldots j_{k-1}\,q\,j_{k+1}\ldots j_{s-1}}$$

$$=\sum_{pq}g^{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots\widehat{i_n}\ldots i_{r+1}}_{j_1\ldots j_{k-1}\,p\,q\,j_{k+1}\ldots j_{s-1}}$$

and

$$C_k^n\circ S_{k+1}^n(\mathcal A)^{i_1\ldots\widehat{i_k}\ldots i_{r+1}}_{j_1\ldots\widehat{j_k}\ldots j_{s-1}}=\sum_p S_{k+1}^n(\mathcal A)^{i_1\ldots i_{n-1}\,p\,i_{n+1}\ldots i_{r+1}}_{j_1\ldots j_{k-1}\,p\,j_{k+1}\ldots j_{s-1}}$$

$$=\sum_p\left(\sum_q g^{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots i_{n-1}\,i_{n+1}}_{j_1\ldots j_{k-1}\,p\,q\,j_{k+1}\ldots j_{s-1}}\right)$$

$$=\sum_{pq}g^{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots i_{n-1}\,i_{n+1}}_{j_1\ldots j_{k-1}\,p\,q\,j_{k+1}\ldots j_{s-1}},$$

which gives the first equality. Relabeling indices $(i_{n+1},\ldots,i_{r+1})$ as $(i_n,\ldots,i_r)$, and $(j_{k+1},\ldots,j_{s-1})$ as $(j_k,\ldots,j_{s-2})$, gives the second equality. ■

We observe that $p$ and $q$ in (6.3.2) are in the $k$th and $l$th covariant positions of $\mathcal A$, respectively, and that $p$ and $q$ in (6.3.3) are in the $k$th and $(k+1)$th covariant positions of $\mathcal A$, respectively. We also note that the right hand side of (6.3.2) is independent of $n$. See the corresponding remarks following Theorem 6.2.5.

**Example 6.3.6.** For a tensor $\mathcal A$ in $\mathcal T^2_2(V)$, we have from Example 6.2.3 that

$$F_1^2(\mathcal A)^{ij}_{pkl}=\sum_q g_{pq}\mathcal A^{iq}_{kl}.$$

Substituting $\mathcal B=F_1^2(\mathcal A)$, as given by the above expression, into (6.3.1) yields

$$S_1^2\circ F_1^2(\mathcal A)^{ij}_{kl}=\sum_p g^{jp}F_1^2(\mathcal A)^i_{pkl}=\sum_p g^{jp}\left(\sum_q g_{pq}\mathcal A^{iq}_{kl}\right)$$
$$=\sum_q\left(\sum_p g^{jp}g_{pq}\right)\mathcal A^{iq}_{kl}=\sum_q\delta^j_q\mathcal A^{iq}_{kl}=\mathcal A^{ij}_{kl}.$$

Thus, $S_1^2\circ F_1^2(\mathcal A)=\mathcal A$. Likewise, $F_1^2\circ S_1^2(\mathcal A)=\mathcal A$. Since $\mathcal A$ was arbitrary, it follows from Theorem A.4 that $F_1^2$ and $S_1^2$ are inverses of each other, and are therefore linear isomorphisms. ◇

### Theorem 6.3.7.

If $(V,\mathfrak g)$ is a scalar product space, and $1\leq k\leq r$ and $1\leq l\leq s$ are integers, then $F_l^k$ and $S_l^k$ are linear isomorphisms that are inverses.

**Proof.** The proof is an elaboration of Example 6.3.6. Let $\mathcal H$ be a basis for $V$, let $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ be tensors in $\mathcal T_s^r(V)$ and $\mathcal T_{s+1}^{r-1}(V)$, respectively, and let $\mathfrak g_\mathcal H=[g_{ij}]$. By Theorem 6.2.4, $F_l^k(\mathcal A)$ is a tensor in $\mathcal T_{s+1}^{r-1}(V)$ that has the components

$$F_l^k(\mathcal A)^{i_1\ldots i_{k-1}i_k}_{j_1\ldots j_{s+1}}=\sum_q g_{j_1q}\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots i_{k-1}q i_{k+1}\ldots i_r}_{j_2\ldots j_{s+1}}$$

with respect to $\mathcal H$. Relabeling indices $(i_k,\ldots,i_{r-1})$ as $(i_{k+1},\ldots,i_r)$, and $(j_1,\ldots,j_{s+1})$ as $(p,j_1,\ldots,j_s)$, yields

$$F_l^k(\mathcal A)^{i_1\ldots i_k\ldots i_r}_{j_1\ldots j_{l-1}p j_{l+1}\ldots j_s}=\sum_q g_{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots i_{k-1}q i_{k+1}\ldots i_r}_{j_1\ldots j_s}. \tag{6.3.5}$$

By Theorem 6.3.4, the tensor $S_l^k(\mathcal B)$ in $\mathcal T_s^r(V)$ has the components

$$S_l^k(\mathcal B)^{i_1\ldots i_r}_{j_1\ldots j_s}=\sum_p g^{i_kp}\mathcal B^{i_1\ldots i_{k-1} \widehat{i_k} i_{k+1}\ldots i_r}_{j_1\ldots j_{l-1}p j_{l+1}\ldots j_s}. \tag{6.3.6}$$

with respect to $\mathcal H$. Substituting $\mathcal B=F_l^k(\mathcal A)$, as given by (6.3.5), into (6.3.6) gives

$$\begin{aligned}
S_l^k\circ F_l^k(\mathcal A)^{i_1\ldots i_r}_{j_1\ldots j_s}
&=\sum_p g^{i_kp}F_l^k(\mathcal A)^{i_1\ldots \widehat{i_k}\ldots i_r}_{j_1\ldots j_{l-1}p j_{l+1}\ldots j_s}\\
&=\sum_p g^{i_kp}\left(\sum_q g_{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots i_{k-1}q i_{k+1}\ldots i_r}_{j_1\ldots j_s}\right)\\
&=\sum_q\left(\sum_p g^{i_kp}g_{pq}\right)\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots i_{k-1}q i_{k+1}\ldots i_r}_{j_1\ldots j_s}\\
&=\sum_q\delta_q^{i_k}\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots i_{k-1}q i_{k+1}\ldots i_r}_{j_1\ldots j_s}\\
&=\mathcal A^{i_1\ldots i_{k-1}i_k i_{k+1}\ldots i_r}_{j_1\ldots j_s}.
\end{aligned}$$

The rest of the proof uses the argument presented in Example 6.3.6.

## 6.4 Representation of Tensors

Section 5.3 was devoted to the topic of representation of tensors over vector spaces. We now extend that discussion to tensors over scalar product spaces.

Let $(V,\mathfrak g)$ be a scalar product space, let $\mathcal H=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)$ be a basis for $V$, let $(\theta^1,\ldots,\theta^m)$ be its dual basis, and let $\mathfrak g_\mathcal H=[g_{ij}]$. Following Section B.5 and Section B.6, we denote by $\operatorname{Mult}(V^s,V)$ the vector space of $\mathbb R$-multilinear maps from $V^s$ to $V$. Let us define a map

$$\widetilde S_s:\operatorname{Mult}(V^s,V)\longrightarrow\mathcal T^0_{s+1}(V),$$

called the **scalar product map**, by

$$\mathcal{S}_s(\Psi)(v_1,\ldots,v_{s+1})=\langle v_1,\Psi(v_2,\ldots,v_{s+1})\rangle \tag{6.4.1}$$

for all maps $\Psi$ in $\operatorname{Mult}(V^s,V)$ and all vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_{s+1}$ in $V$. When $s=1$, we denote

$$\mathcal{S}_1\quad\text{by}\quad\mathcal{S}.$$

### Theorem 6.4.1 (Representation of Tensors). *With the above setup:*

+   (a) $\mathcal{S}_s$ is a linear isomorphism:
+   
+   $$\mathcal{T}^{0}_{s+1}(V)\cong\operatorname{Mult}(V^s,V).$$

+   (b) *For all maps* $\Psi$ *in* $\operatorname{Mult}(V^s,V)$, *the components of the tensor* $\mathcal{S}_s(\Psi)$ *in* $\mathcal{T}^{0}_{s+1}(V)$ *with respect to* $\mathcal{H}$ *are*
+   
+   $$\mathcal{S}_s(\Psi)_{j_1\ldots j_{s+1}}=\sum_i g_{ij_1}\,\theta^i\bigl(\Psi(h_{j_2},\ldots,h_{j_{s+1}})\bigr).$$

+   (c) *For all tensors* $\mathcal{A}$ *in* $\mathcal{T}^{0}_{s+1}(V)$,
+   
+   $$\mathcal{S}_s^{-1}(\mathcal{A})(h_{j_1},\ldots,h_{j_s})=\sum_i \mathcal{S}_1^1(\mathcal{A})_{j_1\ldots j_s i}h_i,$$
+   
+   where $\mathcal{S}_1^1$ is defined in Section 6.3.

+   (d)
+   
+   $$\mathcal{R}_s=\mathcal{S}_1^1\circ\mathcal{S}_s,$$
+   
+   where $\mathcal{R}_s$ is given by (5.3.1).

**Proof.** (a): It is easily shown that $\mathcal{S}_s$ is a linear map. If $\mathcal{S}_s(\Psi)=0$, then $\langle\Psi(v_2,\ldots,v_{s+1}),v_1\rangle=0$ for all vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_{s+1}$ in $V$. Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is nondegenerate, we have $\Psi(v_2,\ldots,v_{s+1})=0$, and because $v_2,\ldots,v_{s+1}$ were arbitrary, $\Psi=0$. Thus, $\ker(\mathcal{S}_s)=\{0\}$. By Theorem 5.1.3(c), $\mathcal{T}^{0}_{s+1}(V)$ has dimension $m^{s+1}$, and it can be shown that $\operatorname{Mult}(V^s,V)$ has the same dimension. It follows from Theorem 1.1.12 and Theorem 1.1.14 that $\mathcal{S}_s$ is a linear isomorphism.

(b): Since $\Psi(h_{j_2},\ldots,h_{j_{s+1}})$ is a vector in $V$, we have from Theorem 1.2.1(d) that

$$\Psi(h_{j_2},\ldots,h_{j_{s+1}})=\sum_i\theta^i\bigl(\Psi(h_{j_2},\ldots,h_{j_{s+1}})\bigr)h_i,$$

and then from (6.4.1) that

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{S}_s(\Psi)_{j_1\ldots j_{s+1}}&=\mathcal{S}_s(\Psi)(h_{j_1},\ldots,h_{j_{s+1}})=\langle h_{j_1},\Psi(h_{j_2},\ldots,h_{j_{s+1}})\rangle\\
&=\left\langle h_{j_1},\sum_i\theta^i\bigl(\Psi(h_{j_2},\ldots,h_{j_{s+1}})\bigr)h_i\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_i g_{ij_1}\,\theta^i\bigl(\Psi(h_{j_2},\ldots,h_{j_{s+1}})\bigr).
\end{aligned}$$

(c): Since 𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜) is a map in Mult(V^s, V), 𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1}) is a vector in V. We have from Theorem 1.2.1(d) that

𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1}) = Σ_i θ^i(𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1})) h_i.  (6.4.2)

Then

𝒜_{j1…js+1} = 𝒜(h_{j1},…,h_{js+1})
= 𝔖_s(𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜))(h_{j1},…,h_{js+1})  [part(a)]
= ⟨h_{j1}, 𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1})⟩  [(6.4.1)]
= ⟨h_{j1}, Σ_i θ^i(𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1})) h_i⟩  [(6.4.2)]
= Σ_i 𝔤_{ij1} θ^i(𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1})),

hence

Σ_{j1} 𝔤^{ij1} 𝒜_{j1…js+1} = Σ_{j1} 𝔤^{ij1} ( Σ_k 𝔤_{kj1} θ^k(𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1})) )
= Σ_k ( Σ_{j1} 𝔤^{ij1} 𝔤_{j1k} ) θ^k(𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1}))
= Σ_k δ^i_k θ^k(𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1}))
= θ^i(𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1})),

so

Σ_{ij1} 𝔤^{ij1} 𝒜_{j1…js+1} h_i = Σ_i θ^i(𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1})) h_i.  (6.4.3)

By Theorem 6.3.4,

S₁¹(𝒜)^i_{j2…js+1} = Σ_{j1} 𝔤^{ij1} 𝒜_{j1…js+1},

hence

Σ_i S₁¹(𝒜)^i_{j2…js+1} h_i = Σ_{ij1} 𝔤^{ij1} 𝒜_{j1…js+1} h_i.  (6.4.4)

Combining (6.4.2)–(6.4.4) yields

𝔖_s⁻¹(𝒜)(h_{j2},…,h_{js+1}) = Σ_i S₁¹(𝒜)^i_{j2…js+1} h_i,

and relabeling indices (j2,…,js+1) as (j1,…,js) gives the result.
