# Tensors on Scalar Product Spaces

## 6.1 Contraction of Tensors

In Section 5.4, we discussed contraction of tensors over vector spaces. In the setting of scalar product spaces, there is more to say.

Theorem 6.1.1 (Orthonormal Basis Expression for Ordinary Contraction). Let (V,g) be a scalar product space with signature (ε₁,…,εₘ), let (e₁,…,eₘ) be an orthonormal basis for V, and let 𝒜 be a tensor in 𝒯ₛ¹(V). Then:

- (a) For integers s ≥ 2 and 1 ≤ l ≤ s − 1, the tensor Cₗ¹(𝒜) in 𝒯ₛ₋₁⁰(V) is given by
- 
- Cₗ¹(𝒜)(v₁,…,vₛ₋₁) = Σᵢ εᵢ⟨ℛₛ⁻¹(𝒜)(v₁,…,vₗ₋₁,eᵢ,vₗ,…,vₛ₋₁), eᵢ⟩
- 
- for all vectors v₁,…,vₛ₋₁ in V, where ℛₛ is given by (5.3.1).

- (b) For s = 1, the real number C₁¹(𝒜) is given by
- 
- C₁¹(𝒜) = Σᵢ εᵢ⟨ℛ⁻¹(𝒜)(eᵢ), eᵢ⟩,
- 
- where ℛ is given by (5.3.2).

Proof. (a): By Theorem 5.4.5, the components of Cₗ¹(𝒜) with respect to (e₁,…,eₘ) are

Cₗ¹(𝒜)(eⱼ₁,…,eⱼₛ₋₁) = Cₗ¹(𝒜)ⱼ₁…ⱼₛ₋₁ = Σₚ 𝒜ⱼ₁…ⱼₗ₋₁ p p jₗ…ⱼₛ₋₁. (6.1.1)

We have from Theorem 5.3.1(c) that

𝔅ₛ⁻¹(𝒜)(e_{j₁},…,e_{j_{i-1}},e_p,e_{j_i},…,e_{j_{s-1}}) = Σ_q 𝒜_{j₁…j_{i-1}p j_i…j_{s-1}}^q e_q,

hence

⟨𝔅ₛ⁻¹(𝒜)(e_{j₁},…,e_{j_{i-1}},e_p,e_{j_i},…,e_{j_{s-1}}), e_p⟩ = ⟨Σ_q 𝒜_{j₁…j_{i-1}p j_i…j_{s-1}}^q e_q, e_p⟩
= Σ_q 𝒜_{j₁…j_{i-1}p j_i…j_{s-1}}^q ⟨e_q, e_p⟩
= ε_p 𝒜_{j₁…j_{i-1}p j_i…j_{s-1}}^p,

so

𝒜_{j₁…j_{i-1}p j_i…j_{s-1}}^p = ε_p ⟨𝔅ₛ⁻¹(𝒜)(e_{j₁},…,e_{j_{i-1}},e_p,e_{j_i},…,e_{j_{s-1}}), e_p⟩. (6.1.2)

Then (6.1.1) and (6.1.2) yield

C_l¹(𝒜)(e_{j₁},…,e_{j_{s-1}}) = Σ_p ε_p ⟨𝔅ₛ⁻¹(𝒜)(e_{j₁},…,e_{j_{i-1}},e_p,e_{j_i},…,e_{j_{s-1}}), e_p⟩.

The result for arbitrary vectors v₁,…,v_{s-1} in V follows from the multilinearity of C_l¹(𝒜) and 𝔅ₛ⁻¹(𝒜).

(b): We have

Σ_i ε_i ⟨𝔅⁻¹(𝒜)(e_i), e_i⟩ = Σ_i ε_i ⟨Σ_j 𝒜_i^j e_j, e_i⟩ [Th 5.3.2(c)]
= Σ_{ij} ε_i ⟨e_j, e_i⟩ 𝒜_i^j = Σ_i 𝒜_i^i
= C₁¹(𝒜). [Th 5.4.2]

## 6.2 Flat Maps

Let (V,𝔤) be a scalar product space, let ℋ = (h₁,…,h_m) be a basis for V, and let (θ¹,…,θ^m) be its dual basis. Recall from (5.1.1) that 𝒯₀¹(V) = V and 𝒯₁⁰(V) = V*. The flat map of Section 3.3 can therefore be expressed as

F : 𝒯₀¹(V) ⟶ 𝒯₁⁰(V).

More generally, let 1 ≤ k ≤ r and 1 ≤ l ≤ s + 1 be integers (so that r ≥ 1 and s ≥ 0). The (k,l)-flat map is denoted by

F_l^k : 𝒯_s^r(V) ⟶ 𝒯_{s+1}^{r-1}(V)
and defined for all tensors 𝒜 in 𝒯_s^r(V), all covectors η¹,…,η^{r-1} in V*, and all vectors v₁,…,v_{s+1} in V as follows, where ^ indicates that an expression is omitted:

[F1] For r = 1 (so that k = 1):

F_l^1(𝒜)(v₁, ..., v_{s+1}) = 𝒜(v_l^F, v₁, ..., \widehat{v_l}, ..., v_{s+1}).

[F2] For r ≥ 2 and 1 ≤ k ≤ r − 1:

F_l^k(𝒜)(η¹, ..., η^{r−1}, v₁, ..., v_{s+1}) = 𝒜(η¹, ..., η^{k−1}, v_l^F, η^k, ..., η^{r−1}, v₁, ..., \widehat{v_l}, ..., v_{s+1}).

[F3] For r ≥ 2 and k = r:

F_l^r(𝒜)(η¹, ..., η^{r−1}, v₁, ..., v_{s+1}) = 𝒜(η¹, ..., η^{r−1}, v_l^F, v₁, ..., \widehat{v_l}, ..., v_{s+1}).

The computational procedure is most transparent for [F2]: extract the vector in the lth covariant position, flat it, and insert the result in the kth contravariant position.

Example 6.2.1. To illustrate [F1], let r = 1 (so that k = 1) and s = 0 (so that l = 1), and let w be a vector in 𝒯₀¹(V) = V. Then the covector F₁¹(w) in 𝒯₁⁰(V) = V* is given by

F₁¹(w)(v) = w(v^F) for all vectors v in V. We have from (1.2.3) and (3.3.1) that w(v^F) = v^F(w) = ⟨v, w⟩ = ⟨w, v⟩ = w^F(v) = F(w)(v), hence F₁¹(w)(v) = F(w)(v). Since v and w were arbitrary, F₁¹ = F.

To illustrate [F2], let r = 2 (so that k = 1) and s = 0 (so that l = 1), and let 𝒜 be a tensor in 𝒯₀²(V). Then the tensor F₁¹(𝒜) in 𝒯₁¹(V) is given by F₁¹(𝒜)(η, v) = 𝒜(v^F, η).

To illustrate [F3], let r = 2 (so that k = 2) and s = 0 (so that l = 1), and let 𝒜 be a tensor in 𝒯₀²(V). Then the tensor F₁²(𝒜) in 𝒯₁¹(V) is given by F₁²(𝒜)(η, v) = 𝒜(η, v^F).

Example 6.2.2. Let 𝒜 be a tensor in 𝒯₁¹(V). For r = s = 1, there are flat maps for k = 1 and l = 1,2. For k = 1 and l = 1, the tensor F₁¹(𝒜) in 𝒯₂⁰(V) is given by F₁¹(𝒜)(v₁, v₂) = 𝒜(v₁^F, v₂). For k = 1 and l = 2, the tensor F₂¹(𝒜) in 𝒯₂⁰(V) is given by F₂¹(𝒜)(v₁, v₂) = 𝒜(v₂^F, v₁).

Example 6.2.3. For a tensor 𝒜 in 𝒯₂²(V), the tensor F₁²(𝒜) in 𝒯₃¹(V) is given by

F₁²(𝒜)(η¹, v₁, v₂, v₃) = 𝒜(η¹, v₁^F, v₂, v₃).

By Theorem 3.3.1(a), hⱼ^F = Σₚ gⱼₚ θᵖ, so F₁²(𝒜) has the components

```
F₁²(𝒜)ⁱ_{jkl} = F₁²(𝒜)(θⁱ, hⱼ, hₖ, hₗ) = 𝒜(θⁱ, hⱼ^F, hₖ, hₗ)
= 𝒜(θⁱ, Σₚ gⱼₚ θᵖ, hₖ, hₗ) = Σₚ gⱼₚ 𝒜(θⁱ, θᵖ, hₖ, hₗ)
= Σₚ gⱼₚ 𝒜ⁱᵖ_{kl}
```

with respect to ℋ.

Theorem 6.2.4. Let (V, g) be a scalar product space, let ℋ be a basis for V, and let g_ℋ = [gᵢⱼ]. Let 𝒜 be a tensor in 𝒯ₛʳ(V), and let 1 ≤ k ≤ r and 1 ≤ l ≤ s+1 be integers. Then the tensor Fₗᵏ(𝒜) in 𝒯_{s+1}^{r-1}(V) has the components

```
Fₗᵏ(𝒜)^{i₁...i_{k-1} p i_k...i_{r-1}}_{j₁...j_{l-1} j_{l+1}...j_{s+1}} = Σₚ gⱼₗₚ 𝒜^{i₁...i_{k-1} p i_k...i_{r-1}}_{j₁...ĵₗ...j_{s+1}}
```

with respect to ℋ.

Proof. The proof is an elaboration of Example 6.2.3. We consider only the case [F2]. The tensor Fₗᵏ(𝒜) in 𝒯_{s+1}^{r-1}(V) is given by

```
Fₗᵏ(𝒜)(η¹,...,η^{r-1}, v₁,...,v_{s+1})
= 𝒜(η¹,...,η^{k-1}, vₗ^F, η^k,...,η^{r-1}, v₁,...,v̂ₗ,...,v_{s+1})
```

for all covectors η¹,...,η^{r-1} in V* and all vectors v₁,...,v_{s+1} in V. Let ℋ = (h₁,...,h_m), and let (θ¹,...,θ^m) be its dual basis. By Theorem 3.3.1(a), hⱼ^F = Σₚ gⱼₚ θᵖ, so Fₗᵏ(𝒜) has the components

```
Fₗᵏ(𝒜)^{i₁...i_{r-1}}_{j₁...j_{s+1}} = Fₗᵏ(𝒜)(θ^{i₁},...,θ^{i_k},...,θ^{i_{r-1}}, h_{j₁},...,h_{j_{s+1}})
= 𝒜(θ^{i₁},...,θ^{i_{k-1}}, h_{jₗ}^F, θ^{i_k},...,θ^{i_{r-1}}, h_{j₁},...,h_{j_{l-1}}, h_{j_{l+1}},...,h_{j_{s+1}})
= 𝒜(θ^{i₁},...,θ^{i_{k-1}}, Σₚ gⱼₗₚ θᵖ, θ^{i_k},...,θ^{i_{r-1}}, h_{j₁},...,h_{j_{l-1}}, h_{j_{l+1}},...,h_{j_{s+1}})
= Σₚ gⱼₗₚ 𝒜(θ^{i₁},...,θ^{i_{k-1}}, θᵖ, θ^{i_k},...,θ^{i_{r-1}}, h_{j₁},...,h_{j_{l-1}}, h_{j_{l+1}},...,h_{j_{s+1}})
= Σₚ gⱼₗₚ 𝒜^{i₁...i_{k-1} p i_k...i_{r-1}}_{j₁...ĵₗ...j_{s+1}}
```

with respect to ℋ.

## 6.2 Flat Maps

**Theorem 6.2.5.**

Let $(V,\mathfrak g)$ be a scalar product space, let $\mathcal H$ be a basis for $V$, and let $\mathfrak g=[g_{ij}]$. Let $\mathcal A$ be a tensor in $T^r_s(V)$, and let $r\ge 2$ and $s>0$ be integers. Then:

- (a) For integers $1\le k<l\le r$ and $1\le n\le s$, the components of the tensor $C_n^k\circ F_n^l(\mathcal A)$ in $T^{r-2}_{s}(V)$ with respect to $\mathcal H$ are

$$C_n^k\circ F_n^l(\mathcal A)^{i_1\cdots i_{r-2}}_{j_1\cdots j_s}=\sum_{pq}g_{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}\,p\,i_{k}\cdots i_{l-2}\,q\,i_{l-1}\cdots i_{r-2}}_{j_1\cdots j_s}. \tag{6.2.1}$$

- (b) For integers $1\le k\le r$ and $1\le n\le s$, the components of the tensor $C_n^k\circ F_n^k(\mathcal A)$ in $T^{r-2}_{s}(V)$ with respect to $\mathcal H$ are

$$C_n^k\circ F_n^k(\mathcal A)^{i_1\cdots i_{r-2}}_{j_1\cdots j_s}=C_n^k\circ F_n^{k+1}(\mathcal A)^{i_1\cdots i_{r-2}}_{j_1\cdots j_s}$$
$$=\sum_{pq}g_{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}\,p\,q\,i_k\cdots i_{r-2}}_{j_1\cdots j_s}. \tag{6.2.2}$$

**Proof.** By Theorem 6.2.4, the components of the tensor $F_n^l(\mathcal A)$ in $T^{r-1}_{s+1}(V)$ with respect to $\mathcal H$ are

$$F_n^l(\mathcal A)^{i_1\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots j_{s+1}}=\sum_q g_{j_nq}\mathcal A^{i_1\cdots i_{l-1}\,q\,i_l\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots \widehat{j_n}\cdots j_{s+1}}. \tag{6.2.3}$$

(a): We have

$$C_n^k\circ F_n^l(\mathcal A)^{i_1\cdots\widehat{i_k}\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots\widehat{j_n}\cdots j_{s+1}}=\sum_pF_n^l(\mathcal A)^{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}\,p\,i_{k+1}\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots j_{n-1}\,p\,j_{n+1}\cdots j_{s+1}}$$

$$=\sum_p\left(\sum_qg_{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}\,p\,i_{k+1}\cdots i_{l-1}\,q\,i_l\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots\widehat{j_n}\cdots j_{s+1}}\right)$$

$$=\sum_{pq}g_{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}\,p\,i_{k+1}\cdots i_{l-1}\,q\,i_l\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots\widehat{j_n}\cdots j_{s+1}}.$$

Relabeling indices $(i_{k+1},\ldots,i_{l-1},q,i_l,\ldots,i_{r-1})$ as $(i_k,\ldots,i_{l-2},q,i_{l-1},\ldots,i_{r-2})$, and $(j_{n+1},\ldots,j_{s+1})$ as $(j_n,\ldots,j_s)$, gives the result.

(b): We have

$$C_n^k\circ F_n^k(\mathcal A)^{i_1\cdots\widehat{i_k}\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots\widehat{j_n}\cdots j_{s+1}}=\sum_pF_n^k(\mathcal A)^{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}\,p\,i_{k+1}\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots j_{n-1}\,p\,j_{n+1}\cdots j_{s+1}}$$

$$=\sum_p\left(\sum_qg_{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}\,q\,p\,i_{k+1}\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots\widehat{j_n}\cdots j_{s+1}}\right)$$

$$=\sum_{pq}g_{pq}\mathcal A^{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}\,q\,p\,i_{k+1}\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots\widehat{j_n}\cdots j_{s+1}}$$

$$=\sum_{pq}g_{qp}\mathcal A^{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}\,p\,q\,i_{k+1}\cdots i_{r-1}}_{j_1\cdots\widehat{j_n}\cdots j_{s+1}}.$$
