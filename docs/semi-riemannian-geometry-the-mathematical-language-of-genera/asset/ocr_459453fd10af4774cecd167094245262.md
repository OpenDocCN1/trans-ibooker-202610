Proof. (a), (b): In view of preceding remarks, each $h_{i_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes h_{i_r} \otimes \theta^{j_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \theta^{j_s}$ is in $\mathcal{T}_s^r(V)$. Let $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}$ be the tensor in $\mathcal{T}_s^r(V)$ defined by

$\widetilde{\mathcal{A}} = \sum_{\substack{1\le i_1,\dots,i_r\le m \\ 1\le j_1,\dots,j_s\le m}} \mathcal{A}^{i_1\dots i_r}_{j_1\dots j_s} h_{i_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes h_{i_r} \otimes \theta^{j_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \theta^{j_s}.$

Then

$\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(\theta^{i_1},\dots,\theta^{i_r},h_{j_1},\dots,h_{j_s}) = \sum_{\substack{1\le k_1,\dots,k_r\le m \\ 1\le l_1,\dots,l_s\le m}} \mathcal{A}^{k_1\dots k_r}_{l_1\dots l_s} h_{k_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes h_{k_r} \otimes \theta^{l_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \theta^{l_s} (\theta^{i_1},\dots,\theta^{i_r},h_{j_1},\dots,h_{j_s}) = \sum_{\substack{1\le k_1,\dots,k_r\le m \\ 1\le l_1,\dots,l_s\le m}} \mathcal{A}^{k_1\dots k_r}_{l_1\dots l_s} \delta^{i_1}_{k_1}\cdots\delta^{i_r}_{k_r}\delta^{l_1}_{j_1}\cdots\delta^{l_s}_{j_s} = \mathcal{A}^{i_1\dots i_r}_{j_1\dots j_s} = \mathcal{A}(\theta^{i_1},\dots,\theta^{i_r},h_{j_1},\dots,h_{j_s}).$

Since $\mathcal{H}$ and $\Theta$ are bases for $V$ and $V^*$, respectively, and $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ are multilinear, it follows that $\mathcal{A}=\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}$. Thus, the $h_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{i_r}\otimes\theta^{j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\theta^{j_s}$ span $\mathcal{T}_s^r(V)$. If $\sum_{\substack{1\le i_1,\dots,i_r\le m \\ 1\le j_1,\dots,j_s\le m}} a^{i_1\dots i_r}_{j_1\dots j_s} h_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{i_r}\otimes\theta^{j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\theta^{j_s}=0$ for some real numbers $a^{i_1\dots i_r}_{j_1\dots j_s}$, then applying both sides of the preceding identity to $(\theta^{k_1},\dots,\theta^{k_r},h_{l_1},\dots,h_{l_s})$ yields $a^{k_1\dots k_r}_{l_1\dots l_s}=0$. Thus, the $h_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{i_r}\otimes\theta^{j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\theta^{j_s}$ are linearly independent. (c): This follows from part (a).

We see from Theorem 5.1.3 that a tensor is completely determined by its components with respect to a given basis. In fact, this is the classical way of defining a tensor. Choosing another basis generally yields different values for the components. The next result shows how to convert components when changing from one basis to another.

Then
