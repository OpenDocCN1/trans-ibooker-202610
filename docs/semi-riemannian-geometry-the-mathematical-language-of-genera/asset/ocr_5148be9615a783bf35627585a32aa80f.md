Let $(M,\mathbf{g})$ be a regular surface in $\mathbb{R}^3_\nu$, let $(U,\varphi)$ be a chart on $M$, and let $\mathcal{H}=(H_1,H_2)$ and $G$ be the corresponding coordinate frame and coordinate unit normal vector field. In keeping with earlier notation for a vector-valued map, we denote

$$\frac{\partial H_i}{\partial r^j}(q)\qquad\text{by}\qquad\left.\frac{\partial H_i}{\partial r^j}\right|_q$$

for all $q$ in $U$ for $i,j=1,2$. It follows from Theorem 8.4.10(b) and Theorem 12.2.11 that $(G_q,H_1|_q,H_2|_q)$ is a basis for $\mathbb{R}^3_\nu$. Then $(\partial H_i/\partial r^j)$ can be expressed as

$$\frac{\partial H_i}{\partial r^j}=\sum_k\Gamma_{ij}^k H_k+\vartheta_{ij}G,\qquad(12.3.6)$$

where the $\Gamma_{ij}^k$, called the Christoffel symbols, and the $\vartheta_{ij}$ are uniquely determined functions on $U$ for $i,j,k=1,2.$

# Theorem 12.3.3. With the above setup, for $i,j,k=1,2$:

+   (a) $\Gamma_{ij}^k$ and $\vartheta_{ij}$ are functions in $C^\infty(U)$.
+   (b)

$$\Gamma_{ij}^k=\frac12\sum_l\mathfrak{g}^{kl}\left(\frac{\partial\mathfrak{g}_{jl}}{\partial r^i}+\frac{\partial\mathfrak{g}_{il}}{\partial r^j}-\frac{\partial\mathfrak{g}_{ij}}{\partial r^l}\right).$$

+   (c)

$$\vartheta_{ij}=\epsilon_M\left\langle\frac{\partial H_i}{\partial r^j},G\right\rangle.$$

+   (d) $\Gamma_{ij}^k$ and $\vartheta_{ij}$ are symmetric in $i,j$; that is,

$$\Gamma_{ij}^k=\Gamma_{ji}^k\qquad\text{and}\qquad\vartheta_{ij}=\vartheta_{ji}.$$

Proof. (b): It follows from (12.3.6) that

$$\left\langle\frac{\partial H_i}{\partial r^j},H_k\right\rangle=\left\langle\sum_n\Gamma_{ij}^n H_n+\vartheta_{ij}G,H_k\right\rangle=\sum_n\Gamma_{ij}^n\langle H_k,H_n\rangle=\sum_n\mathfrak{g}_{kn}\Gamma_{ij}^n,$$

and then from $\mathfrak{g}_{ij}=\langle H_i,H_j\rangle$ that

$$\frac{\partial\mathfrak{g}_{ij}}{\partial r^k}=\left\langle\frac{\partial H_i}{\partial r^k},H_j\right\rangle+\left\langle H_i,\frac{\partial H_j}{\partial r^k}\right\rangle=\sum_n\left(\mathfrak{g}_{jn}\Gamma_{ik}^n+\mathfrak{g}_{in}\Gamma_{jk}^n\right).\qquad(12.3.7)$$

Thus,

$$\frac{\partial\mathfrak{g}_{jl}}{\partial r^i}=\sum_n\left(\mathfrak{g}_{ln}\Gamma_{ij}^n+\mathfrak{g}_{jn}\Gamma_{il}^n\right)$$

$$\frac{\partial\mathfrak{g}_{il}}{\partial r^j}=\sum_n\left(\mathfrak{g}_{ln}\Gamma_{ij}^n+\mathfrak{g}_{in}\Gamma_{jl}^n\right)$$
