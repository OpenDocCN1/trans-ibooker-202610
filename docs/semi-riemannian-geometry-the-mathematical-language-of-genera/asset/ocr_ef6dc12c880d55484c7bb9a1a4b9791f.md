Arguing as in the proof of Theorem 12.7.4, we assume without loss of generality that the coordinate domain of each chart in $\mathcal B$ is a connected set in $M$. Let $(U,(x^1,x^2,\ldots,x^m))$ be a chart in $\mathcal B$, and let $d(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^m)$ be the corresponding dual coordinate frame. Then $\varpi_U$ and $d(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^m)$ are orientation forms on $U$. Viewing $U$ as a smooth $m$-manifold, it follows from Theorem 16.6.1 that there is a uniquely determined function $f$ in $C^\infty(U)$ such that $\varpi|_U=f\,d(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^m)$, with $f$ either strictly positive or strictly negative. In the former case, $\varpi|_U$ and $d(x^1)\wedge d(x^2)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^m)$ are consistent, and we include $(U,(x^1,x^2,\ldots,x^m))$ in $\mathcal A$. In the latter case, $\varpi|_U$ and $d(-x^1)\wedge d(x^2)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^m)$ are consistent, and we include $(U,(-x^1,x^2,\ldots,x^m))$ in $\mathcal A$. By construction, the dual coordinate frames corresponding to any pair of overlapping charts in $\mathcal A$ are consistent with a restriction of $\varpi$, hence are consistent with each other. It follows from Theorem 16.6.2 that any pair of overlapping charts in $\mathcal A$ is consistent. Thus, $\mathcal A$ is a consistent smooth atlas for $M$.

Let $p$ be a point in $M$, let $(U,(x^1,\ldots,x^m))$ be a chart in $\mathcal A$ at $p$, and let $\mathcal X=(\partial/\partial x^1,\ldots,\partial/\partial x^m)$ and $(d(x^1),\ldots,d(x^m))$ be the corresponding coordinate and dual coordinate frames. From the above construction, there is a uniquely determined strictly positive function $f$ in $C^\infty(U)$ such that $\varpi|_U=f\,d(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^m)$. Then Theorem 7.2.9 gives

$$\varpi_p\left(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^1}\right|_p,\ldots,\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^m}\right|_p\right)=f(p)\,d_p(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d_p(x^m)\left(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^1}\right|_p,\ldots,\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^m}\right|_p\right)$$

$$=f(p)>0.$$

Thus, $\mathcal X_p$ is in $\mathcal D(p)$, or equivalently, $\mathcal D(p)=[\mathcal X_p]$. Since $p$ and $(U,(x^1,\ldots,x^m))$ were arbitrary, $\mathcal D$ is the orientation induced by $\mathcal A$.
