
For a covector field \(\omega\) in \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)\) and a vector field \(Y\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), we have

\[
X(\mathcal{A}(\omega,Y))
= D_0^0(\mathcal{A}(\omega,Y))
= D_0^0(C(Y\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega))
= C(D_2^2(Y\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega))
= C(D_1^0(Y)\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega + Y\otimes D_1^1(\mathcal{A})\otimes\omega + Y\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes D_1^0(\omega))
= C(D_1^0(Y)\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega) + C(Y\otimes D_1^1(\mathcal{A})\otimes\omega) + C(Y\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes D_1^0(\omega))
= \mathcal{A}(\omega,D_0^1(Y)) + D_1^1(\mathcal{A})(\omega,Y) + \mathcal{A}(D_1^0(\omega),Y),
\]

where the second and sixth equalities follow from the manifold version of Example 5.4.6, and the third equality from [D2]. Thus,

\[
D_1^1(\mathcal{A})(\omega,Y)
= X(\mathcal{A}(\omega,Y)) - \mathcal{A}(D_1^0(\omega),Y) - \mathcal{A}(\omega,D_0^1(Y)).
\]

Observe that in the preceding computations, we implicitly used Theorem 15.7.2 to identify \(\mathcal{A}\) with a map in \(\operatorname{Mult}_{C^\infty(M)}(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)\times\mathfrak{X}(M),C^\infty(M))\). The next result generalizes Example 16.2.3 using the same approach.

### Theorem 16.2.4 (Global Expression for Tensor Derivation).

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold, let \(D\) be a tensor derivation on \(M\), and let \(X = D_0^0\) be the vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\) given by Theorem 16.2.2(a). Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a tensor field in \(\mathcal{T}_s^r(M)\), let \(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r\) be covector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)\), and let \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_s\) be vector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_s^r(\mathcal{A})(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s)
&= X(\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s)) \\
&\quad -\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^{i-1},D_1^0(\omega^i),\omega^{i+1},\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s) \\
&\quad -\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_{j-1},D_0^1(Y_j),Y_{j+1},\ldots,Y_s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16.2.1}
\]

**Proof.** The proof is an elaboration of Example 16.2.3. We have

\[
\begin{aligned}
X(\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s))
&= D_0^0(\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s)) \\
&= D_0^0(C(Y_1\otimes\cdots\otimes Y_s\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega^1\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega^r)) \\
&= C(D_{s+r}^{s+r}(Y_1\otimes\cdots\otimes Y_s\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega^1\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega^r)) \\
&= C\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{s}Y_1\otimes\cdots\otimes D_0^1(Y_j)\otimes\cdots\otimes Y_s\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega^1\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega^r \\
&\qquad + Y_1\otimes\cdots\otimes Y_s\otimes D_s^r(\mathcal{A})\otimes\omega^1\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega^r \\
&\qquad + \sum_{i=1}^{r}Y_1\otimes\cdots\otimes Y_s\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega^1\otimes\cdots\otimes D_1^0(\omega^i)\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega^r\Bigr) \\
&= \sum_{j=1}^{s}C(Y_1\otimes\cdots\otimes D_0^1(Y_j)\otimes\cdots\otimes Y_s\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega^1\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega^r) \\
&\quad + C(Y_1\otimes\cdots\otimes Y_s\otimes D_s^r(\mathcal{A})\otimes\omega^1\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega^r) \\
&\quad + \sum_{i=1}^{r}C(Y_1\otimes\cdots\otimes Y_s\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega^1\otimes\cdots\otimes D_1^0(\omega^i)\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega^r) \\
&= \sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_{j-1},D_0^1(Y_j),Y_{j+1},\ldots,Y_s) \\
&\quad + D_s^r(\mathcal{A})(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s) \\
&\quad + \sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^{i-1},D_1^0(\omega^i),\omega^{i+1},\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the second and sixth equalities follow from Theorem 15.14.4, and the third equality from [D2]. The result follows.

### Example 16.2.5.

For a covector field \(\omega\) in \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)=\mathcal{T}_1^0(M)\) and a vector field \(Y\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), setting \(r=0\) and \(s=1\) in Theorem 16.2.4 gives

\[
D_1^0(\omega)(Y)=X(\omega(Y))-\omega(D_0^1(Y)),
\tag{16.2.2}
\]

where \(X=D_0^0\).

### Theorem 16.2.6.

A tensor derivation \(D\) on a smooth manifold \(M\) is uniquely determined by its values on \(C^\infty(M)\) and \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\).

**Proof.** We see from Theorem 16.2.4 that \(D\) is uniquely determined by its values on \(C^\infty(M)\), \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), and \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)\). However, (16.2.2) shows that the values of \(D\) on \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)\) are uniquely determined by its values on \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\).

### Theorem 16.2.7 (Existence of Tensor Derivation).

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold, let \(X\) be a vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), and let

\[
\mathcal{D}_X:\mathfrak{X}(M)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
\]

be a linear map such that

\[
\mathcal{D}_X(fY)=X(f)Y+f\mathcal{D}_X(Y)
\tag{16.2.3}
\]

for all functions \(f\) in \(C^\infty(M)\) and all vector fields \(Y\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\). Then there is a unique tensor derivation \(D\) on \(M\) such that

\[
D_0^0=X \quad\text{and}\quad D_0^1=\mathcal{D}_X.
\tag{16.2.4}
\]

**Proof.** Combining (16.2.6) and (16.2.7) gives

\[
D_1^0(\omega)(fY+Z)=fD_1^0(\omega)(Y)+D_1^0(\omega)(Z),
\]

which proves the claim.

The next step is to define a map

\[
D_1^0:\mathfrak{X}^*(M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lin}_{C^\infty(M)}(\mathfrak{X}(M),C^\infty(M))
\tag{16.2.8}
\]

by the assignment

\[
\omega\longmapsto D_1^0(\omega)
\]

for all covector fields \(\omega\) in \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)\). We have \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)=\mathcal{T}_1^0(M)\), and by Theorem 15.5.3, \(\operatorname{Lin}_{C^\infty(M)}(\mathfrak{X}(M),C^\infty(M))\) can be identified with \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)\). Thus, (16.2.8) can be expressed as \(D_1^0:\mathcal{T}_1^0(M)\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_1^0(M)\), which is what we need for a tensor derivation corresponding to \((r,s)=(0,1)\).

It remains to show that [D1] and [D2] are satisfied. Let \(f\) be a function in \(C^\infty(M)\), let \(\omega\) be a covector field in \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)\), and let \(Y\) be a vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{0+1}^{0+0}(f\otimes\omega)(Y)
&= D_1^0(f\omega)(Y) \\
&= X(f\omega(Y))-f\omega(\mathcal{D}_X(Y)) \qquad [(16.2.5)] \\
&= X(f)\omega(Y)+fX(\omega(Y))-f\omega(\mathcal{D}_X(Y)) \qquad [(15.1.3)] \\
&= D_0^0(f)\omega(Y)+fD_1^0(\omega)(Y) \qquad [(16.2.4), (16.2.5)] \\
&= (D_0^0(f)\otimes\omega+f\otimes D_1^0(\omega))(Y).
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(Y\) was arbitrary,

\[
D_{0+1}^{0+0}(f\otimes\omega)=D_0^0(f)\otimes\omega+f\otimes D_1^0(\omega),
\]

hence [D1] is satisfied. For \((r,s)=(0,1)\), no contractions are possible, so [D2] is trivially satisfied.

**Uniqueness.** It follows from (16.2.2) and (16.2.4) that (16.2.5) is the only option for defining \(D_1^0\).

## 16.3 Form Derivations

A form derivation on a smooth manifold \(M\) is a family \(\mathfrak{D}\) of linear maps

\[
\mathfrak{D}_s:\Lambda^s(M)\longrightarrow\Lambda^s(M)
\]

defined for \(s\ge 0\) such that

\[
\mathfrak{D}_{s+s'}(\omega\wedge\xi)=\mathfrak{D}_s(\omega)\wedge\xi+\omega\wedge\mathfrak{D}_{s'}(\xi)
\tag{16.3.1}
\]

for all forms \(\omega\) in \(\Lambda^s(M)\) and \(\xi\) in \(\Lambda^{s'}(M)\).

### Theorem 16.3.1.

If \(M\) is a smooth manifold and \(\mathfrak{D}\) is a form derivation on \(M\), then, in local coordinates,

\[
\mathfrak{D}_s(d(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^s))=\sum_i d(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathfrak{D}_1(d(x^i))\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^s).
\]

**Proof.** The proof is by induction. For \(s=1\), the result is trivial. Let \(s>1\), and suppose the assertion is true for all indices \(<s\). We have from (16.3.1) that

\[
\mathfrak{D}_s(d(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^s))
=\mathfrak{D}_1(d(x^1))\wedge d(x^2)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^s)
+d(x^1)\wedge\mathfrak{D}_{s-1}(d(x^2)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^s)).
\tag{16.3.2}
\]

By the induction hypothesis,

\[
\mathfrak{D}_{s-1}(d(x^2)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^s))
=\sum_{i=2}^{s}d(x^2)\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathfrak{D}_1(d(x^i))\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^s),
\]

hence

\[
d(x^1)\wedge\mathfrak{D}_{s-1}(d(x^2)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^s))
=\sum_{i=2}^{s}d(x^1)\wedge d(x^2)\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathfrak{D}_1(d(x^i))\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^s).
\tag{16.3.3}
\]

Combining (16.3.2) and (16.3.3) gives the result for \(s\).

### Theorem 16.3.2.

A form derivation \(\mathfrak{D}\) on a smooth manifold \(M\) is uniquely determined by its values on \(C^\infty(M)\) and \(\{d(f):f\in C^\infty(M)\}\).

**Proof.** Let \(\dim(M)=m\), let \(\omega\) be a form in \(\Lambda^s(M)\), and, in local coordinates, let

\[
\omega=\sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_s\le m}\alpha_{i_1\ldots i_s}d(x^{i_1})\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^{i_s}).
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak{D}_s(\omega)
&=\sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_s\le m}\mathfrak{D}_{0+s}(\alpha_{i_1\ldots i_s}d(x^{i_1})\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^{i_s})) \\
&=\sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_s\le m}\Bigl[\mathfrak{D}_0(\alpha_{i_1\ldots i_s})\wedge d(x^{i_1})\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^{i_s})+\alpha_{i_1\ldots i_s}\wedge\mathfrak{D}_s(d(x^{i_1})\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^{i_s}))\Bigr] \\
&=\sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_s\le m}\Bigl[\mathfrak{D}_0(\alpha_{i_1\ldots i_s})d(x^{i_1})\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^{i_s})+\alpha_{i_1\ldots i_s}\sum_{j=1}^{s}d(x^{i_1})\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathfrak{D}_1(d(x^{i_j}))\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^{i_s})\Bigr],
\end{aligned}
\]

where the second equality follows from (16.3.1), and the third equality from Theorem 16.3.1. The result follows.

## 16.4 Lie Derivative

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold, let \(X\) be a vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), and consider the map \(\mathcal{L}_X:\mathfrak{X}(M)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)\) defined by \(\mathcal{L}_X(Y)=[X,Y]\) for all vector fields \(Y\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\). By parts (c) and (d) of Theorem 15.3.2, \(\mathcal{L}_X\) is linear and satisfies

\[
\mathcal{L}_X(fY)=X(f)Y+f\mathcal{L}_X(Y)
\]

for all functions \(f\) in \(C^\infty(M)\) and all vector fields \(Y\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\). It follows from Theorem 16.2.7 that there is a unique tensor derivation

\[
\mathcal{L}_X:\mathcal{T}_s^r(M)\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_s^r(M)
\]

on \(M\), called the **Lie derivative with respect to \(X\)**, such that

\[
\mathcal{L}_X(f)=X(f) \quad\text{and}\quad \mathcal{L}_X(Y)=[X,Y]
\tag{16.4.1}
\]

for all functions \(f\) in \(C^\infty(M)\) and all vector fields \(Y\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), where \((\mathcal{L}_X)_s^r\) has been abbreviated to \(\mathcal{L}_X\).

The Lie derivative enjoys a wealth of algebraic properties.

### Theorem 16.4.1 (Vector Field Properties of Lie Derivative).

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold, let \(X,Y,Z\) be vector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), and let \(f,g\) be functions in \(C^\infty(M)\). Then:

- (a) \(\mathcal{L}_X(Y)=-\mathcal{L}_Y(X)\).
- (b) \(\mathcal{L}_{X+Y}(Z)=\mathcal{L}_X(Z)+\mathcal{L}_Y(Z)\).
- (c) \(\mathcal{L}_X(Y+Z)=\mathcal{L}_X(Y)+\mathcal{L}_X(Z)\).
- (d) \(\mathcal{L}_{fX}(gY)=fg\mathcal{L}_X(Y)+fX(g)Y-gY(f)X\).
- (e) \(\mathcal{L}_X([Y,Z])=[\mathcal{L}_X(Y),Z]+[Y,\mathcal{L}_X(Z)]\).

**Proof.** This follows from Theorem 15.3.2.

We note that Jacobi’s identity in part (e) of Theorem 15.3.2 gives rise to a product rule for Lie brackets in part (e) of the above theorem. The next result contains a product rule for tensor products, and the one after that a product rule for wedge products.

### Theorem 16.4.2 (Tensor Field Properties of Lie Derivative).

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold, let \(X\) be a vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), let \(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\) and \(\mathcal{C}\) be tensor fields in \(\mathcal{T}_s^r(M)\) and \(\mathcal{T}_{s'}^{r'}(M)\), respectively, and let \(f\) be a function in \(C^\infty(M)\). Then:

- (a) \(\mathcal{L}_X(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B})=\mathcal{L}_X(\mathcal{A})+\mathcal{L}_X(\mathcal{B})\).
- (b) \(\mathcal{L}_X(f\mathcal{A})=X(f)\mathcal{A}+f\mathcal{L}_X(\mathcal{A})\).
- (c) \(\mathcal{L}_X(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{L}_X(\mathcal{A})\otimes\mathcal{C}+\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}_X(\mathcal{C})\).

**Proof.** This follows from the properties of \(\mathcal{L}_X\) as a tensor derivation on \(M\).

### Theorem 16.4.3 (Form Derivation Property of Lie Derivative).

If \(M\) is a smooth manifold and \(X\) is a vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), then \(\mathcal{L}_X\) is a form derivation; that is,

\[
\mathcal{L}_X(\omega\wedge\xi)=\mathcal{L}_X(\omega)\wedge\xi+\omega\wedge\mathcal{L}_X(\xi)
\]

for all forms \(\omega\) in \(\Lambda^s(M)\) and \(\xi\) in \(\Lambda^{s'}(M)\).

### Theorem 16.4.4 (Global Expression for Lie Derivative).

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold, and let \(X\) be a vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\). Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a tensor field in \(\mathcal{T}_s^r(M)\), let \(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r\) be covector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)\), and let \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_s\) be vector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_X(\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s))
&=X(\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s)) \\
&\quad-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^{i-1},\mathcal{L}_X(\omega^i),\omega^{i+1},\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s) \\
&\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_{j-1},[X,Y_j],Y_{j+1},\ldots,Y_s).
\end{aligned}
\]

**Remark.** We have from (16.4.1) that \(X(\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s))\) in the above identity can be replaced with \(\mathcal{L}_X(\mathcal{A}(\omega^1,\ldots,\omega^r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s))\).

**Proof.** Since \(\mathcal{L}_X(Y_j)=[X,Y_j]\), the result follows from Theorem 16.2.4.

### Example 16.4.5.

For a covector field \(\omega\) in \(\mathfrak{X}^*(M)=\mathcal{T}_1^0(M)\) and vector fields \(X,Y\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), setting \(r=0\) and \(s=1\) in Theorem 16.4.4 gives

\[
\mathcal{L}_X(\omega)(Y)=X(\omega(Y))-\omega([X,Y]).
\]

### Theorem 16.4.6 (Commutativity of Lie Derivative and Exterior Derivative of Functions).

If \(M\) is a smooth manifold and \(X\) is a vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), then

\[
\mathcal{L}_X\circ d=d\circ\mathcal{L}_X;
\]

that is,

\[
\mathcal{L}_X(d(f))=d(\mathcal{L}_X(f))
\]

for all functions \(f\) in \(C^\infty(M)\).

**Proof.** For a vector field \(Y\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), we have

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_X(d(f))(Y)
&=X(d(f)(Y))-d(f)([X,Y]) \qquad [\mathrm{Ex\ 16.4.5}] \\
&=X(Y(f))-[X,Y](f) \qquad [\mathrm{Th\ 15.4.5(b)}] \\
&=Y(X(f)) \qquad [(15.3.1)] \\
&=d(X(f))(Y) \qquad [\mathrm{Th\ 15.4.5(b)}] \\
&=d(\mathcal{L}_X(f))(Y). \qquad [(16.4.1)]
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(f\) and \(Y\) were arbitrary, the result follows.

### Theorem 16.4.7.

Let \(M\) be a smooth \(m\)-manifold, let \(X\) be a vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), and, in local coordinates, let

\[
X=\sum_i\alpha^i\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}.
\]
