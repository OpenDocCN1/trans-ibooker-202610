
## 8.2 Orientation of Vector Spaces

**Throughout,** \(\mathbb{R}^m\) is assumed to have the standard orientation.

We now present an equivalent approach to orientation using multivectors. Let \(V\) be a vector space of dimension \(m\). In the present context, a nonzero multivector in \(\Lambda^m(V)\) is called an **orientation multivector** on \(V\). As an example, let \((h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) be a basis for \(V\), and let \((\theta^1,\ldots,\theta^m)\) be its dual basis. By Theorem 7.2.9, \(\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m(h_1,\ldots,h_m)=1\), so \(\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m\) is an orientation multivector on \(V\). More generally, we have the following result.

### Theorem 8.2.1.

Let \(V\) be a vector space of dimension \(m\), and let \(\varpi\) be a multivector in \(\Lambda^m(V)\). Then \(\varpi\) is an orientation multivector if and only if \(\varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\neq 0\) for some (hence every) basis \((h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) for \(V\).

**Proof.** Let \(\mathcal{H}=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) be a basis for \(V\), and let \((\theta^1,\ldots,\theta^m)\) be its dual basis. We have from Theorem 7.2.13(b) that

\[
\varpi=\varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\,\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m.
\]

Thus, \(\varpi\) is nonzero if and only if \(\varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\neq 0\). Let \(\mathcal{F}=(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\) be another basis for \(V\). By Theorem 7.3.3(b),

\[
\varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m)=\det\left([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)\varpi(f_1,\ldots,f_m),
\]

and by Theorem 2.4.12, \(\det([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}})\neq 0\). Thus, \(\varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\neq 0\) if and only if \(\varpi(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\neq 0\). The result follows.

### Theorem 8.2.2.

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \(\mathcal{H}=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) and \(\mathcal{F}=(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\) be bases for \(V\), and let \((\theta^1,\ldots,\theta^m)\) be the dual basis corresponding to \(\mathcal{H}\). Then the following are equivalent:

- (a) \(\mathcal{H}\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) are consistent.
- (b) \(\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m(f_1,\ldots,f_m)>0\).
- (c) \(\varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) and \(\varpi(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\) have the same sign for some (hence every) orientation multivector \(\varpi\) on \(V\).

**Remark.** By Theorem 8.2.1, we have \(\varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m),\varpi(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\neq 0\), so the assertion in part (c) makes sense.

**Proof.** (a) \(\Longleftrightarrow\) (b): By Theorem 7.2.9 and Theorem 7.3.3(b),

\[
\det\left([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m(f_1,\ldots,f_m)=\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m(h_1,\ldots,h_m)=1.
\]

The result follows.

(a) \(\Longleftrightarrow\) (c): By Theorem 7.3.3(b),

\[
\varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m)=\det\left([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)\varpi(f_1,\ldots,f_m),
\]

and by Theorem 2.4.12, \(\det([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}})\neq 0\). The result follows.

### Theorem 8.2.3.

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \(\varpi\) be an orientation multivector on \(V\), and let \(\mathfrak{D}\) be the set of bases \((h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) for \(V\) such that \(\varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m)>0\). Then \(\mathfrak{D}\) is an orientation of \(V\), called the **orientation induced by \(\varpi\)**.

**Proof.** Let \(\mathcal{H}=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) and \(\mathcal{F}=(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\) be bases for \(V\), with \(\varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m)>0\). Then

- \(\mathcal{F}\) is in \(\mathfrak{D}\)
- \(\Leftrightarrow \varpi(f_1,\ldots,f_m)>0\)
- \(\Leftrightarrow \varpi(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) and \(\varpi(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\) have the same sign
- \(\Leftrightarrow \mathcal{H}\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) are consistent,

where the last equivalence follows from Theorem 8.2.2. Thus, \(\mathfrak{D}\) is the set of bases for \(V\) that are consistent with \(\mathcal{H}\); that is, \(\mathfrak{D}=[\mathcal{H}]\).

### Example 8.2.4 (\(\mathbb{R}^m\)).

Let \(\mathcal{E}=(e_1,\ldots,e_m)\) be the standard basis for \(\mathbb{R}^m\), let \((\xi^1,\ldots,\xi^m)\) be its dual basis, and let \(\Omega=\xi^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi^m\). It follows from Theorem 7.2.9 that \(\Omega(e_1,\ldots,e_m)=1\), so \(\Omega\) is an orientation multivector on \(\mathbb{R}^m\), and \(\mathcal{E}\) is in the orientation of \(\mathbb{R}^m\) induced by \(\Omega\). Thus, \(\Omega\) induces the standard orientation of \(\mathbb{R}^m\).

Let \(V\) be a vector space, and let \(\varpi\) and \(\vartheta\) be orientation multivectors on \(V\). By Theorem 7.2.12(d) and Theorem 8.2.1, \(\varpi\) spans \(\Lambda^m(V)\) (as does \(\vartheta\)). It follows that \(\varpi=c\vartheta\) for some nonzero real number \(c\). We say that \(\varpi\) and \(\vartheta\) are consistent, and write \(\varpi\sim\vartheta\), if \(c>0\). It is easily shown that \(\sim\) is an equivalence relation on the set of orientation multivectors on \(V\), and that there are precisely two equivalence classes. The equivalence class containing \(\varpi\) is denoted by \([\varpi]\). By definition, \([\varpi]\) comprises all multivectors on \(V\) that are consistent with \(\varpi\). Evidently, \(\varpi\) and \(-\varpi\) are not consistent. Thus, for any orientation multivector \(\varpi\) on \(V\), the equivalence classes of orientation multivectors are \([\varpi]\) and \([-\varpi]\).

Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be a basis for \(V\). It is clear that orientation multivectors in the same equivalence class induce the same orientation, and that orientation multivectors in different equivalence classes induce opposite orientations. We therefore have a bijective map

\[
\iota : \{[\varpi], [-\varpi]\} \longrightarrow \{[\mathcal{H}], [-\mathcal{H}]\}
\]

defined by assigning \([\varpi]\) and \([-\varpi]\) to the orientations induced by \(\varpi\) and \(-\varpi\), respectively. This shows that we are free to specify orientations using either (equivalence classes of) bases for \(V\) or (equivalence classes of) orientation multivectors on \(V\). For purposes of computation, the latter approach is generally more convenient.

### Theorem 8.2.5 (Induced Orientation of Subspace).

Let \((V,\mathfrak{D})\) be an oriented vector space of dimension \(m\ge 2\), let \(U\) be an \((m-1)\)-dimensional subspace of \(V\), and let \(v\) be a vector in \(V\setminus U\). Then:

- (a) There is a unique orientation \(\mathcal{D}_U\) of \(U\), called the **orientation induced by \(v\)**, such that \((h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})\) is a basis for \(U\) that is positively oriented with respect to \(\mathcal{D}_U\) if and only if \((v,h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})\) is a basis for \(V\) that is positively oriented with respect to \(\mathcal{D}\).
- (b) If \(\varpi\) is an orientation multivector on \(V\) that induces \(\mathcal{D}\), then \(\iota_v(\varpi)|_U\) is an orientation multivector on \(U\) that induces \(\mathcal{D}_U\).
- (c) \(\mathcal{D}_U\) is independent of the choice of orientation multivector on \(V\) that induces \(\mathcal{D}\).

**Proof.** (a), (b): Let \((h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})\) be an \((m-1)\)-tuple of vectors in \(U\). Since \(v\) is in \(V\setminus U\), by Theorem 1.1.2, \((h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})\) is a basis for \(U\) if and only if \((v,h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})\) is a basis for \(V\). Suppose \((h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})\) is in fact a basis for \(U\). It is clear that \(\iota_v(\varpi)|_U\) is a multivector in \(\Lambda^{m-1}(U)\). By definition,

\[
\iota_v(\varpi)|_U(h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})=\varpi(v,h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1}). \tag{8.2.2}
\]

We have from Theorem 8.2.1 that \(\varpi(v,h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})\ne 0\), and then from (8.2.2) that \(\iota_v(\varpi)|_U(h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})\ne 0\). This shows that \(\iota_v(\varpi)|_U\) is nonzero, and is therefore an orientation multivector on \(U\). Let \(\mathcal{D}_U\) be the orientation of \(U\) induced by \(\iota_v(\varpi)|_U\). It follows from (8.2.2) and Theorem 8.2.3 that \((h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})\) is positively oriented with respect to \(\mathcal{D}_U\) if and only if \((v,h_1,\ldots,h_{m-1})\) is positively oriented with respect to \(\mathcal{D}\). Thus, \(\mathcal{D}_U\) has the desired property. Uniqueness follows from the (almost tautological) observation that an orientation of a vector space is determined by the bases it contains.

(c): This follows from part (a).

A remark is that, in contrast to part (c) of Theorem 8.2.5, the orientation induced on \(U\) is not independent of the choice of vector in \(V\setminus U\). In particular, if \(v\) induces \(\mathcal{D}_U\), then \(-v\) induces \(-\mathcal{D}_U\).

### Theorem 8.2.6.

Let \((V,\mathcal{D})\) and \((\widetilde V,\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})\) be oriented vector spaces, let \(A:V\longrightarrow\widetilde V\) be a linear isomorphism, and let \(A(\mathcal{D})=\{A(\mathcal{H}):\mathcal{H}\in\mathcal{D}\}\). Then either \(A(\mathcal{D})=\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\), in which case \(A\) is said to be **orientation-preserving**, or \(A(\mathcal{D})=-\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\), in which case \(A\) is said to be **orientation-reversing**.

**Proof.** Let \(\mathcal{H}\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) be bases in \(\mathcal{D}\), and observe that, by Theorem 1.1.13(a), \(A(\mathcal{H})\) and \(A(\mathcal{F})\) are bases for \(\widetilde V\). Let \([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}=[a^i_j]\). We have from (2.2.6) and (2.2.7) that \(h_j=\sum_i a^i_j f_i\), hence \(A(h_j)=\sum_i a^i_j A(f_i)\), so \([\operatorname{id}_{A(\mathcal{H})}^{A(\mathcal{F})}]=[\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}]\). By definition, \(\mathcal{H}\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) are consistent, and therefore, so are \(A(\mathcal{H})\) and \(A(\mathcal{F})\). Thus, either \(A(\mathcal{H})\) and \(A(\mathcal{F})\) are in \(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\), or \(A(\mathcal{H})\) and \(A(\mathcal{F})\) are in \(-\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\). Since \(\mathcal{H}\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) were arbitrary, either \(A(\mathcal{D})\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\) or \(A(\mathcal{D})\subseteq-\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\). By Theorem 1.1.9, \(A^{-1}\) is a linear isomorphism, so a similar argument shows that either \(A^{-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})\subseteq\mathcal{D}\) or \(A^{-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})\subseteq-\mathcal{D}\), hence either \(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq A(\mathcal{D})\) or \(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq A(-\mathcal{D})\). It is easily shown using (8.2.1) that \(A(-\mathcal{H})=-A(\mathcal{H})\) for any basis \(\mathcal{H}\) in \(\mathcal{D}\), so either \(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq A(\mathcal{D})\) or \(-\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq A(\mathcal{D})\). The result follows.
