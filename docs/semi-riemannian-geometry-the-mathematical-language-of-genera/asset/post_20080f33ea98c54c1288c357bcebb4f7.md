Not surprisingly, interior multiplication behaves well with respect to basic algebraic structure, but its handling of wedge products is more complex.

**Theorem 7.4.1.** Let V be a vector space, let v, w be vectors in V, let η, ζ be multivectors in Λˢ(V), and let c be a real number. Then:

(a) \(i_{v+w}(η)=i_v(η)+i_w(η)\).

(b) \(i_{cv}(η)=c\,i_v(η)\).

(c) \(i_v(η+ζ)=i_v(η)+i_v(ζ)\).

(d) \(i_v(cη)=c\,i_v(η)\).

*Proof.* Straightforward.

**Theorem 7.4.2.** Let V be a vector space, let v be a vector in V, and let η¹, η², ⋯, ηˢ be covectors in V*. Then

\[
i_v(η^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge η^s)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}(-1)^{j-1}η^j(v)\,η^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{η^j}\wedge\cdots\wedge η^s.
\]

where ^ indicates that an expression is omitted.

*Proof.* For vectors w₁, ⋯, wₛ₋₁ in V, we have

\[
\begin{aligned}
i_v(η^1\wedge\cdots\wedge η^s)(w_1,\ldots,w_{s-1})
&=(η^1\wedge\cdots\wedge η^s)(v,w_1,\ldots,w_{s-1})\\
&=\det\begin{pmatrix}
η^1(v)&η^1(w_1)&\cdots&η^1(w_{s-1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
η^s(v)&η^s(w_1)&\cdots&η^s(w_{s-1})
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{s}(-1)^{j-1}η^j(v)\det\begin{pmatrix}
η^1(w_1)&\cdots&η^1(w_{s-1})\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
η^s(w_1)&\cdots&η^s(w_{s-1})
\end{pmatrix}^{(j)}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{s}(-1)^{j-1}η^j(v)η^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{η^j}\wedge\cdots\wedge η^s(w_1,\ldots,w_{s-1}),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the second equality follows from Theorem 7.2.8, and the third equality follows from expanding the determinant along the first column. Since w₁, ⋯, wₛ₋₁ were arbitrary, the result follows.

The next result shows that interior multiplication satisfies a novel product rule.

## Theorem 7.4.3. Let V be a vector space, let v be a vector in V, and let η and ζ be multicovectors in Λ^s(V) and Λ^{s'}(V), respectively. Then

i_v(η ∧ ζ) = i_v(η) ∧ ζ + (-1)^s η ∧ i_v(ζ).

# Proof.

For reasons exhibited by the proof of Theorem 7.3.1(b), it suffices to consider multicovectors of the form η = ξ^1 ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^s and ζ = ξ^{s+1} ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^{s+s'}, where ξ^j is a covector in V* for j = 1,2,…,s + s'. By Theorem 7.4.2,

i_v(η ∧ ζ) = i_v(ξ^1 ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^s ∧ ξ^{s+1} ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^{s+s'}) = Σ_{j=1}^s (-1)^{j-1} ξ^j(v) (ξ^1 ∧ ⋯ ∧ \widehat{ξ^j} ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^s) ∧ (ξ^{s+1} ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^{s+s'}) + Σ_{j=s+1}^{s+s'} (-1)^{j-1} ξ^j(v) (ξ^1 ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^s) ∧ (ξ^{s+1} ∧ ⋯ ∧ \widehat{ξ^j} ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^{s+s'}) = ( Σ_{j=1}^s (-1)^{j-1} ξ^j(v) ξ^1 ∧ ⋯ ∧ \widehat{ξ^j} ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^s ) ∧ ξ^{s+1} ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^{s+s'} + (-1)^s ξ^1 ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^s ∧ ( Σ_{j=s+1}^{s+s'} (-1)^{j-(s+1)} ξ^j(v) ξ^{s+1} ∧ ⋯ ∧ \widehat{ξ^j} ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξ^{s+s'} ) = i_v(η) ∧ ζ + (-1)^s η ∧ i_v(ζ).

## 7.5 Multicovector Scalar Product Spaces

In Section 4.5, we showed how to construct the dual of a scalar product space. Building on that foundation, we now generalize to multicovectors.

Let (V, g) be a scalar product space, let H be a basis for V, and let Θ = (θ^1, …, θ^m) be its dual basis. By Theorem 7.2.12(a), (θ^I : I ∈ I_{s,m}) is a basis for Λ^s(V). We define a bilinear function

g^Λ = ⟨·,·⟩^Λ : Λ^s(V) × Λ^s(V) ⟶ ℝ

as follows. For multicovectors η, ζ in Λ^s(V), with

η = Σ_{I ∈ I_{s,m}} a_I θ^I    and    ζ = Σ_{J ∈ I_{s,m}} b_J θ^J,    (7.5.1)

let

⟨η, ζ⟩^Λ = Σ_{I,J ∈ I_{s,m}} det((g_Θ^*)_I^J) a_I b_J,    (7.5.2)

where 𝔤* is the scalar product on V* and

𝔤*_Θ = [ ⟨θ¹,θ¹⟩* … ⟨θ¹,θᵐ⟩* ; ⋮ ⋱ ⋮ ; ⟨θᵐ,θ¹⟩* … ⟨θᵐ,θᵐ⟩* ]

is the matrix of 𝔤* with respect to Θ.

It would appear that the definition of 𝔤^∧ is dependent on the choice of basis for V. Remarkably, this turns out not to be the case.

## Theorem 7.5.1. With the above setup, 𝔤^∧ is independent of the choice of basis for V.

Proof. Let ℱ be another basis for V, let Φ = (φ¹,…,φᵐ) be its dual basis, and let (φᴶ : J ∈ ℐ_{s,m}) be the corresponding basis for Λˢ(V). Let

η = Σ_{I∈ℐ_{s,m}} c_I φ^I   and   ζ = Σ_{J∈ℐ_{s,m}} d_J φ^J.

In the notation of (7.5.1) and (7.5.2), we need to show that

Σ_{I,J∈ℐ_{s,m}} det((𝔤*_Θ)_J^I) a_I b_J = Σ_{I,J∈ℐ_{s,m}} det((𝔤*_Φ)_J^I) c_I d_J.   (7.5.3)

Let

P = [id_V]_{ℱ}^{ℋ}   and   Q = 𝔤*_Φ.   (7.5.4)

We begin with a few preliminary computations. Since Q is symmetric, we have from Theorem 2.1.6 that

P^L (Q^I)^T = P^L (Q^T)_I = P^L Q_I = (P Q)_I^L   (7.5.5)

and

P^K ((P Q)^L)^T = P^K ((P Q)^T)_L = (P Q^T P^T)^K_L = (P Q P^T)^K_L.   (7.5.6)

We also have

P Q P^T = [id_V]_{ℱ}^{ℋ} 𝔤*_Φ ([id_V]_{ℱ}^{ℋ})^T = ([id_V]_{ℋ}^{ℱ})^{-1} 𝔤_{ℱ}^{-1} (([id_V]_{ℋ}^{ℱ})^{-1})^T   [Th 2.2.5(b), Th 4.5.3(b)] = ((([id_V]_{ℋ}^{ℱ})^T) 𝔤_{ℱ} [id_V]_{ℋ}^{ℱ})^{-1}   [Th 2.1.3] = 𝔤_{ℋ}^{-1}   [Th 3.1.3] = 𝔤*_Θ,   [Th 4.5.3(b)]   (7.5.7)

and from Theorem 7.3.6 that
$c_I = \sum_{K \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} a_K \det(P_I^K) \quad \text{and} \quad d_J = \sum_{L \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} b_L \det(P_J^L). \tag{7.5.8}$
Using (7.5.4) and (7.5.8), the right-hand side of (7.5.3) can be expressed as
$\sum_{I,J \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \det\big((\mathfrak{g}_\Phi^*)_J^I\big) c_I d_J$
$= \sum_{I,J \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \left[ \det(Q_J^I) \left( \sum_{K \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} a_K \det(P_I^K) \right) \left( \sum_{L \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} b_L \det(P_J^L) \right) \right] \tag{7.5.9}$
$= \sum_{K,L \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \left[ \left( \sum_{I,J \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \det(P_I^K) \det(P_J^L) \det(Q_J^I) \right) a_K b_L \right] .$

The term in large parentheses in the last line of (7.5.9) can be expressed as
$\sum_{I,J \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \det(P_I^K) \det(P_J^L) \det(Q_J^I)$
$= \sum_{I \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \left[ \det(P_I^K) \left( \sum_{J \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \det(P_J^L) \det(Q_J^I) \right) \right]$
$= \sum_{I \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \left( \det(P_I^K) \det\big(P^L (Q^I)^\mathsf{T}\big) \right) \quad \text{[Th 2.4.10(b)]}$
$= \sum_{I \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \left( \det(P_I^K) \det\big((PQ)_I^L\big) \right) \quad \text{[(7.5.5)]}$
$= \det\left(P^K \big((PQ)^L\big)^\mathsf{T}\right) \quad \text{[Th 2.4.10(b)]}$
$= \det\left((PQ P^\mathsf{T})_L^K\right) \quad \text{[(7.5.6)]}$
$= \det\left((\mathfrak{g}_\Theta^*)_L^K\right). \quad \text{[(7.5.7)]}$

Then (7.5.9) and (7.5.10) give
$\sum_{I,J \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \det\big((\mathfrak{g}_\Phi^*)_J^I\big) c_I d_J = \sum_{K,L \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \det\big((\mathfrak{g}_\Theta^*)_L^K\big) a_K b_L,$
which is equivalent to (7.5.3).

## Theorem 7.5.2.

Let $(V,\mathfrak{g})$ be a scalar product space of signature $(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_m)$, let $\mathcal{E}$ be an orthonormal basis for $V$, let $(\xi^1,\dots,\xi^m)$ be its dual basis, and let $I = (i_1,\dots,i_s)$ and $J$ be multi-indices in $\mathcal{I}_{s,m}$. Then
$\langle \xi^I, \xi^J \rangle^\Lambda = \begin{cases} \varepsilon_{i_1}\cdots\varepsilon_{i_s} & \text{if } I = J \\ 0 & \text{if } I \neq J, \end{cases}$
hence each $\xi^I$ is a unit vector.

Proof. Let Ξ = (ξ¹, …, ξᵐ). Since 𝔤_ℰ = diag(ε₁, …, εₘ), we have from Theorem 4.5.3(b) that

𝔤_Ξ* = 𝔤_ℰ⁻¹ = 𝔤_ℰ = diag(ε₁, …, εₘ),

and then from (7.5.2) that

⟨ξ^I, ξ^J⟩^∧ = det(diag(ε₁, …, εₘ)^I_J).

If I = J, then diag(ε₁, …, εₘ)^I_J = diag(ε_{i₁}, …, ε_{i_s}). On the other hand, if I ≠ J, then diag(ε₁, …, εₘ)^I_J has at least one row (and at least one column) of 0s. The result follows.

We are now in a position to define the multicovector counterpart of the dual scalar product space described in Section 4.5.

## Theorem 7.5.3. Let (V, 𝔤) be a scalar product space, let ℰ be an orthonormal basis for V, and let (ξ¹, …, ξᵐ) be its dual basis. Then:

+   (a) (Λ^s(V), 𝔤^∧) is a scalar product space, called the multicovector scalar product space corresponding to (V, 𝔤)
+   (b) (ξ^I : I ∈ ℐ_{s,m}) is an orthonormal basis for Λ^s(V).
+   (c) If (V, 𝔤) is an inner product space, then so is (Λ^s(V), 𝔤^∧).

Proof. (a): We need to show that 𝔤^∧ is a scalar product. By construction, 𝔤^∧ is bilinear, and clearly it is symmetric. According to Theorem 7.2.12(a), (ξ^I : I ∈ ℐ_{s,m}) is a basis for Λ^s(V). Suppose η is a multicovector in Λ^s(V) such that ⟨η, ζ⟩^∧ = 0 for all multicovectors ζ in Λ^s(V), and let

η = Σ_{I ∈ ℐ_{s,m}} a_I ξ^I.   (7.5.11)

Then Theorem 7.5.2 gives

0 = ⟨η, ξ^I⟩^∧ = ⟨ Σ_{J ∈ ℐ_{s,m}} a_J ξ^J, ξ^I ⟩^∧ = Σ_{J ∈ ℐ_{s,m}} a_J ⟨ξ^J, ξ^I⟩^∧ = ± a_I,

hence a_I = 0 for all multi-indices I in ℐ_{s,m}, so η = 0. Thus, 𝔤^∧ is nondegenerate.

(b): It was just observed that (ξ^I : I ∈ ℐ_{s,m}) is a basis for Λ^s(V). By Theorem 7.5.2, it is orthonormal.

(c): Since 𝔤 is an inner product, we have from Theorem 7.5.2 that

⟨ξ^I, ξ^J⟩^∧ = { 1 if I = J
                  0 if I ≠ J. }

With η as in (7.5.11),

⟨η, η⟩^∧ = ⟨ Σ_{I ∈ ℐ_{s,m}} a_I ξ^I, Σ_{J ∈ ℐ_{s,m}} a_J ξ^J ⟩^∧ = Σ_{I,J ∈ ℐ_{s,m}} ⟨ξ^I, ξ^J⟩^∧ a_I a_J
          = Σ_{I ∈ ℐ_{s,m}} (a_I)² ≥ 0,

and there is equality if and only if each a_I = 0, in which case η = 0.
