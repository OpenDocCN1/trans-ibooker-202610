# Analysis in \(\mathbb{R}^m\)

# 10.1  Derivatives

In this section, we review some of the key results in the differential calculus of one or more real variables. For the most part, proofs are not provided.

Let \(U\) be an open set in \(\mathbb{R}^m\), let \(F: U \to \mathbb{R}^n\) be a map, and let \(p\) be a point in \(U\). We say that \(F\) is differentiable at \(p\) if there is a linear map \(L_p: \mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n\) such that

\[\lim_{\lVert v\rVert\to 0}\frac{\lVert F(p+v)-F(p)-L_p(v)\rVert}{\lVert v\rVert}=0.\]

It can be shown that if such a map exists, it is unique. We call this map the **differential of \(F\) at \(p\)** and henceforth denote it by

\[d_p(F): \mathbb{R}^m \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^n.\]

In the literature, the differential of \(F\) at \(p\) is also called the derivative of \(F\) at \(p\) or the total derivative of \(F\) at \(p\), and is denoted variously by \(dF_p\), \(dF_p\), \(DF(p)\), \(D_p(F)\), or \(F'(p)\). We have chosen to include parentheses in the notation \(d_p(F)\) to set the stage for viewing \(d_p\) as a type of map.

It is usual to characterize \(d_p(F)\) as being a “linear approximation” to \(F\) in the vicinity of \(p\). For a more geometric interpretation, let us define the **graph of \(F\)** by

\[\operatorname{graph}(F)=\{(p,F(p))\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}:p\in U\}.\]

We can think \(d_p(F)(\mathbb{R}^m)\), which is a vector space, as being the “tangent space” to \(\operatorname{graph}(F)\) at \(F(p)\). This is a generalization of the tangent line and tangent plane familiar from the differential calculus of one or two real variables.
