
(b) If ∇ is symmetric, then J is the unique Jacobi field in 𝔛_M(ρ_{p,v}) such that J(0) = 0 and (∇J/dr)(0) = w.

Remark. That ρ_{p,v}(r) = exp_p(rv) follows from Theorem 18.9.3. By Theorem 18.11.4(b), σ is a geodesic variation of ρ_{p,v}. We have from (18.9.4) that the differential map of exp_p : ℰ_p ⟶ M at rv is

d_{rv}(exp_p) : T_{rv}(T_p(M)) ⟶ T_{exp_p(rv)}(M).

It would be more precise in part (a) to write J(r) = r d_{rv}(exp_p)(w_{rv}). Stating part (a) as above amounts to making the identification T_{rv}(T_p(M)) = T_p(M).

Proof. (a): Setting ψ(s) = v + sw in Theorem 18.11.4(d) yields

∂σ/∂s (r, s) = r d_{r(v+sw)}(exp_p)(w).

Setting s = 0 in the preceding identity and using (18.11.3) gives the result.

(b): Since ∇ is symmetric, it follows from Theorem 18.11.3 and part (a) that J is a Jacobi field in 𝔛_M(ρ_{p,v}). From the identity in part (a), we obtain J(0) = 0 and

∇J/dr (r) = d_{rv}(exp_p)(w) + r ∇/dr (d_{rv}(exp_p)(w)).

By Theorem 18.9.4, d_0(exp_p) is the identity diffeomorphism. Setting r = 0 in the preceding identity yields (∇J/dr)(0) = w. The uniqueness property of J now follows from Theorem 18.11.1.

## Example 18.11.6. Let (M, ∇) be a smooth manifold with a connection, and let γ(r) : [0,1] ⟶ M be a (radial) geodesic. Consider the vector fields J and K in 𝔛_M(γ) defined by

J(r) = dγ/dr (r)      and      K(r) = r dγ/dr (r).

We have

∇/dr (dγ/dr) = 0      and      R∘γ (dγ/dr, dγ/dr) dγ/dr = 0,

where the second identity follows from Theorem 18.7.1(a). It is now easily shown that J and K are Jacobi fields.

## Chapter 19

## Semi-Riemannian Manifolds

Looking back at the material presented on smooth manifolds and smooth manifolds with a connection, we see that many of the concepts and results motivated by our study of regular surfaces and regular surfaces in $\mathbb{R}^3$ have been recovered—but not all. Missing from our recent efforts has been any discussion of “length” and “area”. To address this shortfall, it is necessary to endow smooth manifolds with additional structure, which entails a consideration of semi-Riemannian manifolds.

### 19.1  Semi-Riemannian Manifolds

The following material builds on portions of the discussion in Section 12.2. Let $M$ be a smooth $m$-manifold, and let $\mathbf{g}$ be a symmetric tensor field in $\mathcal{T}^0_2(M)$, so that $\mathbf{g}_p$ is a symmetric tensor in $\mathcal{T}^0_2(T_p(M))$ for all $p$ in $M$. We say that $\mathbf{g}$ is a metric on $M$ if:

+   [G1] $\mathbf{g}_p$ is nondegenerate on $T_p(M)$ for all $p$ in $M$.  
+   [G2] $\operatorname{ind}(\mathbf{g}_p)$ is independent of $p$ in $M$.

When [G1] is satisfied, $\mathbf{g}_p$ is a scalar product on $T_p(M)$ for all $p$ in $M$; or equivalently, $(T_p(M),\mathbf{g}_p)$ is a scalar product space for all $p$ in $M$.

Suppose $\mathbf{g}$ is in fact a metric. In that case, the pair $(M,\mathbf{g})$ is said to be a semi-Riemannian $m$-manifold. We ascribe to $\mathbf{g}$ those properties of $\mathbf{g}_p$ that are independent of $p$. Accordingly, $\mathbf{g}$ is said to be bilinear, symmetric, nondegenerate, and so on. The common value of the $\operatorname{ind}(\mathbf{g}_p)$ is denoted by $\nu$ and called the index of $\mathbf{g}$ or the index of $M$. The common signature of the $\mathbf{g}_p$ is denoted by $(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_m)$ and called the signature of $\mathbf{g}$ or the signature of $M$. For brevity, we usually denote

$$\mathbf{g}_p(\cdot,\cdot) \quad\text{by}\quad \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle.$$

When ν = 0, we say that M is a Riemannian m-manifold, and when m ≥ 2 and ν = 1, M is said to be a Lorentz m-manifold. A semi-Riemannian m-manifold with a connection is a triple (M, 𝔤, ∇), where (M, 𝔤) is a semi-Riemannian m-manifold and ∇ is a connection on M. For the time being, we do not assume that 𝔤 and ∇ are related.

We have already encountered several semi-Riemannian manifolds, although we were not in a position to use such terminology at the time. For example, semi-Euclidean (m, ν)-space ℝᵥᵐ = (ℝᵐ, 𝔰) is a semi-Riemannian m-manifold of index ν. In particular, Euclidean m-space ℝ₀ᵐ = (ℝᵐ, 𝔢) is a Riemannian m-manifold, and Minkowski m-space ℝ₁ᵐ = (ℝᵐ, 𝔪) is a Lorentz m-manifold. With tensor notation now at our disposal, we can express 𝔰 in local coordinates as

```
𝔰 = { Σᵢ=1ᵐ d(xⁱ) ⊗ d(xⁱ)                     if ν = 0
       Σᵢ=1ᵐ⁻ᵛ d(xⁱ) ⊗ d(xⁱ) − Σᵢ=m−ν+1ᵐ d(xⁱ) ⊗ d(xⁱ)   if 1 ≤ ν ≤ m − 1
      − Σᵢ=1ᵐ d(xⁱ) ⊗ d(xⁱ)                     if ν = m. }
```

To our list of semi-Riemannian manifolds, let us add the unit sphere 𝓢² and hyperbolic space ℋ², which are Riemannian 2-manifolds, and the pseudosphere 𝒫², which is a Lorentz 2-manifold. In fact, each of the regular surfaces in ℝ₀³ described in Sections 13.1–13.8 is a Riemannian 2-manifold. We now provide a less obvious example of a Riemannian 2-manifold.

## Example 19.1.1.

Let ℙ² = {(r, s) ∈ ℝ² : s > 0}, which is an open set in the smooth 2-manifold ℝ². It follows from Theorem 14.3.3(b), (14.3.4), and Theorem 14.8.4 that ℙ² is a 2-submanifold of ℝ², and that T₍ᵣ,ₛ₎(ℙ²) can be identified with ℝ². We define a tensor field 𝔭 in 𝒯₂⁰(ℙ²) by

𝔭₍ᵣ,ₛ₎(v, w) = 1/s² ⟨v, w⟩

for all points (r, s) in ℙ² and all vectors v, w in T₍ᵣ,ₛ₎(ℙ²), where ⟨·,·⟩ is the Euclidean inner product. It is easily shown that 𝔭₍ᵣ,ₛ₎ is an inner product for all (r, s) in ℙ². Thus, (ℙ², 𝔭) is a Riemannian 2-manifold, called the Poincaré half-plane. ◇

## Theorem 19.1.2 (Open Set).

If (M, 𝔤) is a semi-Riemannian m-manifold of index ν and U is an open set in M, then (U, 𝔤|ᵤ) is a semi-Riemannian m-submanifold of (M, 𝔤) of index ν.

Proof. By Theorem 14.8.4, U is an m-submanifold of M. From Theorem 14.3.3(b), we have the identification Tₚ(U) = Tₚ(M) for all p in U. It follows that (𝔤|ᵤ)|ₚ is nondegenerate on Tₚ(U) for all p in U, and ind((𝔤|ᵤ)ₚ) = ind(𝔤ₚ) = ν for all p in U. Thus, 𝔤|ᵤ satisfies [G1] and [G2]. □

### 19.1  Semi-Riemannian Manifolds

Let $(M,g)$ be a semi-Riemannian $m$-manifold, and let $X,Y$ be vector fields in $\mathfrak{X}(M)$. We say that $X$ is a **unit vector field** if $X_p$ is a unit vector for all $p$ in $M$, and that $X$ and $Y$ are **orthogonal vector fields** if $X_p$ and $Y_p$ are orthogonal for all $p$ in $M$.

Let us define a function

$$\langle X,Y\rangle:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}$$

in $C^\infty(M)$ by the assignment

$$p\longmapsto\langle X_p,Y_p\rangle$$

for all $p$ in $M$. Similarly, for a smooth curve $\lambda:(a,b)\longrightarrow M$ and vector fields $J,K$ in $\mathfrak{X}_M(\lambda)$, we define a function

$$\langle J,K\rangle:(a,b)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}$$

in $C^\infty((a,b))$ by the assignment

$$t\longmapsto\langle J(t),K(t)\rangle$$

for all $t$ in $(a,b)$.

Let $(U,(x^i))$ be a chart on $M$, and let $\mathcal{X}=(\partial/\partial x^1,\ldots,\partial/\partial x^m)$ be the corresponding coordinate frame. We define functions $g_{ij}$ in $C^\infty(U)$ by

$$g_{ij}(p)=\left\langle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p,\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^j}\right|_p\right\rangle \tag{19.1.1}$$

for all $p$ in $U$ for $i,j=1,\ldots,m$. The **matrix** of $g$ with respect to $\mathcal{X}$ is denoted by $g_{\mathcal{X}}$ and defined by

$$g_{\mathcal{X}}(p)=\begin{bmatrix}g_{11}(p)&\cdots&g_{1m}(p)\\ \vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\ g_{m1}(p)&\cdots&g_{mm}(p)\end{bmatrix}$$

for all $p$ in $U$. Recall from Section 3.1 that the matrix of $g_p$ with respect to $\mathcal{X}_p$ is $(g_p)_{\mathcal{X}_p}=[g_{ij}(p)]$. Thus, as a matter of notation,

$$g_{\mathcal{X}}(p)=(g_p)_{\mathcal{X}_p}.$$

The **inverse matrix** of $g$ with respect to $\mathcal{X}$ is denoted by $g_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}$ and defined by

$$g_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(p)=g_{\mathcal{X}}(p)^{-1}$$

for all $p$ in $U$. It is usual to express the entries of $g_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(p)$ with superscripts:

$$g_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(p)=\begin{bmatrix}g_{11}(p)&\cdots&g_{1m}(p)\\ \vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\ g_{m1}(p)&\cdots&g_{mm}(p)\end{bmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}g^{11}(p)&\cdots&g^{1m}(p)\\ \vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\ g^{m1}(p)&\cdots&g^{mm}(p)\end{bmatrix}.$$

The assignment $p\longmapsto g^{ij}(p)$ defines functions $g^{ij}$ in $C^\infty(U)$ for $i,j=1,\ldots,m$. Since $[g_{ij}]$ and $[g^{ij}]$ are symmetric matrices, the functions $g_{ij}$ and $g^{ij}$ are symmetric in $i,j$.
