Let $(M,\nabla)$ be a smooth manifold with a connection, and let $X$ be a vector field in $\mathfrak{X}(M)$. It can be shown that $\nabla_X$ is determined pointwise in the following sense: for all points $p$ in $M$ and all vector fields $Y$ in $\mathfrak{X}(M)$,

$$\nabla_X(Y)_p = \nabla_{\tilde{X}}(Y)_p$$

for all vector fields $X,\tilde{X}$ in $\mathfrak{X}(M)$ such that $X_p = \tilde{X}_p$. For each point $p$ in $M$ and vector $v$ in $T_p(M)$, we define a vector $\nabla_v(Y)$ in $T_p(M)$ by

$$\nabla_v(Y) = \nabla_X(Y)_p \tag{18.1.4}$$

where $X$ is any vector field in $\mathfrak{X}(M)$ such that $X_p = v$. By Theorem 15.1.2, such an $X$ always exists. Since $\nabla_X$ is determined pointwise, $\nabla_v(Y)$ is independent of the choice of vector field and is therefore well-defined. It follows from (18.1.4) that for all vector fields $X,Y$ in $\mathfrak{X}(M)$,

$$\nabla_{X_p}(Y) = \nabla_X(Y)_p \tag{18.1.5}$$

By definition, $\operatorname{Lin}(\mathfrak{X}(M),\mathfrak{X}(M))$ is the vector space of $\mathbb{R}$-linear maps from the vector space $\mathfrak{X}(M)$ to itself. We make $\operatorname{Lin}(\mathfrak{X}(M),\mathfrak{X}(M))$ into a ring over $\mathbb{R}$ by defining multiplication to be composition of maps. Let $\Theta,\Upsilon,\Psi$ be three such maps, and define the bracket of $\Theta$ and $\Upsilon$ by

$$[\Theta,\Upsilon] = \Theta \circ \Upsilon - \Upsilon \circ \Theta.$$

It is easily shown that with this definition, $\operatorname{Lin}(\mathfrak{X}(M),\mathfrak{X}(M))$ is a Lie algebra over $\mathbb{R}$. In particular, Jacobi's identity is satisfied:

$$[[\Theta,\Upsilon],\Psi] + [[\Upsilon,\Psi],\Theta] + [[\Psi,\Theta],\Upsilon] = 0.$$

For vector fields $X,Y,Z$ in $\mathfrak{X}(M)$, we have from parts (c) and (d) of Theorem 18.1.2 that $\nabla_X,\nabla_Y,\nabla_Z$ are maps in $\operatorname{Lin}(\mathfrak{X}(M),\mathfrak{X}(M))$, hence

$$[[\nabla_X,\nabla_Y],\nabla_Z] + [[\nabla_Y,\nabla_Z],\nabla_X] + [[\nabla_Z,\nabla_X],\nabla_Y] = 0. \tag{18.1.6}$$

# 18.2 Christoffel Symbols

In our study of regular surfaces in $\mathbb{R}_p^3$, we defined Christoffel symbols and used them to construct the covariant derivative, which we now think of as equivalent to a connection (see Example 18.1.1). Our approach to smooth manifolds with a connection proceeds in reverse: we start with a connection and then use it to define Christoffel symbols.

Let $(M,\nabla)$ be a smooth $m$-manifold with a connection, let $X$ be a vector field in $\mathfrak{X}(M)$, let $(U,(x^i))$ be a chart on $M$, and let $\mathcal{X} = (\partial/\partial x^1,\dots,\partial/\partial x^m)$ be the corresponding coordinate frame. It can be shown that $\nabla$ restricts unambiguously to the smooth $m$-manifold $U$. For simplicity of notation, let us denote the restriction

$$\nabla|_U \quad \text{by} \quad \nabla.$$
