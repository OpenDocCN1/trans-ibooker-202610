This keeps the “intrinsic” geometry of the deformed plane intact, thereby preserving the Gauss curvature at each point. The sphere has constant positive Gauss curvature, while the tractoid, which is shaped like a bugle, has constant negative Gauss curvature. The Gauss curvature of the hyperboloid of one sheet (two sheets) is negative (positive) but nonconstant. The torus has a region where the Gauss curvature is positive, and one where it is negative, with a transition zone in between where the Gauss curvature is 0.

It is interesting to observe that the hyperboloid of one sheet and the pseudosphere are defined in terms of the same underlying surface. The difference in their Gauss curvatures is due entirely to the fact that one resides in the inner product space ℝ³₀, and the other in the Lorentz vector space ℝ³₁. A similar remark applies to the hyperboloid of two sheets and hyperbolic space.

## 12.9 Riemann Curvature Tensor in ℝ³_ν

The Riemann curvature tensor for a regular surface \((M,g)\) in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\) is the map

\[
R: \mathfrak{X}(M)^3 \to \mathfrak{X}(M)
\]

defined by

\[
R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X(\nabla_Y(Z)) - \nabla_Y(\nabla_X(Z)) - \nabla_{[X,Y]}(Z) \tag{12.9.1}
\]

for all vector fields \(X,Y,Z\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\); that is,

\[
(R(X,Y)Z)_p = (\nabla_X(\nabla_Y(Z)))_p - (\nabla_Y(\nabla_X(Z)))_p - (\nabla_{[X,Y]}(Z))_p
\]

for all \(p\) in \(M\). The large parentheses are included to make it clear that each of the four terms in the preceding identity is a vector field in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\) evaluated at the point \(p\), and as such is a vector in \(T_p(M)\). Since \((R(X,Y)Z)_p\) is not a real number, using the term “tensor” to describe \(R\) is something of a misnomer. This conflict is resolved in Theorem 19.5.5. The expression \(R_p(X_p,Y_p)Z_p\) has no meaning—at least not yet.

We presented an instance in Example 12.4.4 where the second order covariant derivatives \(\nabla^2_{X,Y}(Z)\) and \(\nabla^2_{Y,X}(Z)\) are not equal. As the next result shows, the difference between these two vector fields is precisely \(R(X,Y)Z\).

### Theorem 12.9.1.

If \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) is a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), then

\[
R(X,Y)Z = \nabla^2_{X,Y}(Z) - \nabla^2_{Y,X}(Z)
\]

for all vector fields \(X,Y,Z\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\).

Proof. We have from Theorem 12.4.1(a) and (12.6.1) that

\[
\nabla_{[X,Y]}(Z) = \nabla_{\nabla_X(Y)-\nabla_Y(X)}(Z) = \nabla_{\nabla_X(Y)}(Z) - \nabla_{\nabla_Y(X)}(Z).
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
R(X,Y)Z
&= \nabla_X(\nabla_Y(Z)) - \nabla_Y(\nabla_X(Z)) - \nabla_{[X,Y]}(Z)\\
&= [\nabla_X(\nabla_Y(Z)) - \nabla_{\nabla_X(Y)}(Z)] - [\nabla_Y(\nabla_X(Z)) - \nabla_{\nabla_Y(X)}(Z)]\\
&= \nabla^2_{X,Y}(Z) - \nabla^2_{Y,X}(Z),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the last equality follows from (12.4.4).

For computational purposes, it is helpful to have a local coordinate expression for \(R\).

### Theorem 12.9.2.

Let \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) be a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), let \(X,Y,Z\) be vector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), and, in local coordinates, let

\[
X\circ\varphi = \sum_i \alpha^i H_i, \qquad Y\circ\varphi = \sum_j \beta^j H_j, \qquad Z\circ\varphi = \sum_k \gamma^k H_k.
\]

Then

\[
R(X,Y)Z\circ\varphi = \sum_l \left(\sum_{ijk} \alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k R^l_{ijk}\right) H_l, \tag{12.9.2}
\]

where

\[
R^l_{ijk} = \frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{jk}}{\partial r^i} - \frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{ik}}{\partial r^j} + \sum_n \left(\Gamma^l_{in}\Gamma^n_{jk} - \Gamma^l_{jn}\Gamma^n_{ik}\right) \tag{12.9.3}
\]

for \(i,j,k,l=1,2\).

Proof. We have from (12.4.20) that

\[
\begin{aligned}
(\nabla^2_{X,Y}(Z)\circ\varphi)^l
&= \sum_{ij}^{(3)}\alpha^i\beta^j \frac{\partial^2\gamma^l}{\partial r^i\partial r^j}
+ \sum_{ijk}^{(4)}\alpha^i\beta^j \frac{\partial\gamma^k}{\partial r^i}\Gamma^l_{jk}
+ \sum_{ijk}^{(5)}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{jk}}{\partial r^i}\\
&\quad + \sum_{ijk}^{(6)}\alpha^i\beta^j \frac{\partial\gamma^k}{\partial r^j}\Gamma^l_{ik}
+ \sum_{ijkn}^{(7)}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \Gamma^n_{jk}\Gamma^l_{in}
- \sum_{ijk}^{(8)}\alpha^i\beta^j \frac{\partial\gamma^k}{\partial r^k}\Gamma^l_{ij}
- \sum_{ijkn}^{(9)}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \Gamma^n_{ij}\Gamma^l_{kn}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Reversing the roles of \(X\) and \(Y\) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
(\nabla^2_{Y,X}(Z)\circ\varphi)^l
&= \sum_{ij}\alpha^j\beta^i \frac{\partial^2\gamma^l}{\partial r^i\partial r^j}
+ \sum_{ijk}\alpha^j\beta^i \frac{\partial\gamma^k}{\partial r^i}\Gamma^l_{jk}
+ \sum_{ijk}\alpha^j\beta^i\gamma^k \frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{jk}}{\partial r^i}\\
&\quad + \sum_{ijk}\alpha^j\beta^i \frac{\partial\gamma^k}{\partial r^j}\Gamma^l_{ik}
+ \sum_{ijkn}\alpha^j\beta^i\gamma^k \Gamma^n_{jk}\Gamma^l_{in}
- \sum_{ijk}\alpha^j\beta^i \frac{\partial\gamma^k}{\partial r^k}\Gamma^l_{ij}
- \sum_{ijkn}\alpha^j\beta^i\gamma^k \Gamma^n_{ij}\Gamma^l_{kn}\\
&= \sum_{ij}^{(3)}\alpha^i\beta^j \frac{\partial^2\gamma^l}{\partial r^i\partial r^j}
+ \sum_{ijk}^{(6)}\alpha^i\beta^j \frac{\partial\gamma^k}{\partial r^j}\Gamma^l_{ik}
+ \sum_{ijk}^{(10)}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{ik}}{\partial r^j}
+ \sum_{ijk}^{(4)}\alpha^i\beta^j \frac{\partial\gamma^k}{\partial r^i}\Gamma^l_{jk}\\
&\quad + \sum_{ijkn}^{(11)}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \Gamma^n_{ik}\Gamma^l_{jn}
- \sum_{ijk}^{(8)}\alpha^i\beta^j \frac{\partial\gamma^k}{\partial r^k}\Gamma^l_{ij}
- \sum_{ijkn}^{(9)}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \Gamma^n_{ij}\Gamma^l_{kn},
\end{aligned}
\]

where the numbering of sums that was initiated in the proof of Theorem 12.4.3 is continued. It follows from Theorem 12.9.1 that the \(l\)th component of \(R(X,Y)Z\) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
((\nabla^2_{X,Y}(Z) - \nabla^2_{Y,X}(Z))\circ\varphi)^l
&= \sum_{ijk}^{(5)}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{jk}}{\partial r^i}
+ \sum_{ijkn}^{(7)}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \Gamma^n_{jk}\Gamma^l_{in}\\
&\quad - \sum_{ijk}^{(10)}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{ik}}{\partial r^j}
- \sum_{ijkn}^{(11)}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \Gamma^n_{ik}\Gamma^l_{jn}\\
&= \sum_{ijk}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k \left(\frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{jk}}{\partial r^i} - \frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{ik}}{\partial r^j} + \sum_n (\Gamma^l_{in}\Gamma^n_{jk} - \Gamma^l_{jn}\Gamma^n_{ik})\right)\\
&= \sum_{ijk}\alpha^i\beta^j\gamma^k R^l_{ijk}.
\end{aligned}
\]

We observed in Section 12.3 that the Christoffel symbols are intrinsic. It follows from (12.9.2) and (12.9.3) that the same is true of the Riemann curvature tensor.

### Example 12.9.3 (S²).

Substituting into (12.9.3) the values of the Christoffel symbols for \(S^2\) given in Section 13.4 yields

\[
R^1_{122} = \sin^2(\theta), \quad R^1_{212} = -\sin^2(\theta), \quad R^2_{121} = -1, \quad R^2_{211} = 1,
\]

with the remaining \(R^l_{ijk}\) equal to \(0\). Observe that the \(R^l_{ijk}\) are independent of \(\varphi\), as would be expected due to the symmetry of the sphere.

It is a remarkable feature of (12.9.2) that no partial derivatives of the component functions appear in the expression. This crucial observation underlies the next two results.

### Theorem 12.9.4.

If \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) is a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), then \(R\) is determined pointwise on \(M\) in the following sense: for all points \(p\) in \(M\), if \(X,Y,Z\) and \(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z}\) are vector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\) such that

\[
(X_p,Y_p,Z_p) = (\tilde{X}_p,\tilde{Y}_p,\tilde{Z}_p),
\]

then

\[
(R(X,Y)Z)_p = (R(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y})\tilde{Z})_p.
\]

Proof. In local coordinates, let

\[
X\circ\varphi = \sum_i \alpha^i H_i \quad \text{and} \quad \tilde{X}\circ\varphi = \sum_i \tilde{\alpha}^i H_i.
\]

Then \(X_p = \tilde{X}_p\) translates into \(\alpha^i(p) = \tilde{\alpha}^i(p)\) for \(i=1,2\), with corresponding identities for the other two vector fields. The result now follows from (12.9.2).

### Theorem 12.9.5.

If \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) is a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), then \(R\) is \(C^\infty(M)\)-multilinear in the following sense: for all vector fields \(X,Y,Z,W\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\) and all functions \(f\) in \(C^\infty(M)\),

\[
R(fX+W,Y)Z = fR(X,Y)Z + R(W,Y)Z,
\]

with corresponding identities for the other two arguments of \(R\).

Proof. This follows from reasoning similar to that used in the proof of Theorem 12.9.4.

### Theorem 12.9.6.

Let \((M,\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{O})\) be an oriented regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), let \(\mathcal{N}\) be the Gauss map, and let \(X,Y\) be vector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\). Then:

(a)

\[
\langle D_X(Y), \mathcal{N}\rangle = \langle \mathcal{W}(X), Y\rangle.
\]

(b)

\[
D_X(Y) = \nabla_X(Y) + \epsilon_M \langle \mathcal{W}(X), Y\rangle \mathcal{N}. \tag{12.9.4}
\]

Proof. (a): Applying \(D_X\) to both sides of \(\langle Y,\mathcal{N}\rangle = 0\) and using Theorem 12.3.1(e) yields \(\langle D_X(Y),\mathcal{N}\rangle = \langle -D_X(\mathcal{N}),Y\rangle\). The result now follows from Theorem 12.8.1.

(b): Let \(p\) be a point in \(M\), and recall the definitions of \(\tan_p\) and \(\operatorname{nor}_p\) given in Section 12.4. By Theorem 4.1.4,

\[
D_X(Y)_p = \tan_p(D_X(Y)_p) + \operatorname{nor}_p(D_X(Y)_p),
\]

and by (12.4.2),

\[
\tan_p(D_X(Y)_p) = \nabla_X(Y)_p.
\]

It follows from Theorem 12.7.3(b) that \(T_p(M)^\perp = \mathbb{R}\mathcal{N}_p\), and then from Theorem 4.1.5 and (12.2.3) that

\[
\operatorname{nor}_p(D_X(Y)_p) = \frac{\langle \mathcal{N}_p, D_X(Y)_p\rangle}{\langle \mathcal{N}_p,\mathcal{N}_p\rangle}\mathcal{N}_p
= \epsilon_M \langle D_X(Y)_p,\mathcal{N}_p\rangle\mathcal{N}_p.
\]

Thus,

\[
D_X(Y)_p = \nabla_X(Y)_p + \epsilon_M \langle D_X(Y)_p,\mathcal{N}_p\rangle\mathcal{N}_p.
\]

Since \(p\) was arbitrary,

\[
D_X(Y) = \nabla_X(Y) + \epsilon_M \langle D_X(Y),\mathcal{N}\rangle\mathcal{N}.
\]

The result now follows from part (a).

### Theorem 12.9.7.

If \((M,\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{O})\) is an oriented regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\) and \(X,Y,Z\) are vector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), then:

(a)

\[
R(X,Y)Z = \epsilon_M[\mathfrak{h}(Y,Z)\mathcal{W}(X) - \mathfrak{h}(X,Z)\mathcal{W}(Y)].
\]

(b)

\[
\nabla_X(\mathcal{W}(Y)) - \nabla_Y(\mathcal{W}(X)) = \mathcal{W}([X,Y]).
\]

Proof. By Theorem 12.9.6(b),

\[
D_X(D_Y(Z)) = D_X(\nabla_Y(Z) + \epsilon_M \langle \mathcal{W}(Y),Z\rangle\mathcal{N}) \tag{12.9.5}
\]

\[
= D_X(\nabla_Y(Z)) + \epsilon_M D_X(\langle \mathcal{W}(Y),Z\rangle\mathcal{N})
\]

and

\[
D_X(\nabla_Y(Z)) = \nabla_X(\nabla_Y(Z)) + \epsilon_M \langle \mathcal{W}(X),\nabla_Y(Z)\rangle\mathcal{N}, \tag{12.9.6}
\]

and by parts (d) and (e) of Theorem 12.3.1 and Theorem 12.8.1,

\[
D_X(\langle \mathcal{W}(Y),Z\rangle\mathcal{N})
= D_X(\langle \mathcal{W}(Y),Z\rangle)\mathcal{N} + \langle \mathcal{W}(Y),Z\rangle D_X(\mathcal{N}) \tag{12.9.7}
\]

\[
= [\langle D_X(\mathcal{W}(Y)),Z\rangle + \langle \mathcal{W}(Y),D_X(Z)\rangle]\mathcal{N} - \langle \mathcal{W}(Y),Z\rangle\mathcal{W}(X).
\]
