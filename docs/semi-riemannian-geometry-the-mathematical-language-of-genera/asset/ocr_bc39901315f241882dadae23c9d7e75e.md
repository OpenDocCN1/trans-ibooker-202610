and in the latter case,

g^{-1}(a) = d,      g^{-1}(b) = c,      and      \frac{dg}{du}(u) = -\left|\frac{dg}{du}(u)\right|.

Either way,

\int_{g^{-1}(a)}^{g^{-1}(b)} \left\|\frac{d\lambda}{dt}(g(u))\right\| \frac{dg}{du}(u) du = \int_{d}^{c} \left\|\frac{d\lambda}{dt}(g(u))\right\| \left|\frac{dg}{du}(u)\right| du = \int_{c}^{d} \left\|\frac{d(\lambda \circ g)}{du}(u)\right\| du = L(\lambda \circ g).

The result follows.

# 12.2 Regular Surfaces in $\mathbb{R}^3_{\nu}$

A regular surface is by definition a subset of $\mathbb{R}^3$. We now view a regular surface as a subset of $\mathbb{R}^3_{\nu}$, where $\nu$ is left unspecified. The scalar product on $\mathbb{R}^3_{\nu}$ is given by

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle = \begin{cases} \mathfrak{e} & \text{if } \nu = 0 \\ \mathfrak{m} & \text{if } \nu = 1, \end{cases}

where $\mathfrak{e}$ and $\mathfrak{m}$ are the Euclidean inner product and Minkowski scalar product, respectively.

Let $M$ be a regular surface, and let $p$ be a point in $M$. We obtain a symmetric tensor $\mathfrak{g}_p$ in $\mathcal{T}^0_2(T_p(M))$ by restricting the scalar product on $\mathbb{R}^3_{\nu}$ to $T_p(M) \times T_p(M)$:

\mathfrak{g}_p = \begin{cases} \mathfrak{e}|_{T_p(M)} & \text{if } \nu = 0 \\ \mathfrak{m}|_{T_p(M)} & \text{if } \nu = 1. \end{cases} \quad (12.2.1)

For brevity, we usually denote

\mathfrak{g}_p(\cdot,\cdot) \quad \text{by} \quad \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle.

Whether the notation $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ refers to the scalar product on $\mathbb{R}^3_{\nu}$ or the tensor $\mathfrak{g}_p$ will be clear from the context.

The first fundamental form on $M$ is the map denoted by $\mathfrak{g}$ and defined by the assignment $p \longmapsto \mathfrak{g}_p$ for all $p$ in $M$. In the literature, $\mathfrak{g}$ is often denoted by $I$. For vector fields $X,Y$ in $\mathfrak{X}(M)$, we define a function

\mathfrak{g}(X,Y) = \langle X,Y\rangle : M \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}

in $C^{\infty}(M)$ by the assignment

p \longmapsto \mathfrak{g}_p(X_p,Y_p) = \langle X_p,Y_p\rangle
