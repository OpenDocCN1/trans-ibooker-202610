are said to be **disjoint**. Every permutation in $\mathcal{S}_m$ can be written as the product of disjoint cycles, and in exactly one way (up to order of terms).

A cycle of length 2 is called a **transposition**. Any cycle of length 3 or more can be written in several ways as the product of transpositions. For example,

(a₁ a₂ a₃ a₄ … aₖ₋₁ aₖ) = (a₁ a₂)(a₂ a₃)(a₃ a₄)⋯(aₖ₋₁ aₖ)  (B.2.2)

and

(a₁ a₂ a₃ a₄ … aₖ₋₁ aₖ) = (a₁ aₖ)(a₁ aₖ₋₁)⋯(a₁ a₃)(a₁ a₂).  (B.2.3)

The inverse of a cycle is easily obtained:

(a₁ a₂ a₃ a₄ … aₖ₋₁ aₖ)⁻¹ = (a₁ aₖ aₖ₋₁ … a₄ a₃ a₂).  (B.2.4)

It needs to be emphasized that since composition of functions proceeds from right to left, so does multiplication of permutations, including multiplication of transpositions. For example, (1 2)(2 3) sends 1 to 2, and 2 to 3, and 3 to 2 to 1, so that (1 2)(2 3) = (1 2 3).

# **Example B.2.1.** Consider the permutation

$\sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\ 4 & 5 & 1 & 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$  (B.2.5)

in $\mathcal{S}_5$, which has the cycle decomposition $\sigma = (1\ 4\ 3)(2\ 5)$. Although there are no cycles of length 1 in this example, it is conventional to suppress such cycles in a cycle decomposition. Since the cycles in a cycle decomposition are disjoint, they commute, so we could equally write $\sigma = (2\ 5)(1\ 4\ 3)$. The inverse of $\sigma$ is found simply by interchanging the rows of (B.2.5) and reordering columns to obtain

$\sigma^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\ 3 & 5 & 4 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$

which has the cycle decomposition $\sigma^{-1} = (1\ 3\ 4)(2\ 5)$. The cycle decomposition of $\sigma^{-1}$ can be obtained directly from the cycle decomposition of $\sigma$ using (B.2.4):

$\sigma^{-1} = (1\ 4\ 3)^{-1}(2\ 5)^{-1} = (1\ 3\ 4)(2\ 5)$.

With the aid of (B.2.2) and (B.2.3), we obtain alternative cycle decompositions of $\sigma^{-1}$:

$\sigma^{-1} = (1\ 3)(3\ 4)(2\ 5) = (1\ 4)(1\ 3)(2\ 5)$.

Let $\tau = (2\ 4)$, and consider the permutation

$\tau\sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\ 2 & 5 & 1 & 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$,  (B.2.6)

which has the cycle decomposition $\tau\sigma = (1\ 2\ 5\ 4\ 3)$. We note that (B.2.6) is obtained simply by switching the entries 2 and 4 in the second row of (B.2.5).
