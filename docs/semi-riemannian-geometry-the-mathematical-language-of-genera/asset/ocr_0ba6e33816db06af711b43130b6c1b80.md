The two terms on the right-hand side of (16.10.8) are

$$\begin{aligned}
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{Y(f)(p)-Y(f)(\Phi_{-t}(p))}{t}
&=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{Y(f)(\Phi_{-t}(p))-Y(f)(p)}{-t}\\
&=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{Y(f)(\Phi_t(p))-Y(f)(p)}{t}\\
&=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(\Phi_t)_*(Y(f))(p)-Y(f)(p)}{t}\\
&=\mathcal{L}_X(Y(f))(p)\\
&=X(Y(f))(p).
\end{aligned}$$

and

$$\begin{aligned}
\lim_{t\to 0}Y(h_t)(\Phi_{-t}(p))&=Y(h_0)(\Phi_0(p))\\
&=Y(X(f))(p).
\end{aligned}$$

Substituting (16.10.9) and (16.10.10) into (16.10.8) gives

$$\begin{aligned}
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(\Phi_t)^*(Y)_p-(\Phi_t)_*(Y)_p(f)}{t}
&=X(Y(f))(p)-Y(X(f))(p)\\
&=[X,Y](f)(p)\\
&=[X,Y]_p(f)\\
&=\mathcal{L}_X(Y)_p(f).
\end{aligned}$$

Since $f$ was arbitrary,

$$\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(\Phi_t)_*(Y)_p-Y_p}{t}=\mathcal{L}_X(Y)_p.$\quad\square$$

The second right-hand side of (16.10.4) allows us to think of $\mathcal{L}_X(Y)_p$ as a type of “directional derivative” of $Y$ in the “direction” $X_p$, in the following sense. Ordinarily there is no connection between vectors in $T_p(M)$ and those in other tangent spaces of $M$. The maximal flow of $X$ establishes a link between $T_p(M)$ and those tangent spaces of $M$ that correspond to points that lie on the maximal integral curve with starting point $p$. To find $\mathcal{L}_X(Y)_p$, the diffeomorphism $\Phi_t$ is used to compute the pullback of $Y$ at $p$, yielding a vector $(\Phi_t)_*(Y)_p$ in $T_p(M)$, which is then compared with $Y_p$ by taking a limit. This makes sense because all computations are performed in $T_p(M)$.

In Section 16.4, the Lie derivative was formulated as a tensor derivation. In particular, the Lie derivative of a vector field was defined in terms of the Lie bracket. It is more usual in the literature for the Lie derivative of a vector field to be defined using the first equality in (16.10.4). Theorem 16.10.5 shows that the two approaches are equivalent.
