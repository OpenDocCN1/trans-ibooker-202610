**Proof.** It is clear that \(\iota\) is a linear map. Let \((h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) be a basis for \(V\), and let \((\theta^1,\ldots,\theta^m)\) be its dual basis. If \(v\) is a vector in \(V\) such that \(\iota(v)=0\), then \(\eta(v)=0\) for all covectors \(\eta\) in \(V^*\). In particular, \(\theta^i(v)=0\) for \(i=1,\ldots,m\). It follows from Theorem 1.2.1(d) that \(v=0\), hence \(\ker(\iota)=\{0\}\). By Theorem 1.1.12, \(\iota\) is injective. Using Theorem 1.2.1(c) twice yields

$$\dim(V)=\dim(V^*)=\dim(V^{**}).$$

The result now follows Theorem 1.1.14.

In view of Theorem 1.2.2, and especially because \(\iota\) was defined without choosing specific bases for \(V\) and \(V^*\), we adopt the following convention:

**Throughout, we identify \(V^{**}\) with \(V\), and write \(V^{**}=V\).**

Let \(v\) be a vector in \(V\), and let \(\eta\) be a covector in \(V^*\). Having made the identification \(V^{**}=V\), we henceforth denote

$$\iota(v)\quad\text{by}\quad v.$$

Thus, (1.2.1) and (1.2.2) both become

$$v(\eta)=\eta(v). \tag{1.2.3}$$

In particular, we have

$$h_j(\theta^i)=\theta^i(h_j)$$

for \(i,j=1,\ldots,m\).

# ## 1.3 Pullback of Covectors

In Section 1.2, we introduced the dual space of a vector space. Continuing with that theme, we now associate with a given linear map a corresponding linear map between their dual spaces.

Let \(V\) and \(W\) be vector spaces, and let \(A:V\longrightarrow W\) be a linear map. **Pullback by \(A\)** is the linear map

$$A^*:W^*\longrightarrow V^*$$

defined by

$$A^*(\eta)=\eta\circ A$$

for all covectors \(\eta\) in \(W^*\); that is,

$$A^*(\eta)(v)=\eta(A(v)). \tag{1.3.1}$$
