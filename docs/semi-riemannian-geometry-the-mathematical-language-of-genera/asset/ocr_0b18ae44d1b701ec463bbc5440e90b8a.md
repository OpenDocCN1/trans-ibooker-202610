We then have from Theorem 10.1.17 that $G=(G\circ\varphi^{-1})\circ\varphi$ is smooth on $U$.

(b): This follows from (12.2.3) and part (c).

Continuing with the setup of Theorem 12.2.11, we note that the existence of a unit normal smooth vector field corresponding to each chart on $M$ does not guarantee the existence of a unit normal smooth vector field along $M$. The reason is that the unit normal vector fields corresponding to different charts may not agree on the overlaps of images of their coordinate domains. In Section 12.7, we place additional structure on $M$ that resolves this problem.

Let us now turn our attention to a special class of regular surfaces in $\mathbb{R}^3_\nu$. Recall from Section 3.1 that the quadratic function $q$ corresponding to $\mathbb{R}^3_\nu$ is given by $q(\cdot)=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. We consider three level sets of $q$, the first of which we have seen previously. For $\nu=0$, the **unit sphere** is

$$S^2=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^3_0:q(p)=1\}=q^{-1}(1). \tag{12.2.6}$$

For $\nu=1$, we define the **pseudosphere** by

$$P^2=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^3_1:q(p)=1\}=q^{-1}(1), \tag{12.2.7}$$

and **hyperbolic space** by

$$H^2=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^3_1:q(p)=-1\}=q^{-1}(-1). \tag{12.2.8}$$

Thus, $S^2$ is the set of (spacelike) unit vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3_0$, $P^2$ is the set of spacelike unit vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3_1$, and $H^2$ is the set of timelike unit vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3_1$. Taken together, $S^2$, $P^2$, and $H^2$ are called the **hyperquadrics** in $\mathbb{R}^3_\nu$ and are denoted collectively by $Q^2$. We have the following table:

| $Q^2$ | $\nu$ | Type of vectors |
|---|---:|---|
| $S^2$ | 0 | spacelike |
| $P^2$ | 1 | spacelike |
| $H^2$ | 1 | timelike |
