Let $M$ be a smooth manifold, and let $\varpi$ and $\vartheta$ be orientation forms on $M$. By Theorem 16.6.1(a), there is a uniquely determined nowhere-vanishing function $f$ in $C^\infty(M)$ such that $\varpi = f\vartheta$. We say that $\varpi$ and $\vartheta$ are **consistent (on $M$)**, and write $\varpi \sim \vartheta$, if $f$ is strictly positive on $M$. It is easily shown that $\sim$ is an equivalence relation on the set of orientation forms on $M$. The equivalence class containing $\varpi$ is denoted by $[\varpi]$. Since $\varpi$ and $-\varpi$ are not consistent, $[\varpi]$ and $[-\varpi]$ are distinct equivalence classes. We emphasize again that there is no guarantee that a given smooth manifold has even one orientation form, so the preceding equivalence relation may be vacuous. On the other hand, if the equivalence relation does exist, there could be more than two equivalence classes.

# **Theorem 16.6.2.** Let $M$ be a smooth $m$-manifold, let $(U,(x^i))$ and $(\widetilde{U},(\widetilde{x}^i))$ be overlapping charts on $M$, let $\mathcal{X}$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}$ be the corresponding coordinate frames, and let $(d(x^1),\ldots,d(x^m))$ and $(d(\widetilde{x}^1),\ldots,d(\widetilde{x}^m))$ be the corresponding dual coordinate frames. Then $(U,(x^i))$ and $(\widetilde{U},(\widetilde{x}^i))$ are consistent if and only if $d(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^m)$ and $d(\widetilde{x}^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(\widetilde{x}^m)$ are consistent on $U\cap\widetilde{U}$.

*Proof.* Let $p$ be a point in $U\cap\widetilde{U}$. Then $\mathcal{X}_p$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_p$ are coordinate bases at $p$, and $(d_p(x^1),\ldots,d_p(x^m))$ and $(d_p(\widetilde{x}^1),\ldots,d_p(\widetilde{x}^m))$ are the corresponding dual coordinate bases. We have from Theorem 7.3.3(a) that

$d_p(\widetilde{x}^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d_p(\widetilde{x}^m) = f(p)\,d_p(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d_p(x^m)$,

where

$f(p) = \det\big([\mathrm{id}_{T_p(M)}]_{\mathcal{X}_p}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_p}\big)$.

The assignment $p\longmapsto f(p)$ defines a function $f$ in $C^\infty(U\cap\widetilde{U})$ such that

$d(\widetilde{x}^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(\widetilde{x}^m) = f\,d(x^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge d(x^m)$

on $U\cap\widetilde{U}$. By Theorem 2.5.3, $f$ is nowhere-vanishing. The result follows.
