8.2.2,
\varpi_{\alpha}|_{p}\left(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{1}_{\beta}}\right|_{p},...,\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{m}_{\beta}}\right|_{p}\right)>0
for all α, hence
\Omega_{p}\left(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{1}_{\beta}}\right|_{p},...,\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{m}_{\beta}}\right|_{p}\right)>0.

Since p was arbitrary, we have from the preceding inequality that Ω is nowhere-vanishing and is therefore an orientation form. Since (Uβ, xβi) was arbitrary, it also follows from the preceding inequality and Theorem 16.6.3 that Ω induces 𝔇.

# Theorem 16.6.5 (Orientability Criterion for Smooth Manifolds). A smooth manifold is orientable if and only if it has an orientation form.

Proof. (⇒): This follows from Theorem 16.6.4.
(⇐): This follows from Theorem 16.6.3.

# Theorem 16.6.6. Let (r¹,...,rᵐ) be standard coordinates on ℝᵐ, and let (d(r¹),...,d(rᵐ)) be the corresponding standard dual coordinate frame. Then d(r¹)∧⋯∧d(rᵐ) induces the standard orientation of ℝᵐ.

Proof. According to Example 15.1.6, we have the identification (∂/∂r¹,...,∂/∂rᵐ) = (e₁,...,eₘ), where (e₁,...,eₘ) is the standard basis for ℝᵐ. By Theorem 15.8.4, d(r¹)∧⋯∧d(rᵐ)(e₁,...,eₘ)=1. Thus, d(r¹)∧⋯∧d(rᵐ) is an orientation form on ℝᵐ. By Theorem 16.6.3, [(e₁,...,eₘ)] is in the orientation of ℝᵐ induced by d(r¹)∧⋯∧d(rᵐ).
