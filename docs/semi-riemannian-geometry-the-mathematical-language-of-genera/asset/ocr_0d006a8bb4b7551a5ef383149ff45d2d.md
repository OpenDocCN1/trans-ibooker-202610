For a matrix $P = [p^i_j]$ in $\mathrm{Mat}_{m\times m}$, the trace of $P$ is defined by

$\operatorname{tr}(P) = \sum_i p^i_i.$

# Theorem 2.1.7. If $P$ and $Q$ are matrices in $\mathrm{Mat}_{m\times m}$, then:

+   (a) $\operatorname{tr}(P+Q)=\operatorname{tr}(P)+\operatorname{tr}(Q)$.
+   (b) $\operatorname{tr}(PQ)=\operatorname{tr}(QP)$.
+   (c) If $Q$ is invertible, then $\operatorname{tr}(Q^{-1}PQ)=\operatorname{tr}(P)$.

Proof. (a): Straightforward.

(b): Let $P = [p^i_j]$ and $Q = [q^i_j]$. By Theorem 2.1.5(a),

$\operatorname{tr}(PQ)=\sum_i P^{(i)}Q_{(i)}=\sum_i\left(\sum_j p^i_j q^j_i\right)=\sum_j\left(\sum_i q^i_j p^j_i\right)$
$=\sum_j Q^{(j)}P_{(j)}=\operatorname{tr}(QP)$.

(c): This follows from part (b).

# 2.2 Matrix Representations

Matrices have many desirable computational properties. For this reason, when computing in vector spaces, it is often convenient to reformulate arguments in terms of matrices. We employ this device often.

Let $V$ be a vector space, let $\mathcal{H}=(h_1,\dots,h_m)$ be a basis for $V$, and let $v$ be a vector in $V$, with

$v = \sum_i a^i h_i.$

The matrix representation of $v$ with respect to $\mathcal{H}$ is denoted by $[v]_{\mathcal{H}}$ and defined by

$[v]_{\mathcal{H}} = \begin{bmatrix} a^1 \\ \vdots \\ a^m \end{bmatrix}.$

We refer to $a^1,\dots,a^m$ as the components of $v$ with respect to $\mathcal{H}$. In particular,

$[h_i]_{\mathcal{H}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \vdots \\ 1 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad (2.2.1)$

where 1 is in the $i$th position and 0s are elsewhere for $i=1,\dots,m$.
