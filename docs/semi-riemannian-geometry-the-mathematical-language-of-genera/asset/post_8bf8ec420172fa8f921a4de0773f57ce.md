
## Example 7.2.1.

Let \(\eta,\zeta\) be covectors in \(\Lambda^1(V)=V^*\), and let \(v_1,v_2\) be vectors in \(V\). With \(\mathcal{S}_2=\{\mathrm{id},(1\,2)\}\), we have

\[
(\eta\wedge\zeta)(v_1,v_2)=\frac{1}{1!1!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_2}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\eta(v_{\sigma(1)})\zeta(v_{\sigma(2)})=\eta(v_1)\zeta(v_2)-\eta(v_2)\zeta(v_1).
\]

Now let \(\eta\) be a multicovector in \(\Lambda^2(V)\), let \(\zeta\) be a covector in \(\Lambda^1(V)=V^*\), and let \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) be vectors in \(V\). With

\[
\mathcal{S}_3=\{\mathrm{id},(1\,2),(1\,3),(2\,3),(1\,2\,3),(1\,3\,2)\},
\]

we have

\[
\begin{aligned}
(\eta\wedge\zeta)(v_1,v_2,v_3)
&=\frac{1}{2!1!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_3}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\eta(v_{\sigma(1)},v_{\sigma(2)})\zeta(v_{\sigma(3)})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left[\eta(v_1,v_2)\zeta(v_3)-\eta(v_2,v_1)\zeta(v_3)-\eta(v_3,v_2)\zeta(v_1)-\eta(v_1,v_3)\zeta(v_2)+\eta(v_2,v_3)\zeta(v_1)+\eta(v_3,v_1)\zeta(v_2)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left[\eta(v_1,v_2)\zeta(v_3)+\eta(v_1,v_2)\zeta(v_3)+\eta(v_2,v_3)\zeta(v_1)+\eta(v_3,v_1)\zeta(v_2)+\eta(v_2,v_3)\zeta(v_1)+\eta(v_3,v_1)\zeta(v_2)\right]\\
&=\eta(v_1,v_2)\zeta(v_3)+\eta(v_2,v_3)\zeta(v_1)+\eta(v_3,v_1)\zeta(v_2).
\end{aligned}
\]

Wedge products behave well with respect to basic algebraic structure.

## Theorem 7.2.2.

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \(\eta,\eta^1,\eta^2\) and \(\zeta,\zeta^1,\zeta^2\) be multicovectors in \(\Lambda^s(V)\) and \(\Lambda^{s'}(V)\), respectively, and let \(c\) be a real number. Then:

- (a) \((\eta^1+\eta^2)\wedge\zeta=\eta^1\wedge\zeta+\eta^2\wedge\zeta\).
- (b) \(\eta\wedge(\zeta^1+\zeta^2)=\eta\wedge\zeta^1+\eta\wedge\zeta^2\).
- (c) \((c\eta)\wedge\zeta=\eta\wedge(c\zeta)=c(\eta\wedge\zeta)\).

Proof. Straightforward.

## Theorem 7.2.3.

If \(V\) is a vector space, and \(\eta\) and \(\zeta\) are multicovectors in \(\Lambda^s(V)\) and \(\Lambda^{s'}(V)\), respectively, then:

- (a) \(\eta\wedge\zeta=(-1)^{ss'}\zeta\wedge\eta\).
- (b) If \(s=s'=1\), then \(\eta\wedge\zeta=-\zeta\wedge\eta\).
- (c) If \(s=1\), then \(\eta\wedge\eta=0\).

Proof. (a): Let \(v_1,\dots,v_{s+s'}\) be vectors in \(V\), and define a permutation \(\sigma\) in \(\mathcal{S}_{s+s'}\) by

\[
\sigma=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & \cdots & s & s+1 & \cdots & s+s'\\
s'+1 & \cdots & s'+s & 1 & \cdots & s'
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

We have

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma(\eta\otimes\zeta)(v_1,\dots,v_{s+s'})
&=\eta\otimes\zeta(v_{\sigma(1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(s)},v_{\sigma(s+1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(s+s')})\\
&=\eta\otimes\zeta(v_{s'+1},\dots,v_{s'+s},v_1,\dots,v_{s'})\\
&=\eta(v_{s'+1},\dots,v_{s'+s})\,\zeta(v_1,\dots,v_{s'})\\
&=\zeta(v_1,\dots,v_{s'})\,\eta(v_{s'+1},\dots,v_{s'+s})\\
&=\zeta\otimes\eta(v_1,\dots,v_{s'},v_{s'+1},\dots,v_{s+s'}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(v_1,\dots,v_{s+s'}\) were arbitrary, \(\sigma(\eta\otimes\zeta)=\zeta\otimes\eta\). Then

\[
\operatorname{Alt}(\zeta\otimes\eta)
=\operatorname{Alt}(\sigma(\eta\otimes\zeta))
=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{Alt}(\eta\otimes\zeta)\quad[\text{Th 7.1.3(a)}]
=(-1)^{ss'}\operatorname{Alt}(\eta\otimes\zeta),\quad[\text{Th B.2.4}]
\]

hence

\[
\eta\wedge\zeta
=\frac{(s+s')!}{s!s'!}\operatorname{Alt}(\eta\otimes\zeta)
=(-1)^{ss'}\frac{(s'+s)!}{s'!s!}\operatorname{Alt}(\zeta\otimes\eta)
=(-1)^{ss'}\zeta\wedge\eta.
\]

- (b): This follows from part (a).
- (c): This follows from part (b).

## Theorem 7.2.4.

If \(V\) is a vector space, and \(\mathcal{A}\) and \(\mathcal{B}\) are multicovectors in \(\Lambda^s(V)\) and \(\Lambda^{s'}(V)\), respectively, then

\[
\operatorname{Alt}(\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A})\otimes\mathcal{B})
=\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B})
=\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{B})).
\]

Proof. Let us define a map \(\iota:\mathcal{S}_s\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{s+s'}\) as follows. For each permutation \(\sigma\) in \(\mathcal{S}_s\), let \(\iota(\sigma)\) be the permutation in \(\mathcal{S}_{s+s'}\) that restricts to \(\sigma\) on \(\{1,\dots,s\}\) and fixes \(s+1,\dots,s+s'\); that is,

\[
\iota(\sigma)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & \cdots & s & s+1 & \cdots & s+s'\\
\sigma(1) & \cdots & \sigma(s) & s+1 & \cdots & s+s'
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Evidently, \(\iota\) is a group isomorphism between \(\mathcal{S}_s\) and the subgroup \(\iota(\mathcal{S}_s)\) of \(\mathcal{S}_{s+s'}\). It is easily shown that

\[
\operatorname{sgn}(\iota(\sigma))=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\iota(\sigma)(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B})=\sigma(\mathcal{A})\otimes\mathcal{B}. \tag{7.2.2}
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Alt}(\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A})\otimes\mathcal{B})
&=\operatorname{Alt}\left(\left[\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\sigma(\mathcal{A})\right]\otimes\mathcal{B}\right)\quad[(7.1.4)]\\
&=\operatorname{Alt}\left(\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\left(\sigma(\mathcal{A})\otimes\mathcal{B}\right)\right)\quad[\text{Th 5.1.2(a)}]\\
&=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{Alt}\left(\sigma(\mathcal{A})\otimes\mathcal{B}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\iota(\sigma))\operatorname{Alt}\left(\iota(\sigma)(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B})\right)\quad[(7.2.2)]\\
&=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_s}\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B})\quad[\text{Th 7.1.3(a)}]\\
&=\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}),\quad[\operatorname{card}(\mathcal{S}_s)=s!]
\end{aligned}
\]

which proves the first equality. The proof of the second equality is similar.

The proof is by induction. Let \(k\ge 4\), and suppose the assertion is true for all indices \(<k\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
(\eta^1\wedge\eta^2)\wedge\eta^3\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta^k
&=\frac{(s_1+s_2+s_3+\cdots+s_k)!}{(s_1+s_2)!s_3!\cdots s_k!}
\operatorname{Alt}\left((\eta^1\wedge\eta^2)\otimes\eta^3\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta^k\right)\\
&=\frac{(s_1+s_2+s_3+\cdots+s_k)!}{(s_1+s_2)!s_3!\cdots s_k!}
\operatorname{Alt}\left(\frac{(s_1+s_2)!}{s_1!s_2!}\operatorname{Alt}(\eta^1\otimes\eta^2)\otimes(\eta^3\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta^k)\right)\\
&=\frac{(s_1+\cdots+s_k)!}{s_1!\cdots s_k!}
\operatorname{Alt}\left(\operatorname{Alt}(\eta^1\otimes\eta^2)\otimes(\eta^3\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta^k)\right)\\
&=\frac{(s_1+\cdots+s_k)!}{s_1!\cdots s_k!}
\operatorname{Alt}(\eta^1\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta^k),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the first equality follows from the induction hypothesis, the second equality from (7.2.1), and the last equality from Theorem 7.2.4.

The next result shows that wedge products and determinants are closely related, which is not so surprising.

## Theorem 7.2.8.

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \(\eta^1,\dots,\eta^s\) be covectors in \(V^*\), and let \(v_1,\dots,v_s\) be vectors in \(V\). Then

\[
\eta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta^s(v_1,\dots,v_s)
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
\eta^1(v_1) & \cdots & \eta^1(v_s)\\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
\eta^s(v_1) & \cdots & \eta^s(v_s)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Proof. We have

\[
\begin{aligned}
\eta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta^s(v_1,\dots,v_s)
&=s!\,\operatorname{Alt}(\eta^1\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta^s)(v_1,\dots,v_s) && [\text{Th 7.2.7}]\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\sigma(\eta^1\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta^s)(v_1,\dots,v_s) && [(7.1.4)]\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\eta^1\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta^s(v_{\sigma(1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(s)}) && [(7.1.1)]\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_s}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\eta^1(v_{\sigma(1)})\cdots\eta^s(v_{\sigma(s)})\\
&=\det([\eta^i(v_j)]). && [(2.4.1)]
\end{aligned}
\]

## Theorem 7.2.9.

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \((h_1,\dots,h_m)\) be a basis for \(V\), and let \((\theta^1,\dots,\theta^m)\) be its dual basis. Then, by Theorem 7.2.8,

\[
\theta^{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{j_s}(h_{i_1},\dots,h_{i_s})
=
\det\left((\theta^i(h_j))_{(i_1,\dots,i_s)}^{(j_1,\dots,j_s)}\right)
=
\det\left((I_m)_{(i_1,\dots,i_s)}^{(j_1,\dots,j_s)}\right).
\]

If \((i_1,\dots,i_s)=(j_1,\dots,j_s)\), then \((I_m)_{(i_1,\dots,i_s)}^{(j_1,\dots,j_s)}=I_s\), and if \((i_1,\dots,i_s)\ne(j_1,\dots,j_s)\), then \((I_m)_{(i_1,\dots,i_s)}^{(j_1,\dots,j_s)}\) has at least one row (and at least one column) of \(0\)s. The result follows.

## Theorem 7.2.10.

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \(\eta^1,\dots,\eta^s\) be covectors in \(V^*\), and let \(\sigma\) be a permutation in \(S_s\). Then

\[
\eta^{\sigma(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta^{\sigma(s)}
=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\eta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta^s.
\]

Proof. For vectors \(v_1,\dots,v_s\) in \(V\), we have

\[
\begin{aligned}
\eta^{\sigma(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta^{\sigma(s)}(v_1,\dots,v_s)
&=\det\left([\eta^{\sigma(i)}(v_j)]\right) && [\text{Th 7.2.8}]\\
&=\det\left([\eta^{\sigma(j)}(v_i)]\right) && [\text{Th 2.4.5(c)}]\\
&=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\det\left([\eta^i(v_i)]\right) && [\text{Th 2.4.2}]\\
&=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\det\left([\eta^i(v_j)]\right) && [\text{Th 2.4.5(c)}]\\
&=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\eta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta^s(v_1,\dots,v_s), && [\text{Th 7.2.8}]
\end{aligned}
\]

where \([\eta^{\sigma(j)}(v_i)]=([\eta^{\sigma(i)}(v_j)])^T\). Since \(v_1,\dots,v_s\) were arbitrary, the result follows.

## Theorem 7.2.11.

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \(H=(h_1,\dots,h_m)\) and \(F\) be bases for \(V\), and let \((\varphi^1,\dots,\varphi^m)\) be the dual basis corresponding to \(F\). Then

\[
\varphi^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\varphi^m(h_1,\dots,h_m)=\det\left([\mathrm{id}_V]^F_H\right).
\]

Proof. By Theorem 1.2.1(d), \(h_j=\sum_i\varphi^i(h_j)f_i\) for \(j=1,\dots,m\). Then (2.2.6) and (2.2.7) give \([\mathrm{id}_V]^F_H=[\varphi^i(h_j)]\). The result now follows from Theorem 7.2.8.

## Theorem 7.2.12 (Basis for \(\Lambda^s(V)\)).

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \((h_1,\dots,h_m)\) be a basis for \(V\), and let \((\theta^1,\dots,\theta^m)\) be its dual basis. Then:

- (a) If \(1\le s\le m\), then

  \[
  \{\theta^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{i_s}:1\le i_1<\cdots<i_s\le m\}
  \]

  is an unordered basis for \(\Lambda^s(V)\).

- (b) If \(s>m\), then \(\Lambda^s(V)=\{0\}\).
- (c) \(\Lambda^s(V)\) has dimension \(\binom{m}{s}\).
- (d) Any nonzero multicovector in \(\Lambda^m(V)\) spans \(\Lambda^m(V)\).
- (e) \((\eta^1,\dots,\eta^m)\) is a basis for \(\Lambda^{m-1}(V)\), where

  \[
  \eta^i=(-1)^{i-1}\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\theta^i}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m
  \]

  for \(i=1,\dots,m\), and where \(\widehat{\theta^i}\) indicates that an expression is omitted.

Proof. Let

\[
\Omega_s=\{\theta^{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\theta^{i_s}:1\le i_1,\dots,i_s\le m\}
\]

and

\[
\Theta_s=\{\theta^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{i_s}:1\le i_1<\cdots<i_s\le m\}.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{span}(\Theta_s)
&=\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{Alt}(\Omega_s)) && [\text{Th 7.2.7}]\\
&=\operatorname{Alt}(\operatorname{span}(\Omega_s))\\
&=\operatorname{Alt}(\mathcal{T}_s^0(V)) && [\text{Th 5.1.3(b)}]\\
&=\Lambda^s(V), && [\text{Th 7.1.3(e)}]
\end{aligned}
\]

so \(\Theta_s\) spans \(\Lambda^s(V)\) for all \(s\ge1\).

- (a): If

  \[
  \sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_s\le m} a_{i_1\dots i_s}\,\theta^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{i_s}=0
  \]

  for some real numbers \(a_{i_1\dots i_s}\), then evaluating both sides of the preceding identity at \((h_{j_1},\dots,h_{j_s})\) and using Theorem 7.2.9 yields \(a_{j_1\dots j_s}=0\) for all \(1\le j_1<\cdots<j_s\le m\). Thus, \(\Theta_s\) is linearly independent.

- (b): Since \(s>m\), each element of \(\Theta_s\) contains a repeated covector, so the result follows from Theorem 7.2.6.
- (c): This follows from parts (a) and (b).
- (d): By part (c), \(\Lambda^m(V)\) is 1-dimensional.
- (e): For \(s=m-1\), we have \(\Theta_{m-1}=\{(-1)^{i-1}\eta^i:i=1,\dots,m\}\), so the result follows from part (a).

Part (a) of the next result is a generalization of Theorem 1.2.1(e).

## Theorem 7.2.13.

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \(\eta\) be a multicovector in \(\Lambda^s(V)\), let \((h_1,\dots,h_m)\) be a basis for \(V\), and let \((\theta^1,\dots,\theta^m)\) be its dual basis. Then:

- (a)

  \[
  \eta=\sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_s\le m}\eta(h_{i_1},\dots,h_{i_s})\,\theta^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{i_s}.
  \]

- (b) If \(s=m\), then

  \[
  \eta=\eta(h_1,\dots,h_m)\,\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m.
  \]

Proof. (a): By Theorem 7.2.12(a),

\[
\eta=\sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_s\le m}a_{i_1\dots i_s}\,\theta^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{i_s}
\]

for some real numbers \(a_{i_1\dots i_s}\). Evaluating both sides of the preceding identity at \((h_{j_1},\dots,h_{j_s})\) and using Theorem 7.2.9 yields \(a_{j_1\dots j_s}=\eta(h_{j_1},\dots,h_{j_s})\) for all \(1\le j_1<\cdots<j_s\le m\).

(b): This follows from part (a).
