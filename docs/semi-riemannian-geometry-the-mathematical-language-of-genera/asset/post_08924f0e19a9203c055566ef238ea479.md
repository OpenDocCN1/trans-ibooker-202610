
## 18.10 Normal Coordinates

Let $(M,\nabla)$ be a smooth $m$-manifold with a connection, let $p$ be a point in $M$, and let $(\widetilde{e}_1,\dots,\widetilde{e}_m)$ be a basis for $T_p(M)$. The map

$\ell_p : \mathbb{R}^m \longrightarrow T_p(M)$

defined by

$\ell_p(r^1,\dots,r^m)=\sum_i r^i\widetilde{e}_i \qquad (18.10.1)$

is a linear isomorphism. Let $V$ be a normal neighborhood of $p$ in $M$, let $\exp_p : \widetilde{V} \longrightarrow V$ be the corresponding diffeomorphism, and consider the smooth map

$\ell_p^{-1}\circ\exp_p^{-1} : V \longrightarrow \ell_p^{-1}(\widetilde{V})$

Then

$(V,\ell_p^{-1}\circ\exp_p^{-1}=(x^1,\dots,x^m)) \qquad (18.10.2)$

is a chart at $p$, called **normal coordinates at $p$**. Let $\mathcal{X}_p=((\partial/\partial x^1)|_p,\dots,(\partial/\partial x^m)|_p)$ and $(d_p(x^1),\dots,d_p(x^m))$ be the corresponding coordinate and dual coordinate bases at $p$.

### Theorem 18.10.1. With the above setup:

(a)

$\widetilde{e}_i = \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p$

for $i=1,\dots,m$.

(b)

$x^i\circ\exp_p = d_p(x^i)$

for $i=1,\dots,m$.

(c)

$\exp_p^{-1}(q)=\sum_i x^i(q)\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p$

for all $q$ in $V$.

Proof. (a): Let $\mathcal{E}=(e_1,\dots,e_m)$ be the standard basis for $\mathbb{R}^m$, and let $\varphi=\ell_p^{-1}\circ\exp_p^{-1}$. We have

$d_{\varphi(p)}(\varphi^{-1})=d_{\varphi(p)}(\exp_p\circ\ell_p)=d_{\ell_p(\varphi(p))}(\exp_p)\circ d_{\varphi(p)}(\ell_p)\quad[\text{Th 14.4.5}] =d_0(\exp_p\circ\ell_p)\quad[\text{Th 14.4.4}]=\ell_p.\quad[\text{Th 18.9.4}]$

Then Theorem 14.8.5(d) and (18.10.1) give

$\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p = \ell_p(e_i)=\widetilde{e}_i.$

for i, j = 1, ..., m. Observe that the Γ^k_ij(p) are independent of the choice of (r^1, ..., r^m). Setting

```
(r^1, ..., r^i, ..., r^m) = (0, ..., 1, ..., 0)
```

yields

```
Γ^k_ii(p) = 0    (18.10.4)
```

for i = 1, ..., m; and setting

```
(r^1, ..., r^i, ..., r^j, ..., r^m) = (0, ..., 1, ..., 1, ..., 0)
```

gives

```
0 = Γ^k_ii(p) + Γ^k_ij(p) + Γ^k_ji(p) + Γ^k_jj(p) = 2Γ^k_ij(p)
```

for i ≠ j = 1, ..., m, where the last equality follows from Theorem 18.6.3(b) and (18.10.4).

## 18.11 Jacobi Fields

On intuitive grounds, it seems that the way geodesics radiate from a given point on a smooth manifold, and the rapidity with which they spread out, should be related to the “curvature” of the smooth manifold at that point. This line of reasoning leads to a consideration of Jacobi fields.

Let (M, ∇) be a smooth manifold with a connection, and let γ(r) : (a, b) ⟶ M be a geodesic. We say that the vector field J in 𝔛_M(γ) is a Jacobi field if it satisfies Jacobi’s equation:

```
R(J, dγ/dr) dγ/dr + ∇²J/dr² = 0,    (18.11.1)
```

where we denote

```
∇/dr ∘ ∇/dr  by  ∇²/dr².
```

By Theorem 18.7.1(a), Jacobi’s equation is equivalent to

```
R(dγ/dr, J) dγ/dr = ∇²J/dr².
```

### Theorem 18.11.1 (Existence of Jacobi Field). Let (M, ∇) be a smooth manifold with a connection, and let p be a point in M. Let γ(r) : (a, b) ⟶ M be a geodesic, with γ(0) = p, and let v, w be vectors in T_p(M). Then there is a unique Jacobi field J in 𝔛_M(γ) such that J(0) = v and (∇J/∂r)(0) = w.

Proof. It can be shown that Jacobi’s equation is equivalent to a system of ordinary differential equations. The result now follows from the theory of differential equations.

Let $(M, \nabla)$ be a smooth manifold with a connection, and let

$\sigma(r,s): (a,b) \times (-\varepsilon,\varepsilon) \longrightarrow M$

be a parametrized surface. Recall from Section 14.9 that for a given point $s$ in $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, the corresponding longitude curve $\sigma_s(r): (a,b) \longrightarrow M$ is defined by $\sigma_s(r) = \sigma(r,s)$. Let $\gamma(r): (a,b) \longrightarrow M$ be a geodesic. We say that $\sigma$ is a **geodesic variation** of $\gamma$ if $\sigma_0 = \gamma$ and each $\sigma_s$ is a geodesic.

Suppose $\sigma$ is in fact a geodesic variation of $\gamma$. Then

$\frac{d\gamma}{dr}(r) = \frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial r}(r,0)$

(18.11.2)

for all $r$ in $(a,b)$. The **variation field** of $\sigma$ is the vector field $J$ in $\mathfrak{X}_M(\gamma)$ defined by

$J(r) = \frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial s}(r,0)$

(18.11.3)

for all $r$ in $(a,b)$. Since $\sigma_s$ is a geodesic and $\partial \sigma / \partial r$ is its velocity, we have

$\frac{\nabla}{\partial r}\left(\frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial r}\right)(r,s) = 0$

(18.11.4)

for all $(r,s)$ in $(a,b) \times (-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$. See Figure 18.11.1.

### Example 18.11.2 ($\mathcal{S}^2$). In the notation of Section 13.4, consider the parametrized surface

$\varphi(\theta,\phi): (0,\pi) \times (-\pi,\pi) \longrightarrow \mathcal{S}^2$

given by

$\varphi(\theta,\phi)=(\sin(\theta)\cos(\phi),\sin(\theta)\sin(\phi),\cos(\theta)).$

The image of the smooth curve

$\gamma(\theta)=\varphi(\theta,0)=(\sin(\theta),0,\cos(\theta)):(0,\pi)\longrightarrow S^2$

is a portion of the unit circle in the $xz$-plane centered at the origin, which is a great circle of $S^2$. It follows from Example 18.8.10 that $\gamma$ is a geodesic. Thus, $\varphi$ is a geodesic variation of $\gamma$.

### Theorem 18.11.3.

If $(M,\nabla)$ is a smooth manifold with a symmetric connection and $\gamma$ is a geodesic, then the variation field of any geodesic variation of $\gamma$ is a Jacobi field in $\mathcal{X}_M(\gamma)$.

*Proof.* Let $\sigma(r,s):(a,b)\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow M$ be a geodesic variation of $\gamma$, and let $J$ be its variation field. Then

```
$$\begin{aligned}
0&=\frac{\nabla}{\partial s}\left(\frac{\nabla}{\partial r}\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}\right)\right)\\
&=\frac{\nabla}{\partial r}\left(\frac{\nabla}{\partial s}\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}\right)\right)+R\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s},\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}\right)\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}\\
&=\frac{\nabla}{\partial r}\left(\frac{\nabla}{\partial r}\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s}\right)\right)+R\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s},\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}\right)\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}\\
&=\frac{\nabla^2}{\partial r^2}\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s}\right)+R\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s},\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}\right)\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}.
\end{aligned}$$
```

Setting $s=0$ and using (18.11.2) and (18.11.3) gives (18.11.1).

### Theorem 18.11.4.

Let $(M,\nabla)$ be a smooth manifold with a connection, let $p$ be a point in $M$, and let $\psi(s):(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow T_p(M)$ be a smooth curve. Consider the parametrized surface $\sigma(r,s):[0,1]\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow M$ given by

$$\sigma(r,s)=\exp_p(r\psi(s)),$$

where it is assumed that $r\psi(s)$ is in $\mathcal{E}_p$ for all $(r,s)$ in $[0,1]\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$. Then:

- (a) For each point $s$ in $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, $\sigma_s$ is the radial geodesic $\rho_{p,\psi(s)}$ with starting point $p$ and initial velocity $\psi(s)$.
- (b) $\sigma$ is a geodesic variation of $\sigma_0$.
- (c) $$\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}(r,s)=d_{r\psi(s)}(\exp_p)(\psi(s)).$$
- (d) $$\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s}(r,s)=r\,d_{r\psi(s)}(\exp_p)\left(\frac{d\psi}{ds}(s)\right).$$

**Proof.** (a): By Theorem 18.9.3,

\[
\sigma_s(r)=\sigma(r,s)=\exp_p(r\psi(s))=\rho_{p,\psi(s)}(r)
\]

for all \(r\) in \([0,1]\).

(b): This follows from part (a).

(c), (d): We define a smooth map

\[
\widetilde{\sigma}(r,s):[0,1]\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow T_p(M)
\]

by

\[
\widetilde{\sigma}(r,s)=r\psi(s),
\]

so that \(\sigma=\exp_p\circ\widetilde{\sigma}\). Then

\[
\frac{\partial\widetilde{\sigma}}{\partial r}(r,s)=\psi(s)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{\partial\widetilde{\sigma}}{\partial s}(r,s)=r\frac{d\psi}{ds}(s).
\]

hence

\[
\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}(r,s)
=\frac{\partial(\exp_p\circ\widetilde{\sigma})}{\partial r}(r,s)
=d_{\sigma(r,s)}(\exp_p)\left(\frac{\partial\widetilde{\sigma}}{\partial r}(r,s)\right)
=d_{r\psi(s)}(\exp_p)(\psi(s)).
\]

Similarly,

\[
\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s}(r,s)
=\frac{\partial(\exp_p\circ\widetilde{\sigma})}{\partial s}(r,s)
=d_{\sigma(r,s)}(\exp_p)\left(\frac{\partial\widetilde{\sigma}}{\partial s}(r,s)\right)
=d_{r\psi(s)}(\exp_p)\left(r\frac{d\psi}{ds}(s)\right)
=r\,d_{r\psi(s)}(\exp_p)\left(\frac{d\psi}{ds}(s)\right).
\]

### Theorem 18.11.5.

*Let $(M,\nabla)$ be a smooth manifold with a connection, let $p$ be a point in $M$, and let $v,w$ be vectors in $T_p(M)$. Let $\rho_{p,v}(r)=\exp_p(rv):[0,1]\to M$ be the radial geodesic with starting point $p$ and initial velocity $v$. Let $\sigma(r,s):[0,1]\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M$ be the geodesic variation of $\rho_{p,v}(r)$ defined by*

\[
\sigma(r,s)=\exp_p(r(v+sw)),
\]

*where it is assumed that $r(v+sw)$ is in $E_p$ for all $(r,s)\in[0,1]\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$. Then:*

(a) *The variation field $J$ of $\sigma$ is given by*

\[
J(r)=r\,d_{rv}(\exp_p)(w)
\]

*for all $r$ in $[0,1]$.*
