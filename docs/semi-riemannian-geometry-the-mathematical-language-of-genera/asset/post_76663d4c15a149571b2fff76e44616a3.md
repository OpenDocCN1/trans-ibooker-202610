
**Proof.** (a), (b): Straightforward.

(c): Let \(\mathcal{H}=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\), \(\mathcal{F}=(f_1,\ldots,f_n)\), and \(\mathcal{G}=(g_1,\ldots,g_r)\), and let \([A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}=[a_j^i]\) and \([C]_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{G}}=[c_l^k]\). According to (2.2.2) and (2.2.3),

\[
A(h_j)=\sum_i a_j^i f_i \qquad\text{and}\qquad C(f_l)=\sum_k c_l^k g_k.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
C(A(h_j))
&=C\left(\sum_k a_j^k f_k\right)
=\sum_k a_j^k C(f_k)
=\sum_k a_j^k\left(\sum_i c_k^i g_i\right)\\
&=\sum_i\left(\sum_k c_k^i a_j^k\right)g_i
=\sum_i\left([C]_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{G}}\right)^{(i)}_{(\cdot)}
\left([A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)_{(\cdot)j}g_i,
\end{aligned}
\]
from which the result follows.

### Theorem 2.2.3 (Representation of Linear Maps)

Let \(V\) and \(W\) be vector spaces of dimensions \(m\) and \(n\), respectively, and let \(\mathcal{H}\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) be respective bases. Define a map

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}:\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}
\]

by

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}(A)=[A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}
\]

for all maps \(A\) in \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\), where \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\) is defined in Example 1.1.1.

Then:

- (a) \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\) is a linear isomorphism with respect to the additive structure of \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\):

  \[
  \operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\cong\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}.
  \]

- (b) If \(V=W\), then \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\) is a ring isomorphism with respect to the multiplicative structure of \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,V)\).

**Remark.** We showed in Example 1.1.1 that \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,V)\) is both a vector space and a ring, and remarked at the beginning of Section 2.1 that the same is true of \(\operatorname{Mat}_{m\times m}\), so the assertion in part (b) makes sense.

**Proof.** (a): By parts (a) and (b) of Theorem 2.2.2, \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\) is a linear map, and it is easily shown that \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\) is injective. We claim that \(\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}\) and \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\) both have dimension \(mn\). Let \(E_{ij}\) be the matrix in \(\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}\) with 1 in the \(ij\)-th position and 0s elsewhere for \(i=1,\ldots,n\) and \(j=1,\ldots,m\). It is readily demonstrated that the \(E_{ij}\) comprise a basis for \(\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}\), which therefore has dimension \(mn\). Let \(\mathcal{H}=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) and \(\mathcal{F}=(f_1,\ldots,f_n)\), and using Theorem 1.1.10, define linear maps \(L_{ij}\) in \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\) by

\[
L_{ij}(h_k)=
\begin{cases}
f_i & \text{if } k=j,\\
0 & \text{if } k\ne j.
\end{cases}
\]

for \(i=1,\ldots,n\) and \(j=1,\ldots,m\). Then

\[
\left(\sum_{ij}a^i_jL_{ij}\right)(h_k)=\sum_{ij}a^i_jL_{ij}(h_k)=\sum_i a^i_kf_i
\]

for all real numbers \(a^i_j\). Let \(B\) be a map in \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\), with \([B]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}=[b^i_j]\). We have from (2.2.4) that

\[
\left(\sum_{ij}b^i_jL_{ij}\right)(h_k)=\sum_i b^i_kf_i=B(h_k)
\]

for \(k=1,\ldots,m\). By Theorem 1.1.10, \(\sum_{ij}b^i_jL_{ij}=B\), so the \(L_{ij}\) span \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\).

Suppose \(\sum_{ij}a^i_jL_{ij}=0\) for some real numbers \(a^i_j\), where \(0\) denotes the zero map in \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\). It follows from (2.2.4) that \(\sum_i a^i_kf_i=0\) for \(k=1,\ldots,m\). Since \(\mathcal F\) is a basis for \(W\), \(a^i_k=0\) for \(i=1,\ldots,n\) and \(k=1,\ldots,m\), so the \(L_{ij}\) are linearly independent. Thus, the \(L_{ij}\) comprise a basis for \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\), and therefore, \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\) has dimension \(mn\). This proves the claim. Suppose \(\mathcal L^{\mathcal F}_{\mathcal H}(A)=O_{n\times m}\) for some map \(A\) in \(\operatorname{Lin}(V,W)\). It follows from Theorem 1.1.10, (2.2.2), and (2.2.3) that \(A\) is the zero map, hence \(\ker(\mathcal L^{\mathcal F}_{\mathcal H})=\{0\}\). The result now follows from Theorem 1.1.12 and Theorem 1.1.14.

(b): This follows from Theorem 2.2.2(c) and part (a).

### Theorem 2.2.4

Let \(V\) and \(W\) be vector spaces, let \(\mathcal H\) and \(\mathcal F\) be respective bases, let \(A:V\to W\) be a linear map, and let \(v\) be a vector in \(V\). Then

\[
[A(v)]_{\mathcal F}=[A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}[v]_{\mathcal H}.
\]

**Proof.** Let \(\mathcal H=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) and \(v=\sum_i a^i h_i\). It follows from (2.2.1) and (2.2.2) that

\[
[A(h_i)]_{\mathcal F}=[A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}[h_i]_{\mathcal H}.
\]

By parts (a) and (b) of Theorem 2.2.2,

\[
\begin{aligned}
[A(v)]_{\mathcal F}
&=\left[A\left(\sum_i a^i h_i\right)\right]_{\mathcal F}
=\left[\sum_i a^i A(h_i)\right]_{\mathcal F}
=\sum_i a^i [A(h_i)]_{\mathcal F}\\
&=\sum_i a^i\left([A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}[h_i]_{\mathcal H}\right)
=[A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}\left(\sum_i a^i [h_i]_{\mathcal H}\right)
=[A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}\begin{bmatrix}a^1\\ \vdots\\ a^m\end{bmatrix}
=[A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}[v]_{\mathcal H}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(V\) be a vector space, and let \(\mathcal H\) and \(\mathcal F\) be bases for \(V\). Setting \(A=\operatorname{id}_V\) in Theorem 2.2.4 yields

\[
[v]_{\mathcal F}=[\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}[v]_{\mathcal H}.
\]

This shows that \([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}\) is the matrix that transforms components with respect to \(\mathcal H\) into components with respect to \(\mathcal F\). For this reason, \([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}\) is called the change of basis matrix from \(\mathcal H\) to \(\mathcal F\). Let \([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}=[a^i_j]\). Then (2.2.2) and (2.2.3) specialize to

\[
h_j=\sum_i a^i_j f_i
\]

for \(j=1,\ldots,m\) and

\[
[\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}
=\left[\,[h_1]_{\mathcal F}\quad\cdots\quad [h_m]_{\mathcal F}\,\right].
\]

### Theorem 2.2.5

Let \(V\) and \(W\) be vector spaces, let \(\mathcal H\) and \(\mathcal F\) be respective bases, and let \(A:V\to W\) be a linear isomorphism. Then:

- (a) \([A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}=[\operatorname{id}_W]_{A(\mathcal H)}^{\mathcal F}\)
- (b) \([A^{-1}]_{\mathcal F}^{\mathcal H}=\left([A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}\right)^{-1}.\)

**Remark.** By Theorem 1.1.13(a), \(A(\mathcal H)\) is a basis for \(W\), so the assertion in part (a) makes sense.

**Proof.** (a): Let \(\mathcal H=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\), so that

\[
[A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}=\left[\,[A(h_1)]_{\mathcal F}\quad\cdots\quad[A(h_m)]_{\mathcal F}\,\right]
= [\operatorname{id}_W]_{A(\mathcal H)}^{\mathcal F}.
\]

(b): By Theorem 2.2.2(c),

\[
I_m=[\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal H}=[A^{-1}\circ A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal H}
=[A^{-1}]_{\mathcal F}^{\mathcal H}[A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F},
\]

from which the result follows. \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.2.6 (Change of Basis)

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \(\mathcal H\) and \(\mathcal F\) be bases for \(V\), and let \(A:V\to V\) be a linear map. Then

\[
[A]_{\mathcal F}^{\mathcal F}=\left([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal F}^{\mathcal H}\right)^{-1}[A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal H}[\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal F}^{\mathcal H}.
\]

**Proof.** By Theorem 2.2.2(c),

\[
[A]_{\mathcal F}^{\mathcal F}=[\operatorname{id}_V\circ A\circ\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal F}^{\mathcal F}
=[\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}[A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal H}[\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal F}^{\mathcal H}.
\]

The result now follows from Theorem 2.2.5(b). \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.2.7

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \(\mathcal H\) and \(\widetilde{\mathcal H}\) be bases for \(V\), let \(\Theta\) and \(\widetilde{\Theta}\) be the corresponding dual bases, and let \(A:V\to V\) be a linear map. Then

\[
[A^*]_{\widetilde{\Theta}}^{\Theta}=\left([A]_{\mathcal H}^{\widetilde{\mathcal H}}\right)^T,
\]

where \(A^*\) is the pullback by \(A\).

**Proof.** Let \(\mathcal H=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) and \(\widetilde{\mathcal H}=(\tilde h_1,\ldots,\tilde h_m)\), let \(\Theta=(\theta^1,\ldots,\theta^m)\) and \(\widetilde{\Theta}=(\tilde\theta^1,\ldots,\tilde\theta^m)\), and let \([A]_{\mathcal H}^{\widetilde{\mathcal H}}=[a^i_j]\). By (2.2.2) and (2.2.3), \(A(h_j)=\sum_i a^i_j \tilde h_i\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
A^*(\tilde\theta^j)
&=\sum_i A^*(\tilde\theta^j)(h_i)\,\theta^i\\
&=\sum_i \tilde\theta^j(A(h_i))\,\theta^i\\
&=\sum_i \tilde\theta^j\left(\sum_k a^k_i \tilde h_k\right)\theta^i
=\sum_{i,k} a^k_i \tilde\theta^j(\tilde h_k)\,\theta^i\\
&=\sum_i a^j_i\,\theta^i.
\end{aligned}
\]

Again by (2.2.2) and (2.2.3),

\[
[A^*]_{\widetilde{\Theta}}^{\Theta}=[a^j_i]=\left([a^i_j]\right)^T=\left([A]_{\mathcal H}^{\widetilde{\mathcal H}}\right)^T.
\]

## 2.3 Rank of Matrices

Consider the \(n\)-dimensional vector space \(\operatorname{Mat}_{1\times n}\) of row matrices and the \(m\)-dimensional vector space \(\operatorname{Mat}_{m\times 1}\) of column matrices. Let \(P\) be a matrix in \(\operatorname{Mat}_{m\times n}\). The row rank of \(P\) is defined to be the dimension of the subspace of \(\operatorname{Mat}_{1\times n}\) spanned by the rows of \(P\):

\[
\operatorname{rowrank}(P)=\dim(\operatorname{span}\{P^{(1)},\ldots,P^{(m)}\}).
\]

Similarly, the column rank of \(P\) is defined to be the dimension of the subspace of \(\operatorname{Mat}_{m\times 1}\) spanned by the columns of \(P\):

\[
\operatorname{colrank}(P)=\dim(\operatorname{span}\{P_{(1)},\ldots,P_{(n)}\}).
\]

To illustrate, for

\[
P=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{bmatrix},
\]

we have \(\operatorname{rowrank}(P)=\operatorname{colrank}(P)=2\). As shown below, it is not a coincidence that the row rank and column rank of \(P\) are equal.

### Theorem 2.3.1

Let \(V\) and \(W\) be vector spaces, let \(\mathcal H\) and \(\mathcal F\) be respective bases, and let \(A:V\to W\) be a linear map. Then

\[
\operatorname{rank}(A)=\operatorname{colrank}([A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F}).
\]

**Proof.** Let \(\mathcal H=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\). It follows from Theorem 2.2.1 and (2.2.3) that

\[
([A]_{\mathcal H}^{\mathcal F})_{(j)}=[A(h_j)]_{\mathcal F}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal F}(A(h_j)).
\]
