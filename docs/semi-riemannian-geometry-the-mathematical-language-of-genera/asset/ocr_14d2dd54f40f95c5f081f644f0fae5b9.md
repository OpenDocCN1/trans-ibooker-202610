Any 2-dimensional subspace of \(\mathbb{R}^3_1\) can be represented by a plane \(\Pi\) through the origin. This gives precisely three possibilities, as depicted in Figure 4.8.2, where dashed lines indicate intersections. \(\Pi_1\) does not intersect \(\Lambda_3\): it is spacelike and contains only spacelike vectors. \(\Pi_2\) intersects \(\Lambda_3\) in a pair of lines (minus the origin): it is timelike and contains spacelike, timelike, and lightlike vectors. \(\Pi_3\) intersects \(\Lambda_3\) in a single line (minus the origin): it is lightlike and contains spacelike and lightlike vectors, but no timelike vectors. We observe that according to this analysis, every 2-dimensional subspace of \(\mathbb{R}^3_1\) contains spacelike vectors.
