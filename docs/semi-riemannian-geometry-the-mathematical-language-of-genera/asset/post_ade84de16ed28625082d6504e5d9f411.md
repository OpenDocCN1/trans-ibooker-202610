
## Chapter 10

## Analysis in \(\mathbb{R}^m\)

### 10.1 Derivatives

In this section, we review some of the key results in the differential calculus of one or more real variables. For the most part, proofs are not provided.

Let \(U\) be an open set in \(\mathbb{R}^m\), let \(F: U \to \mathbb{R}^n\) be a map, and let \(p\) be a point in \(U\). We say that \(F\) is differentiable at \(p\) if there is a linear map \(L_p: \mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n\) such that

\[
\lim_{\lVert v\rVert\to 0}\frac{\lVert F(p+v)-F(p)-L_p(v)\rVert}{\lVert v\rVert}=0.
\]

It can be shown that if such a map exists, it is unique. We call this map the **differential of \(F\) at \(p\)** and henceforth denote it by

\[
d_p(F): \mathbb{R}^m \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^n.
\]

In the literature, the differential of \(F\) at \(p\) is also called the derivative of \(F\) at \(p\) or the total derivative of \(F\) at \(p\), and is denoted variously by \(dF_p\), \(DF(p)\), \(D_p(F)\), or \(F'(p)\). We have chosen to include parentheses in the notation \(d_p(F)\) to set the stage for viewing \(d_p\) as a type of map.

It is usual to characterize \(d_p(F)\) as being a “linear approximation” to \(F\) in the vicinity of \(p\). For a more geometric interpretation, let us define the **graph of \(F\)** by

\[
\operatorname{graph}(F)=\{(p,F(p))\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}:p\in U\}.
\]

We can think of \(d_p(F)(\mathbb{R}^m)\), which is a vector space, as being the “tangent space” to \(\operatorname{graph}(F)\) at \(F(p)\). This is a generalization of the tangent line and tangent plane familiar from the differential calculus of one or two real variables.

**Theorem 10.1.3.** Let \(U\) be an open set in \(\mathbb{R}^m\), let \(p\) be a point in \(U\), and let \(c\) be a real number. If the functions \(f,g:U\to\mathbb{R}\) are differentiable at \(p\), then:

- (a) \(d_p(cf+g)=c\,d_p(f)+d_p(g)\).
- (b) \(d_p(fg)=f(p)d_p(g)+g(p)d_p(f)\).

**Proof.** For a vector \(v=(a^1,\ldots,a^m)\) in \(\mathbb{R}^m\), we have from Theorem 10.1.2 and the properties of partial derivatives that

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_p(cf+g)(v)
&=\sum_i a^i\frac{\partial(cf+g)}{\partial x^i}(p)\\
&=\sum_i a^i\left[c\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^i}(p)+\frac{\partial g}{\partial x^i}(p)\right]\\
&=c\sum_i a^i\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^i}(p)+\sum_i a^i\frac{\partial g}{\partial x^i}(p)\\
&=c\,d_p(f)(v)+d_p(g)(v)\\
&=(c\,d_p(f)+d_p(g))(v).
\end{aligned}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_p(fg)(v)
&=\sum_i a^i\frac{\partial(fg)}{\partial x^i}(p)\\
&=\sum_i a^i\left[f(p)\frac{\partial g}{\partial x^i}(p)+g(p)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^i}(p)\right]\\
&=f(p)\sum_i a^i\frac{\partial g}{\partial x^i}(p)+g(p)\sum_i a^i\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^i}(p)\\
&=f(p)d_p(g)(v)+g(p)d_p(f)(v)\\
&=(f(p)d_p(g)+g(p)d_p(f))(v).
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(v\) was arbitrary, the result follows.

Let \(U\) be an open set in \(\mathbb{R}^m\), let \(f:U\to\mathbb{R}\) be a function, and suppose \((\partial f/\partial x^i)(p)\) exists for all \(p\) in \(U\) for \(i=1,\ldots,m\). The **partial derivative of \(f\) with respect to \(x^i\)** is the function

\[
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^i}:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
\]

defined by the assignment

\[
p\longmapsto\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^i}(p)
\]

for all \(p\) in \(U\). Now suppose \((\partial/\partial x^i)(\partial f/\partial x^j)(p)\) exists for all \(p\) in \(U\) for \(i,j=1,\ldots,m\). The **second order partial derivative of \(f\) with respect to \(x^i\) and \(x^j\)** is the function

\[
\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^i\partial x^j}:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
\]

defined by the assignment

\[
p\longmapsto\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^i\partial x^j}(p)
\]

for all \(p\) in \(U\) for \(i,j=1,\ldots,m\), where we denote

\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^j}\right)(p)
\quad\text{by}\quad
\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^i \partial x^j}(p).
\]

Iterating in an obvious way, higher-order partial derivatives of \(f\) are obtained. For \(k \ge 2\), a \(k\)th-order partial derivative of \(f\) is denoted by

\[
\frac{\partial^k f}{\partial x^{i_1} \partial x^{i_2} \cdots \partial x^{i_k}},
\]

where the integers \(1 \le i_1, i_2, \ldots, i_k \le m\) are not necessarily distinct.

Let us denote by \(C^0(U)\) the set of functions \(f:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\) that are continuous on \(U\). For \(k\ge1\), we define \(C^k(U)\) to be the set of functions \(f:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\) such that all partial derivatives of \(f\) of order \(\le k\) exist and are continuous on \(U\).

**Theorem 10.1.4 (Criterion for Differentiability of Functions).** If \(U\) is an open set in \(\mathbb{R}^m\) and \(f\) is a function in \(C^1(U)\), that is, if \(\partial f/\partial x^i\) exists and is continuous on \(U\) for \(i=1,\dots,m\), then \(f\) is differentiable on \(U\).

From Theorem 10.1.1, Theorem 10.1.2, and Theorem 10.1.4, we obtain the following result, which summarizes the relationship of continuity to differentiability for a function on an open set in \(\mathbb{R}^m\).

**Theorem 10.1.5.** Let \(U\) be an open set in \(\mathbb{R}^m\), and let \(f:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\) be a function. Then:

- (a) If \(f\) is differentiable on \(U\), then \(f\) is continuous on \(U\), and \(\partial f/\partial x^i\) exists on \(U\) for \(i=1,\dots,m\).
- (b) If \(\partial f/\partial x^i\) exists and is continuous on \(U\) for \(i=1,\dots,m\), then \(f\) is differentiable on \(U\).

**Theorem 10.1.6 (Equality of Mixed Partial Derivatives).** Let \(U\) be an open set in \(\mathbb{R}^m\), let \(f\) be a function in \(C^r(U)\), where \(r\ge1\), and let \(1\le k\le r\) be an integer. Then the mixed partial derivatives of \(f\) of order \(k\) are independent of the order of differentiation. That is, if \(1\le i_1,i_2,\ldots,i_k\le m\) are not necessarily distinct integers and \(j_1,j_2,\ldots,j_k\) are the same integers in some order, then

\[
\frac{\partial^k f}{\partial x^{i_1}\partial x^{i_2}\cdots\partial x^{i_k}}
=
\frac{\partial^k f}{\partial x^{j_1}\partial x^{j_2}\cdots\partial x^{j_k}}.
\]

Let \(U\) be an open set in \(\mathbb{R}^m\), and let \(f:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\) be a function. We say that \(f\) is (Euclidean) smooth (on \(U\)) if \(f\) is in \(C^k(U)\) for all \(k\ge0\). The set of smooth functions on \(U\) is denoted by \(C^\infty(U)\). In view of Theorem 10.1.5, \(C^\infty(U)\) is the set of functions \(f\) on \(U\) with partial derivatives of all orders on \(U\). It is sometimes said that smooth functions are “infinitely differentiable”.

When \(m=n\), the determinant \(\det(J_F(p))\) is called the **Jacobian determinant** of \(F\) at \(p\). Let \(1\le i_1<\cdots<i_s\le m\) and \(1\le j_1<\cdots<j_s\le n\) be integers, where \(1\le s\le\min(m,n)\). Using multi-index notation, the submatrix of \(J_F(p)\) consisting of the intersection of rows \(i_1,\ldots,i_s\) and columns \(j_1,\ldots,j_s\) in that order is

\[
J_F(p)^{(i_1,\ldots,i_s)}_{(j_1,\ldots,j_s)}=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{\partial F^{i_1}}{\partial x^{j_1}}(p) & \cdots & \frac{\partial F^{i_1}}{\partial x^{j_s}}(p)\\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
\frac{\partial F^{i_s}}{\partial x^{j_1}}(p) & \cdots & \frac{\partial F^{i_s}}{\partial x^{j_s}}(p)
\end{pmatrix}
\]

In the literature, the above matrix is commonly denoted by

\[
\frac{\partial(F^{i_1},\ldots,F^{i_s})}{\partial(x^{j_1},\ldots,x^{j_s})}(p).
\]

**Theorem 10.1.10 (Classical Chain Rule).** Let \(U\) and \(V\) be open sets in \(\mathbb{R}^m\) and \(\mathbb{R}^n\), respectively, let \(F:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^n\) and \(G:V\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^k\) be maps such that \(F(U)\subseteq V\), and let \(p\) be a point in \(U\). If \(F\) is differentiable at \(p\) and \(G\) is differentiable at \(F(p)\), then \(G\circ F\) is differentiable at \(p\) and

\[
J_{G\circ F}(p)=J_G(F(p))\,J_F(p).
\]

Equivalently, let \(F=(F^1,\ldots,F^n)\) and \(G=(G^1,\ldots,G^k)\), and let \((x^1,\ldots,x^m)\) and \((y^1,\ldots,y^n)\) be coordinates on \(\mathbb{R}^m\) and \(\mathbb{R}^n\), respectively. Then

\[
\frac{\partial(G^i\circ F)}{\partial x^j}(p)
=
\sum_{l=1}^n
\frac{\partial G^i}{\partial y^l}(F(p))\,
\frac{\partial F^l}{\partial x^j}(p)
\tag{10.1.4}
\]

for \(i=1,\dots,k\) and \(j=1,\dots,m\).

**Proof.** Let \(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}\), and \(\mathcal{G}\) be the standard bases for \(\mathbb{R}^m,\mathbb{R}^n\), and \(\mathbb{R}^k\), respectively. The Jacobian matrix of \(G\) at \(F(p)\) is the \(k\times n\) matrix

\[
J_G(F(p))=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{\partial G^1}{\partial y^1}(F(p)) & \cdots & \frac{\partial G^1}{\partial y^n}(F(p))\\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
\frac{\partial G^k}{\partial y^1}(F(p)) & \cdots & \frac{\partial G^k}{\partial y^n}(F(p))
\end{pmatrix}
\tag{10.1.5}
\]

We have

\[
\begin{aligned}
[J_{G\circ F}(p)]^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{E}}
&= [d_p(G\circ F)]^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{E}} && \text{(10.1.3)}\\
&= [d_{F(p)}(G)\circ d_p(F)]^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{E}} && \text{(Th 10.1.9)}\\
&= [d_{F(p)}(G)]^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{F}}\,[d_p(F)]^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{E}} && \text{(Th 2.2.2(c))}\\
&= J_G(F(p))\,J_F(p) && \text{(10.1.3)}\\
&= \left[\sum_l \frac{\partial G^i}{\partial y^l}(F(p))\,
\frac{\partial F^l}{\partial x^j}(p)\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
