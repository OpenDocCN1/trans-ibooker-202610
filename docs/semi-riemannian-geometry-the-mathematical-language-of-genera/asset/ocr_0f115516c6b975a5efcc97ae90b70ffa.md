# Semi-Riemannian Manifolds

Looking back at the material presented on smooth manifolds and smooth manifolds with a connection, we see that many of the concepts and results motivated by our study of regular surfaces and regular surfaces in $\mathbb{R}^3$ have been recovered—but not all. Missing from our recent efforts has been any discussion of “length” and “area”. To address this shortfall, it is necessary to endow smooth manifolds with additional structure, which entails a consideration of semi-Riemannian manifolds.

# 19.1  Semi-Riemannian Manifolds

The following material builds on portions of the discussion in Section 12.2. Let $M$ be a smooth $m$-manifold, and let $\mathbf{g}$ be a symmetric tensor field in $\mathcal{T}^0_2(M)$, so that $\mathbf{g}_p$ is a symmetric tensor in $\mathcal{T}^0_2(T_p(M))$ for all $p$ in $M$. We say that $\mathbf{g}$ is a metric on $M$ if:

+   [G1] $\mathbf{g}_p$ is nondegenerate on $T_p(M)$ for all $p$ in $M$.  
+   [G2] $\operatorname{ind}(\mathbf{g}_p)$ is independent of $p$ in $M$.

When [G1] is satisfied, $\mathbf{g}_p$ is a scalar product on $T_p(M)$ for all $p$ in $M$; or equivalently, $(T_p(M),\mathbf{g}_p)$ is a scalar product space for all $p$ in $M$.

Suppose $\mathbf{g}$ is in fact a metric. In that case, the pair $(M,\mathbf{g})$ is said to be a semi-Riemannian $m$-manifold. We ascribe to $\mathbf{g}$ those properties of $\mathbf{g}_p$ that are independent of $p$. Accordingly, $\mathbf{g}$ is said to be bilinear, symmetric, nondegenerate, and so on. The common value of the $\operatorname{ind}(\mathbf{g}_p)$ is denoted by $\nu$ and called the index of $\mathbf{g}$ or the index of $M$. The common signature of the $\mathbf{g}_p$ is denoted by $(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_m)$ and called the signature of $\mathbf{g}$ or the signature of $M$. For brevity, we usually denote

$$\mathbf{g}_p(\cdot,\cdot) \quad\text{by}\quad \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle.$$
