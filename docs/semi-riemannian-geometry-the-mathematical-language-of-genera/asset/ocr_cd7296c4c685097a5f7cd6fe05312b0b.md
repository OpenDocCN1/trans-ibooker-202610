Not surprisingly, interior multiplication behaves well with respect to basic algebraic structure, but its handling of wedge products is more complex.

**Theorem 7.4.1.** Let V be a vector space, let v, w be vectors in V, let η, ζ be multivectors in Λˢ(V), and let c be a real number. Then:

(a) \(i_{v+w}(η)=i_v(η)+i_w(η)\).

(b) \(i_{cv}(η)=c\,i_v(η)\).

(c) \(i_v(η+ζ)=i_v(η)+i_v(ζ)\).

(d) \(i_v(cη)=c\,i_v(η)\).

*Proof.* Straightforward.

**Theorem 7.4.2.** Let V be a vector space, let v be a vector in V, and let η¹, η², ⋯, ηˢ be covectors in V*. Then

\[
i_v(η^1 \wedge \cdots \wedge η^s)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}(-1)^{j-1}η^j(v)\,η^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{η^j}\wedge\cdots\wedge η^s.
\]

where ^ indicates that an expression is omitted.

*Proof.* For vectors w₁, ⋯, wₛ₋₁ in V, we have

\[
\begin{aligned}
i_v(η^1\wedge\cdots\wedge η^s)(w_1,\ldots,w_{s-1})
&=(η^1\wedge\cdots\wedge η^s)(v,w_1,\ldots,w_{s-1})\\
&=\det\begin{pmatrix}
η^1(v)&η^1(w_1)&\cdots&η^1(w_{s-1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
η^s(v)&η^s(w_1)&\cdots&η^s(w_{s-1})
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{s}(-1)^{j-1}η^j(v)\det\begin{pmatrix}
η^1(w_1)&\cdots&η^1(w_{s-1})\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
η^s(w_1)&\cdots&η^s(w_{s-1})
\end{pmatrix}^{(j)}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{s}(-1)^{j-1}η^j(v)η^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{η^j}\wedge\cdots\wedge η^s(w_1,\ldots,w_{s-1}),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the second equality follows from Theorem 7.2.8, and the third equality follows from expanding the determinant along the first column. Since w₁, ⋯, wₛ₋₁ were arbitrary, the result follows.
