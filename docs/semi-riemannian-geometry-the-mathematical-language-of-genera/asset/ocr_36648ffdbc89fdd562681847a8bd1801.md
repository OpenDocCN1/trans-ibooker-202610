Let $V$ be a vector space of dimension $m$, and let $A : V \longrightarrow V$ be a linear map. We say that a real number $\kappa$ is an eigenvalue of $\boldsymbol{A}$ if there is a nonzero vector $v$ in $V$ such that $A(v) = \kappa v$, or equivalently, $(\kappa \,\mathrm{id}_V - A)(v) = 0$. In that case, $v$ is said to be an eigenvector of $\boldsymbol{A}$ corresponding to $\kappa$. Since $A(c v) = c \kappa v$ for all real numbers $c$, if there is one eigenvector of $A$ corresponding to $\kappa$, then there are infinitely many.

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a basis for $V$. The characteristic polynomial of $\boldsymbol{A}$ is denoted by $\mathrm{char}_A(x)$ and defined to be the characteristic polynomial of $[A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{H}}$:

$$\mathrm{char}_A(x) = \det\left(xI_m - [A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{H}}\right).$$

We observe that

$$\mathrm{char}_A(0) = (-1)^m \det(A). \tag{4.7.1}$$

# Theorem 4.7.2. With the above setup, $\mathrm{char}_A(x)$ is independent of the choice of basis.

Proof. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be another basis for $V$. By Theorem 2.2.6,

$$xI_m - [A]_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}} = [x \,\mathrm{id}_V - A]_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}}$$

$$= \left([\mathrm{id}_V]_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{H}}\right)^{-1} [x \,\mathrm{id}_V - A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{H}} [\mathrm{id}_V]_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{H}}.$$

The result now follows from Theorem 4.7.1.
