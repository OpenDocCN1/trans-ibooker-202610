It can be shown that \(\int_M \omega\) does not depend on the choice of open cover or the choice of partition of unity.

Integration of differential forms has the properties desired of an integral.

# **Theorem 16.7.2.**

Let \((M,\mathcal D)\) be an oriented smooth \(m\)-manifold, let \(\omega,\xi\) be forms in \(\Lambda^m(M)\) that have compact support, and let \(c\) be a real number. Then:

+   (a)
+   
+   \[
+   \int_M(c\omega+\xi)=c\int_M\omega+\int_M\xi.
+   \]
+   
+   (b)
+   
+   \[
+   \int_{-M}\omega=-\int_M\omega,
+   \]
+   where \(-M\) denotes \((M,-\mathcal D)\).

# ## 16.8  Line Integrals

Due to its endpoints, a closed interval \([a,b]\) in \(\mathbb R\) is not a smooth 1-manifold. However, as discussed in Section 17.1, \([a,b]\) is what we will later refer to as a smooth 1-manifold with boundary. For present purposes, it is convenient to ignore the distinction and treat \([a,b]\) as a smooth 1-manifold.

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold, and let \(\lambda(t):[a,b]\longrightarrow M\) be a (not necessarily smooth) curve. We say that \(\lambda\) is piecewise smooth if there is a finite series of real numbers \(a=a_0<a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_k=b\) such that the restriction of \(\lambda\) to \([a_i,a_{i+1}]\) is a smooth curve for \(i=1,\ldots,k\). Suppose \(\lambda\) is in fact piecewise smooth, and let \(\omega\) be a covector field in \(\mathfrak X^*(M)\). Motivated by (16.7.3), the line integral of \(\omega\) over \(\lambda\) is denoted by \(\int_\lambda\omega\) and defined by

\[
\int_\lambda\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{[a_i,a_{i+1}]}\lambda^*(\omega), \tag{16.8.1}
\]

where the \(i\)th integrand on the right-hand side is interpreted as the restriction of \(\lambda^*(\omega)\) to \([a_i,a_{i+1}]\). When \(\lambda\) is smooth, (16.8.1) simplifies to

\[
\int_\lambda\omega=\int_{[a,b]}\lambda^*(\omega). \tag{16.8.2}
\]

Clearly, \([a_i,a_{i+1}]\) is a domain of integration in \(\mathbb R\), and by Theorem 9.4.2, it is compact for \(i=1,\ldots,k\). It follows from Theorem 15.11.8, (16.7.2), and (16.8.1) that

\[
\int_\lambda\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{a_i}^{a_{i+1}}\omega_{\lambda(t)}\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dt}(t)\right)dt. \tag{16.8.3}
\]

When \(\lambda\) is smooth, (16.8.3) simplifies to

\[
\int_\lambda\omega=\int_a^b\omega_{\lambda(t)}\left(\frac{d\lambda}{dt}(t)\right)dt. \tag{16.8.4}
\]
