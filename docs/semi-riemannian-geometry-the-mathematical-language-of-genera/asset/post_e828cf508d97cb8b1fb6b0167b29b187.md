
## 7.3 Pullback of Multicovectors

The pullback of covariant tensors was briefly considered in Section 5.2. The corresponding theory for multicovectors is far richer.

Before proceeding, we pause to consider multi-index notation, which was discussed in Section 2.1 in the context of matrices. Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \((h_1,\dots,h_m)\) be a basis for \(V\), and let \((\theta^1,\dots,\theta^m)\) be its dual basis. For an integer \(1 \le s \le m\), let \(I = (i_1,\dots,i_s)\) be a multi-index in \(\mathcal{I}_{s,m}\), and let us denote

\[
(h_{i_1},\dots,h_{i_s}) \quad\text{by}\quad h_I
\]

and

\[
\theta^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{i_s} \quad\text{by}\quad \theta^I.
\]

In this notation, the unordered basis for \(\Lambda^s(V)\) in Theorem 7.2.12(a) can be expressed concisely as \(\{\theta^I : I \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}\}\), and the identity in Theorem 7.2.13(a) becomes

\[
\eta = \sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \eta(h_I)\,\theta^I.
\]

If we order \(\mathcal{I}_{s,m}\) in some fashion, for example, lexicographically, then the basis \(\{\theta^I : I \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}\}\) of Theorem 7.2.12(a) can be similarly ordered, yielding an ordered basis for \(\Lambda^s(V)\), which we denote by

\[
(\theta^I : I \in \mathcal{I}_{s,m}).
\]

Kronecker’s delta can be generalized to the multi-index setting. Let \(J = (j_1,\dots,j_s)\) be another multi-index in \(\mathcal{I}_{s,m}\), and define

\[
\delta^J_I = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } I=J \\ 0 & \text{if } I\ne J. \end{cases}
\]

Then the conditional identity in Theorem 7.2.9 is simply \(\theta^J(h_I)=\delta^J_I\). As discussed in Appendix A, the complement of the multi-index \((i)\) in \(\mathcal{I}_{1,m}\) is

\[
(i)^c = (1,\dots,\hat{i},\dots,m) = (1,\dots,i-1,i+1,\dots,m)
\]

for \(i=1,\dots,m\). In this notation, the multicovectors comprising the basis in Theorem 7.2.12(e) can be expressed as \(\eta^i = (-1)^{i-1}\theta^{(i)^c}\). We will find multi-index notation of great utility in what follows.

Let \(V\) and \(W\) be vector spaces, and let \(A: V \longrightarrow W\) be a linear map. Pullback by \(A\) (for multicovectors) is the family of linear maps

\[
A^*: \Lambda^s(W) \longrightarrow \Lambda^s(V)
\]

defined for \(s\ge1\) by

\[
A^*(\eta)(v_1,\dots,v_s) = \eta(A(v_1),\dots,A(v_s)) \tag{7.3.1}
\]

for all multicovectors \(\eta\) in \(\Lambda^s(W)\) and all vectors \(v_1,\dots,v_s\) in \(V\). It follows from the multilinearity and alternating properties of \(\eta\) and the linearity of \(A\) that \(A^*(\eta)\) is in \(\Lambda^s(V)\), so the definition makes sense. We refer to \(A^*(\eta)\) as the pullback of \(\eta\) by \(A\).

### Theorem 7.3.1

Let \(V\) and \(W\) be vector spaces, let \(A: V \longrightarrow W\) be a linear map, and let \(\eta\), \(\zeta\) be multicovectors in \(\Lambda^s(W)\), and let \(\xi\) be a multicovector in \(\Lambda^{s'}(W)\). Then:

- (a) \(A^*(\eta+\zeta)=A^*(\eta)+A^*(\zeta)\).
- (b) \(A^*(\eta\wedge\xi)=A^*(\eta)\wedge A^*(\xi)\).

**Proof.** (a) Straightforward.

(b) We begin with a preliminary result. Let \(\zeta^1,\dots,\zeta^{s+s'}\) be (not necessarily distinct) covectors in \(W^*\). For vectors \(v_1,\dots,v_{s+s'}\) in \(V\), we have

\[
\begin{aligned}
A^*(\zeta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\zeta^{s+s'})(v_1,\dots,v_{s+s'})
&=\bigl(\zeta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\zeta^{s+s'}\bigr)(A(v_1),\dots,A(v_{s+s'}))\\
&=\det([\zeta^i(A(v_j))]) &&\text{[Th 7.2.8]}\\
&=\det([A^*(\zeta^i)(v_j)])\\
&=A^*(\zeta^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge A^*(\zeta^{s+s'})(v_1,\dots,v_{s+s'}). &&\text{[Th 7.2.8]}
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(v_1,\dots,v_{s+s'}\) were arbitrary,

\[
A^*(\zeta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\zeta^{s+s'}) = A^*(\zeta^1)\wedge\cdots\wedge A^*(\zeta^{s+s'}). \tag{7.3.2}
\]

Now for the main proof. Let \((\varphi^1,\dots,\varphi^n)\) be the dual basis corresponding to a basis for \(W\). By Theorem 7.2.12(a), \(\eta\) and \(\xi\) can be expressed as

\[
\eta = \sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,n}} a_I \varphi^I \qquad \text{and}\qquad \xi = \sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{s',n}} b_J \varphi^J.
\]

Then

\[
\eta\wedge\xi = \left(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,n}} a_I\varphi^I\right)\wedge \left(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{s',n}} b_J\varphi^J\right)
= \sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,n},\,J\in\mathcal{I}_{s',n}} a_I b_J\, \varphi^I\wedge\varphi^J,
\]

hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
A^*(\eta\wedge\xi)
&= \sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,n},\,J\in\mathcal{I}_{s',n}} a_I b_J\, A^*(\varphi^I\wedge\varphi^J)\\
&= \sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,n},\,J\in\mathcal{I}_{s',n}} a_I b_J\, A^*(\varphi^I)\wedge A^*(\varphi^J)\\
&= \left(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,n}} a_I A^*(\varphi^I)\right)\wedge \left(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{s',n}} b_J A^*(\varphi^J)\right)\\
&= A^*(\eta)\wedge A^*(\xi),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the second equality follows from (7.3.2).

### Theorem 7.3.2 (Pullback of a Multicovector)

Let \(V\) and \(W\) be vector spaces of dimensions \(m\) and \(n\), respectively, let \(\mathcal{H}\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) be respective bases, and let \((\theta^1,\dots,\theta^m)\) and \((\varphi^1,\dots,\varphi^n)\) be the corresponding dual bases. Let \(A:V\longrightarrow W\) be a linear map, and let \(I\) be a multi-index in \(\mathcal{I}_{s,n}\), where \(s\le \min(m,n)\). Then

\[
A^*(\varphi^I) = \sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \det\!\left(\left([A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)^I_J\right)\theta^J.
\]

**Proof.** Let \(\mathcal{H}=(h_1,\dots,h_m)\). By Theorem 7.2.13(a),

\[
A^*(\varphi^I) = \sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} A^*(\varphi^I)(h_J)\,\theta^J. \tag{7.3.3}
\]

Let \([A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}=[a^i_j]\). Then (2.2.2) and (2.2.3) give

\[
\varphi^i(A(h_j)) = \varphi^i\!\left(\sum_k a^k_j f_k\right)=\sum_k a^k_j\,\varphi^i(f_k)=a^i_j,
\]

hence

\[
[A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}} = [\varphi^i(A(h_j))]. \tag{7.3.4}
\]

Let \(I=(i_1,\dots,i_s)\) and \(J=(j_1,\dots,j_s)\). We have

\[
\begin{aligned}
A^*(\varphi^I)(h_J)
&= A^*(\varphi^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\varphi^{i_s})(h_{j_1},\dots,h_{j_s})\\
&= \bigl(A^*(\varphi^{i_1})\wedge\cdots\wedge A^*(\varphi^{i_s})\bigr)(h_{j_1},\dots,h_{j_s}) &&\text{[Th 7.3.1(b)]}\\
&= \det\!\left(\left[A^*(\varphi^i)(h_j)\right]_{(i_1,\dots,i_s)}^{(j_1,\dots,j_s)}\right) &&\text{[Th 7.2.8]} \tag{7.3.5}\\
&= \det\!\left(\left[\varphi^i(A(h_j))\right]_{(i_1,\dots,i_s)}^{(j_1,\dots,j_s)}\right)\\
&= \det\!\left(\left([A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)^I_J\right). &&\text{[(7.3.4)]}
\end{aligned}
\]

Substituting (7.3.5) into (7.3.3) gives the result.

Special cases of Theorem 7.3.2 provide a number of useful identities.

### Theorem 7.3.3

Let \(V\) and \(W\) be vector spaces of dimension \(m\), let \(\mathcal{H}=(h_1,\dots,h_m)\) and \(\mathcal{F}=(f_1,\dots,f_m)\) be respective bases, and let \((\theta^1,\dots,\theta^m)\) and \((\varphi^1,\dots,\varphi^m)\) be the corresponding dual bases. Let \(A:V\longrightarrow W\) be a linear map, and let \(\eta\) be a multicovector in \(\Lambda^m(W)\). Then:

- (a) \(A^*(\varphi^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\varphi^m) = \det\!\left([A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)\,\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m\).
- (b) \(A^*(\eta)(h_1,\dots,h_m) = \det\!\left([A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)\,\eta(f_1,\dots,f_m)\).

**Proof.** (a) Since \(\mathcal{I}_{m,m}=\{(1,\dots,m)\}\), this follows from Theorem 7.3.2.

(b) It follows from Theorem 7.2.13(b) that

\[
\eta = \eta(f_1,\dots,f_m)\,\varphi^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\varphi^m,
\]

and then from part (a) that

\[
\begin{aligned}
A^*(\eta)
&= \eta(f_1,\dots,f_m)\,A^*(\varphi^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\varphi^m)\\
&= \eta(f_1,\dots,f_m)\det\!\left([A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m. \tag{7.3.6}
\end{aligned}
\]

Applying both sides of the preceding identity to \((h_1,\dots,h_m)\) and using Theorem 7.2.9 gives the result.

### Theorem 7.3.4

Let \(V\) be a vector space of dimension \(m\), let \(A:V\to V\) be a linear map, let \(\eta\) be a multicovector in \(\Lambda^m(V)\), and let \(v_1,\dots,v_m\) be vectors in \(V\). Then:

- (a) \(A^*(\eta)=\det(A)\,\eta\).
- (b) \(\eta(A(v_1),\dots,A(v_m))=\det(A)\,\eta(v_1,\dots,v_m)\).

**Proof.** (a) Take \(W=V\) and \(\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}\) in Theorem 7.3.3. Then, using (7.3.6),

\[
A^*(\eta)=\det\left([A]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{H}}\right)\eta(h_1,\dots,h_m)\,\theta^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^m
=\det(A)\,\eta,
\]

where the last equality follows from Theorem 7.2.13(b).

(b) By part (a),

\[
\eta(A(v_1),\dots,A(v_m)) = A^*(\eta)(v_1,\dots,v_m) = \det(A)\,\eta(v_1,\dots,v_m).
\]

The next result is reminiscent of Theorem 2.4.9.

### Theorem 7.3.5

Let \(V\) be a vector space of dimension \(m\), let \(\eta\) be a nonzero multicovector in \(\Lambda^m(V)\), and let \(v_1,\dots,v_m\) be vectors in \(V\). Then \(v_1,\dots,v_m\) are linearly independent if and only if \(\eta(v_1,\dots,v_m)\ne 0\).

**Proof.** Let \(\mathcal{H}=(h_1,\dots,h_m)\) be a basis for \(V\), and let \((\theta^1,\dots,\theta^m)\) be its dual basis. Let \(\mathcal{V}=(v_1,\dots,v_m)\), and let \(A:V\to V\) be the linear map defined by \(A(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{V}\). Since \(\eta\) is nonzero, we have from Theorem 7.2.13(b) that

\[
\eta(h_1,\dots,h_m)\ne 0, \tag{7.3.7}
\]

and from Theorem 7.3.4(b) that

\[
\eta(v_1,\dots,v_m) = \det(A)\,\eta(h_1,\dots,h_m). \tag{7.3.8}
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_1,\dots,v_m \text{ are linearly independent}
&\Longleftrightarrow \mathcal{V} \text{ is a basis for } V\\
&\Longleftrightarrow A \text{ is a linear isomorphism} \quad [\mathrm{Th\ 1.1.13(a)}]\\
&\Longleftrightarrow \det(A)\ne 0 \quad [\mathrm{Th\ 2.5.3}]\\
&\Longleftrightarrow \eta(v_1,\dots,v_m)\ne 0. \quad [(7.3.7), (7.3.8)]
\end{aligned}
\]

We note in passing that \((\Longleftarrow)\) follows from Theorem 7.1.2.

### Theorem 7.3.6 (Change of Basis)

Let \(V\) be a vector space, let \(\mathcal{H}\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) be bases for \(V\), let \((\theta^1,\dots,\theta^m)\) and \((\varphi^1,\dots,\varphi^m)\) be the corresponding dual bases, and let \((\theta^I:I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m})\) and \((\varphi^J:J\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m})\) be the corresponding bases for \(\Lambda^s(V)\). Let \(\eta\) be a multicovector in \(\Lambda^s(V)\), with

\[
\eta=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} a_I\theta^I \qquad\text{and}\qquad \eta=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} b_J\varphi^J.
\]

Then

\[
a_I = \sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} b_J \det\!\left(\left([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)^J_I\right).
\]

**Proof.** Setting \(W=V\) and \(A=\operatorname{id}_V\) in Theorem 7.3.2 yields

\[
\varphi^J = \sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \det\!\left(\left([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)^J_I\right)\theta^I,
\]

hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} a_I\theta^I
&=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} b_J\varphi^J\\
&=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} b_J\left[\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} \det\!\left(\left([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)^J_I\right)\theta^I\right]\\
&=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\left[\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}} b_J \det\!\left(\left([\operatorname{id}_V]_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{F}}\right)^J_I\right)\right]\theta^I,
\end{aligned}
\]

from which the result follows.

## 7.4 Interior Multiplication

Let \(V\) be a vector space of dimension \(m\), and let \(v\) be a vector in \(V\). **Interior multiplication by \(v\)** is the family of linear maps

\[
i_v:\Lambda^s(V)\longrightarrow\Lambda^{s-1}(V)
\]

defined for \(s\ge2\) by

\[
i_v(\eta)(w_1,\dots,w_{s-1}) = \eta(v,w_1,\dots,w_{s-1})
\]

for all multicovectors \(\eta\) in \(\Lambda^s(V)\) and all vectors \(w_1,\dots,w_{s-1}\) in \(V\). Since any \(m+1\) vectors in \(V\) are linearly dependent, it follows from Theorem 7.3.5 that \(i_v\) is the zero map when \(s>m\). Recalling from (7.1.2) and (7.1.3) that \(\Lambda^1(V)=V^*\) and \(\Lambda^0(V)=\mathbb R\), we extend the preceding definition to \(s=1\) as follows:

\[
i_v:\Lambda^1(V)\longrightarrow\Lambda^0(V)
\]

is given by

\[
i_v(\eta)=\eta(v).
\]
