Proof. Let $(V,\mathfrak{g})$ be a scalar product space of dimension $m \ge 1$. By Theorem 4.2.1, $V$ contains a unit vector. Applying Theorem 4.2.2 inductively, we construct an orthonormal $m$-tuple of vectors in $V$. It follows from Theorem 4.2.3 that this is an orthonormal basis for $V$.

It is convenient to restrict attention to orthonormal bases satisfying a certain condition.

# **Throughout, all orthonormal bases $(e_1,\dots,e_m)$ are assumed ordered such that any +1s among $\langle e_1,e_1\rangle,\dots,\langle e_m,e_m\rangle$ precede any $-1$s.**

To illustrate, let $\mathcal{E} = (e_1,\dots,e_{m-2},e_{m-1},e_m)$ be the standard basis for $\mathbb{R}_\nu^m$, and let $\mathcal{F} = (e_1,\dots,e_{m-2},e_m,e_{m-1})$. Then $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ are both orthonormal bases for $\mathbb{R}_\nu^m$. Since $\langle e_1,e_1\rangle = \cdots = \langle e_{m-1}\rangle = 1$ and $\langle e_m,e_m\rangle = -1$, we see that $\mathcal{E}$ satisfies the preceding convention but $\mathcal{F}$ does not.

Let $(V,\mathfrak{g})$ be a scalar product space, and let $V = V^+ \oplus V^-$ be a direct sum of the type given by Theorem 4.1.6(a). The index of $\mathfrak{g}$, also called the index of $V$, is defined by

$$\operatorname{ind}(\mathfrak{g}) = \dim(V^-).$$

We often denote

$$\operatorname{ind}(\mathfrak{g})\quad\text{by}\quad\nu.$$

According to Theorem 4.1.6(b), $\operatorname{ind}(\mathfrak{g})$ is independent of the choice of direct sum. Taking $\mathbb{R}_\nu^m = (\mathbb{R}^m,\mathfrak{s})$ as an example, we see from (4.1.2) that $\operatorname{ind}(\mathfrak{s}) = \nu$.

Let $\mathcal{E} = (e_1,\dots,e_m)$ be an orthonormal basis for $V$. The signature of $\mathfrak{g}$, also called the signature of $V$, is defined to be the $m$-tuple

$$\bigl(\langle e_1,e_1\rangle,\dots,\langle e_m,e_m\rangle\bigr).$$

In light of the above convention on orthonormal bases, the signature of $\mathfrak{g}$ has +1s in the first $m-\nu$ positions and $-1$s in the remaining $\nu$ positions. Let us denote

$$\langle e_i,e_i\rangle\quad\text{by}\quad\varepsilon_i$$

for $i = 1,\dots,m$. Then

$$\langle e_i,e_j\rangle = \varepsilon_i\delta_{ij}\tag{4.2.1}$$

for $i,j = 1,\dots,m$, where $\delta_{ij}$ is Kronecker's delta. In this notation, the signature of $\mathfrak{g}$ is

$$(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_m).$$

We note that

$$\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}} = \operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_m),\tag{4.2.2}$$

hence

$$\det(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}}) = \varepsilon_1\cdots\varepsilon_m = (-1)^\nu.\tag{4.2.3}$$
