# Theorem 8.2.3. Let V be a vector space, let ϖ be an orientation multicovector on V, and let 𝔇 be the set of bases (h₁, …, hₘ) for V such that ϖ(h₁, …, hₘ) > 0. Then 𝔇 is an orientation of V, called the orientation induced by ϖ.

Proof. Let ℋ = (h₁, …, hₘ) and ℱ = (f₁, …, fₘ) be bases for V, with ϖ(h₁, …, hₘ) > 0. Then

+   ℱ is in 𝔇
+   ⇔ ϖ(f₁, …, fₘ) > 0
+   ⇔ ϖ(h₁, …, hₘ) and ϖ(f₁, …, fₘ) have the same sign
+   ⇔ ℋ and ℱ are consistent,

where the last equivalence follows from Theorem 8.2.2. Thus, 𝔇 is the set of bases for V that are consistent with ℋ; that is, 𝔇 = [ℋ].

# Example 8.2.4 (ℝᵐ). Let ℰ = (e₁, …, eₘ) be the standard basis for ℝᵐ, let (ξ¹, …, ξᵐ) be its dual basis, and let Ω = ξ¹ ∧ ⋯ ∧ ξᵐ. It follows from Theorem 7.2.9 that Ω(e₁, …, eₘ)=1, so Ω is an orientation multicovector on ℝᵐ, and ℰ is in the orientation of ℝᵐ induced by Ω. Thus, Ω induces the standard orientation of ℝᵐ.

Let V be a vector space, and let ϖ and ϑ be orientation multicovectors on V. By Theorem 7.2.12(d) and Theorem 8.2.1, ϖ spans Λᵐ(M) (as does ϑ). It follows that ϖ = cϑ for some nonzero real number c. We say that ϖ and ϑ are consistent, and write ϖ ∼ ϑ, if c > 0. It is easily shown that ∼ is an equivalence relation on the set of orientation multicovectors on V, and that there are precisely two equivalence classes. The equivalence class containing ϖ is denoted by [ϖ]. By definition, [ϖ] comprises all multicovectors on V that are consistent with ϖ. Evidently, ϖ and −ϖ are not consistent. Thus, for any orientation multicovector ϖ on V, the equivalence classes of orientation multicovectors are [ϖ] and [−ϖ].

Let ℋ be a basis for V. It is clear that orientation multicovectors in the same equivalence class induce the same orientation, and that orientation multicovectors in different equivalence classes induce opposite orientations. We therefore have a bijective map

ι : {[ϖ], [−ϖ]} ⟶ {[ℋ], [−ℋ]}

defined by assigning [ϖ] and [−ϖ] to the orientations induced by ϖ and −ϖ, respectively. This shows that we are free to specify orientations using either (equivalence classes of) bases for V or (equivalence classes of) orientation multicovectors on V. For purposes of computation, the latter approach is generally more convenient.
