## 12.6 Lie Bracket in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\)

Let \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) be a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\). **Lie bracket** is the map

\[
[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\mathfrak{X}(M)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
\]

defined by

\[
[X,Y]=\nabla_X(Y)-\nabla_Y(X) \tag{12.6.1}
\]

for all vector fields \(X,Y\) in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\).

### Theorem 12.6.1

Let \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) be a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), let \((U,\varphi)\) be a chart on \(M\), let \(X,Y\) be vector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), and, in local coordinates, let

\[
X\circ\varphi=\sum_i\alpha^i H_i \quad\text{and}\quad Y\circ\varphi=\sum_j\beta^j H_j.
\]

Then

\[
[X,Y]\circ\varphi=\sum_j\left\{\sum_i\left(\alpha^i\frac{\partial\beta^j}{\partial r^i}-\beta^i\frac{\partial\alpha^j}{\partial r^i}\right)\right\}H_j
\]

\[
=\sum_j\left(\sum_i(\alpha^i\beta^j_{;i}-\beta^i\alpha^j_{;i})\right)H_j.
\]

**Proof.** By Theorem 12.4.2(c),

\[
\nabla_X(Y)\circ\varphi=\sum_j\left(\sum_i\alpha^i\beta^j_{;i}\right)H_j
\]

and

\[
\nabla_Y(X)\circ\varphi=\sum_j\left(\sum_i\beta^i\alpha^j_{;i}\right)H_j,
\]

hence

\[
[X,Y]\circ\varphi=\nabla_X(Y)\circ\varphi-\nabla_Y(X)\circ\varphi
=\sum_j\left(\sum_i(\alpha^i\beta^j_{;i}-\beta^i\alpha^j_{;i})\right)H_j.
\]

We have from (12.3.9) that

\[
\sum_i(\alpha^i\beta^j_{;i}-\beta^i\alpha^j_{;i})
=
\sum_i\left\{\alpha^i\left(\frac{\partial\beta^j}{\partial r^i}+\sum_k\beta^k\Gamma^j_{ik}\right)
-\beta^i\left(\frac{\partial\alpha^j}{\partial r^i}+\sum_k\alpha^k\Gamma^j_{ik}\right)\right\}
\]

\[
=
\sum_i\left(\alpha^i\frac{\partial\beta^j}{\partial r^i}-\beta^i\frac{\partial\alpha^j}{\partial r^i}\right)
+\sum_{ik}\alpha^i\beta^k\Gamma^j_{ik}
-\sum_{ik}\alpha^k\beta^i\Gamma^j_{ik}
\]

\[
=
\sum_i\left(\alpha^i\frac{\partial\beta^j}{\partial r^i}-\beta^i\frac{\partial\alpha^j}{\partial r^i}\right),
\]

where the last equality relies on Theorem 12.3.3(d). The result follows.

The next result shows that the Lie bracket on a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), formulated above in terms of the covariant derivative, can also be expressed in terms of the induced Euclidean derivative.

### Theorem 12.6.2

If \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) is a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\) and \(X,Y\) are vector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), then

\[
\nabla_X(Y)-\nabla_Y(X)=D_X(Y)-D_Y(X).
\]

**Proof.** We have from Theorem 12.3.5(c) that

\[
D_X(Y)\circ\varphi=\sum_j\left(\sum_i\alpha^i\beta^j_{;i}\right)H_j
+\left(\sum_{ij}\alpha^i\beta^j\vartheta_{ij}\right)G
\]

and

\[
D_Y(X)\circ\varphi=\sum_j\left(\sum_i\beta^i\alpha^j_{;i}\right)H_j
+\left(\sum_{ij}\beta^i\alpha^j\vartheta_{ij}\right)G.
\]

Then Theorem 12.3.3(d) gives

\[
(D_X(Y)-D_Y(X))\circ\varphi
=
\sum_j\left(\sum_i\alpha^i\beta^j_{;i}-\beta^i\alpha^j_{;i}\right)H_j.
\]

The result now follows from Theorem 12.6.1.

Here is a counterpart of Theorem 10.4.2.

### Theorem 12.6.3

Let \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) be a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), let \(X,Y,Z\) be vector fields in \(\mathfrak{X}(M)\), and let \(f,g\) be functions in \(C^\infty(M)\). Let \((U,\varphi)\) be a chart on \(M\) and let \((H_1,H_2)\) be the corresponding coordinate frame. Then:

- (a) \([Y,X]=-[X,Y]\).
- (b) \([X+Y,Z]=[X,Z]+[Y,Z]\).
- (c) \([X,Y+Z]=[X,Y]+[X,Z]\).
- (d) \([fX,gY]=fg[X,Y]+f\nabla_X(g)Y-g\nabla_Y(f)X\).
- (e) \([X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]=(0,0,0)\). (Jacobi's identity)
- (f) \([H_i\circ\varphi^{-1},H_j\circ\varphi^{-1}]=(0,0,0)\) for \(i,j=1,2\).

**Proof.** (a)–(d): Parts (a)–(d) of Theorem 12.4.1 and (12.6.1) give the result.

(e): We have from parts (a) and (c) of Theorem 12.4.1 and (12.6.1) that

\[
[X,[Y,Z]]
=\nabla_X([Y,Z])-\nabla_{[Y,Z]}(X)
=\nabla_X(\nabla_Y(Z)-\nabla_Z(Y))-\nabla_{\nabla_Y(Z)-\nabla_Z(Y)}(X)
\]
\[
=\nabla_X(\nabla_Y(Z))-\nabla_X(\nabla_Z(Y))-\nabla_{\nabla_Y(Z)}(X)+\nabla_{\nabla_Z(Y)}(X).
\]

Likewise,

\[
[Y,[Z,X]]
=\nabla_Y(\nabla_Z(X))-\nabla_Y(\nabla_X(Z))-\nabla_{\nabla_Z(X)}(Y)+\nabla_{\nabla_X(Z)}(Y)
\]

\[
[Z,[X,Y]]
=\nabla_Z(\nabla_X(Y))-\nabla_Z(\nabla_Y(X))-\nabla_{\nabla_X(Y)}(Z)+\nabla_{\nabla_Y(X)}(Z).
\]

Summing the preceding identities and using (12.4.4) yields

\[
[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]
\]
\[
=[\nabla^2_{X,Y}(Z)-\nabla^2_{Y,X}(Z)]
+[\nabla^2_{Y,Z}(X)-\nabla^2_{Z,Y}(X)]
+[\nabla^2_{Z,X}(Y)-\nabla^2_{X,Z}(Y)]
\]
\[
=R(X,Y)Z+R(Y,Z)X+R(Z,X)Y=(0,0,0),
\]

where the second identity follows from Theorem 12.9.1, and the last identity from Theorem 12.9.13.

(f): This follows from Theorem 12.6.1.

## 12.7 Orientation in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\)

In Section 12.2, we defined a regular surface to be a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\) provided its first fundamental form satisfies certain properties. We then proceeded to demonstrate an equivalent formulation based on the existence of a particular type of unit normal vector field. Aside from an increase in geometric intuition, the latter approach offers computational advantages. For example, as remarked in connection with (12.2.4), it is usually more convenient to compute the index of a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\) indirectly using its sign. In this section, we explore orientation in the context of regular surfaces in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\). The basic definition is given in terms of atlases, but once again unit normal vector fields play a prominent role. In what follows, we rely heavily on the discussion of orientation of vector spaces given in Section 8.2.

### Theorem 12.7.1

Let \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) be a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), let \((U,\varphi)\) be a chart on \(M\), let \(\mathcal{H}=(H_1,H_2)\) and \(G\) be the corresponding coordinate frame and coordinate unit normal vector field, and let \(q\) be a point in \(U\). Then \((G_q,H_1|_q,H_2|_q)\) is a basis for \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\) that has the standard orientation.

**Proof.** This follows from Theorem 8.4.10(b) and Theorem 12.2.11.

Let \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) be a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\), and let \((U,\varphi)\) and \((\tilde{U},\tilde{\varphi})\) be overlapping charts on \(M\). Let \(\mathcal{H}\) and \(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}\) be the corresponding coordinate frames, and let \(G\) and \(\tilde{G}\) be the corresponding coordinate unit normal vector fields. Let \(W=\varphi(U)\cap\tilde{\varphi}(\tilde{U})\), and let \(p\) be a point in \(W\). Recall from Section 8.2 that the coordinate bases \(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi^{-1}(p)}\) and \(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(p)}\) are said to be consistent if

\[
\det\left([\mathrm{id}_{T_p(M)}]_{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(p)}}^{\mathcal{H}_{\varphi^{-1}(p)}}\right)>0.
\]

We say that \((U,\varphi)\) and \((\tilde{U},\tilde{\varphi})\) are consistent if \(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi^{-1}(p)}\) and \(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(p)}\) are consistent for all \(p\) in \(W\).

### Theorem 12.7.2

With the above setup, \((U,\varphi)\) and \((\tilde{U},\tilde{\varphi})\) are consistent if and only if \(G_{\varphi^{-1}(p)}=\tilde{G}_{\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(p)}\) for all \(p\) in \(W\).

**Proof.** This follows from Theorem 8.4.11(b).

Let \((M,\mathfrak{g})\) be a regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\). An atlas for \(M\) is said to be consistent if every pair of overlapping charts in the atlas is consistent. We say that \(M\) is orientable if it has a consistent atlas. Suppose \(M\) is in fact orientable, and let \(\mathfrak{A}\) be a consistent atlas for \(M\). The triple \((M,\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{A})\) is called an oriented regular surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3_\nu\). Let \(p\) be a point in \(M\), let \((U,\varphi)\) be a chart in \(\mathfrak{A}\) at \(p\), and let \(\mathcal{H}\) be the corresponding coordinate frame. Let

\[
\mathfrak{O}(p)=[\mathcal{H}_{\varphi^{-1}(p)}],
\]

where we recall from Section 8.2 that \([\mathcal{H}_{\varphi^{-1}(p)}]\) is the equivalence class of all bases for \(T_p(M)\) (not just coordinate bases) that are consistent with \(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi^{-1}(p)}\).
