
and

C_n^k ∘ F_n^{k+1}(𝒜)^{i_1...î_k...i_{r-1}}_{j_1...ĵ_n...j_{s+1}} = Σ_p F_n^{k+1}(𝒜)^{i_1...i_{k-1} p i_{k+1}...i_{r-1}}_{j_1...j_n-1 p j_{n+1}...j_{s+1}} [Th 5.4.5] = Σ_p ( Σ_q 𝔤_{pq} 𝒜^{i_1...i_{k-1} p q i_{k+1}...i_{r-1}}_{j_1...j_n...j_{s+1}} ) [(6.2.3)] = Σ_{pq} 𝔤_{pq} 𝒜^{i_1...i_{k-1} p q i_{k+1}...i_{r-1}}_{j_1...j_n...j_{s+1}},

which gives the first equality. Relabeling indices (i_{k+1},...,i_{r-1}) as (i_k,...,i_{r-2}), and (j_{n+1},...,j_{s+1}) as (j_m,...,j_s), gives the second equality.

We observe that p and q in (6.2.1) are in the k th and l th contravariant positions of 𝒜, respectively, and that p and q in (6.2.2) are in the k th and (k+1)th contravariant positions of 𝒜, respectively. We also note that the right-hand side of (6.2.1) is independent of n. This makes sense because F_n^l lowers the l th contravariant index to the n th covariant position, and then C_n^k contracts over the k th contravariant index and the n th covariant index. In a manner of speaking, the n th covariant position is simply a temporary location for the lowered l th contravariant index to reside prior to its being contracted with the k th contravariant index.

## Theorem 6.2.6. Let (V,𝔤) be a scalar product space, let 𝒜 be a tensor in 𝒯_s^r(V), and let 1 ≤ k ≤ r be an integer. Then

F_1^k(𝒜) = C_1^k(𝔤 ⊗ 𝒜).

Proof. All the components to follow are computed with respect to a basis ℋ for V. By Theorem 6.2.4, the tensor F_1^k(𝒜) in 𝒯_{s+1}^{r-1}(V) has the components

F_1^k(𝒜)^{i_1...i_{r-1}}_{j_1...j_{s+1}} = Σ_p 𝔤_{j_1 p} 𝒜^{i_1...i_{k-1} p i_k...i_{r-1}}_{j_2...j_{s+1}}. (6.2.4)

It follows from Theorem 5.1.5 that the tensor 𝔤 ⊗ 𝒜 in 𝒯_{s+2}^r(V) has the components

(𝔤 ⊗ 𝒜)^{i_1...i_r}_{j_1...j_{s+2}} = 𝔤_{j_1 j_2} 𝒜^{i_1...i_r}_{j_3...j_{s+1}},

and then from Theorem 5.4.5 that the tensor C_1^k(𝔤 ⊗ 𝒜) in 𝒯_{s+1}^{r-1}(V) has the components

C_1^k(𝔤 ⊗ 𝒜)^{i_1...î_k...i_r}_{j_2...j_{s+2}} = Σ_p 𝔤_{p j_2} 𝒜^{i_1...i_{k-1} p i_{k+1}...i_r}_{j_3...j_{s+2}}.

Relabeling (i_{k+1},...,i_r) as (i_k,...,i_{r-1}), and (j_2,...,j_{s+2}) as (j_1,...,j_{s+1}), gives

C_1^k(𝔤 ⊗ 𝒜)^{i_1...i_{r-1}}_{j_1...j_{s+1}} = Σ_p 𝔤_{p j_1} 𝒜^{i_1...i_{k-1} p i_k...i_{r-1}}_{j_2...j_{s+1}}. (6.2.5)

The result now follows from (6.2.4) and (6.2.5).

## 6.3 Sharp Maps

Let $(V,\mathfrak{g})$ be a scalar product space, let $\mathcal{H} = (h_1,\dots,h_m)$ be a basis for $V$, and let $(\theta^1,\dots,\theta^m)$ be its dual basis. Recall from (5.1.1) that $\mathcal{T}_0^1(V)=V$ and $\mathcal{T}_1^0(V)=V^*$. The sharp map in Section 3.3 can therefore be expressed as

S : $\mathcal{T}_1^0(V) \longrightarrow \mathcal{T}_0^1(V)$.

More generally, let $1 \le k \le r+1$ and $1 \le l \le s$ be integers (so that $r \ge 0$ and $s \ge 1$). The $(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l})$-sharp map is denoted by

S$_l^k : \mathcal{T}_s^r(V) \longrightarrow \mathcal{T}_{s-1}^{r+1}(V)$

and defined for all tensors $\mathcal{A}$ in $\mathcal{T}_s^r(V)$, all covectors $\eta^1,\dots,\eta^{r+1}$ in $V^*$, and all vectors $v_1,\dots,v_{s-1}$ in $V$ as follows, where $\widehat{\phantom{x}}$ indicates that an expression is omitted:

[S1] For $s = 1$ (so that $l = 1$):

S$_1^k(\mathcal{A})(\eta^1,\dots,\eta^{r+1}) = \mathcal{A}(\eta^1,\dots,\widehat{\eta^k},\dots,\eta^{r+1},\eta^{k\sharp})$

[S2] For $s \ge 2$ and $1 \le l \le s-1$:

S$_l^k(\mathcal{A})(\eta^1,\dots,\eta^{r+1},v_1,\dots v_{s-1}) = \mathcal{A}(\eta^1,\dots,\widehat{\eta^k},\dots,\eta^{r+1},v_1,\dots,v_{l-1},\eta^{k\sharp},v_l,\dots,v_{s-1})$

[S3] For $s \ge 2$ and $l = s$:

S$_s^k(\mathcal{A})(\eta^1,\dots,\eta^{r+1},v_1,\dots,v_{s-1}) = \mathcal{A}(\eta^1,\dots,\widehat{\eta^k},\dots,\eta^{r+1},v_1,\dots,v_{s-1},\eta^{k\sharp})$

The computational procedure is most transparent for [S2]: extract the covector in the $k$th contravariant position, sharp it, and insert the result in the $l$th covariant position.

## Example 6.3.1. To illustrate [S1], let $r = 0$ (so that $k = 1$) and $s = 1$ (so that $l = 1$), and let $\zeta$ be a covector in $\mathcal{T}_1^0(V)=V^*$. Then the tensor S$_1^1(\zeta)$ in $\mathcal{T}_0^1(V)=V$ is given by

S$_1^1(\zeta)(\eta) = \zeta(\eta^{\sharp})$

for all covectors $\eta$ in $V^*$. We have from (1.2.3) and (4.5.2) that

$\zeta(\eta^{\sharp}) = \langle \zeta,\eta \rangle^* = \langle \eta,\zeta \rangle^* = \eta(\zeta^{\sharp}) = \zeta^{\sharp}(\eta) = S(\zeta)(\eta)$

hence S$_1^1(\zeta)(\eta) = S(\zeta)(\eta)$. Since $\eta$ and $\zeta$ were arbitrary,

S$_1^1 = S.$

To illustrate [S2], let $r = 0$ (so that $k = 1$) and $s = 2$ (so that $l = 1$), and let $\mathcal{A}$ be a tensor in $\mathcal{T}_2^0(V)$. Then the tensor S$_1^1(\mathcal{A})$ in $\mathcal{T}_1^1(V)$ is given by

S$_1^1(\mathcal{A})(\eta, v) = \mathcal{A}(\eta^{\sharp}, v)$.

To illustrate [S3], let $r=0$ (so that $k=1$) and $s=2$ (so that $l=2$), and let $\mathcal A$ be a tensor in $\mathcal T^0_2(V)$. Then the tensor $S^1_2(\mathcal A)$ in $\mathcal T^1_1(V)$ is given by

$$S^1_2(\mathcal A)(\eta,v)=\mathcal A(v,\eta^S).$$

**Example 6.3.2.** Let $\mathcal A$ be a tensor in $\mathcal T^1_1(V)$. For $r=s=1$, there are sharp maps for $k=1,2$ and $l=1$. For $k=1$ and $l=1$, the tensor $S^1_1(\mathcal A)$ in $\mathcal T^0_2(V)$ is given by

**Example 6.3.3.** For a tensor $\mathcal B$ in $\mathcal T^1_3(V)$, the tensor $S^2_1(\mathcal B)$ in $\mathcal T^2_2(V)$ is given by

with respect to $\mathcal H$.
