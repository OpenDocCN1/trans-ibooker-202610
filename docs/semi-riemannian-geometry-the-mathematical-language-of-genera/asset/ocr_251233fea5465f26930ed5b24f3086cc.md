This shows that parallel translation of a vector from one point to another in $\mathrm{PIn}$ is independent of the choice of smooth curve joining the two points. In particular, parallel translation of a vector along the entire trajectory of a closed smooth curve in $\mathrm{PIn}$, such as a circle, takes the vector to itself.

**Example 18.5.4 ($S^2$).** Let us view the regular surface $S^2$, discussed in Section 13.4, as a smooth 2-manifold with the connection defined in Example 18.1.1. Let

$\theta(t):(a,b)\longrightarrow(0,\pi)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\phi(t):(a,b)\longrightarrow(-\pi,\pi)$

be diffeomorphisms, and consider the smooth curve $\lambda(t):(a,b)\longrightarrow S^2$ given by

$$\lambda(t)=\varphi(\theta(t),\phi(t))=\bigl(\sin(\theta(t))\cos(\phi(t)),\sin(\theta(t))\sin(\phi(t)),\cos(\theta(t))\bigr).$$

The coordinate basis at $\lambda(t)$ is $(H_1|_{(\theta(t),\phi(t))},H_2|_{(\theta(t),\phi(t))})$, where

$$H_1|_{(\theta(t),\phi(t))}=\bigl(\cos(\theta(t))\cos(\phi(t)),\cos(\theta(t))\sin(\phi(t)),-\sin(\theta(t))\bigr)$$

$$H_2|_{(\theta(t),\phi(t))}=\bigl(-\sin(\theta(t))\sin(\phi(t)),\sin(\theta(t))\cos(\phi(t)),0\bigr).$$

Setting

$$v=\frac{d\lambda}{dt}(t_0) \tag{18.5.2}$$

in Theorem 18.5.1 and recalling the results on Christoffel symbols in Section 13.4, the differential equations in Example 18.5.2 become

$$\frac{d\alpha^1}{dt}(t)-\cos(\theta(t))\sin(\theta(t))\frac{d\phi}{dt}(t)\alpha^2(t)=0$$

$$\frac{d\alpha^2}{dt}(t)+\cot(\theta(t))\left(\frac{d\phi}{dt}(t)\alpha^1(t)+\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)\alpha^2(t)\right)=0, \tag{18.5.3}$$

where we have used

$$(x\circ\lambda(t),x^2\circ\lambda(t))=(x^1,x^2)\circ\lambda(t)=\varphi^{-1}\circ\lambda(t)=(\theta(t),\phi(t)).$$

For a given point $\theta_0$ in $(0,\pi)$, let $\theta$ be the function that has constant value $\theta_0$, and let $\phi$ be the identity function. As well, let us denote $t$ by $\phi$, so that $\phi_0=t_0$ and $\phi$ is in $(-\pi,\pi)$. Setting $c_0=\cos(\theta_0)$ and $s_0=\sin(\theta_0)$, we have

$$\lambda(\phi)=(s_0\cos(\phi),s_0\sin(\phi),c_0) \tag{18.5.4}$$

$$\frac{d\lambda}{d\phi}(\phi)=(-s_0\sin(\phi),s_0\cos(\phi),0) \tag{18.5.5}$$

$$H_1|_{(\theta_0,\phi)}=(c_0\cos(\phi),c_0\sin(\phi),-s_0) \tag{18.5.6}$$

$$H_2|_{(\theta_0,\phi)}=(-s_0\sin(\phi),s_0\cos(\phi),0).$$
