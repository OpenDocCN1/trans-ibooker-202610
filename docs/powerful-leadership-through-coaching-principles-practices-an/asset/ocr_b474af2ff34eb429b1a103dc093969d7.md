# Coaching with a Mindset of Abundance

He humbly deferred his personal and professional fame and success to his teams and players. He defined his success in terms of their success, not only in basketball but also in life. He spent hours drilling and practicing, but he never talked about winning. He focused on being prepared and maximizing everyone’s potential.

You can be abundant only when you feel good about yourself. Only then do you want to help those around you feel good about themselves, too. This comes from inner security and confidence. When you see people acting in a controlling, abusive, arrogant, or demeaning manner toward others, their behavior is a symptom of their lack of self-esteem and inner security. They need to put someone else down to feel good about themselves.

By contrast, Coach Wooden always sought to build people up. All players knew that Coach Wooden cared deeply about them as persons above and beyond any of the Xs and Os or basketball drills.

> “Outstanding leaders go out of the way to boost the self-esteem of their personnel,” as Wal-Mart founder Sam Walton said. “If people have confidence and believe in themselves, it’s amazing what they can accomplish.”
