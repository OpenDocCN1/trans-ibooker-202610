# MACHINES THAT SERVE PEOPLE

How far away are we from having the ability to alter what defines us as humans? Few people today would argue against titanium joints for the infirm, pacemakers to steady a heartbeat, or brainwave-detecting sensors for commanding the paralyzed limbs of spine injury patients. Today, we are already capable of altering the genetic makeup of people to change the physical direction from which we grow. In animals, scientists have discovered genes that control not only physical traits but certain behaviors as well.

The abilities to change human physical composition, reprogram our behaviors, and gain access to seemingly limitless computing and storage capacity to make real-time decisions are potentially the building blocks of a new race, made up of modified and computerized humans—smarter, stronger, more moral, and so on. We may not even be that far from determining how to store human intelligence and consciousness inside a computer, to live a life virtually (or if that’s too bold, to serve as a backup copy in case the all-flesh version is damaged).

Heady stuff (pun intended).
