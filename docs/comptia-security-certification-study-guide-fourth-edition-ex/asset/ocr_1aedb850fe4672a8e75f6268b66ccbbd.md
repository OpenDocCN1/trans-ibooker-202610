# Risk Control Assessment and Risk Control Self-Assessment

*Risk control assessment* is the term used when an assessment is performed by a third party that understands the business risk objectives and performs a review of the effectiveness of the security controls to minimize risk.

*Risk control self-assessment (RCSA)* is the term used to describe the process of internal staff identifying risk and evaluating the effectiveness of the controls that are protecting the asset from risk. The goal of the RCSA is to ensure the reliability of the controls in place, to ensure the safety of the assets, and to ensure compliance with laws, regulations, and the security policy.

# Risk Terms

The Security+ exam will test you on a number of other terms related to risk and performing risk analysis. Some of these terms have been discussed in other areas of the chapters, but a nice listing of the terms makes it easy to review them before taking the certification exam:

+   - **Risk awareness** Involves raising awareness of the risks to your assets and understanding the impact of the risks, how to mitigate those risks, and how to manage any residual risks.
+   - **Inherent risk** Refers to the natural risks associated with a specific activity or business process before a mitigation technique is applied to reduce the risk.
+   - **Residual risk** The amount of risk that remains after a mitigation technique has been applied to reduce the risk.
+   - **Control risk** Also known as *risk control*, this is the process of identifying threats against the assets and identifying controls that can be put in place to protect them.
+   - **Risk appetite** The level of risk the company is willing to accept based on its goals and objectives. The risk appetite will help determine if a control is needed to protect an asset.

# Regulations That Affect Risk Posture

Every cybersecurity program should contain a list of controls that should be used to protect company assets such as systems, network devices, and data. The term *security posture* refers to how secure those assets are based on the mitigation techniques implemented. There are a number of steps a company can take to improve its security posture and lower its risk posture, such as performing a risk assessment, implementing security controls to mitigate risk, educating employees on security and risk, and implementing an incident response plan.

Regulations that govern your organization have a direct impact on your risk posture. For example, if a company is processing credit cards, it must follow the compliance requirements set out by the *Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)*. Following these requirements helps create a more secure environment when handling credit card information and, as a result, lowers your risk posture.
