## CERTIFICATION OBJECTIVE 20.03: Performing a Penetration Test

When performing a penetration test, you are simulating true attacks that the organization would experience from a real attacker. This means that you will build up a toolkit of common penetration testing tools, which are programs used by attackers to compromise systems, crack encryption, or tap into the communication.

### Considerations and Techniques Used in a Penetration Test

You need to understand a number of considerations and techniques before diving into the fun part of penetration testing, which is working with the tools used to perform a penetration test. In this section you learn a number of penetration testing terms that the Security+ objectives make a specific reference to.

### Types of Testing

When performing security testing, and specifically penetration testing, understand that there are different types of testing. You can perform what was formerly known as a black box test, a white box test, or a gray box test. The following outlines the differences among the three:

- **Unknown environment test** Formerly known as a *black box* test. When an unknown environment test is being performed, or a pentester (penetration tester) is hired to perform the test, the goal is for the tester to have no information on the organization or its network configuration. The tester will have to act as a hacker and discover the details of the organization and its configuration on their own and then simulate the attacks. This type of test would take the longest because the tester has to figure out what assets the organization has before trying to compromise them.
- **Known environment test** Formerly known as a *white box* test. With a known environment test, you (or the consultants you hire to do the test) are given all the details about the organization’s assets and configuration. In this type of test, the goal is to see if the systems can be compromised. Although this type of test is quicker than the unknown environment test, it does not give the tester any idea of how easy or hard it may be for someone to discover information about the organization.
- **Partially known environment test** Formerly known as a *gray box* test. A partially known environment test is in the middle: the tester gets some details about the organization and its configuration, but only limited details. For example, the tester may get a list of IP addresses used by the organization and will have to figure out what is running on those IP addresses and then simulate an attack.

> **Exam Watch**
> The Security+ exam objectives use the terms *known, unknown, and partially known environment tests*, but I have found there are still references to black box, white box, and gray box testing on the exam. For this reason, know both sets of terminology for the certification exam. Remember that a black box test is when the tester has been given no details on the network setup, whereas with a white box test, the tester is given all the details of the environment.

### Penetration Testing Concepts

When you’re performing a penetration test, there are some core concepts you should be familiar with. The following list of concepts comprises the key points identified by the Security+ objectives, but these are not the only concepts to know (PenTest+ covers more details on performing a penetration test):

- **Rules of engagement** The rules of engagement should be set in the initial stages of the penetration test. They are the rules for the penetration test, such as what should be tested, when testing should occur, and who is allowed to communicate with the pentest team.
- **Lateral movement** Lateral movement is using a compromised system to compromise another system on the network. For example, after grabbing the password hashes on one server, the pentester may try to connect to a system that is not vulnerable using the password hashes in a technique known as pass-the-hash.
- **Bug bounty** A bug bounty is compensation and recognition that is paid to a person who has identified a bug in a piece of software and reports that bug to the vendor of the software.
- **Pivoting** Pivoting is when the tester compromises a server, such as a web server, and then uses that system to gain access to other systems connected to the same network as that system. It is important to note that penetration testing is a long, drawn-out process, like hacking, and may take time before the final system is attacked.

### Passive and Active Reconnaissance

When conducting a penetration test, you will perform passive and active reconnaissance, which is the discovering of information either by not communicating with the target systems (passive) or by scanning the target systems (active). The following are some techniques used to discover assets during a penetration test:

- **Drones** You could use drones to fly over a facility and take video footage of the layout of the land and the building. This could help you understand weaknesses in physical security controls such as understanding the use of fences, guards, and exits.
- **War driving** War driving is a wireless network attack technique where the attacker drives around using a wireless scanner such as Kismet to discover wireless networks and their characteristics, such as the operating channel, frequency, and security protocols configured.
- **War flying** War flying is a form of war driving where the attacker uses low-flying private planes to scan areas for wireless networks. Using a wireless scanner such as Kismet, attackers fly at 1500 feet to discover a multitude of wireless networks.
- **Footprinting** As you will learn later in this chapter, *footprinting* is another term for reconnaissance, which is discovering information about the intended target. You can use Internet resources to passively discover information about a company, or you can use scanning tools to actively discover information about systems running on the target network.

### OSINT

You can find a wealth of information about a company on the Internet, such as e-mail addresses as well as servers or devices connected to the Internet. Using Open Source Intelligence (OSINT) tools, you can easily retrieve information about a company that is available publicly. Maltego is an example of an OSINT tool that can collect e-mail addresses, files, phone numbers, DNS records, IP addresses, and geo-locations of assets for a company.

### Exercise Types

When performing a penetration test, you can use different types of testing. You could be performing the pentest so that you can attempt to hack into company assets (the goal of a red team), or maybe your goal is to see if the defensive team (the blue team) can detect that the attack is occurring and stop it. The following are the different security teams your company may employ:

- **Red team** The red team is the cybersecurity team that takes a methodological approach to attacking systems and attempting to bypass security controls. They are the attackers.
- **Blue team** The blue team is the cybersecurity team responsible for detecting an attack and protecting company assets by stopping the attack in its tracks. They are the defenders.
- **White team** The white team in cybersecurity is the team that oversees the security test and records the progress of both the red team and blue team and assigns the scores. The white team also reviews the security reports created by both the red team and the blue team to rate them based on their accuracy.
- **Purple team** A purple team in cybersecurity is a team that performs the functions of both the red team and the blue team.

> **Exam Watch**
> For the exam remember that the red team is responsible for performing attacks against the systems during a security test (the bad guys), while the blue team is responsible for detecting the attack and preventing a compromise (the good guys).
