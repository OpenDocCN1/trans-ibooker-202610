To add the points P and Q, a line is drawn through the two points. This line will intersect the elliptic curve in exactly one more point, called –R. The point –R is reflected in the x-axis to the point R. The law for addition in an elliptic curve group is P + Q = R (Bos, et al., 2004).

The line through P and –P is a vertical line that does not intersect the elliptic curve at a third point; thus, the points P and –P cannot be added as previously. It is for this reason that the elliptic curve group includes the point at infinity, O. By definition, P + (–P) = O. As a result of this equation, P + O = P in the elliptic curve group. O is called the additive identity of the elliptic curve group; all elliptic curves have an additive identity (see Figure 15.3).
