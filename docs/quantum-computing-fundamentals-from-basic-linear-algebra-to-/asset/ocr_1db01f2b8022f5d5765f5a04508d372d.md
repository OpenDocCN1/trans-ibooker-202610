This means we have two eigenvalues:

$\lambda_1 = 8$

$\lambda_2 = -1$

For a $2 \times 2$ matrix, you will always get two eigenvalues. In fact, for any $n \times n$ matrix, you will get n eigenvalues, but they may not be unique.

Now that you have the eigenvalues, how do you calculate the eigenvectors?

We know the following:

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 2 \\ 9 & 2 \end{bmatrix} $$

$\lambda_1 = 8$

$\lambda_2 = -1$

We are seeking unknown vectors, so let us label the vector $\begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \end{bmatrix}$.

Now recall the equation that gives us eigenvectors and eigenvalues:

$A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}$

Let us take one of our eigenvalues and plug it in:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 2 \\ 9 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \end{bmatrix} = 8 \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ \begin{bmatrix} 5x + 2y \\ 9x + 2y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 8X \\ 8Y \end{bmatrix} $$

This gives us two equations:

$5x + 2y = 8x$

$9x + 2y = 8y$

Now we take the first equation and do a bit of algebra to isolate the y value. Subtract the 5x from each side to get

$2y = 3x$

Then divide both sides by 2 to get

$y = 3/2 x$
