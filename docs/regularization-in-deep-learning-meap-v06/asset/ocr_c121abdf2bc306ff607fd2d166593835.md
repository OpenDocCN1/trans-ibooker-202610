Under-parameterized regime where EMC is sufficiently lower than the training sample size n. Enhancing the training procedure will reduce the test risk.

Critical regime where EMC is comparable to the training sample size n. Enhancing the training procedure may either decrease or increase the test risk.

Over-parameterized regime where EMC is sufficiently larger than the training sample size n. Enhancing the training procedure will reduce the test risk.

Both the left and the right zones represent areas where the test risk decreases as the training procedure enhances, such as enlarging the model size or training for more epochs. In the middle zone, however, the test risk may either increase or decrease if the training procedure changes.

When training over-capacitated linear models such as the polynomial regression with an excessive degree of polynomial as in the previous chapter, explicitly penalizing the magnitude of the parameters could introduce the inductive bias over smoother functions and thus improves generalization. When training over-capacitated deep neural networks, the stochastic gradient descent algorithm plays an implicit regularization effect by favoring low-variance models and thus improving generalization. The following section will focus on training interpolating polynomial regression model with smoothing constraints and show empirical evidence for the “double descent” phenomenon as the model becomes overly complex.
