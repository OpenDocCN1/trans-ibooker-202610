Before observing any data, we would assume a particular prior distribution (along with its governing parameters) to w, which could be a simple standard normal distribution w~N(0,1), or other forms based on our prior knowledge or domain expertise about the system under study.
After assuming a specific prior distribution for w, we would then update this prior distribution to form a posterior distribution based on the observed data, in particular, the likelihood of seeing the available data we have. For example, using the Bayes’ rule, the updated posterior distribution for w could be N(0.2,0.9) after observing some data. The prior distribution thus serves as a helpful inductive bias to warm-start the estimation process, which iteratively gets updated in the form of the posterior distribution calibrated by the observed data. Having a good prior distribution could thus greatly accelerate the process of locating the best posterior distribution. Figure 6.2 illustrates this example.
