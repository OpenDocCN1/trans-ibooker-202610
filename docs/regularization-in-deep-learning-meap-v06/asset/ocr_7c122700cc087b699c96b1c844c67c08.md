Also, (X,y) is just one collection of observations, which are assumed to be independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.) after drawing these random samples from P(x,y). If we keep drawing samples and generating additional datasets from the true distribution P(x,y), we will likely get a different incarnation of training dataset than (X,y). In fact, this is exactly the motivation behind data augmentation, a technique to create additional artificial samples (called bootstrapped samples) for training purposes. By artificially generating additional training sets, the model will be exposed to more realizations of the dataset and will hopefully learn to become more robust to changes in the data. The resulting sample may not always contribute to the model learning since it may contain more irrelevant samples then the original dataset, but in general exposing more variations in the training datasets helps improving the model training.
