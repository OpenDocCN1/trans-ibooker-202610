This chapter examines the typical training process for one of the most fundamental models in supervised learning: linear regression, which develops a linear model that maps the input features to the output target. By going over the process of training a simple linear regression model, we will better understand the different training components, including data, model, cost, and optimization. We will cover the mathematical intuition in each part, which is essential to understand various modeling operations. We will also put these concepts in a proper context to motivate their usage, as we believe effective learning starts with understanding the context. We will also accompany the math with just enough implementation details to better put things in perspective and appreciate both theory and practice at the same time. After going over the building blocks, we will introduce the classical view on generalization, covering key concepts such as empirical risk minimization and bias-variance decomposition. The necessary foundational knowledge in machine learning models will help us transition into the modern view on generalization covered in the next chapter.
