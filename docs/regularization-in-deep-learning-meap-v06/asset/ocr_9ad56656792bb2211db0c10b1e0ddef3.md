Q(w_{t+1}, D) \approx Q(w_t, D) + (w_{t+1} - w_t) \nabla_w Q(w_t, D)

Since SGD requires w_{t+1} = w_t - \nabla_w Q(w_t, D_t), we have w_{t+1} - w_t = - \nabla_w Q(w_t, D_t). Plugging back to the previous equation gives the following:

Q(w_{t+1}, D) \approx Q(w_t, D) - \eta \nabla_w Q(w_t, D_t) \nabla_w Q(w_t, D)

As we are interested in the average-case behavior of SGD updates, we apply the expectation operator to both sides, which gives:

E[Q(w_{t+1}, D)] \approx Q(w_t, D) - \eta E[\nabla_w Q(w_t, D_t)] \nabla_w Q(w_t, D)

where we have used the fact that E[Q(w_t, D)] = Q(w_t, D), since there is no randomness in the historical updates.

Now let us bring in the unbiasedness property of SGD estimator, i.e., E[Q(w, D_t)] = Q(w, D). Since the expectation operator and the gradient operator are exchangeable, we have the following:

E[\nabla_w Q(w_t, D_t)] = \nabla_w E[Q(w_t, D_t)] = \nabla_w Q(w_t, D)

Plugging in the previous Taylor expansion approximation gives:

E[Q(w_{t+1}, D)] \approx Q(w_t, D) - \eta \nabla_w Q(w_t, D) \nabla_w Q(w_t, D) = Q(w_t, D) - \eta [\nabla_w Q(w_t, D)]^2
