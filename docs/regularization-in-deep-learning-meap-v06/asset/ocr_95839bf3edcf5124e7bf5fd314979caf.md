$$\frac{\partial}{\partial w_j} RSS(\mathbf{w}) = -2\Phi_j^T (\mathbf{y} - \Phi \mathbf{w})$$
$$= -2 \sum_{i=1}^n \Phi_{ij} \left( y_i - \sum_{k=1}^d \Phi_{ik} w_k \right)$$
[Separate \( w_j \) with other fixed terms]
$$= -2 \sum_{i=1}^n \Phi_{ij} \left( y_i - \sum_{k \neq j} \Phi_{ik} w_k - \Phi_{ij} w_j \right)$$
[Express \( w_j \) as a standalone term]
$$= -2 \sum_{i=1}^n \Phi_{ij} \left( y_i - \sum_{k \neq j} \Phi_{ik} w_k \right) + 2w_j \sum_{i=1}^n \Phi_{ij}^2$$
[Re-express for notational convenience]
$$= -2\rho_j + 2w_j z_j$$

Where \(\rho_j = \sum_{i=1}^n \Phi_{ij} (y_i - \sum_{k \neq j} \Phi_{ik} w_k)\) and \(z_j = \sum_{i=1}^n \Phi_{ij}^2\)

Moving on, let us calculate the partial derivative of the LASSO penalty with respect to \( w_j \). Since the gradient is not available for \( |w_j| \) when \( w_j = 0 \), we can resort to the notation of sub-gradient, which generalizes the gradient to non-differentiable points and refers to all planes (sets of candidates) that lower bound the raw function, in this case \( |w_j| \). Pictorially, as shown in figure 7.22, the sub-gradient at the origin, which is non-differentiable, is a collection of all dotted lines below the function \( |w_j| \). Denoting the sub-gradient as \( V \), we have \( V \in [-1,1] \) when \( w_j = 0 \).
