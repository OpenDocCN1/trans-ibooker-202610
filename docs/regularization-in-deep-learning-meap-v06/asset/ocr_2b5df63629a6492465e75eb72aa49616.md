Figure 3.3 The “double descent” curve that unifies the classical and modern views on generalization. When the model complexity is below the interpolation threshold, the classical bias-variance tradeoff dominates, highlighted by an increasing test risk as the model increases its representational capacity. However, the test risk starts to drop after passing the interpolation threshold. In both cases, the training risk continues to drop as the model increases complexity and remains zero after passing the interpolation threshold.

This “double descent” phenomenon frequently occurs in machine learning models such as the polynomial regression in the previous chapter and deep learning models such as convolutional neural networks. As the model increases complexity in the underparameterized region, the test risk will decrease in the first descent and increase later on, following the U-shaped curve that highlights the bias-variance tradeoff. As the model becomes sufficiently complex to interpolate the training data, increasing the model complexity will enter the over-parameterized region and incur a second descent.

However, it remains an uncharted and exciting area of research as to why this almost universal phenomenon occurs. Things become counter-intuitive when models are over-parameterized in the modern regime. For example, the “double descent” phenomenon typically occurs when no particular explicit regularization technique exists in deep neural networks. It could, however, be avoided when using proper regularization techniques.
