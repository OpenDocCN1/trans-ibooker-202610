In practice, multiple models generalize well to the test set, forming a collection of generalizable models, as represented by the dashed circle in figure 1.14. So our goal is to build a generalizable model that lives in the dashed circle. The purpose of regularizing an overfitting model is to control its complexity and thus increase the chance of reaching anywhere inside the dashed circle. A typical training process follows that, by minimizing the training error, or more specifically, the empirical risk (an indicator on model generalizability such as the average training loss of a model), the resulting best-trained model will hopefully generalize well to the test set, on average. This assumes the available training set and test set are similar in distribution, which may not be the case in practice. We will shed more light on the notion of empirical risk in chapter 2. However, for now, it suffices to know that our model evaluation criterion during training may not be empirical risk alone. A regularized version of empirical risk helps generalize to the test set, especially when the training set is not similar to the distribution test set.
