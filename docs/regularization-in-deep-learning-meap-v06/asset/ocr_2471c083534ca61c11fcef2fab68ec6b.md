# 3.3 Summary

+   - The "double descent" curve, a widely observed phenomenon in training machine learning and deep learning models, subsumes the classical U-shaped curve on bias-variance tradeoff
+   - The twofold descent curve exists as a function of either model complexity or training iteration
+   - The problem of overfitting could be resolved by either increasing the number of parameters, which moves into the over-parameterization regime and implicitly regularizes the model using stochastic gradient descent, or reducing the model capacity by a proper and explicit regularization technique
+   - The effective model complexity is a recently proposed notion to denote the maximum number of samples on which a training procedure achieves close to zero training error on average
+   - More training data may not necessarily boost the model performance due to sample non-monotonicity
+   - The second derivative could be used as a measure of roughness of the fitted polynomial model and combined with MSE via a weighting factor to form the overall cost
