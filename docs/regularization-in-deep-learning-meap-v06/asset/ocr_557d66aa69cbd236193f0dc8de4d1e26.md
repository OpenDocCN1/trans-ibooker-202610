When learning one task, the potentially complex dependence structure in the feature space may impede the model from spotting certain important features, which may stand out when learning another related task. By learning these two tasks together, the model eavesdrops in a way from the other task and utilizes this outside knowledge in the current learning task, thus boosting its learning performance. It should be noted that most MLT deep learning models assume that the tasks come from the same data generating process. Although this assumption benefits the sharing nature among multiple related tasks, it may not always hold in practice. It becomes a challenge to build a robust MTL model when the learning tasks are unrelated or merely loosely related to each other. Part of this challenge can be addressed by allowing the MTL model to learn what to share, such as the cross-stitch networks introduced later. Besides the level of sharing, allowing the model to automatically learn the hierarchical structure with levels of granularities also helps improving the model’s generalization performance. Even if our goal is to learn one task only, leveraging on the knowledge of other tasks, even sometimes adversarial, could often help deliver a better predictive performance.
