Figure 1.7 The evolution of training and test performances as more efforts are invested in exam preparation. We start in the underfitting region, where training and test performances improve as the mental problem-solving model becomes powerful. The model starts to overfit after passing the sweet spot, as reflected by the decreasing test performance. When being in the overfitting region, it is essential to look out for the test performance since the training performance will (only) continue improving as the model gets even more complex.

However, if we continue to invest more hours into the study and aim for perfect scores in the practice exam, we may risk overfitting our mental model. At some point, we may start to memorize the solution to each training question, especially when we cannot work out an immediate solution. Given limited study time, perfectly memorizing the answers can directly get us full marks in the practice exam. Unfortunately, pushing too hard does not guarantee an equal payoff. Our test performance will start to drop due to a lack of generalization capacity, and the generalization gap will grow. Besides, given so many practice questions in the market, who knows the answers provided are noise-free and 100% correct? The point is, beware of lower test performance if we only focus on getting the correct answers instead of learning the patterns.

Remember, our goal is to train a good enough mental model through the practice exam and use the trained mental model to obtain high marks in the final test exam hopefully. A model that generalizes well to the test set means the complexity of the model is good enough, and the core problem-solving patterns learned can be well applied to new questions in the final exam. We wish to reduce the generalization gap between training and test performances as much as possible. However, a higher model complexity does not necessarily translate to a smaller generalization gap.

There is often a sweet spot that represents the best performance in the final exam, and it often is associated with good enough training performance in the practice exam.
