Figure 1.8 Four possible bias-variance scenarios. The first region has high bias and low variance, due to the trained model being too simple and possibly underfitting. The second region is the ideal state, with bias and variance both being low. The third region has high bias and variance, suggesting that the model deserves further training to learn the underlying pattern. The fourth region indicates an overfitting model that has low bias but high variance.

In figure 1.8, the first and fourth regions correspond to underfitting and overfitting, respectively. When training a model, we usually start from the third region, where both errors are high. As the model gradually improves and becomes more complex, we will come across the first region due to better pattern recognition - the model’s predictions become more stable by learning the underlying patterns. Now, if we are lucky, we may reach the second region, where both errors are low, without much trouble. However, it is more common in practical model training to see excessively trained and overly complex models that may get stuck in the fourth region.

Proper regularization could help us reach the target region, namely the low bias and low variance region, in multiple ways. As shown in figure 1.9, example training paths could follow 3 -> 1 -> 2, 3 -> 1 -> 4 -> 2, or others, depending on the specific regularization strategies used.
