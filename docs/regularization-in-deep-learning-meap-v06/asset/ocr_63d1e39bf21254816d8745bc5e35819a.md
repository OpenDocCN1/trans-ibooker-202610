# 1.3 Understanding underfitting and overfitting

Underfitting refers to poor performance in training and test sets due to the trained model
being too simple or the training data being too insufficient. Overfitting means the trained
model performs well on the training set, a specific subset of the overall population, but does
not generalize well to other test sets from the same population. In other words, the
generalization error resulting from predicting the test set, or the generalization gap between
the training set performance and the test set performance, is pretty big. However, why do
we care so much about model generalizability, i.e., its predictive performance on the future
unseen test set? To answer this question, let us go back to the exam example earlier. This
time, we will use the amount of effort to proxy the model complexity; the harder we study,
the more complex our mental model will be.

It helps to observe the evolution of model performance in both training and final exams.
As shown in figure 1.7, as we spend more hours studying and honing our mental model, we
improve our problem-solving skills and get better scores in both training (practice) and test
(final) exams. We may also notice that moving from 60 to 70 marks is much easier than
from 80 to 90, or even 100; the amount of effort needed exponentially increases for the
same unit improvement in exam marks.
