Recall the old aphorism in statistics: all models are wrong, but some are useful. Since a
model is essentially a hypothesis that we assume to approximate the underlying data
generating function, the inductive bias exists in all models, expressed either explicitly or
implicitly. For example, a linear model assumes linearity in the weights, while a non-linear
model applies non-linear transformations depending on the model used. Inductive bias thus
allows us to infuse the model with adequate external knowledge from the real world or based
on past experience, particularly when the training data is insufficient to support a proper
training process.
