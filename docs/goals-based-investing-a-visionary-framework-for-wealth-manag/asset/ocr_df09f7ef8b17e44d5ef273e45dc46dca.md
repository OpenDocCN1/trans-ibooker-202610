INVESTORS DO NOT ALWAYS ACT RATIONALLY. AFTER ALL, WE ARE only human, and we often respond emotionally to events. Most traditional finance theories assume that people *do* act rationally, considering all available information when they make investment decisions. Behavioral finance challenges that assumption and explores how individuals actually respond to stimuli. It suggests how advisors can help investors make sound decisions.

Harry Markowitz, Bill Sharpe, and Eugene Fama won Nobel Prizes for their work in defining investment theory. Markowitz is generally recognized as the father of modern portfolio theory.
